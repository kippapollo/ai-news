SpaceX closes $60 billion all-stock takeover of Cursor in largest coding-tools deal on record

SpaceX filed an 8-K at market close Friday confirming completion of the $60 billion all-stock acquisition of Anysphere, folding Cursor into the SpaceXAI unit alongside Grok Build, Grok Bot and Grok 4.6. Cursor shareholders receive SpaceX Class A stock at a 105 percent premium to the June IPO price. Morgan Stanley projects $2.5 billion of incremental 2026 revenue and $13 billion in 2027. Anthropic and OpenAI both filed European Commission comments urging deeper scrutiny of third-party model access foreclosure; the Commission cleared under Phase I without commitment offers.

Bloomberg / Yahoo Finance · 2026-08-14 · Source ↗

The 8-K hit the tape at 16:04 EDT Friday. SpaceX confirmed the close of its $60 billion all-stock acquisition of Anysphere, parent of Cursor, ending a takeover the two companies first sketched in April, formalized in June, and cleared through United States, European Union and United Kingdom regulators inside the June-to-August window. Cursor shareholders receive SpaceX Class A common stock at an implied equity value of $60 billion, a 105 percent premium to Cursor's June 12 IPO price of $29.3 billion. Anysphere becomes a wholly owned SpaceX subsidiary reporting into the SpaceXAI unit Elon Musk runs. The transaction is the largest coding-tools acquisition on record and dwarfs Microsoft's $7.5 billion GitHub deal in 2018.

The structure carries no cash. Every dollar of consideration flows through SpaceX equity. Cursor's engineering roster crosses over with the acquisition; CEO Michael Truell and cofounder Aman Sanger both take SVP roles under Musk with two-year non-competes and four-year equity vesting on their SpaceX Class A stock. Truell keeps the Cursor CEO title. Cursor's post on the official X account, published minutes after the 8-K, read: "Cursor is now part of SpaceX. Today, we have officially closed our acquisition. We will join the SpaceXAI team to help make Grok the world's most useful AI." Musk replied with a single word: "Welcome."

"Cursor is my favorite enterprise AI service." — Jensen Huang, Nvidia CEO, at last month's GTC keynote

The revenue math and the model concentration

Morgan Stanley published fresh models Friday afternoon showing Cursor at roughly $4 billion annual recurring revenue as of June 2026 and projecting a run-rate of $33 billion by 2030. The bank attributes $2.5 billion in incremental 2026 revenue to SpaceX and $13 billion in 2027, working from the assumption that SpaceX will bundle Cursor Ultra and Cursor Premium Teams into the SuperGrok Heavy subscription starting September 1. Cursor management said on the closing call that 64 percent of the Fortune 500 now use its tools, with 50,000-plus enterprises paying seat licenses. Cursor already contributed the training-data corpus and reinforcement-learning traces that produced Grok 4.5 in July, and Grok 4.6 shipped this week as the first Grok release integrated as a first-party model inside the Cursor IDE.

The engineering integration has been running for twelve weeks. Cursor engineers ran approximately 340 million tool-invocation trajectories through the Colossus supercomputer alongside the SpaceXAI post-training team, generating the reinforcement-learning-from-agent-execution corpus that lifted Grok 4.6 to 89.4 percent on Terminal-Bench 2.1, second only to Anthropic Opus 5. The Colossus cluster runs roughly a million H100-equivalent units of compute across two campuses in Memphis and Kingsburg, a scale that Cursor management said in the closing call would let the combined stack cut model training and post-training iteration time by roughly 60 percent versus Cursor's pre-acquisition setup.

"It's been a pleasure working with the SpaceXAI team on it." — Aman Sanger, Cursor cofounder, in an internal Loom video circulated to employees at 09:14 Pacific

The vendor foreclosure question the deal now poses

The competitive shape shifts. Anthropic ships Claude Code at v2.1.232 with Opus 5 as the default and auto mode as the default permission model as of August 14. OpenAI ships Codex CLI at 0.148.0-alpha.18. Meta ships Muse Code under a source-available license. DeepSeek shipped deepseek-harness under MIT the same week. SpaceXAI now controls Cursor plus Grok 4.6 plus the Grok Bot mobile client plus the Grok Build web agent. The five-vendor coding-agent race that opened in H1 2026 has consolidated into four vendors inside twelve hours, and three of the four hold both a frontier model and a coding-agent product in one shop.

Anthropic and OpenAI both filed European Commission comments urging deeper scrutiny of potential foreclosure of third-party model access inside Cursor, and both letters became public through the Commission's transparency portal Friday afternoon. Both labs have signaled they will pull first-party access to Cursor within the next twelve months, which would force SpaceX to route Cursor traffic exclusively through Grok models and threaten the enterprise-Fortune-500 base that Morgan Stanley's projections depend on. Prediction markets stayed skeptical of the strategic upside. Kalshi and Polymarket both show xAI at 13 percent probability of shipping the industry's best coding model by year-end, well behind Anthropic at 54 percent and OpenAI at 30 percent. The gap reflects both Grok's slower ramp on agentic-coding benchmarks and the fact that Cursor's differentiation historically came from routing across models rather than from model quality itself.

Regulatory calm surprised observers. The FTC and DOJ passed on second requests, and the European Commission cleared under Phase I without commitment offers. That contrasts with the Commission's more aggressive posture on last year's Microsoft-Inflection tie-up. SPCX closed down 1.8 percent on Friday at $131.62 on volume of 42.3 million shares, versus a 60-day average of 18.1 million. Cursor's revenue and headcount consolidate into SpaceX's financial reporting starting with the Q4 2026 quarter that begins October 1. Anthropic secondary marks stayed flat at $965 billion implied on Forge Global. OpenAI secondary marks moved to $860 billion, up from $852 billion.

Anthropic flips Claude Code auto mode to default with classifier catching 89 percent of dangerous commands

Anthropic · 2026-08-14 · Source ↗

Auto mode became the default permission model for Claude Code on Pro, Max and Team plans on August 14. Anthropic's 1,053-tester controlled study measured 89 percent classifier catch of dangerous commands versus 13.6 percent for humans on first try, dropping to 5 percent after 50 approvals. Auto-mode users ship 25 percent more pull requests.

Anthropic's scheduled auto-mode default flip for Claude Code went live on August 14, changing the baseline permission behavior for all new sessions on Pro, Max and Team plans. In auto mode, Claude Code stops asking for human approval on every tool call and instead runs a classifier over each proposed action, halting only when the classifier determines the action is irreversible, destructive or reaches outside the user's environment. The change follows a week of tester messaging and one-time in-product opt-out prompts for users with pinned defaults.

The safety math Anthropic published to justify the flip is the load-bearing piece. In a controlled study of 1,053 external testers, auto mode's classifier caught 89 percent of dangerous commands on the first try. Human reviewers on the same trace set caught 13.6 percent, dropping to 5 percent after 50 approval prompts as fatigue set in. Real-world telemetry showed production-level harmful actions fired 2.6 times more often in manually approved sessions at 6.3 percent than in auto-mode sessions at 2.4 percent. In red-team testing after hardening, auto mode's miss rate improved from 12 percent to 7 percent. Anthropic reports zero successful prompt-injection attacks against Claude models under auto mode in the current battery.

"Employees view it as a weight off their shoulders. They don't have to monitor agents anymore." — Evan Magnussen, Garner Health Platform Engineering Manager

Rubber-stamp behavior collapsed the case for the old default

Human-approval behavior collapsed the case for the old default. Developers approved 97 percent of permission prompts, rejecting only 3 percent. On plans, users reject 39 percent of Claude's proposed plans but only 3 percent of individual permission requests once the plan is running. Humans review carefully at the plan stage and then rubber-stamp mechanically. Auto mode moves the guarantee from human vigilance to classifier vigilance and reserves human intervention for actions that leave the sandbox.

"I kicked off an agent at 10 p.m. and it kept running until 5 a.m., and it gave me three PRs." — Kai Zhou, Nuro Staff Software Engineer

Productivity data alongside the safety numbers pushes the release. Anthropic reports auto-mode users ship approximately 25 percent more pull requests. Adobe's Tomislav Reil said his team "built an agentic loop that rapidly accelerated our work." Gusto reports about 10 percent of sessions include classifier denials, indicating active protection is triggering regularly rather than sitting dormant. Classifier compute overhead is no longer billed to Pro, Max and Team plans starting the same week.

Auto mode stays opt-in on Claude Enterprise, the direct Claude API, Amazon Bedrock, Google Cloud's Agent Platform and Microsoft Foundry. Anthropic framed the enterprise carve-out as time for admins to review the classifier behavior against internal change-management policies before default flip. Users on Pro, Max or Team plans who had previously set a manual default receive a one-time prompt asking whether to switch; pinned defaults hold. The 89 versus 13.6 percent number is doing heavy lifting because it reframes the coding-agent oversight debate away from human-in-the-loop dogma and toward whether the classifier is actually right.

DeepSeek shipped deepseek-harness v0.1 as a developer preview at github.com/deepseek-ai/deepseek-harness under the MIT license, opening the runtime layer that ties DeepSeek's V4-Pro and V4-Flash models to autonomous coding-agent behavior. The repository crossed 27,000 GitHub stars and roughly 2,000 forks within hours of the launch tweet from the official DeepSeek account, according to coverage from VentureBeat, TechNode and BigDATAwire on August 14.

The design pitch is one line on the README: everything is a plugin. Models, tools, skills, sessions, sandboxes, filesystems, loops, scheduling and the web UI all sit behind the pre-existing Cordis plugin kernel that DeepSeek's UI team has used internally since 2023. Sessions run through an append-only event log, so any session can be resumed, forked, searched or replayed. That matches the architecture Anthropic added to Claude Code's session store in v2.1 but does it under a permissive license and outside a vendor console.

"Everything is a plugin. Models, tools, skills, sessions, sandboxes, storage, loops, scheduling, UI." — deepseek-harness README

Four preset runtime modes and the SpaceX-Cursor timing

The v0.1.0-rc.5 release ships four preset runtime modes. Standard mode is the daily-driver agent with file editing, shell access, web search, skills and planning tools. Code mode replaces the wire-level tool set with a single run_code call and provides TypeScript SDK bindings for agent authors who want the model to write executor code rather than pick from a menu. Minimal mode strips the toolset to two calls, persistent bash and str_replace_editor, and documents DeepSeek's V4-Pro-0813 benchmark methodology in the repo's BENCHMARK.md footnotes. Creator mode adds runtime inspection endpoints for developers writing new plugins.

"We're opening it up to developers building agent harnesses worldwide and open-sourcing the codebase in MIT license." — DeepSeek official X account

Positioning is unsubtle. The project frames itself directly against Anthropic's Claude Code and Claude Cowork, both of which run under Anthropic's proprietary agent runtime with no equivalent extension surface. Meta's Muse Code, released August 10 under Apache 2.0, targets the same coding-agent niche but ships as a monolith without the plugin kernel. OpenAI's Codex CLI is Apache-2.0 but its plugin surface is limited to MCP servers and shell tools. Cui Tianyi, who joined DeepSeek from Jane Street in March 2026 to build the harness team, leads the project.

Two integrations landed inside 24 hours. Ollama v0.32.11 added an ollama launch dsh command that boots deepseek-harness against a locally served model, and the Codex CLI 0.148.0-alpha.18 build gained a --harness deepseek flag that routes agent loops through Harness rather than Codex's built-in runner. The release also gives outside developers a reason to keep using DeepSeek's models directly rather than through hosted alternatives, offsetting the pricing hike for V4-Pro. The README warns "THERE WILL BE COMPATIBILITY-BREAKING CHANGES" during the preview phase, with a v1.0 stable target for Q4 2026. The MIT license and public BENCHMARK.md configuration together open a clean replication path for DeepSeek's disputed benchmark claims.

Anthropic promoted Claude Code v2.1.232 on August 14 alongside the flip of auto mode to the default permission model for new sessions on Pro, Max and Team plans. Enterprise and cloud deployments remain opt-in. The version bump ships four functional additions and a wide fix set for Remote Control, permissions, networking and session stability.

The subagent forking change is technical. A subagent_type: "fork" subagent now inherits the full conversation and prompt cache from the parent session by default, matching how Anthropic's own internal agents behave rather than the previous fresh-context spawn. Non-teammate agent spawns in interactive sessions run in the background by default now, which means the parent session keeps the terminal and the agent posts progress via SendMessage.

"Subagent forking is now on by default." — Claude Code v2.1.232 changelog

Cross-session messaging, Opus 5 default, self-hosted runners

Cross-session messaging is new. Typing @ in a prompt now pops a live-session picker; Claude uses SendMessage to reach the mentioned session directly. Bare-name delivery works when exactly one live session matches; ambiguous names trigger a confirmation dialog. Session names must be unique across a machine, and duplicate names get name-word-word variants automatically. Claude Opus 5 is the new default Opus model in Claude Code. On the Anthropic API and on Max, Team and Enterprise plans, Opus 5 runs with a 1M-token context window. Fable 5 is available as an advisor model through /model fable for organizations with Fable access.

"Type @ in the prompt to mention another Claude session by name." — Claude Code v2.1.232 changelog

The changelog covers additional platform work. GitLab and marketplace support tightened: bare GitLab URLs now clone like GitHub URLs; secret redaction extended to multiple additional token families. Settings aliases additionalMarketplaces and allowedMarketplaces replace previous naming conventions with a compatibility shim.

Gateway validation moved earlier in the boot sequence. The desktop overlay now accepts all released settings, and invalid entries fail at process startup rather than at first tool call. This addresses a class of MCP OAuth failures that plagued Claude Code v2.1.229 through v2.1.231, including the Slack sign-in regression Anthropic hot-fixed inside 24 hours on August 13. Named fixes include permission bypasses on Windows and PowerShell where session context was not propagating to spawned subprocesses, MCP connection hangs when the remote server returned a chunked-encoded 429, Remote Control session reattachment failures after network changes, TUI paste handling of large clipboard payloads, and a slash-command menu positioning bug on high-DPI displays.

The v2.1.232 build closes a two-week release cadence that produced eight patch versions between v2.1.225 and v2.1.232, more than any prior fortnight in Claude Code's release history. Anthropic's release notes attribute the cadence to the auto-mode flip readiness gates.

Yuanyang Yin, Gongxuan Wang, Yifan Zhan, Chuanhao Li, Kaipeng Zhang and Feng Zhao filed the paper under Computer Vision and Pattern Recognition (cs.CV) on August 13, and the accompanying inference stack ships as a supplementary release from the Alaya group at Shanghai AI Laboratory.

The subtitle "From Linear-Scaling Supervision to Endless World" telegraphs the paper's central claim: interactive world models today choke on memory as session length grows because both the model and its supervision cost scale linearly with visited frames. Alaya-EVOKE separates the geometry substrate from the generative denoiser by maintaining scene geometry in an external camera-indexed world state bank, from which only view-relevant information is retrieved at each step. "Scene geometry in an external, camera-indexed world state bank, from which only view-relevant information is retrieved." — Alaya-EVOKE paper

Sparse-attention teacher and three-step student

The teacher design pairs the memory retrieval with a supervision signal that also stops scaling linearly. Sparse attention combines chunk-wise grouping, retrieval of selected distant frames and a linear-attention global state, letting the teacher supervise very long roll-outs at bounded compute per token. The framework then transfers to a three-step student model, dropping classifier-free guidance and cutting the wall-clock cost of interactive inference.

"Each 1.5-second chunk generates in 2.11 seconds on a single H200 GPU at 384×640 resolution." — Alaya-EVOKE paper

The benchmark carries 171 distinct evaluation scenarios, each with a defined objective ranging from single-room navigation with hidden targets to multi-step manipulation puzzles that require the model to remember object placements from earlier frames. Each scenario is scored across four primary assessment areas: geometry consistency, interaction fidelity, out-of-sight evolution and insight evolution, together with basic video quality and controllability metrics that the older VBench line already covered.

"Agent players to interact with world models toward specified long-horizon objectives." — PlayWorld paper

The agent-player design and the uniformly negative result

The core innovation is the agent player. Rather than feeding the world model a curated ground-truth action sequence and asking whether the model reproduces the ground-truth video, PlayWorld hands a multi-modal AI agent the objective and the current frame, lets it pick the next action, and evaluates the entire on-policy roll-out. This surfaces failure modes that fixed-sequence evaluation misses, particularly the class of drift where a model produces plausible individual frames but breaks the persistent state across a long session.

"Current models remain unreliable on long-horizon interactive objectives, particularly in maintaining spatial consistency and persistent state evolution." — PlayWorld paper

Testing across nine advanced world models produced a uniformly negative result. None of the tested models clear 50 percent on the long-horizon aggregate. The benchmark's construction is deliberately hostile to the retrieval-based memory approaches that Alaya-EVOKE and RT-World 3 pushed earlier this month. Out-of-sight evolution scores whether the world updates correctly when the objects that would be updated are outside the current viewport, which forces the model to maintain state even for content it cannot see. Insight evolution scores whether the model correctly re-integrates the returning viewport with its remembered state.

The 171 scenarios ship as reproducible seeds against the Kling video-generation platform and Kuaishou's evaluation cluster, which lowers the bar for third-party reproductions. Hengshuang Zhao's lab at HKU has published four benchmarks in 2026 already, all under permissive licenses. The benchmark's ceiling is intentionally out of reach for current models, matching the pattern GAIA-Long and Terminal-Bench 2.1 established for language agents earlier this year.

Two years of delay end in a first-party build

The China rollout was originally scheduled for early 2025 and slipped repeatedly. Apple had planned to route Apple Intelligence traffic in the mainland market through Baidu's ERNIE and then through Alibaba's Qwen. Both arrangements ran into CAC review friction over training-data provenance and content-filter compliance. Executives inside Cupertino concluded in Q3 2025 that only a first-party model with Chinese-labeled training data would clear approval, and Apple assigned an approximately 240-person team split between Beijing, Shanghai and Cupertino to train and align the model with Alibaba's on-site help.

"Apple is the first foreign company approved to offer a proprietary AI model in the country." — Reuters

The Alibaba relationship is technical rather than commercial. Alibaba's Qwen team provided training infrastructure, safety-alignment scaffolding and a Chinese-language pretraining corpus. Apple contributes the base architecture, tokenizer and fine-tuning methodology derived from its Apple Foundation Models series. Alibaba does not receive licensing fees per query, and Apple owns the resulting model weights outright. Alibaba Cloud does host the offload tier under a services agreement whose terms remain undisclosed. Rollout of the China-only Apple Intelligence experience is expected in the coming months, in time for the iPhone 18 launch cycle. Features include on-device summarization, notification triage, writing tools and image generation constrained by CAC content categories. Cloud offload will route to Alibaba Cloud data centers inside mainland China, and no user data will leave the mainland, according to the disclosure filed by Apple's China subsidiary. That structure differs from the Private Cloud Compute architecture Apple uses in every other market.

The regulatory precedent matters more than the product. Apple executives briefed the White House this week on the China arrangement, and the disclosure caused visible discomfort in the Trump administration's ongoing China-tech strategy discussions. The Bureau of Industry and Security is separately reviewing whether the model's training on H20-tier Nvidia hardware inside China qualifies as an export-controlled activity under the January 2025 rule updates. Apple's Chinese smartphone market share dropped to 14.1 percent in Q2 2026 from 17.2 percent a year earlier, per IDC. Huawei retook the number-one position at 18.9 percent, with Mate 80 Pro featuring an on-device Pangu model trained on Huawei's Ascend 910C hardware. Xiaomi at 15.4 percent shipped its own MiMo-Turbo. Baidu, whose ERNIE was originally selected for Apple Intelligence and then quietly dropped, closed down 2.3 percent in Hong Kong.

France's DGFiP confirms tax-authority breach exposing up to 2 million taxpayer records via MFA bypass

The Register · 2026-08-14 · Source ↗

France's Directorate General of Public Finances confirmed Friday that a threat actor using the alias ZeroBytes accessed its systems in late June through stolen credentials plus a novel MFA bypass technique, extracting personal and financial data for up to 2 million French taxpayers. Public disclosure came 47 days after intrusion and only after ZeroBytes advertised the data on a criminal forum.

France's Directorate General of Public Finances confirmed Friday that a threat actor operating under the alias ZeroBytes accessed the agency's systems in late June through a combination of stolen credentials and a multi-factor-authentication bypass technique, extracting personal and financial data for up to 2 million French taxpayers. The disclosure came 47 days after the initial intrusion and only after ZeroBytes advertised the stolen dataset on a well-known Russian-language cybercrime forum on August 12.

The breach is one of the largest single-day disclosures of French public-sector data on record and lands in the middle of an active French government push to embed AI into tax-collection workflows. DGFiP has been rolling out an AI-assisted revenue-verification system called ARBOR since 2024 that cross-references tax filings against real-estate registers, employer withholding data and financial-account information. The exposed records include the exact inputs and outputs of that verification pipeline for a substantial fraction of French taxpayers.

"Access had been severed at the end of June as part of an audit." — DGFiP official statement

Novel MFA bypass and 47-day disclosure gap

Data exposed in the breach includes internal tax identifiers, full civil names, dates and places of birth, postal addresses, family compositions, number of tax dependents, reference tax income (revenu fiscal de reference) and withholding tax rates. The Register reported the breach affected approximately 2 million French taxpayers, both individuals and professionals, though FrenchBreaches placed the figure closer to 700,000 records at 678,438 confirmed. The discrepancy reflects an ongoing DGFiP internal audit; ZeroBytes claimed 2 million records on the criminal forum, and DGFiP has not yet confirmed the total.

"A novel MFA bypass technique." — DGFiP description of the attack chain

The MFA bypass technique used against DGFiP has drawn particular attention. The attacker obtained legitimate credentials through a credential-stuffing or targeted-phishing operation and then chained those credentials with what DGFiP described as "a novel MFA bypass technique" that let the threat actor complete the second-factor challenge without possessing the underlying MFA token. The technique is under investigation by ANSSI, France's national cybersecurity agency, and Interior Ministry cybercrime prosecutors. Investigators have not publicly disclosed whether the bypass exploited a session-token replay vulnerability or a browser-in-the-middle attack against DGFiP's authentication flow.

The 47-day disclosure gap has become a political issue in Paris. DGFiP detected the unauthorized access during routine security audits in late June, severed the access and made no public disclosure until Friday, when ZeroBytes' criminal-forum posting forced the agency's hand. Finance Minister Amelie de Montchalin ordered a full internal review Thursday evening, and Prime Minister Elisabeth Borne's cabinet is scheduled to discuss the breach disclosure timeline at Monday's Council of Ministers meeting. The delayed disclosure appears to violate CNIL's 72-hour notification requirement under GDPR, and CNIL confirmed Friday it has opened a formal inquiry. The AI-adjacent dimension is the exposure of ARBOR training data: DGFiP has trained approximately 340 models over the past 18 months for tax-fraud detection and taxpayer-behavior scoring, drawing heavily from the exposed records. Cumulative disclosed victim counts across French public-sector breaches in 2026 exceed 22 million, and Friday's disclosure adds a further 2 million if the ZeroBytes claim proves accurate.
