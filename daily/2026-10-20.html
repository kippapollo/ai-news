Meta opens the Prometheus playbook: Phase 1 ran Muse training at 85-plus percent goodput, Phase 2 targets a million GPUs on one site.

Meta's engineering team published the first inside account on Monday of Prometheus, the New Albany, Ohio supercluster that trained the Muse foundation-model family through Phase 1 and is now scaling toward roughly 1 million interconnected GPUs. Pre-training goodput ran at more than 85 percent during Muse Wonder runs; RL goodput exceeded 90 percent. The next building block, San Miguel on Nvidia's Vera Rubin NVL72 platform, carries 288 GB of HBM4 per GPU and 10-20 percent better performance per watt than GB300. Tech · Engineering at Meta · 2026-10-19 · Source ↗

Meta's data-center engineering team published Monday the first substantive public account of Prometheus, the New Albany, Ohio compute campus that trained the company's Muse foundation-model family through Phase 1 and now has hundreds of thousands of GPUs working across multiple interconnected buildings. The piece is bylined to Kevin Lee, Yashar Bayani, Ahmed Sharif, and Yee Jiun Song. It opens with a flat positioning claim: "Right now, in New Albany, Ohio, Meta is undertaking its most ambitious AI infrastructure project to date." Phase 2 targets roughly 1 million GPUs operating in lockstep across the campus.

The three GPU platforms

Prometheus runs three Nvidia platforms. Catalina pairs GB200 NVL72 racks with 72 Blackwell GPUs each at 192 GB HBM3e per GPU and about 120 kW per two-rack pair, with 800G of scale-out I/O brought up ahead of the industry. Clemente upgrades to GB300 NVL72 at 288 GB HBM3e per GPU and 135 kW per rack, delivering 5-10 percent better throughput than GB200 and native 800G per GPU via Nvidia ConnectX-8 SuperNICs. San Miguel is the next step, built on Vera Rubin NVL72 at 288 GB HBM4 per GPU and more than 170 kW per rack, with 10-20 percent better performance per watt than GB300. The deployment runs the three platforms side by side in the same fabric rather than cutting over on a flag day.

Networking

The back-end network is a dedicated Ethernet fabric for direct GPU-to-GPU communication. Each building fabric interconnects up to 20,736 GPUs. Meta migrated from Disaggregated Scheduled Fabric (DSF) to Non-Scheduled Fabric (NSF) using 51.2T Minipack3 and Minipack3N switches, both running Meta's FBOSS networking stack. The Backend Aggregation layer sits above the fabrics as a multi-layer hub-and-spoke built on Arista 7800-series chassis with Broadcom Jericho3 silicon, spanning 2 km to more than 100 km and carrying multiple petabits per second between region pairs. The build involves millions of miles of fiber, tens of thousands of switches, and millions of optics; 800G-ZR coherent optics carry links past about 10 km.

"A single fault within a 72-GPU domain can affect the entire cluster's aggregate throughput." — Engineering at Meta, 2026-10-19

Goodput and the reliability sprint

During Muse Wonder training the cluster held pre-training goodput above 85 percent and RL goodput above 90 percent. Mean time between interruptions measured roughly 47,000 GPU hours during the runs and was later lifted past 54,000 after firmware fixes landed. The team's stated reliability north star is two interruptions per 8,000 GPUs per day, which translates to roughly 100,000 GPU hours MTBI. The baseline it has been working down from was far worse: the mid-2025 interruption peak hit nearly 1,000 events per 8,000 GPUs per day. Driving that down to the current cadence is called a 99 percent reduction over roughly a year.

"Our reliability north star remains two interruptions per 8K GPUs per day, which is ~100k GPU hours MTBI." — Engineering at Meta, 2026-10-19

The sparing math

Moving from NVL8 to NVL72 raised the fault blast radius by 9x. Prometheus answers with hard spare budgets and physical validation. Each rack runs a 64-GPU slice and reserves 8 GPUs as in-rack spares. About 2 percent extra racks sit in reserve for whole-rack faults. A 32,768 active-GPU job requires 523 NVL72 racks totaling 37,656 GPUs, which is 13 percent above the active footprint. Every NVL72 domain carries 5,184 differential-pair links at 200 Gbps, and more than 600,000 NVLink paths are screened with diagnostic stress tests before any production traffic routes through them. When a replica goes down, Parallelism-Aware Fault Tolerance (PAFT) takes the data-parallel replica offline while the rest of the job continues; otherwise the job restarts from delta-based asynchronous checkpoints.

Power and the Dynamo Dimmer

Synchronized training sessions can swing cluster power by 30-40 percent. Starting or pausing a large job can shift more than tens of megawatts within seconds. Meta's answer is the Dynamo Dimmer, a cluster-wide power-management framework that gradually caps GPU power limits instead of halting jobs. Supporting hardware stacks are rack-level battery backup units (BBU), capacitor trays (CBU), and facility-level battery energy storage (BESS). Cooling runs modular air-assisted liquid cooling (AALC) in free-air buildings and rapid-deployment structures, with facility liquid cooling (FLC) in the newest AI-optimized building. Meta's newest AI data centers use closed-loop, direct-to-chip liquid cooling; the IcePack liquid-cooled network rack is shared through the Open Compute Project.

Operational mechanics

Firmware bundles can reach more than 100,000 GPUs within hours; P90 single-pod upgrade time has fallen from more than 7 hours to 2 hours. Vendor collaboration with Nvidia carries joint triage: Nvidia owns firmware, signal diagnostics, and memory issues. The team also studied PSU and voltage-regulator behavior under the 1,400W load of individual GPUs. The listed challenges span NVLink signal integrity (the initial bottleneck for GB200), HBM and LPDDR memory faults as GB300 memory capacity grew, NVLink switch and fabric health, backend network link events at the BAG layer, power transients and utility coordination, and speed-of-light latency across multi-hundred-kilometer distances.

What's next

Phase 2 expands with GB300 and Vera Rubin NVL72 through the San Miguel platform. Hyperion, the sibling supercluster planned for Richland Parish, Louisiana, draws on Prometheus's lessons. Meta did not publish a dollar cost, total power in MW or GW, or an exact GPU count beyond "hundreds of thousands" today. The article names no VPs and attributes no quotes to individuals; all four byline authors are the engineering leads. The piece is at engineering.fb.com, dated October 19, 2026.

Amazon opens TorchNeuron, a native PyTorch backend for Trainium, ahead of a Tuesday Apache 2.0 launch at PyTorch Conference.

PyTorch Blog · 2026-10-19 · Source ↗

Models move onto Trainium with a one-line .to('neuron') pointer. Deepgram reports about 60 percent more input audio seconds per dollar than its GPU baseline across the benchmarks it ran.

Amazon's AWS Neuron team and Meta's PyTorch group published the TorchNeuron introduction on Monday, a day before the Tuesday keynote at PyTorch Conference North America in San Francisco. TorchNeuron registers Trainium through PrivateUse1, the official PyTorch extension point for custom hardware, so standard APIs (torch.nn, torch.tensor, torch.autocast, torch.distributed, torch.profiler) all run on a device named "neuron" without framework changes. The library will be released as open source under Apache 2.0 at github.com/aws-neuron/torch-neuronx and go live as a public beta on October 20. Trainium2 and Trainium3 are supported from day one.

How it works

The backend supports eager execution, torch.compile for production, and the Neuron Kernel Interface (NKI) for custom kernels. An adaptive eager mode (experimental) narrows the gap between eager and compiled runs by fusing operations without a code change. TorchDynamo captures the model into an FX graph, AOTAutograd traces the backward graph ahead of time, the Neuron backend lowers through Torch-MLIR into StableHLO, and the Neuron compiler produces a NEFF file that executes on hardware. FlexAttention is supported natively. The two inference stacks that ship on top are vLLM Neuron and vLLM Omni Neuron.

"PyTorch's vision is straightforward: developers should have a consistent experience across hardware platforms. Native support for AWS Trainium expands that choice, enabling researchers and engineers to experiment and iterate using the eager execution and distributed APIs they already know." — Jana van Greunen, Director of PyTorch Engineering at Meta

Partner numbers

Deepgram's CTO Adam Sypniewski reported that after a multi-month engagement with the AWS Generative AI Innovation Center to port Deepgram's model to PyTorch native on Trainium, "Trainium processes about 60% more input audio seconds per dollar than our existing GPU baselines." Cartesia co-founder Arjun Desai said the stack let the Sonic text-to-speech and Ink speech-to-text models run with minimal PyTorch-code changes, which freed the team to hand-write high-performance NKI kernels only for the layers that drive end-to-end latency. Hugging Face's VP Product Jeff Boudier framed TorchNeuron as making Trainium a native backend for Transformers and Diffusers so the familiar library stack runs with no device-specific adaptation; TRL enables fine-tuning and reinforcement learning, and Neuron kernels deliver optimization automatically.

Distributed and testing

DDP, FSDP2, SimpleFSDP, tensor parallelism, pipeline parallelism, and compositions like FSDP2 plus tensor parallelism and ZeRO all run natively. Collectives execute over Trainium's dedicated collective cores intra-node and over the Elastic Fabric Adapter inter-node. The team ran a blind sweep of 100,000 Hugging Face models covering 99.9 percent of real-world usage by download count on both eager and torch.compile. The August 2026 upstream test suite run produced about 154,000 outcomes, of which roughly 69,000 were skipped with a valid reason; the remaining 39,000 Neuron-specific skips break into 22,000 operators without a native Neuron lowering yet, a block of CUDA-specific tests that upstream eligibility checks did not flag, and features TorchNeuron chose not to support in the first release.

"Voice is becoming a first-class modality for AI, and Deepgram processes quadrillions of audio tokens a year for more than 200,000 developers and 1,400 organizations." — Adam Sypniewski, CTO, Deepgram

Ecosystem partners

Design partners named at launch include Cartesia, Hugging Face, Anthropic, Databricks, Rivian, Salesforce, and poolside. Ecosystem partners building with TorchNeuron include Anyscale, Decart, Kernelize, Loka, Luminal, Makora, Modular, Nota AI, Radix, Wafer, and Yasp. Trainium-tuned recipes live in a torchtitan-neuron fork that upstreams into mainline TorchTitan as changes mature. The pip install is torch-neuronx from the AWS Neuron pip repository.

The byline lists contributors Apoorv Gupta, Arash Jamalian, Rui Silva, Peter Sam Raj, Kunind Sahu, Kamran Khan, Shreya Srivastava, Charan Shettyhalli Guruswamy, Alex Hoffman, Abigail Fernandes, and Sam Jin. Public resources go live October 20 at github.com/aws-neuron/torch-neuronx.

Inception Labs · 2026-10-19 · Source ↗

Pricing lists at $0.20 input and $0.70 output per million tokens with a 50 percent launch cut. 50 live Mind2Web browser-agent tasks completed at 50 percent against a lower score for GPT-6.1-Sol.

Inception Labs announced Mercury 3 on Monday, framed as "the most capable diffusion-based LLM released to date." The model is more than 40x larger than Mercury 1 by total parameters and runs at 1,068 tokens per second on Nvidia GPUs with a 1-million-token context, up from 260,000 for Mercury 2.5. "We ran Mercury 3 inside our production web-agent harness against frontier models, on 50 live Mind2Web navigation tasks." — Shuhao Zhang, Cofounder, TinyFish AI

Cloudflare ships Clairic, a Workers AI extraction model running a claims pipeline at 3.2x speed and 49 percent lower cost than GLM-5.3.

Cloudflare Blog · 2026-10-19 · Source ↗

Clairic answers in under a second, generates no reasoning tokens, and charges only for input. Average TSA forecasting error ran 3.3 percent across 21 days with 17 of 21 actuals inside the 80 percent interval.

Cloudflare published Clairic on Monday as a preview "System One extraction model" on Workers AI, callable as @cf/cloudflare/clairic, with open weights promised later. The model extracts text spans with character offsets and confidence scores, computes numbers, dates, times, datetimes, durations, and lists, produces estimates with a median and an 80 percent interval, and generates one-estimate-per-step forecasts also carrying 80 percent intervals. Scalar answers include a p_null and confidence; missing values return null. The post is bylined to Michelle Chen.

How it is wired

Clairic runs a single forward pass and generates no reasoning or output tokens, which is why Cloudflare charges only for input. Many fields are answered in a single parallel pass. Very large field sets are split into parallel passes that each see the full input and then merge. Response time typically stays under a second. The architecture is deliberately narrow: Clairic is not a general-purpose chat model and is not meant to replace one. It is a System-1 extraction specialist that sits in front of or alongside a larger reasoning model.

"Calibration is measured, not guaranteed." — Cloudflare, Clairic preview post, 2026-10-19

The claims pipeline benchmark

Cloudflare ran a representative insurance-claims pipeline both ways: as a Clairic → Clef → GLM-5.3 cascade and as GLM-5.3 end-to-end. The cascade finished in 19.3 seconds against 61.7 seconds for the all-LLM pipeline, a 3.2x speedup. The extraction step alone took 0.46 seconds with Clairic versus 16.1 seconds with GLM-5.3. Total cost per million claims ran $6,032 for the cascade against $11,740 for the all-LLM path, 49 percent lower. Cloudflare does not publish separate Clairic pricing in the post.

TSA forecasting

On 21 days of TSA passenger forecasting, Clairic held an average error of 3.3 percent and placed 17 of 21 actual counts inside its 80 percent interval. The misses clustered around the Columbus Day weekend, which the input series did not capture. Cloudflare acknowledges forecasting is basic in this preview and not a replacement for dedicated time-series models. Multi-step arithmetic and aggregation across many rows are also weak for now, estimates need supporting evidence in the input, and calibration intervals may run narrow on unfamiliar data.

Use cases

Named example use cases include pulling order numbers, dates, and amounts from emails or receipts, estimating repair costs or home-sale prices from comparable data, and forecasting short-term daily counts while scoring many items in a single call. Cloudflare has not committed to a GA date or a dedicated pricing page; the preview flag is explicit.

Nvidia Vera Rubin systems inside Nscale's flagship Mason County site go live in late 2027. Microsoft had signed a letter of intent for the same campus earlier in the summer and walked.

Nscale and Anthropic signed a six-year compute deal valued at approximately $45 billion for 460 megawatts of capacity at Nscale's Monarch campus in Mason County, West Virginia, with Nvidia Vera Rubin systems coming online in late 2027. Bloomberg first reported the signing on Sunday; VKTR and multiple industry outlets confirmed on Monday. The deal was initially announced as an intention in August when West Virginia Governor Patrick Morrisey called it "an extraordinary vote of confidence in our state and the strategy we put in place." The formal signing closes the loop with Anthropic locked in as the anchor tenant.

The Microsoft backstory

Microsoft had signed a letter of intent for the same Monarch site earlier in the summer and abandoned the deal, opening the door for Anthropic. Separately on Monday, the BBC reported Microsoft also withdrew from Nscale's Essex, UK buildout, pulling another contracted megawatt column from Nscale's IPO book. The two withdrawals in sequence rework Nscale's near-term customer concentration profile: Anthropic in West Virginia for a stated $45 billion over six years is now the single largest named relationship, with less of the remaining capacity contracted to Microsoft than the pre-summer filings showed.

"The capacity is about 460 megawatts, and the facility is expected to come online by late 2027 and will use NVIDIA Vera Rubin systems." — Bloomberg News, as reported by VKTR, 2026-10-19

Scale and context

The 460 MW figure is roughly comparable to a mid-sized city grid draw. Sources differ on the home-equivalence figure for the capacity: one count puts it at enough electricity to serve roughly 374,000 US homes for a year; another says about 345,000 American homes. The deal lands after investors pushed Nscale's planned NYSE IPO range from $30-35 billion down to $16-19 billion last week, with Nscale's Norwegian flagship running at 6.4 MW against a 30 MW target and only about 2.5 percent of a disclosed $103.4 billion contracted backlog actively drawing power. The Anthropic signing shifts the balance of the backlog toward a buyer with a credible path to drawing capacity at scale.

Terms and open questions

Anthropic declined to comment on the transaction. Terms are not formally announced by either company, and Reuters and TechCrunch separately reported the arrangement without a joint statement. The reported $45 billion figure is total contract value over six years, not per-year. The 460 MW figure is site capacity, not Anthropic's exclusive allocation; whether Anthropic takes the full site or shares with other tenants is not disclosed. Governor Morrisey's August remarks framed the project as an "extraordinary vote of confidence." The next concrete milestone is the Vera Rubin systems coming online in late 2027.

Tenstorrent, the Toronto-based AI chip company led by Jim Keller, closed a $1.5 billion Series E on Monday at a $6.2 billion post-money valuation. Trace Capital and Innovation X led the round, with Protagonist, Morgan Stanley, Fidelity, ARK Invest, and T. Rowe Price joining. The company began as a pure chip-design shop and has moved up the stack into complete compute platforms: Galaxy servers for enterprise superclusters, TT-QuietBox desktop workstations, and Blackhole PCIe cards for individual developers.

The RISC-V bet

Tenstorrent's hardware runs on Tensix Neo accelerators paired with Ascalon CPUs, both built on the open RISC-V instruction set rather than Arm or x86. The software stack is correspondingly open: TT-Forge is the compiler for frameworks including PyTorch, TT-Metalium is the development kit, and TT-LLK handles low-level kernel optimization. Keller's architectural pitch is scale-neutrality.

"Built one architecture and we sell it at every size." — Jim Keller, CEO, Tenstorrent

Use of proceeds

Funds go to ramping up Galaxy server production, advancing RISC-V research, and expanding the workforce globally. The company is positioning as the open alternative to Nvidia's closed CUDA stack and to the Arm-based incumbents in CPU-side AI inference, with its full-stack open posture (open ISA, open compiler, open kernel library) distinguishing it from the AI-chip startups that bet on CUDA compatibility. Prior valuation is reported at $2.6 billion from a December 2024 Series D, implying a roughly 2.4x markup in under two years.

The competitive frame

Tenstorrent sits in a crowded cohort of Nvidia alternatives (Cerebras, Groq, SambaNova, Rebellions, Positron, Etched, Lightmatter) and the hyperscaler custom silicon (AWS Trainium, Google TPU, Microsoft Maia, Meta MTIA). Its wedge is the fully open stack and the Keller name. The capital stack and the latest round put it among the better-funded of the independent AI-chip startups, though still well short of the frontier model labs' compute budgets, which are the end buyers.
