Google agrees to backstop $19 billion of Anthropic's Kentucky lease, writes a TPU-only lock into the deed.

TeraWulf disclosed on Tuesday that Google LLC has agreed to credit-backstop Anthropic's lease obligations at the 401 MW Justified Data Campus in Hawesville, Kentucky, a 20-year commitment first announced July 6. The three CB-1, CB-2 and CB-3 agreements activate once each hall's final data hall is placed in service, with Fluor Enterprises signing a definitive construction contract on the same day. In exchange, Anthropic and its affiliates must run only Google-approved TPUs on the premises for the term, and Google cannot exit via lease termination inside the first six years unless the TPU pods can consume less than 50 percent of tenant critical power. On default, Google can pay rent and assume the lease or pay a termination fee that includes the right to acquire an equity interest in Raylan Data, the Brookfield-backed site operator. The backstop lifts once Anthropic or a guarantor affiliate holds investment-grade ratings for three straight months. TeraWulf CFO Patrick Fleury called the arrangement "investment-grade backing from Google LLC, an Alphabet subsidiary," and said it supports expected debt financing tied to roughly $19 billion in contracted lease revenue. Phased ready-for-service dates begin late 2027 and run through Q2 2028; Anthropic also holds two successive five-year renewal options.

TeraWulf 8-K · unite.ai · 2026-11-04 · Source ↗

TeraWulf's Tuesday 8-K puts a precise structure on a question the market has been chasing since Anthropic signed its July lease on the 401-megawatt Justified Data Campus in Hawesville, Kentucky: how does a company that lost $23.3 billion GAAP in Q3 obtain $19 billion of 20-year occupancy without a cloud anchor? The answer, now on file, is that Google LLC, Anthropic's largest financial counterparty and one of its three remaining training partners, agreed to credit-backstop the lease. Three separate backstop agreements, labeled CB-1, CB-2 and CB-3 after the three data-hall buildings, activate when the final data hall under each lease starts operating, and support the debt financing TeraWulf expects to raise against the long-dated contracted revenue.

What Google gets

The TPU-only clause is the most load-bearing provision. Anthropic and its affiliates must use only Google-approved TPUs on the premises for the term of the agreement, and Google cannot use the lease-termination route in the first six years unless the TPU pods can consume less than 50 percent of tenant critical power. Translated: Google has written a 20-year chip-exclusivity covenant against a 401-megawatt load into the real-estate stack, with a six-year hold on its own exit. After an Anthropic payment default or insolvency, Google can either pay rent at the then-current rate and assume the lease, or pay a termination fee that carries the right to acquire an equity interest in Raylan Data, the Brookfield-backed operating counterparty that controls the site. The backstop lifts only when Anthropic, or a guarantor affiliate, holds specified investment-grade ratings for at least three consecutive months.

"Investment-grade backing from Google LLC, an Alphabet subsidiary." — Patrick Fleury, TeraWulf CFO, 2026-11-04

Construction and timing

TeraWulf signed a definitive construction agreement with Fluor Enterprises on Tuesday covering the three data-hall buildings on the Hawesville site. Fluor's guaranteed maximum price is still being established, which is normal for a project moving from LOI into early-works. Phased ready-for-service deliveries are expected to begin in late 2027 and run through the second quarter of 2028, with rent obligations landing as each hall comes online. Anthropic's revenue to TeraWulf is contingent on delivery; absent the Google backstop, the paper would price as merchant risk against a company whose Q3 S-1 disclosed a $567 million operating loss and $895.4 billion of cumulative compute commitments. With the backstop, the paper is priced against Google. Anthropic also holds two successive five-year renewal options that could extend the economic life of the site to 2048.

The structural read

Two things change on this filing. First, the AI data-center market has acquired a template for financing hyperscaler-adjacent capacity without hyperscaler ownership. A land owner, a Brookfield operator, an AI tenant and a credit backstop from one of the tenant's cloud providers can, together, produce the equivalent of an investment-grade lease. Oracle, Blackstone, Apollo, SoftBank and KKR have all been reaching for variants of this structure since the $40 billion Apollo-SpaceX GPU financing closed; the Google-Anthropic version is the first one where the backstop provider is itself a competing frontier lab.

"We will have a very happy Thanksgiving." — Patrick Fleury, TeraWulf CFO, to CNBC, 2026-11-04

Second, Anthropic's chip optionality on this campus has just contracted sharply. The November 3 S-1 identified $430 to $637 billion of locked-in cloud commitments across Amazon, Google and Oracle; the Hawesville lease now carries an explicit 20-year TPU-only covenant and a Google-held right to acquire equity in the site operator if Anthropic defaults. Amazon's Trainium roadmap and AMD's Instinct MI450 commitments remain in place on other campuses, but the question of whether Anthropic can run frontier training on anything other than TPUs inside Justified is now answered on the site: no.

Moonshot opens Kimi K3.1 Pro weights: 2.8T parameters, 104B active, 1M context.

Moonshot AI · Hugging Face · 2026-11-04 · Source ↗

Moonshot released Kimi K3.1 Pro as open weights on Hugging Face. The model is a Mixture-of-Experts at 2.8 trillion total and 104 billion active parameters per token across 93 layers and 896 experts with 16 selected per token, trained with quantization-aware training at MXFP4 weights and MXFP8 activations and running a 1,048,576-token context. Moonshot self-reports 92.1 on BrowseComp, 94.2 on GPQA-Diamond, 70.0 on HLE-Full with tools, 65.7 on Terminal-Bench 4.0, 51.7 on SWE Marathon v1.1, 79.6 on OSWorld V2.1 and 77.1 on the internal Kimi Code Bench 2.0, benchmarked against Claude Opus 5.5, GPT-6 Astra and DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash. License is a custom "Kimi K3.1 Pro License" rather than a standard open-source one.

Kimi K3.1 Pro is the first weights release at this scale from Moonshot since the K3 generation opened in September. The model carries 2.8 trillion total parameters and 104 billion active parameters per token, distributed across 93 layers (one dense) with 896 experts and 16 routed per token. The architecture is multimodal from the first token, accepting both text and image inputs, and the context runs to 1,048,576 tokens.

The quantization choice

Moonshot shipped the weights in MXFP4 with MXFP8 activations under a quantization-aware training regime, which is a deliberate choice against the more common bfloat16 open release. The practical consequence is that the weights file is roughly a quarter the size of a bf16 version at the same scale, and inference can run on narrower hardware without requiring post-training calibration. The tradeoff is that fine-tuning tooling must support MXFP4 natively or re-upcast; most open frameworks support one but not both.

Self-reported benchmarks

The model card reports Terminal-Bench 4.0 at 65.7, SWE Marathon v1.1 at 51.7, BrowseComp at 92.1, GPQA-Diamond at 94.2, HLE-Full with tools at 70.0, OSWorld V2.1 at 79.6 and an internal Kimi Code Bench 2.0 at 77.1, benchmarked against Claude Opus 5.5, GPT-6 Astra and DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash. The scores land at or near parity with Opus 5.5 on several agentic benchmarks and sit above DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash across the board. All but GPQA and BrowseComp are reported by Moonshot without third-party replication.

"MXFP4 weights and MXFP8 activations, trained with quantization-aware training." — Kimi K3.1 Pro model card, 2026-11-04

License and availability

The release is under a custom "Kimi K3.1 Pro License" rather than Apache 2.0 or MIT, which is consistent with Moonshot's prior releases. The model card does not state explicit commercial-use terms; readers should consult the LICENSE file on the Hugging Face repository. No arXiv paper accompanied the release.

Meta disclosed Wednesday that it has acquired Conductor, a startup whose platform orchestrates multiple AI coding agents in parallel. Conductor's founders will join Meta Superintelligence Labs. Financial terms were not disclosed. The move puts Meta into direct competition with Anthropic's Claude Code multi-agent surfaces, OpenAI's Codex orchestration and the Cursor/Windsurf swarm patterns, and gives MSL an in-house parallelization primitive after a summer of model releases focused on individual-agent quality. Axios framed the deal as a continuation of Meta's acqui-hiring of agent-infrastructure startups and small research teams across the second half of 2026.

Conductor's product sits in the orchestration layer above individual coding-agent surfaces: developers define a task, and the platform fans it out to multiple parallel agent processes that work on separate branches or sub-problems before the results are reconciled back together. The pattern has emerged across the agent-tooling landscape in 2026 as a practical workaround for the serial-dependency ceiling single agents hit on large codebases. Meta's own internal tooling for Llama development has been moving in this direction, and the Conductor acquisition compresses that roadmap.

What Meta gets

The founders join Meta Superintelligence Labs, the Alexandr Wang-led unit that absorbed Scale AI's leadership plus the Zuckerberg talent push earlier this year. MSL has shipped a cadence of research notes and a post-training pipeline but not a user-facing coding-agent product; Conductor supplies the orchestration primitives that the FAIR-descended lab has been building around. Terms were not disclosed, which is standard for Meta acqui-hires in the agent space this year.

"The founders will join Meta Superintelligence Labs." — Axios reporting, 2026-11-04

Training the draft model to be rejected lifts GLM-5.3 on DeepSWE by seven points.

arXiv 2611.05345 · Subconscious Systems, MIT · 2026-11-04 · Source ↗

Hongyin Luo, Wei Fang and collaborators from Subconscious Systems, MIT, Princeton and Northeastern argue speculative decoding isn't just a speed trick. In a verification block, a token's numerical precision depends on its slot position (noise rises roughly four percent per slot), so a draft model can steer which tokens get the most-precise encoding. They train the draft to be rejected at reasoning decision points using reverse DPO. On DeepSWE's 113 software-engineering tasks, GLM-5.3-nvfp4 lifts 60.6 percent to 67.6 percent (+7.0 points, 95 percent CI 2.6 to 11.4). On NL2Repo-Bench, DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash lifts 65.0 to 66.4 (+1.4, p=0.011). The adversarial draft is just 2.15B parameters for GLM and 449M for DeepSeek (0.28 and 0.06 percent of system weights), and acceptance rate barely moves.

The authors' hypothesis is that numerical precision is slot-dependent inside a verification block. Repeated forward passes on identical prefixes show that slot 0 matches single-token decoding, and later slots grow noisier at about four percent per slot. Over long horizons (past roughly 30 turns), verify-written context produces more-extreme deviations than fresh-sampled context. If that is true, making the draft model produce a rejection at a key reasoning point forces the target model to resample that decision from slot 0 of a fresh block, where precision is highest.

Method

The target model acts as a judge to extract the "committing sentence" in each reasoning segment. Continuations from a shared prefix are labeled preferred (principle, evidence, or test-before-commit) or adversarial (likely, risk, simpler, or commit-before-test). The draft is then trained with DPO in reverse to favor the adversarial side. For GLM-5.3-nvfp4 the draft is 2.15B parameters; for DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash it is 449M. The target weights, the verification rule, and the output distribution are unchanged; only the draft is retrained.

"Noise rises about four percent per slot; the draft can influence which tokens get the most precise encoding." — arXiv 2611.05345, 2026-11-04

Results and limits

On DeepSWE, GLM-5.3 improves by 7.0 points with a wide confidence interval (2.6 to 11.4). DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash improves by 4.0 points on DeepSWE but the result is not significant (CI −1.3 to 7.3). On NL2Repo-Bench the DeepSeek gain is small but significant: +1.4 at p=0.011. Forward-direction drafts (agreeing with the target) drop GLM to 55.7 percent. Drafts trained on unrelated preferences give no gain. Acceptance length moves by about 1.8 percent on DeepSeek and is essentially flat on GLM, so throughput is preserved. The mechanism rests on the slot-precision hypothesis, which the paper states but doesn't fully test directly.

The right at issue is the press-publishers' right under Article 15(1) of the 2019 CDSM Directive (not copyright). Two infringement modes

First, circumvention of access controls: retrieving an article by defeating a paywall or a bot block can make the reproduction unlawful and may create a separate tort.
