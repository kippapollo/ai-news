Nvidia and SK Group unveil a $500 billion AI partnership anchored by a 2-gigawatt Vera Rubin factory

Jensen Huang and Chey Tae-won took the stage at the San Francisco AI Summit Friday afternoon to announce a $500 billion multi-year build spanning SK Telecom's 2-gigawatt AI factory on Nvidia's DSX platform and Vera Rubin accelerators, an extended SK hynix HBM4 memory supply agreement covering training, agents and physical AI, and joint development of high-bandwidth memory for future Nvidia rack systems. The Telecom facility comes online in 2027. The commitment is the anchor deal inside a wider $950 billion package of Korea-US AI agreements timed to President Lee Jae Myung's state visit.

SK hynix Newsroom / Reuters · 2026-07-25 · Source ↗

Nvidia and SK Group anchored the San Francisco AI Summit Friday with a $500 billion multi-year partnership spanning AI data centers, memory supply and joint silicon development. The centerpiece is a 2-gigawatt AI factory that SK Telecom will build on Nvidia's DSX rack platform powered by Vera Rubin accelerators and SK hynix HBM4 high-bandwidth memory, with the first facility slated to come online in 2027. Nvidia and SK hynix also extended their multi-year memory-supply agreement to guarantee Nvidia access to next-generation HBM through the Vera Rubin generation and beyond, and committed to jointly develop future HBM tuned to training runs, agentic inference and physical-AI workloads.

"If not for the invention of HBM memory in Korea, Nvidia wouldn't have been able to invent the supercomputers that power AI today."— Jensen Huang, Nvidia CEO, San Francisco AI Summit

What the $500 billion actually covers

The commitment is a top-line for a bundle of contracts SK Group signed at the summit. SK hynix's slice, roughly $500 billion on its own, folds together the Vera Rubin HBM4 supply plus a joint roadmap into next-generation HBM. SK Telecom takes the operator role on the 2-gigawatt factory: the operator finances and builds the shell, Nvidia supplies the DSX rack systems and Vera Rubin accelerators, SK hynix supplies memory. Chey Tae-won, SK Group chairman, was on stage with Huang at the announcement. Nvidia CEO Huang and Chey framed the build as the anchor tenant of what the summit hosts called Korea's ambition to become a "global AI powerhouse," and Huang leaned into HBM's Korean provenance to explain why the deal ran to nine zeros: "This really is the golden age for Korea," Huang said, "from AI chips and physical AI to AI infrastructure."

The $950 billion frame

The Nvidia-SK deal sits inside a wider $950 billion package of six agreements signed between Korean companies and US AI firms during President Lee Jae Myung's state visit. SK Group's total across those agreements ran roughly $750 billion, of which the SK hynix-Nvidia partnership accounts for more than $500 billion. Samsung Electronics contributed a separate $200 billion memorandum of understanding with Broadcom spanning HBM4, 2-nanometer foundry manufacturing and 2.3D/2.5D advanced packaging, running through 2030. Naver, Brookfield and Nvidia signed a $10 billion agreement to expand Naver's GAK Sejong AI factory from 55 megawatts to 200 megawatts on Nvidia's DSX platform. The five signed AI data centers together represent about 5 gigawatts of new Korean AI compute capacity coming online across 2027 and 2028.

The state-visit choreography

Lee Jae Myung landed in San Francisco Thursday and moved through separate one-on-ones Friday with Sam Altman of OpenAI, Dario Amodei of Anthropic, Hock Tan of Broadcom and Huang of Nvidia before joining the summit itself. Altman told the summit that "there really would not have been what we have, this incredible AI revolution, without Korea." Amodei, in his meeting with Lee, described Korea's role as "throughout the stack, from semiconductors to infrastructure to an incredible concentration of talent," and separately signed an Anthropic memorandum of understanding with Korea's Ministry of Science and ICT covering AI safety, cybersecurity and red-team assessments of autonomous AI agents. The summit closed with the San Francisco AI Declaration, a Lee-authored framework positioning Korea as a "trusted supplier" in the global AI supply chain and outlining an "AI for All" domestic transition.

Memory as the pinch point

HBM4 supply is the operational fulcrum of the entire $950 billion package. Vera Rubin's per-rack memory footprint runs several times Blackwell's; Nvidia's disclosed 2-gigawatt SK Telecom build means a substantial share of SK hynix's HBM4 output for 2027 is now pre-committed. Samsung's parallel $200 billion Broadcom MOU spans HBM supply into Broadcom's AI accelerators, meaning both Korean memory makers now have multi-year customer contracts for the memory generation the industry hasn't yet mass-produced. Broadcom's inclusion also breaks the pattern of HBM contracts flowing almost exclusively to Nvidia: Samsung selling HBM to Broadcom lets Broadcom's hyperscaler ASIC customers (Google's TPU pipeline chief among them) source memory outside the Nvidia allocation queue.

Research lab commitments

Nvidia used the same summit to announce two research lab commitments. The first is a joint AI research lab with KAIST's Kim Jaechul Graduate School of AI in Seoul, focused on agentic AI for Korea and using Nvidia Nemotron open models plus AI Cloud partner computing; Nvidia called it the first joint lab between a Korean university and a global tech company. The second is a planned Nvidia AI Technology Center at Seoul National University covering foundation models, accelerated computing, physical AI and AI for science. Both are framed as talent-development pipelines that feed the operational demand curve the $950 billion in capacity commitments creates.

Samsung and Broadcom sign a $200 billion memory, foundry and packaging MOU through 2030

CNBC · 2026-07-25 · Source ↗

Samsung's five-year MOU with Broadcom covers HBM4 supply into Broadcom's AI accelerators, 2-nanometer foundry manufacturing and next-generation 2.3D/2.5D packaging, running through 2030. The deal breaks the pattern of HBM contracts flowing almost exclusively to Nvidia and directly challenges TSMC's grip on Broadcom's ASIC packaging.

Samsung Electronics signed a $200 billion memorandum of understanding with Broadcom at the San Francisco AI Summit Friday spanning three of the most critical layers of the AI chip stack: HBM4 memory supply into Broadcom's AI accelerators, 2-nanometer foundry manufacturing for Broadcom's silicon and next-generation 2.3D and 2.5D packaging integration. The five-year commitment runs through 2030 and represents one of the largest disclosed semiconductor partnerships on record, though the MOU form leaves the actual per-year purchase volumes to be locked in through follow-on binding contracts.

"Industry-leading memory solutions such as HBM for Broadcom's future AI accelerators."— Samsung Newsroom, partnership announcement

Why the pattern shift matters

Until Friday, HBM allocation was effectively a two-customer market: Nvidia took the vast majority of SK hynix HBM3E and HBM4 production, with Samsung and Micron splitting what remained across smaller Nvidia allocations and a handful of hyperscaler ASIC programs. Samsung selling roughly $200 billion of HBM plus foundry plus packaging to Broadcom over five years gives Broadcom's hyperscaler ASIC customers (Google's TPU pipeline is the largest disclosed one, with additional Broadcom-designed accelerators inside Meta and unnamed hyperscaler programs) a memory allocation outside the Nvidia queue. That matters because HBM has been the operational pinch point on AI training scale-out through 2025 and 2026; a second guaranteed supply lane changes the negotiating shape for the Nvidia challengers.

The foundry piece

Samsung Foundry's 2-nanometer node has been in yield ramp since Q1 2026 and moved into limited external production in June. The Broadcom commitment is the largest external customer signing to date and directly answers the question of whether Samsung's 2 nm can compete with TSMC's N2 in the customer pipeline; Broadcom, TSMC's second-largest customer after Apple, is now pledging significant 2 nm volume outside Taiwan. The packaging line covers 2.3D (chiplet integration with die-to-die interconnects) and 2.5D (silicon interposer with HBM stacks) with a stated path to 3D die-stacking on Samsung's advanced packaging line.

The MOU caveat

The $200 billion is a five-year total across memory, foundry and packaging combined and is a memorandum of understanding, not a binding purchase commitment. The joint statement from Samsung and Broadcom is explicit that the dollar figure represents "expected demand" through 2030 and that binding purchase orders will follow at the component level. That structure is standard for a semiconductor supply arrangement of this scale, but it means the $200 billion is a ceiling and a signal, not a floor. The deal was signed at the summit itself and announced through Samsung's Global Newsroom Friday afternoon Pacific time.

Kimi K3's 594-gigabyte open weights arrive at 00:00 UTC Sunday

TECHi / Local AI Master · 2026-07-25 · Source ↗

Moonshot's 2.8-trillion-parameter mixture-of-experts model ships to Hugging Face at 00:00 UTC Sunday under a Modified MIT license, as a roughly 594 gigabyte MXFP4 safetensors release requiring roughly 1.4 terabytes of fast memory to actually serve. The frontier open-weights leaderboard puts K3 fourth behind Fable 5, GPT-5.6 Sol and Opus 4.8.

Moonshot's Kimi K3 open weights arrive at 00:00 UTC Sunday July 27 (8 PM Eastern Saturday) on huggingface.co/moonshotai. The release is the largest open-weights drop in the history of the format: 2.8 trillion parameters total in mixture-of-experts form, roughly 594 gigabytes of native MXFP4 safetensors on disk, published under a Modified MIT license that allows commercial use with limited restrictions on training-data reuse. The full 16-bit weights would run roughly 5.6 terabytes; Moonshot is skipping 16-bit and shipping MXFP4 as the primary format, which is the format that the current-generation Blackwell and MI355X hardware natively supports.

"On independent testing it lands fourth among all frontier models — trailing only Claude Fable 5 and GPT-5.6 Sol, and edging past Claude Opus 4.8."— Nathan Lambert, Interconnects

What 1.4 terabytes of fast memory actually means

The disk size (594 GB in MXFP4) is not the memory footprint. To serve K3 at production speed the operator needs roughly 1.4 terabytes of HBM plus context, which puts the single-node bar at an 8-GPU H200 141GB rack (1.128 TB HBM total, insufficient), an 8-GPU B200 192GB rack (1.536 TB HBM total, sufficient for the weights but tight on context) or a 16-GPU B200 configuration. AMD MI355X clusters and Google TPU pods with equivalent memory can also serve K3 in MXFP4, and the community is expected to publish INT4 and NF4 quantizations within days that will drop the footprint further at some quality cost. Moonshot suspended new K3 subscriptions on Monday as demand outran the GPU supply that the US export regime lets into China, so the open-weights release will meaningfully shift where K3 inference actually runs.

Where K3 sits in the pack

K3 has been running on the hosted kimi.com and Moonshot API since the July 17 announcement. Independent benchmarking through the past week puts K3 fourth on the global frontier leaderboard, above Anthropic's Claude Opus 4.8 and below GPT-5.6 Sol, Claude Fable 5 and the still-in-limited-release Opus 5. On the Frontend Code Arena K3 sat at Elo 1,679 through Wednesday, above both Fable 5 (1,631) and GPT-5.6 Sol (1,618). The open-weights release converts that hosted-API footprint into a self-hostable one, at a cost that will meaningfully compete against Nvidia's premium API pricing for organizations with the memory to run it.

What ships versus what does not

The Sunday release includes model weights in MXFP4, tokenizer, inference reference code, and evaluation scripts for the published benchmarks. Moonshot has confirmed it will not release the K3 training data, training-run compute logs or the pretraining recipe; the technical report is scheduled for Q3 2026. The license is a Modified MIT variant with training-data-reuse restrictions similar to the DeepSeek-V4 license. Moonshot's US contact channel for enterprise commercial licensing was set up this week and is running through a partnership with a US-based cloud provider that has not yet been publicly named.

Anthropic shipped Claude Code v2.1.220 Friday as a maintenance release focused on bug fixes and reliability improvements, closing out a week of feature work that included /fork confirmation prompts, git and gh command validation on PowerShell hosts, improved messaging for /ultrareview when the diff exceeds the review budget, better empty-diff messaging on /code-review ultra, and a new capability for published Claude artifacts to call MCP connectors each time someone views them so dashboards can show live data. Claude Opus 5 also became available inside GitHub Copilot's model picker in the same 24-hour window.

"Published artifacts can now call MCP connectors each time someone views them, allowing dashboards to show live data."— Claude Code weekly changelog

Artifact-plus-MCP is the interesting piece

The most substantive change in the week's changelog is the artifact-plus-MCP behavior. Historically a Claude artifact rendered on publication was a static snapshot; the new behavior lets an artifact evaluate MCP connector calls on every view. That converts an artifact from a delivered document into a live dashboard whose data refreshes against whichever MCP servers the author connected. The intended use cases are shared engineering dashboards (CI status, deploy state, on-call queue), business-intelligence artifacts that pull from Postgres or BigQuery through MCP servers, and generated documentation that stays fresh against the underlying system state.

PowerShell validation and /fork

The git and gh command validation on PowerShell hosts is a Windows-side fix that addresses the recurring shell-quoting issues Windows users have run into when Claude Code generates git commands: PowerShell's argument-passing semantics diverge from POSIX shells in ways that were causing spurious command failures. The /fork confirmation is a UX guardrail on the branching command; the /ultrareview diff-too-large messaging replaces a silent error with an actionable prompt telling the user to break the review into smaller chunks.

Opus 5 in Copilot

Anthropic Claude Opus 5 became available in GitHub Copilot on Thursday and is now selectable in Copilot's model picker across VS Code, JetBrains and Visual Studio. Opus 5 is positioned in Copilot's model list as the frontier model choice for "complex, long-running coding tasks that require careful reasoning, effective tool use, and reliable execution across multiple steps." The move continues Copilot's multi-vendor model strategy, which now includes OpenAI, Anthropic, xAI Grok Code and Google Gemini alongside the GitHub-original Copilot-tuned models.

Ollama adds Laguna MLX support, 8-bit quantization for requested families and permission-based skill loading

Releasebot / Ollama Release Notes · 2026-07-25 · Source ↗

The Friday build touches x/create, server, agent, cmd/tui, mlx and model support, adding 8-bit quantization for requested family and draft output head, Laguna MLX support, permission skill loading, plus fixes for the scheduler, updater and quantization handling.

Ollama shipped a Friday release that touches most of the runtime's active surface, following the July 6 v0.31.2 stable and the July 9 Series B closing. The changes span the x/create model authoring path, the HTTP server, the agent runner, the terminal UI, MLX support and model handling. Notable additions: 8-bit quantization is now available for the requested family and for draft output heads, Laguna MLX support lands on Apple Silicon, permission-based skill loading gives the client control over which skills the runtime picks up, and the scheduler, auto-updater and quantization handling all get fixes.

"8-bit quantization for requested family and draft output head, Laguna MLX support, permission skill loading."— Ollama release notes summary, 25 July

What Laguna MLX support unlocks

Laguna MLX is Apple's next-generation MLX runtime, which lands with a redesigned KV-cache manager, faster mixed-precision execution on M4 and M5 Silicon and MXFP4 kernels tuned for Apple's Neural Engine. Ollama picking up Laguna MLX means the runtime can drive M4 Max, M4 Ultra and M5 Silicon hardware at close to native performance for MXFP4 models like DeepSeek-V4, Muse Spark 1.1 and once Sunday lands, Kimi K3. The 8-bit quantization for requested family is the higher-quality quant tier that Ollama had been shipping only for select vendor-provided models; making it available across arbitrary model families lets community fine-tunes ship at 8-bit without a repackaging round-trip.

Permission skill loading

Permission-based skill loading changes the runtime's handling of Ollama skills (Ollama's equivalent of MCP-style tool packages). Under the previous model, any skill the runtime found on disk was loaded and made available; the new behavior requires an explicit per-skill permission grant, which brings the model in line with the way Claude Code and other Anthropic surfaces handle skill discovery. That closes a subtle security gap where an attacker who could write to a directory the runtime scanned could inject a skill that would be picked up on next launch.

The scheduler and updater fixes

The scheduler fix addresses a race condition in Ollama's job scheduling that showed up under heavy concurrent load and could cause requests to queue past the timeout window. The auto-updater fix cleans up a case where the updater would incorrectly report an available update when the local version was in fact ahead. Neither issue was security-critical, but both were the source of user reports through Q3.
