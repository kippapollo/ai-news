IQuest's Multiscale Policy Optimization tops Hugging Face at 372 upvotes with turn-level credit for long-horizon LLM agents.

arXiv 2610.35013 · Hugging Face Daily Papers · 2026-10-28 · Source ↗

Tao Ren leads 19 authors from IQuest. The core observation: critic values vary primarily across turns, not between tokens inside one turn. MPO pairs Multiscale Advantage Estimation with turn-level importance sampling. Evaluated at 4B and 30B-A3B scales; pass@1 against PPO: BrowseComp-Plus 41.80 vs 40.85, GAIA 34.20 vs 31.71, HLE 7.88 vs 7.22. Full MPO reaches BrowseComp-Plus 48.08, GAIA 39.47, HLE 9.92.

Multiscale Policy Optimization for Long-Horizon LLM Agents, from IQuestLab, led the Hugging Face Daily Papers ranking on October 28 with 372 upvotes, more than ninety ahead of the next entry. The paper's core observation is that in long-horizon agent trajectories "critic values vary primarily across turns" rather than between tokens inside one turn, which implies that smearing credit at the token level dilutes a signal that is actually about which assistant turn caused the outcome.

What MPO does

The method pairs Multiscale Advantage Estimation with turn-level importance sampling, so credit assignment and policy updates operate at the assistant-turn level while finer-grained token signals still contribute. The paper evaluates at two scales (4B and 30B-A3B) and reports pass@1 scores against PPO on three benchmarks: with the paper's β=0 turn-only credit configuration, BrowseComp-Plus 41.80 against 40.85, GAIA 34.20 against 31.71, HLE 7.88 against 7.22. Full MPO reaches BrowseComp-Plus 48.08, GAIA 39.47 and HLE 9.92. Each configuration is a single training run with best-checkpoint selection.

"Token-level credit smears that blame across every token in the step." — HF commentary paraphrasing the paper's motivation, 2026-10-28

Why the top of the leaderboard went to this paper

Turn-level credit is the single most frequently cited bug in open-source agent-training recipes: harness feedback arrives per turn, but PPO-style updates operate per token. The paper's framing points at the mismatch directly and claims a clean win on search, tool-use and automation benchmark families. No GitHub repo is linked from the HF page or arXiv listing as of October 28, so the training recipe cannot yet be independently reproduced, and the table numbers are single-run with no variance intervals in the abstract. Author affiliations are not listed per-author on the HF page beyond the IQuest tag.

Caveat: single-run numbers, no public code, no third-party replication.
