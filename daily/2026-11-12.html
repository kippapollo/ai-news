Google DeepMind's Gemini Robotics 2.5 packs Mercedes-Benz parts at 96 percent, lifts task success 30-100 percent across 200-plus activities.

DeepMind debuted its most capable vision-language-action model Wednesday and said partners Agile Robots SE, Apptronik and Boston Dynamics are getting the rollout now. In live Mercedes-Benz proof-of-concept runs, the model packed automotive parts with a 96 percent success rate; across more than 200 evaluation tasks, success landed between 30 and 100 percent, with completion 20 to 30 percent faster than the previous generation. It builds on Gemini 4 Argon, released late September, and ships with a physical-steerability upgrade and a new jailbreak benchmark for embodied systems. Apptronik's Apollo 2 humanoid and the Franka Duo two-fingered platform run it natively. DeepMind framed the model as "an AI brain" for robots working alongside humans in unstructured industrial settings.

Google DeepMind · SiliconANGLE · Apptronik · 2026-11-11 · Source ↗

Google DeepMind positioned Gemini Robotics 2.5 as a general-purpose "AI brain" for robots, built on Gemini 4 Argon and trained to spend more compute on hard embodied tasks. The company said it tested the model across more than 200 distinct activities, logging success rates between 30 and 100 percent depending on task, and completion times 20 to 30 percent faster than the previous Gemini Robotics 2 generation that shipped July 30. The model is multi-embodiment by design: it drops into Apptronik's Apollo 2 humanoid, the Franka Duo platform with two-fingered parallel grippers, and other partner hardware without per-platform retraining. DeepMind is pushing the release through partner lab integrations rather than open weights.

Mercedes-Benz on the line

The headline number is a live industrial deployment, not a sim. Apptronik ran Apollo 2 on a Mercedes-Benz automotive line and recorded a 96 percent packing-success rate on real parts in the proof-of-concept run. CEO Jeff Cardenas told SiliconANGLE the model "improved significantly" after a few weeks of post-training and said the capability "carried over to Mercedes-Benz production with very little drop-off." Dexterity upgrades landed in the release notes: DeepMind called out tool use on delicate, flexible objects as an area where the new model is markedly better than the prior version.

"This is what we built Apollo for: useful work alongside people in real industrial settings." — Jeff Cardenas, Apptronik CEO, 2026-11-11

Safety and the new jailbreak benchmark

DeepMind said it added a "physical steerability" upgrade intended to give operators tighter runtime controls when robots run into unexpected objects or humans, and introduced a dedicated jailbreak benchmark for embodied systems. The benchmark is meant to measure whether an attacker can talk a robot into unsafe physical actions, a failure mode absent from text-only red-team suites. The paper and scores on the embodied jailbreak benchmark were not yet public at release time.

The partner stack

The rollout list runs through Agile Robots SE, Apptronik and Boston Dynamics Inc., with DeepMind describing additional partners under evaluation. The company framed the move as a bet that the frontier LLM push and the robotics push will stay coupled: Gemini 4 Argon, Google's late-September frontier release, "helped accelerate" the robotics work, per DeepMind. Pricing for Gemini Robotics 2.5 and the terms under which partners access weights or API endpoints were not disclosed in the announcement.

"The next generation of Gemini Robotics... our most capable vision-language-action model to date." — Google DeepMind, 2026-11-11

What's missing and what's next

The 200-task evaluation set is not public. The 30-to-100 percent success-rate band is wide enough that cherry-picked tasks can hide inside it, and DeepMind did not release a per-task table at launch. The 96 percent Mercedes-Benz figure came from Apptronik rather than independent evaluators. Three open questions after Wednesday's drop: whether DeepMind publishes the full eval set and jailbreak scores, whether the partner list expands to automotive OEMs beyond Mercedes-Benz, and whether Google ties the robotics release into the Gemini 4 Carbon pricing arc that shifted from $2/$10 to $4/$20 per million tokens December 1. The companion AlphaGenome 2 release from the same day signals a coordinated frontier-day cadence from DeepMind, with robotics, genomics and the Gemini Decisions API landing in a 24-hour window.

DeepMind's AlphaGenome 2 adds DNA methylation to predicted readouts and adapts to new single-cell data without retraining.

Google DeepMind · SiliconANGLE · University of Cambridge · 2026-11-11 · Source ↗

AlphaGenome 2 predicts how DNA sequences and variants affect gene activity in specific cell types and states, extending v1's readouts (gene expression, histone modification, chromatin accessibility) with DNA methylation. It draws on Human Cell Atlas single-cell data covering millions of cells across hundreds of tissue types. Cambridge professor Sarah Teichmann said the model "adapts directly to new single-cell data without retraining."

DeepMind dropped AlphaGenome 2 the same day as Gemini Robotics 2.5 and framed it as the next step in its biological-sequence modeling arc from AlphaFold forward. The new model keeps the three readouts the first generation predicted: gene expression (which genes are on, and how strongly), histone modification (the epigenetic marks that regulate access to DNA), and chromatin accessibility (which DNA regions are open to transcription machinery). It adds DNA methylation, a key regulator of gene silencing that previous DeepMind genomic models did not predict end-to-end.

Cell types and conditioning

Crucially, AlphaGenome 2 is conditioned on single-cell data from the Human Cell Atlas initiative, which has mapped gene-expression patterns across millions of individual cells in hundreds of tissue types. DeepMind's framing: the same DNA sequence produces very different behavior in a heart muscle cell than in an immune cell, and the model has to know which cell state it is predicting. Professor Sarah Teichmann, co-founder of the Human Cell Atlas and Cambridge professor of Stem Cell Medicine, said the model can adapt even from "very small samples," which matters for rare-disease and under-sampled-tissue contexts where there is no practical path to retraining a frontier model.

"Adapts directly to new single-cell data without retraining." — Prof. Sarah Teichmann, Human Cell Atlas / University of Cambridge, 2026-11-11

Scope and limits

The model is meant for research use, not clinical diagnostics. DeepMind said predictions are intended to guide experimental design rather than replace wet-lab validation, and the model is explicitly not approved for clinical or diagnostic use. No quantitative benchmark table accompanied the launch coverage; DeepMind's own paper, benchmark splits, and availability terms were not reported in the SiliconANGLE write-up. The release fits a cadence: AlphaGenome v1 landed in June 2025, and DeepMind has since expanded along the "digital biology" axis with AlphaProof Code for Lean verification of Signal (announced November 10) and now AlphaGenome 2 for epigenetic prediction.

Google opens Gemini Decisions API with Gemini Decision 1 at $0.10/M input, up to 1,000 questions per 150-250 ms pass.

Google DeepMind · Google blog · 2026-11-11 · Source ↗

Min Choi and Felix Yu posted the Decisions API Wednesday as a purpose-built endpoint for bounded choices: tool selection, policy checks, model routing, context grading. Up to 1,000 independent yes/no, multiple-choice or rubric-score questions resolve in 150-250 ms end-to-end with a confidence score from 0 to 1. Inputs span text, images, audio, video and PDFs up to 256K tokens. Pricing: $0.10 per million input tokens, zero per output token.

Google's pitch on the Decisions API is that most agentic workflows do not need a token-by-token generation. They need a fast, calibrated vote. The new gemini-decision-1 model takes a prompt plus a set of candidate options and returns a score per option in a single forward pass, with every answer carrying a confidence score between 0 and 1 for routing to a human reviewer when the model is uncertain. Three question types are supported: noul (yes/no, with optional true/false criteria), choice (two to 255 options returning the winner plus normalized probabilities), and score (ordered rubric returning a weighted score plus per-level probabilities).

Numbers and the cost case

End-to-end latency sits at 150-250 milliseconds, with median model compute of 50-100 ms. Context stays effective to 16K tokens and latency stays nearly flat from 1 to 100 questions per request; the per-request limit is 1,000 questions. Shared input is billed once per request regardless of how many questions use it; each additional question pays only for its own criteria tokens. Google called the API nearly 5x cheaper than running Gemini 3.5 Flash-Lite through the general-purpose Interactions API on the shared benchmark suite. Thinking tokens, audio transcription and OCR are not billed.

"Evaluate up to 1,000 yes/no, choice, and rubric-scoring questions in a fast, single pass." — Google blog, 2026-11-11

Where it is already running

Google called out four internal deployments. Gemini Notebook uses the Decisions API as a real-time router to pick the right model tier and reasoning budget per query, claiming up to 40 percent lower latency for everyday queries. Stitch applies voice or tap edits to playable wireframes inside a rapid canvas. The CC experiment in Google Labs selects an emoji reaction from more than 200 options. Google Search is running the API as a safety filter over Model Context Protocol tools, detecting adversarial content and behavior before an action fires. The ecosystem list at launch covers OpenRouter, LangChain, Vercel, LiteLLM, Pydantic AI, Pipecat, LiveKit and OpenCode. Gemini on Google Cloud is listed as "coming soon."

IBM's 520-qubit Quantum Nighthawk r3 runs 10,000-op circuits with 25-nanosecond qubit reset.

IBM · SiliconANGLE · 2026-11-11 · Source ↗

IBM introduced Quantum Nighthawk r3 Wednesday with 520 physical qubits supporting circuits up to 10,000 operations. Qubit reset time drops from about 200 milliseconds in the prior generation to 25 nanoseconds, a roughly eight-million-x speedup. IBM said a linked system pairing two r3 chips into a 1,040-qubit configuration is planned for 2027.

IBM's incremental quantum roadmap keeps moving. The 520-qubit Nighthawk r3 lifts qubit count only moderately from prior Heron and Flamingo generations, but the story is in the depth and the reset time. 10,000 operations per circuit is a jump over the thousand-op circuits r2 could run coherently, and the 25-nanosecond qubit reset against the prior 200-millisecond reset is the kind of operational constant that determines how many circuits per second a real workload can execute. Faster reset means faster sampling means faster expectation-value estimation means a quantum advantage window that closes less quickly against classical competitors running tensor-network simulators on GPUs.

Linked systems

IBM said the r3 is the base building block for a two-chip 1,040-qubit linked system targeted at 2027, with low-latency chip-to-chip couplers. The road to error-corrected logical qubits is still gated on surface-code overhead; r3 is firmly in the pre-error-correction era and IBM did not report logical qubit numbers at launch. The commercial channel runs through IBM Quantum Platform with hybrid classical-quantum workloads via Qiskit Runtime.

"Nighthawk r3 is the base building block for the 1,040-qubit linked system in 2027." — IBM, per SiliconANGLE, 2026-11-11

Context

The IBM release lands the same week Google split its eighth-generation TPU lineup into four systems and Nvidia opened NVLink Fusion to Korean NPUs through the SK Telecom Rebellions deal. Quantum sits adjacent to the classical AI compute arms race, with IBM pitching Nighthawk to a different workload mix (chemistry, materials, optimization) than GPUs target. The 520-qubit figure is a physical count; IBM has not indicated when it expects logical qubit counts to cross into double digits.

Microsoft Research's "Lazarus" backdoors AI agents by reprogramming fewer than 10 dormant neurons.

Microsoft Research · aiweekly.co · 2026-11-10 · Source ↗

A Microsoft Research paper published Monday describes "Lazarus," a backdoor technique that identifies dormant neurons in a frozen model and rewires fewer than ten of them to install a trigger-activated covert behavior. The technique leaves standard-benchmark scores effectively unchanged and evades common backdoor-detection probes, raising a fresh supply-chain vector for open-weight agent models.

The Lazarus work builds on a growing body of research on sparse neural backdoors. Rather than training the whole model on triggered examples, attackers scan a released model for "dormant" neurons that contribute near-zero activation to standard tasks, then perform a tiny parameter edit on fewer than 10 of them to install a trigger-activated behavior. Benchmark scores on standard evaluation suites stay effectively unchanged, which is the point: the attack is undetectable by downstream evaluators who rely on performance deltas as a tripwire.

Why this is new

Prior backdoor literature mostly targets fine-tuning data or RL reward signals. Lazarus is a weight-level attack that assumes the attacker never touched training. It fits a supply-chain threat model where a malicious intermediary ships "a lightly fine-tuned open-weight model" to a downstream user, embedding behaviors that only fire on specific triggers. The attack landing against agent models is the most consequential form, since the agent's tool-use capability means a triggered backdoor can convert an undetectable weight edit into real-world actions like exfiltration, transaction authorization or policy override.

"Reprogramming fewer than 10 dormant neurons." — Microsoft Research, Lazarus paper, 2026-11-10

Defense posture

Microsoft Research paired the attack disclosure with a proposed detection pipeline that scans weight-space for anomalous rewiring in near-zero-activation regions. The team said the detector catches Lazarus-style edits but is not yet robust against evasion. The practical implication for teams shipping agent frameworks on open weights: weight-provenance attestation and signed-weight pipelines become table stakes, not optional. The paper is available on arxiv; the Microsoft announcement did not include a hash-verification tool at release.

Stanford's AI100 standing-committee report, published Monday, argues that the scaling era is encountering real constraints: training-data quality is degrading, compute cost per parameter is rising sharply, and the gains from the next order-of-magnitude of pretraining compute may be meaningfully smaller than the prior one. The Stanford One Hundred Year Study on AI (AI100) is a long-running standing report series intended to track progress over decades. The Monday installment lays out the case that the pretraining-scaling era that defined 2020-2025 is hitting ceilings on three axes: data quality (synthetic-data debates aside, high-grade web text is largely used), compute cost (per-parameter training cost is rising faster than hardware FLOPs-per-dollar), and marginal capability (benchmarks like MMLU, HLE and HumanEval are saturating for the frontier models).

What the report says is replacing scaling

The AI100 panel pointed to post-training compute (longer reasoning traces, verifier models, iterative self-distillation) and agentic RL from environment rewards as where the next decade's gains are likely to come from. The panel named METR's independent measurement of Anthropic R&D acceleration at ~1.9x (95 percent CI 1.5x-2.8x) from last week as evidence that the compute-to-capability function is now dominated by non-pretraining factors.

"The scaling era is hitting data and compute limits." — Stanford AI100 report, 2026-11-10

Richard Sutton's weekend essay calling synthetic data "a big mistake" set off public pushback from Sam Altman and Dario Amodei last Sunday; the AI100 report arrives in that debate, mostly aligning with the view that pretraining scaling is no longer the main driver and that post-training and evaluation infrastructure matter more now. Jerry Tworek's framing that Sutton has "reward-hacked the brains of a whole generation of scientists" points to the same shift the AI100 panel is naming.

VulnCheck: only 2 of 442 Project Glasswing CVEs are on CISA's KEV catalog, false-positive rate 55.9 percent.

VulnCheck · aiweekly.co · 2026-11-10 · Source ↗

VulnCheck published pushback Monday on Anthropic's Project Glasswing CVE-discovery pipeline. Of 442 CVEs Anthropic's agents found and that got CVE numbers, only two show up in CISA's Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog, and VulnCheck measured a 55.9 percent false-positive rate against Anthropic's 94.2 percent precision claim. The gap reframes how to count agentic vulnerability research outputs.

Anthropic's Project Glasswing is the agentic-vulnerability-research effort that uses Claude models to triage and report security findings in open-source codebases. Anthropic has published a running count of CVEs issued and claimed a 94.2 percent accuracy figure. VulnCheck's analysis reruns the sample and reaches two different conclusions. First, only two of the 442 attributed CVEs end up on CISA's Known Exploited Vulnerabilities (KEV) catalog, which is the industry proxy for whether a vulnerability is actually being exploited in the wild. Second, when VulnCheck's own triage pipeline reviews the sample, the measured false-positive rate is 55.9 percent, well above Anthropic's claim.

What "false positive" means here

VulnCheck's definition: a report that received a CVE but, on manual review, either describes behavior that is not exploitable, describes a known issue already patched, or mislabels an intended feature as a vulnerability. Anthropic's 94.2 percent figure appears to measure a different thing (model self-judgment or coarse human spot-check). The disagreement is now the defining test case for how the industry counts outputs from LLM security agents.

"2 of 442 Project Glasswing CVEs are on CISA KEV." — VulnCheck analysis, 2026-11-10

The policy angle

KEV listing is what drives federal remediation deadlines. If only two Glasswing CVEs cross that threshold, the practical impact of the pipeline is smaller than the headline CVE count suggests. The VulnCheck pushback also arrives as CVE Numbering Authorities (CNAs) begin to tighten their intake process for LLM-generated submissions, with several CNAs floating per-submitter rate caps to control the flood.

United becomes first US carrier to block third-party AI booking agents on its platforms.

United Airlines · Skift · 2026-11-11 · Source ↗

United updated its platform-use terms Wednesday to bar personal AI booking agents from acting on its reservation systems, citing credential-sharing risks and the FAA/DOT hazmat check-in rules that require confirmed human attestation. The move is the first explicit block of personal AI travel agents by a major US carrier.

United's policy update names third-party AI agents acting on a passenger's behalf as a prohibited class on its booking surfaces. The airline cited two reasons publicly. First, the credential-sharing posture that most agentic-booking workflows use (a passenger hands over a password or session token to an agent) creates liability for passenger-data misuse, under United's reading of its passenger terms of service. Second, FAA and DOT hazmat check-in requires a human-attested answer to a specific question on each ticketed segment, which United said an agent cannot stand in for.

What this breaks

The practical effect is to break personal-agent travel flows that have been rolling out through OpenAI's Operator, Anthropic's computer-use, Google's Agentspace and various consumer wrappers over the last year. OpenAI's ChatGPT Travel integration still runs on carrier integrations negotiated directly; passenger-wielded personal agents going through united.com or the United mobile app are now out of scope.

"We will continue to block personal AI booking agents on our platforms." — United, policy update, 2026-11-11

What comes next

The industry question is whether this becomes the default US carrier stance. Delta and American have not updated terms publicly; Alaska and JetBlue have both been quiet. The OpenAI-Expedia and ChatGPT-Booking.com integrations rely on structured carrier APIs rather than agent-impersonating-human workflows, which puts them outside United's prohibition. The DOT has not yet weighed in on whether personal AI agents satisfy hazmat-attestation rules in a general case.
