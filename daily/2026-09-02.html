Anthropic signs a six-year $35 billion Lambda cloud contract anchored on an Nvidia-leased Texas campus

Anthropic disclosed a $35 billion, six-year cloud agreement with Nvidia-backed Lambda on Tuesday, running on a Hut 8 data center in Nueces County, Texas where Nvidia itself holds the lease and supplies the GPUs. The deal is Lambda's largest disclosed customer contract by more than an order of magnitude and pushes Anthropic's confirmed 2026 compute commitments past $135 billion after last week's $45 billion Nscale contract for West Virginia capacity. Nvidia plays three simultaneous roles: chip supplier to Lambda, Lambda's largest strategic equity backer, and the counterparty on the Hut 8 lease that Anthropic is now paying to fill.

Tech · Economic · TechXplore / WSJ / Bloomberg · 2026-09-01 · Source ↗

Anthropic's $35 billion, six-year Lambda contract is the largest disclosed compute commitment the lab has signed in a summer that already saw three others: a $45 billion Nscale lease last week for 460 megawatts of Vera-Rubin capacity in Mason County, West Virginia; a $50 billion Fluidstack lease from July; a ~$1.25 billion-per-month SpaceX capacity contract disclosed in May. The Tuesday disclosure pushes the running 2026 compute total past $135 billion. The Wall Street Journal reported the deal at the top of the morning; Bloomberg, Reuters, TechXplore and the Financial Times followed by early afternoon.

"Lambda will secure access to a data center being built by another company, Hut 8, which mines bitcoin and builds data centers, including one in Texas that Anthropic will tap for computing power." — TechXplore reporting

The Nvidia triangle

The structural feature that distinguishes this contract from the Nscale, Fluidstack and SpaceX deals is Nvidia's presence at three layers of the transaction. Nvidia sells the GPUs to Lambda, holds a lease on the Hut 8 campus, and sits as a strategic equity backer inside Lambda's cap table. Lambda in turn resells the compute to Anthropic and pays Nvidia an undisclosed sum for data-center access. The circularity has been flagged by 247wallst.com and Yahoo Finance's dedicated AI page, both of which framed the arrangement as "the ultimate circular financing play" and pointed out that a meaningful fraction of Nvidia's next-fiscal-year revenue is now underwritten by capital Nvidia itself extended to the counterparties.

Hut 8's pivot

Hut 8 is a legacy Bitcoin miner that has been repositioning across 2025 and 2026 into AI data-center construction. The Nueces County campus is one of a small number of gigawatt-adjacent sites the company has under active development, and Anthropic is now its named anchor tenant. The site sits on the Texas Gulf Coast in a region where the Electric Reliability Council of Texas grid still has interconnect capacity available, an increasingly scarce condition. Hut 8 chief executive Asher Genoot has spent the past year telling investors that the AI-workload economics per megawatt are materially better than Bitcoin per unit of infrastructure at current AI-token pricing, and the Nvidia lease anchoring converts that thesis into signed capacity.

How this stacks on the earlier deals

Anthropic's disclosed multi-year cloud contracts across 2026 now line up roughly as: $5 billion AMD-tenanted from July; $10 billion Volta from August; the ~$50 billion Fluidstack contract; $45 billion Nscale in West Virginia last week; and the $35 billion Lambda contract Tuesday. Add in the ongoing SpaceX capacity draw at roughly $15 billion of annual run-rate cost and the running total of Anthropic multi-year commitments is above $135 billion, with the biggest tranches loaded in the back half of 2027 and through 2028. That schedule assumes Anthropic's continued revenue trajectory validates the September rumor of a fall IPO at up to a $2 trillion valuation, which the sell side is publicly disputing on the ratio of forward contracted commitments to disclosed Q2 revenue of $11.6 billion.

"Frontier labs are no longer just renting GPUs from AWS or Azure; they are locking multi-decade contracts." — Wall Street Journal coverage, September 1

Lambda's balance-sheet reset

Lambda entered 2026 as one of the smaller Nvidia-backed cloud providers, materially below CoreWeave and Nscale by capacity. The $35 billion Anthropic commitment reprices the company overnight: on standard forward-multiple assumptions it makes Lambda the third-largest AI-first cloud by contracted revenue globally. The company had disclosed a $500 million convertible round in late 2025 anchored by Nvidia, and the September 1 contract is what that round was designed to underwrite. Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang did not appear on the joint disclosure, but Lambda co-founder Stephen Balaban issued a short statement thanking Nvidia and Hut 8 for "aligning the pieces at the pace this deployment requires."

The wall of same-week deals

Combined with the Nscale contract from the previous Tuesday and the Nvidia-MediaTek $3.5 billion convertible bond that closed Monday morning, the last eight trading days have produced roughly $100 billion of new Nvidia-anchored AI infrastructure commitments across three transactions. Bloomberg's newsletter framed the Nvidia-MediaTek arrangement as the first Nvidia investment structured to keep the interconnect layer competitive even inside racks that carry non-Nvidia compute. The Lambda-Anthropic contract makes the more traditional argument: as long as Nvidia can finance the buyer, the buyer buys Nvidia. What the two events share is a common view of the underlying constraint: the bottleneck on AI is no longer chips, or capital, or model quality. It is the power and the data-center concrete, and every large 2026 transaction is now organized around locking down both.

OpenAI's Tuesday launch closes the loop on a healthcare product line the company has been building since ChatGPT Work rolled out earlier in the summer. The Epic integration is read-only: clinicians can import appointment notes, laboratory results, medications and specialist documentation into a ChatGPT session and ask questions across a patient chart, but ChatGPT cannot write information back into the electronic health record. The design decision matters because it sidesteps the primary safety concern that has kept generative AI out of clinical documentation workflows, namely the risk of a hallucinated update propagating into billing, orders or downstream care.

"Even a few unsafe answers can lead to harmful results for humans." — OpenAI Health team

The safety measurement

The 99.1 percent physician-rated safety figure comes from a study of 4,300 responses across 27 clinical use cases with practising physicians grading the output. Accuracy for the public-data connectors was measured separately and lands between 93.2 percent on CMS Coverage and 98.6 percent on DailyMed. Neither number is a substitute for regulatory review, and OpenAI acknowledges that the tail of unsafe answers, even at one percent, is high in absolute terms given the volume of queries the product will absorb once rolled out to every large US hospital system running Epic. The rollout is gated to Business Associate Agreement customers on ChatGPT Work, Codex, apps and connectors.

What the Epic wire enables

The Epic pipe supports four launch use cases: pre-visit chart review, clinical timeline construction, medication reconciliation and handoff summary generation. ChatGPT can be embedded inside certain Epic workflows so a clinician does not have to leave the patient chart, a design choice that concedes to the ergonomics of the existing US hospital IT stack. UCSF Health, the University of California, San Francisco academic medical system, served as pilot partner during a private beta earlier in Q3.

The public-data plug-in

A separate Healthcare Public Data plug-in gives every eligible ChatGPT Health user read-only access to nine federal databases: ClinicalTrials.gov for active trials, CMS Coverage for reimbursement determinations, RxNorm and DailyMed for medication information, PubMed for biomedical literature and four other endpoints covering Medicare data and provider records. The plug-in is available in the United States only at launch, and OpenAI has not disclosed a timeline for adding non-US regulatory or clinical databases.

The medical-liability backdrop

The launch lands in the wake of at least two lawsuits alleging ChatGPT gave harmful medical advice to consumers in the past 12 months. The read-only design, the Business Associate Agreement gating, the physician-rated safety data and the explicit safety-caveat language in the launch post all read as responses to the litigation environment. The technical achievement here is real, but the strategic bet is that a clinical-grade product deployed inside verified provider workflows can outrun the reputational and legal exposure of consumer misuse. Epic itself has been reluctant historically to open EHR data to third-party AI, and the September 1 launch is the first blanket integration the vendor has authorised.

llama.cpp b10730/b10731 ship Qwen4-experimental MTP rollback, lifting Qwen3.8 code decode 49 percent

GitHub · ggml-org/llama.cpp · 2026-09-01 · Source ↗

llama.cpp cut b10730 at 05:08 UTC (qwen4exp: sum the indexer heads by slices, PR #28023) and b10731 at 05:34 UTC (qwen4exp: support recurrent state rollback, PR #28123). The rollback path fixes convolution-state snapshots so MTP speculative decoding on Qwen3.8-Flash-Next UD-Q4_K_XL lifts from 123 tok/s code / 83 tok/s prose to 183 / 144, against 108 baseline without a draft model.

b10731 is the meaningful release. The commit title "qwen4exp: support recurrent state rollback" describes a fix to how the target model reverts state when draft tokens are rejected during MTP speculative decoding. The prior implementation wrote one plane of convolution state per snapshot, which meant a rollback restored incomplete history and forced a fallback to per-token host-memory checkpointing, an expensive path. The b10731 change writes one snapshot per slot for both the delta-net QKV convolution and the PLE convolution, each ending one token earlier than the previous, giving the rollback path a proper multi-plane view of history to restore from.

"support recurrent state rollback · MTP speculative decoding needs the target state to move back by the number of rejected draft tokens" — b10731 commit body

The measured lift

Tested on Qwen3.8-Flash-Next UD-Q4_K_XL with the standalone MTP draft configured at n-max 3 and a single slot, decode throughput lands at 183 tokens per second on code and 144 tokens per second on prose. The prior implementation, falling back to host-memory checkpointing rather than in-device rollback, reached 123 and 83 tokens per second respectively. The pure baseline without a draft model runs at 108 tokens per second. The rollback path delivers roughly 49 percent uplift on code and 73 percent on prose relative to the checkpoint fallback, and 70 to 33 percent above the no-draft baseline. The gap between code and prose reflects the higher rejection rate MTP sees on natural language versus code, which is more speculatively predictable.

The earlier b10730

b10730, cut 26 minutes before b10731, is the setup change: "qwen4exp: sum the indexer heads by slices" (PR #28023). The Qwen4 experimental architecture uses an indexer-head configuration that had been summed monolithically in the prior kernel. The slice-sum reformulation reduces peak register pressure and gives the CUDA MoE fusion path better occupancy on Hopper and Blackwell. Neither b10730 nor b10731 change the model checkpoint format, so anyone running Qwen3.8-Flash-Next MTP-enabled GGUFs from prior weeks picks up the throughput gain by rebuilding.

The context

The Tuesday releases follow the 18-tag Saturday sprint on Aug 30 and the 21-tag Sunday sprint on Aug 31 that between them covered Vulkan top_k radix-select for Qwen 3.8 Flash Next expert routing, CUDA MoE fusion extension to speculative-decoding traffic, ROCm radix TOP_K for long rows, XOR-swizzled flash-attention K/V smem fp16 on CUDA and the batched IQ gemm for large-batch prompt processing. The Tuesday pair closes the loop on the specific rollback bug that had been blocking full MTP throughput on the Qwen 4 architecture and finalizes the Qwen 3.8-Flash-Next launch-week performance envelope on consumer and prosumer GPUs.

The repository pulse

llama.cpp is now cutting signed release tags at multiple-per-day cadence with binary matrices covering macOS arm64 and x64, iOS XCFramework, Ubuntu with CPU/Vulkan/ROCm/OpenVINO/SYCL variants, Windows across five backends, Android arm64 and openEuler on every tag. The repository crossed 126,000 GitHub stars and is now the reference implementation of open-source LLM inference for anyone below hyperscaler scale.

Alibaba AMAP-ML releases DreamX-Creator 1.0: 7B joint audio-video generator with 2K refinement

arXiv · Alibaba AMAP-ML / Zhu et al · 2026-08-31 · Source ↗

The DreamX-Creator paper posted to arXiv on Aug 31 and trended to the top of Hugging Face Daily Papers on Sept 1. A compact 7-billion-parameter generator conditioned on a first frame and text prompt jointly denoises modality-specialized audio and video streams via Gated Cross-Modal Attention, then a 2K Refiner upsamples in one autoregressive step. The AMAP-ML GitHub repo hosts the release.

DreamX-Creator sits in a small class of research releases that ship both a real capability jump and open code the same week. Ten authors led by Jiashu Zhu and Xiangxiang Chu at Alibaba's AMAP-ML lab uploaded the paper on Aug 31 UTC, and the release trended to number one on Hugging Face Daily Papers by Sept 1 morning. The pitch: existing video generators either drop audio entirely or synthesize it in a separate stage, which forfeits the joint modeling of visual dynamics and acoustic events. DreamX-Creator collapses the two stages into one dual-stream denoiser with a compact 7-billion-parameter backbone and a one-step 2K refiner on top.

"Recent video generators often omit audio or synthesize it in a separate stage, limiting reciprocal modeling of visual dynamics and acoustic events." — DreamX-Creator paper abstract

The Gated Cross-Modal Attention

The architecture centers on modality-specialized dual streams for audio and video with a Gated Cross-Modal Attention mechanism that uses token-wise and head-wise gates to control information flow between the streams. The gates are learned per-layer rather than hand-tuned, which the paper argues lets the model discover the specific timesteps in the diffusion schedule where cross-modal information is most useful. The audio stream uses a specialized codec designed for perceptual synchronization; the video stream uses a standard latent diffusion transformer backbone with the audio conditioning routed through the gated cross-modal path.

The 2K Refiner

The generator produces content at a lower base resolution and the 2K Refiner performs an autoregressive one-step upsampling to 2K, keeping wall-clock latency roughly proportional to the base resolution rather than the final output resolution. That design choice makes the model tractable on single-node inference hardware for outputs that traditionally required multi-node clusters. The refinement pipeline is optimized specifically for the audio-video joint output rather than treating video and audio refinement as separate problems.

The training stack

The paper describes an "Audio-Video Data System" that constructs coherent clip pairs from noisier source video, a progressive training schedule that ramps modality complexity, and a "Modality-Aware Multimodal Feedback" reinforcement learning stage that routes perceptual reward signals back into the appropriate stream. The reward routing is important because a naive joint reward encourages the model to over-emphasize whichever modality dominates the source distribution. AMAP-ML's decision to release both weights and training code under a non-exclusive distribution license places it among the more open frontier-adjacent multimodal releases of the quarter.

The competitive frame

DreamX-Creator lands three weeks after Google's Gemini Omni 1.1 Flash generally-available launch with ten-second scene-extension context and one week after ByteDance's DART-SD diamond-topology tool-calling distillation. The Alibaba release is more directly comparable to Google's Omni line: joint audio-video with configurable duration on a single-node inference envelope. At 7 billion active parameters, DreamX-Creator is materially smaller than either the OpenAI Sora line or Google's Omni 1.1 and its open-weight distribution places downstream pressure on the closed video-generation APIs to justify their price envelopes on quality that DreamX-Creator does not yet match at the ceiling.

The FTC and a 22-attorney-general coalition filed suit in Seattle federal court alleging Amazon systematically overcharged 1.2 million advertising customers, including more than 500,000 small and medium businesses, by more than $20 billion since 2019 through hidden surcharges to its sponsored-listings auction. Amazon operates the world's third-largest advertising platform. The suit exposes the company to potentially billions of dollars in civil penalties.

The suit filed Monday and reported by Bloomberg Law, CNBC, CNN, NBC News and UPI on Tuesday morning is the largest federal-plus-state antitrust action against Amazon since the FTC's 2023 monopolization case. The specific allegation is not about pricing generally but about hidden surcharges layered on top of the sponsored-listings auction Amazon runs to sell advertising placements on its platform. The FTC's complaint says the surcharges were introduced in 2019 as an internal rule change that was never disclosed to advertisers, and the practice has generated more than $20 billion of additional revenue for Amazon since.

"Regulators claim Amazon introduced undisclosed mechanisms that raised the amount advertisers paid." — Fortune coverage summary

The scale

The complaint identifies 1.2 million distinct advertising customers affected by the pricing scheme, including more than 500,000 small and medium businesses. The Benzinga piece framed the suit as "really a test case for AI pricing," pointing out that the mechanics of the alleged manipulation, dynamic auction rules changed without advertiser notification, are increasingly common across programmatic advertising platforms that use machine-learning bidders. If the FTC prevails, the precedent could apply to any auction-based ad platform that adjusts internal rules algorithmically without customer disclosure.

The state coalition

Twenty-two state attorneys general joined the FTC as co-plaintiffs. The specific state list has not been published in full but includes California, New York, Colorado and Texas on early reporting. The complaint asks for structural remedies as well as civil penalties, though the initial filing did not specify the structural relief sought. The FTC's request for civil penalties is framed in the billions of dollars and, on standard antitrust-remedy patterns, likely proceeds to a fact-finding phase before any settlement discussion.

Amazon's response

Amazon's initial statement rejected the allegations and framed the FTC action as "an attempt to relitigate advertising practices that other platforms use without regulatory concern." The company said advertisers have visibility into aggregate return-on-ad-spend metrics that reflect the pricing they actually paid. The FTC's counter is that visibility into ROAS is not visibility into the auction mechanism, and that the material misrepresentation is at the auction layer rather than at the outcome layer.

The costs cascaded to shoppers

The complaint also alleges the elevated advertising costs were passed through to consumers via higher product prices on the platform, adding a consumer-protection theory to the antitrust theory. That framing is deliberate: it lets the state attorneys general position the case in language their constituents will recognize, rather than confining the harm to advertiser-versus-platform commercial dispute.
