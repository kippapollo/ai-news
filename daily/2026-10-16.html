Meta opens Muse Spark at 816B parameters under a $20M-capped commercial license.

Meta Superintelligence Labs released Muse Spark Open on Thursday morning Pacific, an 816 billion parameter mixture-of-experts with 42 billion active per token and a 1 million token context window, positioned as "the most capable US open-weight model to date." Weights ship on Hugging Face, Amazon SageMaker, Google Cloud Model Garden and Microsoft Foundry Models with day-one hosted access through Baseten, Fireworks AI, Groq, Modal, Nebius, OpenRouter and Together AI, and a local path on paired Apple M5 Ultra Mac Studios running MLX. Meta's own benchmark table shows Muse Spark Open at 61.8 on JobBench against Thinking Machines Lab's Inkling at 31.1 and NVIDIA Nemotron 3 Ultra at 27.2, at 89.2 on MCP-Atlas ahead of every rival Meta listed, and at 76.4 on Terminal-Bench 2.1 behind Kimi K3 at 85 and both GLM 5.3 and Qwen3.8. The Muse Model License is free for most developers but requires a separate agreement for any hoster past $20 million in annual gross revenue, prohibits military and critical-infrastructure uses, and asks products built on the model to display a "Built with Muse Spark Open" label.

Tech · Meta Superintelligence Labs · 2026-10-15 · Source ↗

Meta Superintelligence Labs placed open weights on four hyperscaler model catalogs and seven hosted-inference providers on Thursday morning, with Muse Spark Open positioned as the top-of-stack US open-weight release. The sibling Muse Wonder and Muse Wonder Full remain closed inside Meta's own stack. The gap is the stated policy: anything that touches the frontier capability line stays behind the Meta API; the open layer runs behind it, labelled "most capable US open-weight model to date" by Meta's own research blog. The pattern mirrors the open-closed split the company has run since April on Muse's debut, except the open track has now been pushed from Scout/Maverick-era dense weights up to an 816 billion parameter MoE.

Specs the model card commits to

Muse Spark Open is 816 billion total parameters with 42 billion active per forward pass, a 1 million token context window, and native multimodal input across text, image, audio and video. The architecture is a mixture-of-experts; the ratio of total to active parameters (roughly 19:1) sits inside the band DeepSeek V5 Thinking and Qwen 5 Max have been shipping. Meta's local-run path uses paired Apple M5 Ultra Mac Studios through Apple's MLX framework, with each Mac Studio holding up to 512 GB of unified memory and 1.2 TB/s of memory bandwidth; Meta describes the paired configuration as the first in its class on a desktop. Engines named by Meta: SGLang, vLLM, TensorRT-LLM, Hugging Face Transformers, Apple MLX, llama.cpp and Unsloth. Hardware optimisation targets: AMD, Apple, Intel and NVIDIA. Customization paths: PyTorch and TorchTitan. Meta's own custom Helion GPU kernels for SGLang are documented as 1.03x to 3.95x at the kernel level on NVIDIA and AMD GPUs, with a 3 percent end-to-end lift on AMD MI350.

"Two Mac Studios behave like one supercomputer with a terabyte of unified memory." — Meta Superintelligence Labs, Muse Spark Open launch post, 2026-10-15

Benchmarks in Meta's own table

JobBench is the agent evaluation, Terminal-Bench 2.1 is the coding evaluation and MCP-Atlas is the tool-use evaluation. Meta's reported table has Muse Spark Open at 61.8 on JobBench against Thinking Machines Lab's Inkling at 31.1 and NVIDIA Nemotron 3 Ultra at 27.2, at 89.2 on MCP-Atlas ahead of every rival Meta listed including the strongest Chinese open models, and at 76.4 on Terminal-Bench 2.1 behind Kimi K3 at 85 with GLM 5.3 and Qwen3.8 also higher. The pattern reads cleanly: agentic workflow and tool-selection quality lead, static coding comes in third among the leading-tier open contenders. Meta's full evaluation report is still to come; the model card on Hugging Face is the primary source for the numbers above.

The license the launch rests on

The Muse Model License reads as a bounded commercial grant. Developers who adapt the model or ship it inside their own software pay no fee. Any company that offers Muse Spark Open as a hosted service past $20 million in annual gross revenue over any trailing 12-month period requires a separate agreement with Meta. The license requires products built on the model to display a "Built with Muse Spark Open" label. Military use is prohibited; critical-infrastructure use is prohibited. Meta describes the terms as "limited commercial" and the research page says these terms let many developers use the model for free. The combination is a deliberate line between hobbyist and small-shop usage (open) and hyperscaler reselling (negotiated), with the $20 million threshold set low enough that any serious hosted provider either sits in the Baseten / Fireworks / Together-style allowlist or pays Meta directly.

"Because of both safety and capabilities, we will generally not open-source our most powerful models." — Alexandr Wang, Chief AI Officer, Meta, cited in Bloomberg's coverage of the release

Safety process named at launch

Meta says Muse Spark Open cleared the release thresholds of the Meta Superintelligence Scaling Framework, the company's own in-house capability-and-risk gate. The research page acknowledges open weights carry their own considerations and names safety post-training as the mitigation layer. The framework covers the "most severe risks" without naming them individually in the launch post; detailed results sit inside the Hugging Face model card and a full evaluation report is promised "soon." Meta also opened a $1 million Prompts for the People bounty for red-teamers who find release-blocking issues.

Where this fits inside the broader open-weight line

Muse Spark Open lands five days after Alibaba's Qwen 5 Max release at 1.1 trillion parameters under Apache 2.0, inside the same open-weight pricing floor the market has been pulling toward since DeepSeek V5 hit $0.14 / $0.56 and GPT-6.1 Sol repriced to $1 / $5. Muse Spark Open's distinctive move is the $20 million operator-revenue cap rather than a parameter-level or capability-level restriction; the operator economics are where Meta chose to hold the line. For developers below the cap, the model is free; for hyperscalers above it, Meta has routed negotiation through a per-partner agreement, which gives Meta a seat at the deal table for every production deployment at commercial scale. That is a different pattern from Apache 2.0 (Qwen 5 Max, Apertus 1.5) and from Mistral's Research License, and it is the pattern the next quarter's open-weight line is likely to react to.

Open questions after the launch

Meta did not disclose training data composition beyond the use of synthetic data generated by closed Muse models. The company has not published training-compute figures or the energy footprint for the pretraining run. The Terminal-Bench 2.1 result is explicitly behind Kimi K3; Meta does not address the gap in the launch post. The relationship between Muse Spark Open's safety posture and the New York Times reporting from October 10 on Mark Zuckerberg's August decision to ship despite internal red-flag tests on the closed Muse models is not addressed in the research page. The $1 million bounty is open; the full evaluation report is pending.

Goodfire's Cammarata argues 90% of Qwen3-32B neurons are already single-concept.

Goodfire · 2026-10-15 · Source ↗

The essay argues superposition is a reading problem more than a storage problem. A preliminary audit finds about 90 percent of Qwen3-32B neurons serve one primary function, 7 percent two related functions, 2–4 percent not yet understood. V-shape, pose and planetary-geology neurons are worked examples.

Nick Cammarata, a research advisor at Goodfire and a Zoom In / Multimodal Neurons co-author who started at OpenAI in 2023, published a long essay on Thursday arguing that neural networks are more interpretable than the field has believed. The essay's preliminary audit of Qwen3-32B puts roughly 90 percent of neuron "poles" at one main job, about 7 percent at two related jobs, and about 2 to 4 percent not yet understood. Cammarata reports that about 97 percent of strong-activation mixtures in small samples were non-arbitrary. He describes the figures as a broad pointer rather than a precise target and labels the strongest version of the claim ("interpretability may be far more tractable") "highly speculative."

The core argument is that superposition and polysemanticity have been systematically misread. Many neurons that look like arbitrary mixtures are actually one pattern described as "a list of nouns." The famously opaque "cat and car" neuron 4e:55 is a V-shape detector, not two concepts in one; cars and dogs share a neuron because they share a pose, not because they're stored together; a "polysemantic commas" neuron tracks Colorado context with commas acting as running summaries; a "pr" neuron fires on "pr" words, Base64 URLs and Bengali that all decode to "pr." Cammarata calls the remaining apparent mixing "recursive generalization": a neuron grows offshoots as it trains, so "iy" becomes "diy" and then "do it yourself."

"Networks use one learning principle, generalization, and it is fractal." — Nick Cammarata, Goodfire, "Already Clean" essay, 2026-10-15

The architectural drivers Cammarata names: residual connections and LayerNorm (and similar changes meant to help deep networks learn) also make deep layers conceptually cleaner. Retraining InceptionV1's last layer with modern choices raised interpretability to about 70 percent; a ConvNeXt of the same size reached about 99 percent. Smaller models learn coarser neurons rather than superposed ones. Squeezing a layer from 480 to 10 neurons produced broader neurons, not overlapping mixtures. The essay's methodological argument is that sparse-autoencoder decompositions have been treating a reading problem (bad tools, short contexts, narrow data) as a storage problem (true superposition), and have unfolded a model that was already clean.

The thesis sits across the standard interpretability line. If Cammarata is right, the implied research agenda inverts: study a network's own neurons and circuits directly instead of training SAEs first, and treat each legible neuron as a building block rather than as a mixture to be decomposed. The practical consequence for safety-critical interpretability work (red-teaming, deception detection, planning-circuit audits) is that the field may have been one architectural generation ahead of where it thought it was, and the Qwen3-32B audit is the first large open-weight model Cammarata has put through the "already clean" lens in public.

The Federal Trade Commission has served separate civil investigative demands on OpenAI and Anthropic seeking records on security incidents, safeguards, marketing claims and privacy policies.
