Andreessen Horowitz raises $1.1 billion for a Machine Age Fund built to buy the physical AI stack

The venture firm most identified with the software-eats-the-world thesis said Friday morning it had closed $1.1 billion for a first-of-its-kind hardware fund. The Machine Age Fund's scope reads like a shopping list of every current binding constraint on AI scaling: processors, memory, networking, storage, robotics, data centers, cooling, materials, electrical, and real estate. It arrives the same week Nvidia's guarantees underwrote OpenAI's Ohio buildout, Broadcom orchestrated more than $60 billion of AI debt with Blackstone and Apollo, and Anthropic added $45 billion of Nscale capacity in West Virginia. The firm framed the raise as an answer to "faster, more efficient systems… cheaper and higher-bandwidth memory across the memory hierarchy," a departure from the a16z playbook that funded Airbnb, Coinbase and Facebook and a bet that the next decade of returns sits inside physical infrastructure rather than on top of it.

TechCrunch · Bloomberg · Seeking Alpha · 2026-08-28 · Source ↗

The Machine Age Fund closes the intellectual arc of a firm that has funded software for its entire fifteen-year existence. Marc Andreessen's 2011 Wall Street Journal essay "Why Software Is Eating the World" served as the founding investment thesis for a partnership that put early money into Airbnb, Coinbase, Facebook, Instagram, Lyft, Pinterest and Slack. Friday's $1.1 billion raise, disclosed at 6:24 AM PDT, is the firm's first pool restricted entirely to hardware infrastructure, and the scope reads as a direct concession that the next decade of returns sits in physical assets that the earlier software cohort largely ignored.

"the strongest tool ever developed for solving problems and bestowing abundance." — a16z, describing AI in the fund announcement

What the money buys

The fund's target list covers ten categories that map onto every currently binding AI-scaling constraint: processors, memory, networking, storage, robotics, data centers, cooling, materials, electrical and real estate. Andreessen Horowitz framed the mandate around "faster, more efficient systems… cheaper and higher-bandwidth memory across the memory hierarchy." The categories cross-index with the news cycle of the past week: Nvidia's guarantees on OpenAI's twenty-year Ohio lease, Broadcom's $60 billion-plus AI debt vehicle with Blackstone and Apollo, Anthropic's $45 billion Nscale contract at the Monarch Compute Campus in West Virginia, SK Hynix's Purdue HBM groundbreaking, and Kioxia-Sandisk's $31 billion flash-memory pledge through 2032.

The turn from software

The firm's own portfolio composition through 2025 tilted overwhelmingly toward software, marketplaces, and fintech. Its 2023-2024 crypto raises drew regulatory scrutiny but stayed digital-only. The Machine Age Fund is the first vehicle where the general partners are explicitly committing to underwriting physical build cycles, land-and-cooling projects, and semiconductor design startups. Bloomberg framed the shift as an acknowledgement that AI-scaling economics have moved out of the browser and into the substation.

The competing capital flows

The $1.1 billion Machine Age raise is small next to the same week's vendor-guaranteed debt structures. Broadcom arranged the Blackstone-Apollo AI-financing vehicle at up to $100 billion once the junior tranche layers on. Anthropic's Nscale contract commits $45 billion of guaranteed capacity spend over six years. Nvidia's OpenAI Ohio guarantee runs to $105 billion. Against those numbers, a pure venture fund is a rounding error. What the fund can plausibly do is seed the next tier of startups that fill in cooling, materials, custom silicon, robotics manipulators, and edge data-center primitives — the kind of small-check bets a hyperscaler will not fund because it is not the operator's core competency.

The strategic reading

The signaling matters more than the check. Andreessen Horowitz has ten thousand LPs, a training-video machine that shapes early-stage founder thinking, and enough press amplification to move category perception on its own. A dedicated Machine Age Fund positions hardware startups as venture-fundable in a way that has been difficult since the last big hardware VC cohort collapsed in the 2015-2018 window. If a16z is genuinely willing to underwrite ten-year hardware timelines with venture-style checks, it removes a longstanding barrier to seeding the physical layer of AI infrastructure at scale.

Federal judge blocks Pentagon's supply-chain-risk designation of Anthropic as unlawful First Amendment retaliation

TechCrunch · Al Jazeera · Forbes · UPI · 2026-08-28 · Source ↗

US District Judge Rita Lin, a Biden appointee sitting in the Northern District of California, ruled Friday that Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth's March order labelling Anthropic a supply-chain risk and directing all federal agencies to stop working with the Claude maker was unlawful First Amendment retaliation and Fifth Amendment denial of due process. The 59-page ruling calls the Pentagon action "arbitrary and capricious" and grants Anthropic its first court win over Hegseth. A second Anthropic case remains pending in Washington, DC over a separate Pentagon designation blocking civilian-agency contracts.

The Lin ruling is the first federal-court decision to place First Amendment limits on the Trump-Hegseth Defense Department's ability to weaponize procurement against AI vendors it disagrees with. Anthropic's public position, articulated across 2025 and 2026, has been that Claude will not be sold for use in lethal autonomous weapons systems or domestic mass surveillance — a stance Hegseth and senior Pentagon officials treated as insubordination and answered in March with a supply-chain-risk designation ordering every federal agency, not just DoD, to halt existing and future work with the company.

"The empty invocation of national security is not a blank check to punish and retaliate against government critics." — US District Judge Rita Lin, N.D. Cal., 2026-08-28

The ruling

Lin's 59-page order finds that the Pentagon's designation both violated Anthropic's First Amendment rights and denied the company due process under the Fifth Amendment. She describes the designation as aiming to "make a public example out of Anthropic for its 'arrogance' in criticizing the government," a phrasing that reads as directly quoting internal Pentagon email traffic surfaced in discovery. The order finds the designation "arbitrary and capricious" under the Administrative Procedure Act and requires federal agencies to unwind procurement bans premised on it.

The unresolved D.C. case

A parallel Anthropic lawsuit remains pending in Washington, DC, targeting a separate Pentagon designation that blocks civilian-agency contracts. The California win narrows but does not eliminate the procurement risk. Anthropic still faces the possibility of losing civilian federal contracts if the D.C. court sides with the Defense Department on the second designation. The company has been running government pilots at USAID, HHS, and DHS-adjacent civilian agencies through 2025 and 2026.

"really troubling" and "at odds with the First Amendment" — Lin, describing the government's litigation posture

The industry read

The ruling establishes a First Amendment precedent that AI vendors can cite when refusing to weaken safety commitments as a condition of federal contract eligibility. The precedent runs against a Trump-administration procurement posture that has treated public safety commitments from labs as evidence of unreliability. The immediate downstream effect: OpenAI, Google DeepMind, xAI and other labs now have judicial cover for maintaining public red-lines on lethal-autonomy and mass-surveillance sales even when those positions cost them Pentagon contracts.

The Marvell drop is the second cautionary print of the AI-infrastructure earnings cycle after Nvidia's own modest post-earnings compression Thursday. The stock still traded down on Friday morning, hitting an intraday low roughly seven percent below Thursday's close before ending the session around minus six percent.

"AI-related bookings remain exceptionally robust, and we expect our revenue growth to accelerate further through the remainder of fiscal 2027." — Matt Murphy, Marvell CEO

The warrant math

The Google warrant is the deal buyers spent Friday morning reprising. Peer moves the same day were quiet: Nvidia -0.2 percent, Broadcom +1 percent, Intel -2 percent, semi ETF SOXX -0.8 percent. The FY28 read

The most important number in the print was the raised FY28 revenue outlook of roughly $18 billion, up from a prior $16.5 billion figure. That implies a growth reacceleration in the fiscal year starting February 2027, driven by the ramp of Marvell's XPU and networking silicon into hyperscaler design wins already announced. The Google warrant is one of at least five active custom-silicon programs. Friday's read: buyers want to see the FY28 print land before crediting the FY29 warrant math.

Chat retention extends from 28 days to account lifetime. Code Review default flips from "Lite" to "Balanced" on the same date. Cloud Agent gains a Sandbox execution backend.

The Friday changelog is the most consequential policy shift GitHub has pushed on Copilot since the Cloud Agent GA in November 2025. Three separate changes land at once, each affecting how enterprises budget, retain and govern the tool. The billing switch to prepaid seats mirrors moves by Salesforce, Adobe and Zoom to reduce revenue leakage from over-issued license pools; the unified experience collapses three previously-separate surfaces onto the Cloud Agent's sessions substrate; and the code-review default flip silently expands Actions-minute consumption for any org that has not explicitly reasserted the Lite setting.

"Revoking a seat does not result in a prorated refund." — GitHub Copilot changelog, 2026-08-28

The billing switch

Copilot Business and Enterprise signups reopen with strengthened vetting: upfront seat payment applies to new signups starting September 1, 2026, and to existing credit-card and PayPal customers on October 1. Seat removal becomes non-refundable on that date. Overage may apply above included allowances, and included allowances may be prorated monthly. GitHub added the specific note that "fully canceling Copilot and returning later may trigger updated account vetting and billing behaviors" — a fraud-mitigation signal aimed at trial-cycling and free-tier abuse.

No earlier than September 28, 2026, GitHub will merge Copilot Cloud Agent, Copilot Chat on github.com, and Copilot Chat in GitHub Mobile into one product with one policy. Code Review default effort level moves from "Lite" to "Balanced" on September 28. Organizations that want to keep Lite as their default must explicitly select it in repo or organization settings before that date. The change materially raises Actions-minute consumption per review, and the cost delta on organizations with hundreds of daily PRs is non-trivial. GitHub is betting that the review-quality lift from Balanced justifies the cost; enterprise buyers should model both sides before September 28.

The v2.1.251 tag is Anthropic's second major Claude Code release of the week after the v2.1.247 push earlier and is the first to expose programmatic control over mid-session model escalation. The two new hooks — PreModelSwitch and PostModelSwitch — let enterprise operators police which model a Claude Code session may use, block escalations to xhigh or max effort tiers on cost or policy grounds, or emit audit events for compliance systems. "Foreground subagent tool calls and results now stream live to Remote Control clients." — Claude Code v2.1.251 release notes

The hooks

PreModelSwitch fires when Claude Code proposes a model change during a session; the hook payload includes the current model, the proposed model, and the trigger reason. PostModelSwitch fires after the change succeeds. Both hooks can return decisions that block, confirm, or annotate the transition. The practical use case: an enterprise Auto Mode Claude Code session that starts on Sonnet 5 and wants to escalate to Opus 5 max effort can be gated at the hook layer, either denying the escalation or forcing a human confirmation. The /cost command adds a per-session prompt-cache line with hit ratio, misses, tokens re-cached, and warm/cold stats — the first surface that gives developers visible feedback on cache health during a run.

The security fixes

The release patches a symlink-swap vulnerability in Read, Write, and Edit that could read or write outside approved locations, and a plugin path-traversal that let commands point outside plugin directories. The Workflow tool's scriptPath now validates before file access. Grep and Glob deny rules apply through symlinked search paths. The CLI adds attach, logs, stop, respawn, and rm verbs, and --resume now prints the exact claude attach command. A second v2.1.250 tag also shipped Aug 28 with generic reliability improvements.

The Aug 28 llama.cpp release cadence is unusually dense even by ggml-org standards. Thirteen tags between b10665 and b10679 land inside a single UTC day, covering four backends (Metal, Vulkan, SYCL, OpenVINO), two new architectures (Nemotron 3.5, Qwen4-Exp), and a correctness fix that retroactively invalidates a range of Vulkan-backed Qwen3.8 inference runs.

"silently wrong tokens under greedy decoding, different output on every server start, and invalid speculative-decoding acceptance, with nothing logged. Hits Qwen3.8's recurrent state." — llama.cpp b10677 release notes

The correctness fix

b10677 (18:27 UTC, PR #27812) is the most consequential release of the day. The Vulkan graph optimizer had been treating two views of one tensor as independent nodes, allowing the optimizer to reorder operations across aliased reads and writes. On Qwen3.8 recurrent-state workloads the result was silently wrong tokens under greedy decoding, non-determinism across server restarts, and invalid speculative-decoding acceptance — with no error logged. The fix restores correctness. Any Vulkan-backed Qwen3.8 inference done between the introduction of the bug and Aug 28 should be treated as suspect.

The Nemotron 3.5 landing

b10665 (00:17 UTC, PR #27804) adds Nvidia's Nemotron 3.5 open-weight model to the llama.cpp registry through the DSpark path, co-authored by Sigbjørn Skjæret and Xuan Son Nguyen. Nemotron 3.5 shipped Aug 11 and had been usable through vLLM and SGLang but not through the dominant CPU/Metal/Vulkan open-inference stack until Friday.

The OpenVINO upgrade

b10672 (13:51 UTC, PR #27843) upgrades OpenVINO to 2026.3.1 with whisper.cpp support, Qwen3.5 on Intel NPU, and four new operators (RELU, POOL_2D, QUICK_GEGLU, ROLL). A new GGML_OPENVINO_NPU_COMPILE_CONFIG env var and static-model build parallelization land alongside. IM2COL+MatMul convolution fuses into a native OpenVINO convolution primitive. The upgrade opens Qwen3.5 NPU inference for Intel accelerators and gives whisper.cpp a first-class OpenVINO path.

The Apple silicon story

b10668, b10673 and adjacent tags land per-device flash-attention tuning data for M3 Max, M4 (10-core), M5 and M5 Pro across the standard F16 and Q4_0/Q4_1/Q5_0/Q5_1/Q8_0 quantization set, generated via the ggml-metal-tuning binary. The M5 and M5 Pro profiles arrive alongside Apple's Aug 26 opening of M6 Mac mini pre-orders and M5 Ultra Mac Studio availability. b10678 (18:51 UTC) reduces graph splits on the Qwen4-Exp architecture that Alibaba unveiled Aug 26 as Qwen3.8-Flash-Next. b10679 (19:23 UTC) adds a --tensor-read-lazy flag to the bench tool with new LLAMA_LAZY_MODE_* constants.

The LM Studio pair on Aug 28 is a rare same-day double release for the desktop local-inference app. 0.4.22 introduces drafter-model support for speculative decoding, previously only accessible through vLLM v0.28 (which shipped the DFlash2 speculative sampling path Aug 26). 0.4.23 targets the Qwen 3.8 Flash Next architecture reliability that Alibaba's Aug 26 launch surfaced as unstable in production.

"Improve reliability of Qwen 3.8 Flash Next" — LM Studio 0.4.23 release notes

The drafter-model support

DFlash, DSpark and MTP assistant drafter models unlock speculative decoding for local inference on consumer hardware. The drafter model runs alongside the primary model, proposes token continuations, and the primary model accepts or rejects them in parallel. When acceptance rates run high the effective throughput can improve substantially on the same hardware. LM Studio requires the llama.cpp engine 2.29.1 or later, and drafter models must be paired with primary models by matching tokenizer families.

The image-in-tool-response fix

0.4.22 adds image support in tool responses on the OpenAI-compatible /v1/responses and /v1/chat/completions endpoints and the Anthropic-compatible /v1/messages endpoint. That closes an API-parity gap with the hosted APIs and unblocks local-hosted agentic workflows that need to return generated or fetched images as part of tool results — the common case for local browser-use and computer-use agents.

The Linux fixes

0.4.22 patches AppImage startup on systems without FUSE 2, AppImage startup when Chromium sandboxing is unavailable, lms wake-up for AppImage installs, .deb desktop icons, in-app updates for AppImage and .deb, recovery after cancelling .deb update authorization, and Wayland keyboard-shortcut restoration after window-focus changes. The Linux fix batch reads as accumulated ecosystem debt — most of these are long-standing issues that the LM Studio team paid down in one release before opening the door on drafter-model workloads.

The disruption question

Researchers cited by Krebs, The Register and Ars Technica agree the arrests disrupt but do not eliminate the Shai-Hulud threat. TeamPCP leaked the worm's source code to GitHub, spawning copycat operations. The group ran a Telegram competition offering prizes for the largest attacks. With source public and a copycat ecosystem in operation, the worm variant tree survives even if the alleged principals are held in custody.
