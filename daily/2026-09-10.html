Google commits €13 billion to Finland and buys 22 years of Loviisa nuclear power for its AI datacenters.

Google announced Wednesday its largest single European investment on record, €13 billion of Finnish datacenter capacity built out over 2027 and 2028 across a Hamina expansion and three new campuses in Kajaani, Vaala and Muhos. The company paired the build with a 22-year power-purchase agreement with Finnish utility Fortum covering up to half the output of the Loviisa nuclear plant, extending Loviisa's operating life to 2050 from a prior 2030 shutdown target. Google estimated the first two years of construction will add €3.6 billion per year to Finnish GDP, and that the completed campuses will support roughly 7,000 direct and indirect jobs. The announcement is Google's second multi-gigawatt nuclear commitment of 2026 and the third by a US hyperscaler in as many months, following NextEra's $1.9 billion DOE loan on Monday to restart Iowa's Duane Arnold reactor for Google workloads.

Application · Tech · Bloomberg / ESG Today / Helsinki Times · 2026-09-09 · Source ↗

Google's Finland commitment landed Wednesday morning in Helsinki with three separate documents: a corporate press release detailing the €13 billion four-campus build-out, a Fortum-side announcement of the 22-year power-purchase agreement covering "up to half" of the Loviisa nuclear plant's roughly 1,050 MW output, and a joint economic-impact briefing with the Finnish government estimating that the first phase of construction (2027 and 2028) will add an average of €3.6 billion per year to Finnish GDP over those two years. It is Google's single largest announced investment in Europe and the largest committed foreign direct investment in Finnish history.

The build-out includes an expansion of Google's existing Hamina campus on the southern coast, which has operated since 2011, and three new datacenter sites in the northern towns of Kajaani, Vaala and Muhos. Kajaani sits next to a decommissioned UPM paper mill and inherits its transmission connection; Vaala and Muhos are colocated with existing hydroelectric infrastructure on the Oulujoki river system. Local reporting in Helsinki Times noted that Finland's electricity spot market has cleared at some of the lowest wholesale prices in Europe for eighteen consecutive months, driven by an existing surplus of nuclear, hydro and wind that Google is now committing to consume for its Gemini training and inference workloads.

"This is the biggest investment we have ever made in Europe, and one of the largest tech investments in Finland's history. It's a vote of confidence in Finnish talent, Finnish energy and the Finnish grid." — Ruth Porat, Google President and Chief Investment Officer, in Wednesday's press release

The Fortum contract and Loviisa's second life

The Fortum power-purchase agreement is the piece of the announcement with the longest-tail implications. Loviisa is a two-reactor VVER-440 pressurized water plant commissioned in 1977 and 1980 that Fortum had scheduled for retirement in 2030. The 22-year Google contract, together with a parallel Finnish regulatory review, extends Loviisa's licensed operating life to 2050. Google specified in the Fortum joint release that the contract covers "up to half" of Loviisa's output. At Loviisa's roughly 1,050 MW rated capacity that is nominally 525 MW of firm baseload, or approximately 4.6 TWh annually assuming continuous operation.

Google paired the nuclear PPA with three secondary renewable contracts covering roughly 1.2 GW of new Finnish wind, a 300 MW grid battery in Muhos, and a set of transmission-line upgrades funded jointly with Fingrid, the Finnish grid operator. The company disclosed that total energy procurement for the four campuses will run roughly 4 GW at nameplate, of which the Loviisa contract satisfies 12 to 13 percent, the wind contracts about 30 percent, and grid draw the balance. Fortum's CEO Markus Rauramo told Reuters the Loviisa extension will require capital investments of roughly €500 million over the next three years for steam-generator replacement and containment upgrades, and that Google's PPA underwrites that spend on a fixed-price basis.

"Without a customer of Google's scale willing to commit for twenty-two years, Loviisa closes in 2030. With them, we can run it to 2050 and probably beyond." — Markus Rauramo, Fortum CEO, on the plant's second operating life

Second nuclear commitment of the week, third of the quarter

The Fortum contract is Google's second multi-gigawatt nuclear-power commitment announced within a week. On Monday, NextEra secured a $1.9 billion DOE loan from the Office of Energy Dominance Financing to restart Iowa's Duane Arnold Energy Center's 615 MW reactor to power Google datacenters under an October 2025 25-year power-purchase agreement, targeting a 2029 restart pending NRC approval. Both Iowa and Finland deals extend the pattern set in Q2 by Microsoft's Three Mile Island reactor restart and Amazon's Talen Susquehanna PPA: US and European hyperscalers are now the marginal buyers of firm-baseload nuclear generation, and their long-dated contracts are what makes non-carbon extensions of end-of-life reactors financeable.

Nick Fox, Google's SVP of infrastructure who signed the Fortum deal, told the Financial Times that Google's total 2026-through-2030 committed nuclear procurement has now passed 6 GW across three continents. That figure is roughly one third of Anthropic's separately-disclosed 14.8 GW of ten-year compute commitments across AWS, Google Cloud, Azure, Nscale and Lambda, and it approaches the 8 GW of total nameplate power that Sam Altman told the Financial Times in July would be OpenAI's minimum 2027 operating footprint.

What Finland gets

The Finnish government's Wednesday briefing on the Google deal broke out the €3.6 billion annualized GDP impact over 2027-2028: €2.1 billion of construction wages and services, €800 million of imported equipment routed through Finnish contractors, and €700 million of tax and grid-connection revenue. Once operating, the four campuses are projected to support roughly 7,000 direct and indirect jobs, up from Hamina's current 400. Prime Minister Petteri Orpo said in a joint press conference with Ruth Porat that the deal will require a review of Finland's data-center electricity-tariff regime, currently set at €0.0005/kWh under a 2011 exemption designed to attract Google to Hamina in the first place, but which has been criticized by Finnish opposition parties as excessive corporate subsidy at a time of consumer electricity-bill increases.

The deal does not close Google's European infrastructure book for the year. Sundar Pichai told CNBC last month that the company is "actively negotiating" a similar-scale package in Portugal and a smaller one in Ireland, and that the Netherlands is under review for a second Eemshaven campus. Google's total announced 2026 non-US infrastructure spend now exceeds $28 billion, roughly one-quarter of Alphabet's overall $130 billion 2026 capex guide.

Google's Finland commitment reframes a week that already contained a $60 billion Qualcomm-Amazon deal and a Chinese 15th Five-Year plan targeting 9,800 exaflops of national compute by 2030. The AI infrastructure market is now consolidating around long-dated firm-power contracts, not just chips. Wednesday added two more geographies to the pattern: JD Cloud committed to a 100,000-GPU cluster built entirely on Moore Threads domestic silicon, and NSA, CISA and FBI issued a joint advisory naming six Chinese AI companies for industrial-scale distillation of US frontier models since late 2024 with billions of tokens extracted from Claude, GPT, Gemini and Grok variants.

The regulatory line hardened. The European Commission opened a formal probe into OpenAI's DseWiki agent incident under the AI Act, Anthropic's compute-commitment stack passed $517 billion of ten-year contracted spend, and the Intercept published 400-plus pages of FOIA-obtained Pentagon contracts showing OpenAI, Google and xAI each hold up-to-$200 million ceilings for military decision-making tools with a specific DoD ask to OpenAI for a variant "designed to rarely say no." Anthropic refused those terms and remains classified as a Section 889 supply-chain risk.

NSA, CISA and FBI name six Chinese AI firms for "industrial-scale" distillation of US frontier models

The Register / Engadget / Time News · 2026-09-09 · Source ↗

The tri-agency joint cybersecurity advisory alleges DeepSeek, Moonshot AI, Alibaba, MiniMax, StepFun and Z.AI have run coordinated, systematic distillation campaigns against Claude, GPT, Gemini and Grok since late 2024, extracting billions of tokens across millions of exchanges likely with Chinese government awareness.

The National Security Agency, Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency and Federal Bureau of Investigation published a joint cybersecurity advisory Wednesday morning naming DeepSeek, Moonshot AI, Alibaba, MiniMax, StepFun and Z.AI as running "aggressive, malicious, and targeted" knowledge-distillation campaigns against US frontier AI companies. It is the first US-government document to name Chinese AI companies collectively as intelligence targets rather than simply flagging one-off Terms-of-Service violations, and the advisory concludes that distillation "functions as the critical core" of the six companies' development programs rather than an ancillary training method.

The advisory covers billions of tokens exchanged across millions of API sessions since at least late 2024, spanning variants of Anthropic's Claude, OpenAI's GPT, Google's Gemini and xAI's Grok. It says the campaigns "likely operate with awareness of, and in some cases direction from" Chinese government entities. For DeepSeek specifically, the agencies claim the company targeted "reasoning capabilities, agentic functions, and specialized optimizations" from GPT-4, GPT-5 and multiple Claude versions to train its R1 and V3 models, and that DeepSeek's widely-cited $5.6 million R1 training cost is "misleading because it excludes the cost of data acquired through distillation." For Alibaba, the advisory claims "industrial-scale distillation" improved the Qwen family across all three of its 2025-2026 release cycles.

"The scale, coordination and persistence of the campaigns we describe is not consistent with commercial exploration. It is consistent with a national program to close a capability gap." — Rob Joyce, NSA Director of Cybersecurity, in the advisory's cover statement

The recommendation nobody expected

The document's second half contains an operational recommendation aimed at OpenAI, Anthropic, Google and xAI: US frontier providers should "quietly degrade" suspect Chinese-linked accounts rather than banning them outright, so as to inject noisy, subtly-wrong training signal into any downstream distillation. The recommendation is unprecedented in a joint cybersecurity advisory. Simon Willison flagged Wednesday evening that the recommendation may violate US providers' own published Terms of Service, since a "quiet degrade" policy would mean the provider knowingly furnishes lower-quality output to a paying customer without notification. Anthropic and OpenAI declined to comment on whether they intend to adopt the recommendation. Google's public response through Kent Walker acknowledged the advisory but committed only to "reviewing the pattern of usage the agencies describe."

Alibaba pushed back Wednesday afternoon. Eddie Wu, Alibaba Cloud CEO, told the FT that Qwen's training-data pipeline is "public, documented and reviewed by external auditors" and that any distillation from US frontier APIs would represent less than 0.4 percent of Qwen 3.8's total 12 trillion-token training set. DeepSeek did not respond. Moonshot AI, whose Kimi K3 flagship trained on 47,000 distillation trajectories according to an August paper the company published itself, said the paper's methodology was "misrepresented" by the US advisory and that the trajectories were sourced "from publicly available benchmark evaluation traces, not from paid API access." Z.AI's founder Tang Jie posted on Weibo that the advisory was "a political document, not a technical one."

What providers can actually see

The advisory's technical appendix lists the fingerprints US providers can use to identify likely distillation traffic: unusually-long context windows, extremely high sampling temperatures, output-token-heavy request patterns, VPN egress from Southeast Asian jurisdictions, and repeated resubmission of near-identical prompts to trigger sample diversity. Providers were also instructed to look for "coordinated prompt-family patterns" indicating an automated agent farm rather than human users. The document was co-published with the UK's National Cyber Security Centre and Japan's Cybersecurity Council, which is the first three-way NSA-NCSC-Japan alignment on a China-specific AI action.

JD Cloud and Moore Threads target a 100,000-GPU cluster built entirely on Chinese silicon

TechNode / Data Center Dynamics · 2026-09-09 · Source ↗

JD Cloud announced at its 2026 Global Technology Explorers Conference the first 100,000-GPU deployment of domestic Chinese GPUs at a major domestic AI cloud provider, built on Moore Threads' "full-featured" GPUs and targeting training, inference and embodied-AI workloads.

JD Cloud's announcement Wednesday at the annual JD Global Technology Explorers Conference in Beijing sets a Chinese milestone: the first 100,000-GPU AI cluster committed to publicly by a top-tier domestic hyperscaler and built end-to-end on Chinese silicon. The cluster's GPUs come from Moore Threads, the Beijing-based fabless designer that only listed on Shanghai's STAR Market in December, at prices JD did not disclose. JD Cloud and Moore Threads have previously partnered on a 10,000-GPU cluster deployed in Q1 2025.

The cluster is targeted at three workload classes: large-model training, large-model inference, and embodied intelligence (JD's internal shorthand for the humanoid-robot software stack it is building with Unitree and Fourier). JD Cloud president Cao Peng told the conference the cluster will be made available on a shared-tenant basis to third-party customers, but did not publish pricing. Moore Threads CEO Zhang Jianzhong said the cluster will use the company's latest "Huagang" microarchitecture, which the company disclosed in July delivers roughly 10x efficiency-per-watt over its 2023 first-generation MTT-S3000 accelerator, though independent benchmarking is not yet public.

"Chinese AI cannot be built on foreign GPUs alone. This is the first cluster in the world to prove that a hundred-thousand-card scale is possible on domestic silicon." — Zhang Jianzhong, Moore Threads CEO, at JD Global Technology Explorers Conference

What Moore Threads actually ships

Moore Threads' current highest-end product is the MTT-S5000, a 2nm SMIC-fabricated accelerator disclosed at Hot Chips 2026 that delivers roughly 620 FP16 TFLOP/s per card at 400 W, or approximately 1.55 FP16 TFLOP/s per watt. That is roughly 40 percent the per-watt density of Nvidia's H200 and 25 percent of the Blackwell B300 that shipped in Q1. To hit 100,000 cards, Moore Threads will need to sustain a production ramp of roughly 30,000 GPUs per quarter through 2027 at SMIC on 5nm and 3nm nodes, since the 2nm process is production-constrained to under 8,000 wafers per quarter.

The cluster's interconnect matters as much as the GPUs. Moore Threads unveiled MTLink 2.0 at the JD conference, a proprietary 800 Gb/s optical interconnect that the company said is fully interoperable with its own MT-Scale networking topology up to 10,000 GPUs and requires a two-tier fabric extension to reach 100,000. Independent networking analysts at SemiAnalysis flagged Wednesday afternoon that a 100,000-GPU MTLink deployment will consume roughly 200,000 800Gb/s transceivers, a volume the domestic Chinese optical-transceiver industry can supply but at 60-70 percent gross margin over Nvidia NVLink 6-based Western equivalents.

NeoHorse-1 recursive self-improvement paper tops Hugging Face Papers at 377 upvotes

arXiv 2609.08183 / Hugging Face Papers · 2026-09-09 · Source ↗

TokenRhythm's 4B and 9B checkpoints, post-trained from Qwen 3.5 with an agentic routing harness and on-policy reverse distillation, lift the 4B macro-average from 58.94 to 64.87 and the 9B from 65.60 to 69.04 across eleven agent-and-coding benchmarks — with the 4B post-trained closing most of the gap to the 9B base.

NeoHorse-1 posted to arXiv Tuesday and dominated Hugging Face's Papers-of-the-Day rankings by mid-Wednesday, reaching 377 upvotes and edging out Tencent Hunyuan's AuK speech-generation report at 178 and Omni Interaction Agent at 116. The paper's contribution is a training recipe for what its authors call "recursive self-improvement via agentic post-training with routing harness," an intelligent-routing layer that dispatches training examples across heterogeneous model checkpoints and dynamically re-weights the training mixture based on six-dimensional semantic evaluation of outputs.

The 36 co-authors, working under the TokenRhythm collective, released two Apache-2.0 checkpoints: NeoHorse-1-4B and NeoHorse-1-9B, both post-trained from Qwen 3.5 with tool-use, coding and instruction-following support. Post-training improvements are large but bounded: the 4B model's macro-average score across eleven agent and coding benchmarks rises from 58.94 (base Qwen 3.5-4B) to 64.87 (NeoHorse-1-4B), and the 9B rises from 65.60 to 69.04. What matters for the RSI framing is that the 4B post-trained checkpoint substantially closes the gap to the 9B base, which the paper interprets as evidence that agentic post-training extracts nearly 4x the parameter-scaling benefit of raw supervised fine-tuning at the same compute budget.

"The base model is a lower bound on capability. The routing harness is the mechanism that lifts the model above it, and the routing harness itself improves as the model improves. This is the first prototype of a self-lifting loop that we can run to completion." — NeoHorse-1 paper, §3.4

The paper's most-controversial claim is that the routing-guided on-policy distillation pipeline can, in principle, be run indefinitely without loss of quality, since the six-dimensional evaluator itself can be jointly upgraded on the improved model's outputs. Two independent responses landed on OpenReview by Wednesday evening from Nathan Lambert (AI2) and Chip Huyen questioning whether the evaluator-quality feedback loop is genuinely self-lifting or bounded by the base evaluator's coverage. The paper does not train the evaluator, which Lambert flagged as the critical missing experiment.
