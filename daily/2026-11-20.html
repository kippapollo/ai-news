Anthropic opens at $111.50, peaks near $2.1T, closes $97.17 — ANT is the first frontier AI stock.

Anthropic opened Thursday on the Nasdaq at $111.50 per share, a 34 percent pop from the $83 IPO price set Wednesday evening, briefly reaching an intraday high of $114.99 that put the fully diluted market cap near $2.1 trillion, before sliding to an intraday low of $92.72 and closing at $97.17 — up 17 percent on the day. Fortune reported volume above 600 million shares and a close just shy of $1.8 trillion. The company is the first pure-play frontier-AI model maker to list, with Jay Goldberg of Seaport Research Partners warning that Nvidia's Huang-era turmoil "could bring Anthropic much tougher questions" once ANT begins public quarterly reporting and Georgetown's Jason Schloetzer predicting closer scrutiny of Anthropic's ties to Nvidia and its customers. Wolfe Research, Citizens, and Melius Research initiated coverage with buy ratings. CFO Krishna Rao told an investor channel the company "currently use[s] about 75 percent of our total computing resources for research and development," and CEO Dario Amodei, speaking from the ticker-ring, said "we will not bend to either business or political pressure." The Nasdaq Composite fell 550 points and the Dow fell 1,101 points as the debut unfolded, a selloff market strategist Matt Maley of Miller Tabak tied in part to buying that "piled in ahead of the catalyst" and now reverses. At the close, Anthropic sits second-most-valuable IPO of all time behind SpaceX's $1.77 trillion June debut, with underwriters' greenshoe option on 147 million more shares pointing to a possible $118 billion in total proceeds if exercised.

Fortune · BigGo Finance · 2026-11-19 · Source ↗

Fortune's Nov 19 live blog opened on a scene that will date the week in AI capital markets: Anthropic's first public trade printed at $111.50 per share, 34 percent above the $83 IPO price that Hugh Son at CNBC had pulled from Anthropic's pricing call the night before. The first print landed about three and a half hours after the opening bell, consistent with Nasdaq's modified Dutch-style opening auction for mega-IPOs and the rhythm SpaceX's June debut had established. The afternoon fade was sharper. By the close ANT settled at $97.17, up 17 percent on the day, closing market cap just under $1.8 trillion, intraday low $92.72, and a Nasdaq Composite that itself fell 550 points while the Dow lost 1,101.

The Amodei moment at the ring

Dario Amodei, visible from the Nasdaq tower in Times Square, said "this moment is bigger than any one company, or any one country," a line he paired with "we will not bend to either business or political pressure" — an answer, by the Fortune reporter's read, to the Steve Eisman "not important anymore" quote inside yesterday's CNBC pricing piece and to the Brian Mast comments pulled into today's House Foreign Affairs Committee release. CFO Krishna Rao, taking a separate CNBC segment, said the company currently uses roughly 75 percent of its total computing resources for research and development, a number that landed against Nvidia interim CEO Colette Kress's Tuesday earnings-call pledge to narrow balance-sheet-adjacent compute commitments and against the $34.9 billion Lambda clause the prospectus had flagged as "may be cancelled." Amodei told the same host the company hopes "to earn an investment-grade credit rating someday," a signal the finance team is positioning for debt issuance alongside equity.

"This moment is bigger than any one company, or any one country. We will not bend to either business or political pressure." — Dario Amodei, CEO, Anthropic — speaking from the Nasdaq ring, 2026-11-19, via Fortune

The mechanics of the pop

Bloomberg's pre-open indication put the opening quote at 25 percent above the IPO price. Reality was 34 percent. Three factors show up in the morning tape commentary. First, size: underwriters upsized the offering from 825 million to 980 million shares, raising $81.3 billion — a number that passes SpaceX's $75 billion June record for largest IPO on record. Second, order book depth: Bloomberg reported the issue was up to seven times oversubscribed. Third, the placement stack: Sequoia, Iconiq, Lightspeed, Accel, and Altimeter all signalled they were buying more during the opening cross, according to the TechCrunch morning write-up that Sam on AI's newsletter cited. The greenshoe, if fully exercised, lifts total proceeds to $118 billion. Miller Tabak's Matt Maley warned the retail bid "piled in ahead of the catalyst and now that event has passed," which matches the fade from $114.99 to $92.72 inside two hours.

OpenAI published a Codex sandbox security note Thursday that reads less as marketing and more as threat-model documentation. Filesystem changes and network access are bounded by user-set permission profiles on macOS, Linux, and Windows; network access is disabled by default; and the note names residual risks explicitly — an agent that reads sensitive local files but writes nothing can still exfiltrate via API calls allowed by the profile, and an agent that modifies source code can still trigger downstream behavior when that code runs outside the sandbox. Linux Foundation · 2026-11-19 · Source ↗

Open-source agentic code review built on Gemini 4, now hosted by the Linux Foundation for a kernel tree over 35 million lines of code with roughly 85,000 changes per year.

Sashiko, the Gemini-powered open-source code-review system built for the Linux kernel, formally moved under the Linux Foundation on Thursday. The project's design: an agent that ingests every patch submitted to the Linux kernel mailing list, cross-references the change against kernel documentation and prior Signed-off-by history, drafts a review comment aligned with kernel maintainer style, and either posts it as a suggestion or routes high-confidence issues to the maintainer directly. The Linux kernel carries over 35 million lines of code with about 85,000 changes landing yearly; Sashiko's goal is to give every patch a Gemini-generated pre-review before a human maintainer sees it.

The Linux Foundation handoff is a governance move: the project was originally sponsored by Google and now gains a neutral steward with reproducible release processes. Maintainers inside the Linux kernel subsystems have cautiously welcomed the project, with Greg Kroah-Hartman quoted in the press note saying "Sashiko reads like a competent junior reviewer, which is exactly the scale of help we need." The comparable Oracle-funded Rust-for-Linux reviewer stays separate.
