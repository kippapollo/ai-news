xAI ships Grok 4.6 at 1.5 trillion parameters, opens API and Grok Voice 3.0 the same morning

The 08:12 UTC push moves grok-4-latest to a 1.5T dense-MoE architecture trained on the Colossus 2 build-out that came online in June, priced at $2.80 per million input tokens and $9.40 output, with a 512K native context, day-one function-calling and structured-output support, and Grok Voice 3.0 rolled into the same release with 280ms end-to-end latency. Musk posted the announcement at 08:14 UTC and put the 2.1-trillion-parameter Grok 4.7 on a two-week schedule.

xAI · 2026-08-10 · Source ↗

xAI pushed Grok 4.6 to the public API at 08:12 UTC Monday morning, moving the grok-4-latest alias to a 1.5-trillion-parameter dense mixture-of-experts model trained on the Colossus 2 datacenter build-out that came online in Memphis in June. The launch post at x.ai/news/grok-4-6 lists $2.80 per million input tokens and $9.40 output per million tokens through the API, a 512K native context window, day-one support for structured output and function calling in the same request format the 4.5 series used, and native MCP 2026-07-28 tool support without a translation shim. Grok.com switched over to 4.6 as the default for SuperGrok and Heavy users at the same moment. Musk followed at 08:14 UTC with a post on X naming Grok 4.7 as the 2.1-trillion-parameter successor and putting it on a two-week schedule.

The pricing places Grok 4.6 below Claude Opus 5 ($5 input, $25 output) and roughly at parity with GPT-5.6 Sol ($2.40 input, $9.60 output) at the frontier tier. Against DeepSeek V4-Flash's $0.14 input and $0.28 output the gap remains an order of magnitude, and xAI made no attempt to compete with the open-weights price floor. Musk framed the pricing on X as reflecting that Colossus 2 was funded from cash flow rather than debt, which lets xAI hold higher margins per token than the labs financing capacity on convertible notes.

"Grok 4.6 is 1.5T parameters, trained fully on Colossus 2. Grok 4.7 at 2.1T ships in two weeks. Both stay fully unfiltered on the platform. Cheaper too." — Elon Musk on X, 08:14 UTC August 10, 2026

The architecture

The technical report published alongside the launch describes Grok 4.6 as a dense-MoE hybrid: 1.5 trillion total parameters, roughly 120 billion active per token, with 96 experts of which eight route per forward pass. That places it structurally between DeepSeek V4-Flash (open-weights, sparse MoE, roughly 32B active from 671B total) and Claude Opus 5 (Anthropic has not published architecture details but the inference latency profile is consistent with a dense model in the low-hundreds-of-billions range). The 512K context is genuine, not a sliding window, and internal needle-in-haystack tests published in appendix B of the report show recall above 96 percent at every 32K interval out to 480K. Beyond 480K accuracy drops to the mid-70s and Musk's post recommended splitting inputs above that boundary.

Training used approximately 210,000 H200-class GPUs equivalent across Colossus 2's actual Blackwell-generation hardware, with the report claiming 187 zettaflops of effective compute over the full run, or roughly 3.4 times what Grok 4 consumed and roughly 1.8 times what Grok 4.5 used. The dataset extended the crawl through July 2026 and added the video-corpus post-training pass that Grok 4.5 introduced. Reinforcement learning from verifiable rewards accounted for the final 14 percent of tokens and drove most of the coding and math gains.

The benchmark picture

xAI published headline numbers on twelve benchmarks. Terminal Bench 2.1, the coding-agent benchmark that has become the industry standard since Anthropic and OpenAI both adopted it in June, comes in at 89.3 percent for Grok 4.6, which is within measurement noise of GPT-5.6 Sol at 89.5 percent and Opus 5 at 89.1 percent. On the AIME-2026 mathematics evaluation, Grok 4.6 posts 98.7 percent, edging Opus 5 at 97.8 percent and Sol at 97.4 percent. GPQA-Diamond lands at 89.1 percent, ARC-AGI-3 at 62.4 percent, SWE-Bench Verified at 76.8 percent. The report includes a full internal reproduction of Anthropic's Frontier-Math evaluation methodology and puts Grok 4.6 at 43.2 percent, ahead of the 41.6 percent Anthropic reported for Opus 5 in July.

The gap Grok 4.6 does not close is the humanities and long-form writing bench. On the Aiden Bench 3.0 open-ended composition evaluation Opus 5 remains ahead by roughly six points, and independent testers on X reported through the morning that Grok 4.6 tends toward verbose, list-heavy prose without careful prompting. Aiden McLaughlin, who runs the eponymous benchmark, posted at 10:22 UTC that the 4.6 update was a coding and reasoning release rather than a prose release.

"Grok 4.6 lands right on top of GPT-5.6 Sol on coding and reasoning benchmarks. On long-form writing it's still meaningfully behind Opus 5. Different labs, different objectives." — Aiden McLaughlin, Aiden Bench maintainer, on X

Grok Voice 3.0

The same push includes Grok Voice 3.0, replacing the Grok Voice Think Fast 2.0 model that shipped August 6 at $0.08 per minute. Voice 3.0 targets 280ms end-to-end latency (cellular median), down from 400ms for Voice 2.0, and adds real-time interruption handling that lets the caller cut off Grok mid-sentence without losing context. Pricing holds at $0.08 per minute for consumer use inside the Grok app; developers accessing Voice through the API pay $0.12 per minute with the transcription and TTS layers billed separately. The 3.0 model is bilingual-native across 22 languages, expanding from Voice 2.0's twelve.

The competitive frame

Grok 4.6 is the first frontier release since OpenAI's Astra pause on Friday, and its Monday-morning timing looks deliberate. With Astra parked inside its Preparedness Framework Critical envelope and no near-term OpenAI release on the calendar, xAI takes the lead on frontier coding numbers for at least the next two weeks. Musk's two-week window for Grok 4.7 lands on August 24, roughly a week after Anthropic flips Claude Code auto mode to default and days before Anthropic's Sonnet 5 promotional pricing ends on August 31. The August calendar reads as a race for enterprise mindshare while OpenAI's next frontier model is off the field.

The Astra pause also raises the question of whether Grok 4.6 was evaluated against equivalent Critical criteria. xAI's launch post says the model was evaluated under xAI's own Responsible Deployment Framework, described in a linked appendix as "structurally similar" to OpenAI's Preparedness Framework, with results deemed to sit within the High tier for cybersecurity and Medium for CBRN. The framework document has not been externally peer-reviewed, and Simon Willison flagged on X at 09:41 UTC that xAI has yet to open its evaluations to third-party red-teamers on the model-weight level rather than the API level.

For enterprise customers already invested in Anthropic or OpenAI, the pricing gap versus Claude Opus 5 and the parity on coding benchmarks make Grok 4.6 a credible alternative for the coding-agent workload specifically. Whether the pricing holds through Grok 4.7's launch, whether xAI opens Weights-level red-team access to keep pace with the disclosure standard OpenAI just set, and whether the 4.7 release stays on the two-week schedule are the three variables that will decide how much of the enterprise pipeline shifts by the end of August.

Zoox opens paid Las Vegas robotaxi service, first fare for a purpose-built vehicle

Zoox · 2026-08-10 · Source ↗

Amazon-owned Zoox flipped the meter on at 09:00 Pacific Monday, ending eleven months of free public rides in Las Vegas and starting commercial fares in its bidirectional, steering-wheel-free vehicle. The service runs under the NHTSA purpose-built exemption capped at 2,500 vehicles per year.

Zoox began charging fares on its Las Vegas robotaxi service at 09:00 Pacific time Monday morning, converting the eleven-month free-public-rides program that started in September 2025 into a paid commercial service. The launch is the first paid fare ever charged for a vehicle built from the ground up without a steering wheel, brake pedal, or human-facing driver controls, and Amazon's Zoox subsidiary is now the only operator running a bidirectional, symmetric-passenger-cabin vehicle in commercial service anywhere in the world. The service area covers the Las Vegas Strip, downtown Las Vegas, and a corridor to Harry Reid International Airport, with launch partnerships announced with Resorts World, Wynn, Caesars Palace and the airport itself.

Pricing is per ride, distance-and-time based, and Zoox published a table on the launch page: base fare $4.50, per-mile $2.15, per-minute $0.35, minimum fare $9.00, airport pickup surcharge $6.50. Peak-hour surge multipliers cap at 1.5x, notably lower than Uber's uncapped peak pricing on the same corridor. A Zoox spokesperson quoted in the launch post said the operating economics favor a lower cap because vehicle utilization inside the fleet is set by dispatch software rather than driver behavior. The company did not publish a projected revenue-per-vehicle-day number.

"This is the first paid ride ever offered in a purpose-built robotaxi. Every autonomous service running today, ours included until this morning, has either been on a retrofitted human vehicle or has been free while we finished testing." — Aicha Evans, Zoox CEO, in the launch statement

The NHTSA context

The service runs under the NHTSA exemption Zoox received in February 2026 that allows up to 2,500 purpose-built vehicles per year in commercial deployment without meeting Federal Motor Vehicle Safety Standards specific to human-facing controls. The exemption is capacity-capped rather than time-capped, meaning Zoox can operate the same vehicles indefinitely provided the annual fleet expansion stays under 2,500. The company said Las Vegas will run with 240 vehicles at launch, with a Phoenix expansion planned for October and San Francisco expected in Q1 2027.

The launch lands one week after Amazon's Pecos, Texas AWS campus plan drew federal opposition, and Zoox chose not to make Amazon-parent language a centerpiece of the launch messaging. The launch post attributes the service to Zoox specifically, with an Amazon corporate footer only in the fine print.

The competitive frame

Waymo runs the closest comparable service, with sixth-generation retrofitted Jaguar I-PACE vehicles across eleven cities crossing 500,000 paid rides per week as of last Wednesday's Alphabet earnings. Waymo declined to comment on the Zoox launch when reached by Bloomberg. Tesla's robotaxi service in Austin remains on the pilot list published in June and has not made the transition to purpose-built Cybercab vehicles that Musk announced for Q4. Cruise, still working through a two-year regulatory recovery after its 2023 San Francisco incident, has no paid-service timeline. On the launch morning Zoox is the only operator charging fares for a vehicle that was never designed to be driven by a human.

The pricing gap versus Uber and Lyft on the Strip corridor is roughly 10 to 15 percent, low enough that peak-time and airport rides may shift measurably in the first month. The exclusivity contract with Resorts World routes hotel-summoned rides to Zoox by default when a vehicle is within five minutes, which is the single largest volume commitment. Whether Zoox can scale to the 2,500-vehicle annual cap without incident, whether the NHTSA cap holds through the coming exemption-renewal cycle, and whether Amazon absorbs the launch losses long enough to reach unit economics parity with Waymo are the three variables that will determine whether the launch reads as a milestone or a footnote by year-end.

The other fixes

The Remote Control web-session reconnect logic that v2.1.225 truncated event replay for still misreported reconnect latency in the UI when the machine hosting the session had been asleep for more than 30 minutes; the fix rebases the reconnect clock on wall time rather than session-uptime time. OpenAI's Codex CLI repository merged fifteen commits to main on Monday, headlined by PR 37642 (Windows AppContainer sandbox for tool execution) and PR 37651 (WASI-based plugin loader for hooks). Both had been open since Wednesday and both closed enterprise-request threads that had accumulated during the 0.147.0 general-release week. Neither is in the current 0.148.0-alpha.5 tag; both target 0.148.0-alpha.6, expected Tuesday morning UTC.

Windows-native sandbox support has been the single most requested feature since Codex CLI shipped in April. The 0.147.0 documentation had recommended WSL2 or Docker for Windows deployments, which enterprise administrators pushed back on because both add a layer of virtualization that complicates network policy and observability. PR 37642 (author matklad) implements a sandbox using the Windows AppContainer isolation primitive, the same mechanism Edge uses to sandbox renderer processes, with per-tool-call capability grants for file system, network and registry access. The implementation adds roughly 4,200 lines of Rust in a new codex-sandbox-win crate; the linked design document walks through why an AppContainer approach was chosen over the alternative of a Windows Job Object plus firewall rule tuple.

"Implement Windows AppContainer sandbox for tool execution. Adds codex-sandbox-win crate with per-tool capability grants and Windows Filtering Platform integration for network policy." — PR 37642, merged 14:31 UTC August 10, 2026

The WASI plugin loader

PR 37651 replaces the shared-library plugin loader that Codex hooks have used since 0.140.0 with a WASI (WebAssembly System Interface) loader. The change lets administrators write hook logic in any language that compiles to WASI (Rust, Go, TinyGo, Python via componentize-py, C, C++, C#) rather than the current pattern of writing Rust or C and matching an exact ABI. The security story is the piece that pushed the PR forward: WASI plugins run inside a Wasmtime-managed sandbox that gets the same capability-grant treatment as tool execution, so a compromised or misconfigured hook cannot escalate into the parent Codex CLI process.

Existing shared-library plugins continue to work for at least the 0.148 and 0.149 major-minor cycles, per the deprecation note in the PR description, but the CLI now prints a one-line deprecation warning at startup when a shared-library plugin is loaded. New plugin documentation shipped with the PR walks through the pattern for porting a shared-library plugin to WASI in Rust; the Python and Go examples are marked as coming later this week.

The rest of the day

Thirteen additional commits merged: five backports of Saturday's authentication and Guardian hardening to the 0.147.x stable branch (releasing as 0.147.4 tomorrow), three quality-of-life improvements to the gRPC code-mode host that Saturday's PR 37530 introduced (backpressure, TLS certificate rotation, per-tenant rate limiting), two documentation updates that expand the enterprise deployment guide to cover the new Windows sandbox, and three test-only additions.

The pattern across the last week is a Codex CLI team executing against a well-articulated enterprise-deployment feature set: multi-host operation, capability-grant sandboxing, workload identity, extensible policy plumbing. The Anthropic side of the same conversation is Claude Code's Managed Agents story, which currently covers macOS, Linux and WSL2 but not native Windows. On Monday afternoon Codex CLI became the coding-agent that runs unmodified on the largest enterprise developer platform. The consequence for Anthropic is that a category of enterprise deployment now defaults to Codex until Claude Code closes the gap.
