Nasdaq confirms correction, $2.5 trillion of AI market value erased in one session.

The Nasdaq Composite fell 5.52 percent on Thursday, locking in a technical correction from its October 6 high and wiping roughly $2.5 trillion from the index in a single day. Nvidia shed 9.22 percent and about $450 billion in market capitalization; AMD dropped 15 percent; Qualcomm fell 9.49 percent after its fiscal-Q1 guide of $2.15 to $2.35 against a $2.32 Street; the Philadelphia Semiconductor Index lost about 12 percent, its worst session since March 2020. Neocloud exposure took the deepest damage: IREN fell 14.05 percent to $18.66, Nebius dropped more than 13 percent, and CoreWeave closed down double digits after Bloomberg reported Microsoft is tightening oversight of Nscale, Nebius and IREN projects and will not exercise an option to more than double its Texas Nscale lease. The VIX jumped 69.66 percent to 28.18. Vanda Research measured roughly $358 million of retail selling in U.S. single stocks by mid-afternoon, the heaviest capitulation since March 2020. Nomura cross-asset strategist Charlie McElligott described the move in four words: "They're shooting the generals." AMD CEO Lisa Su said the market "can sometimes be a little bit short-sighted" and reiterated her view that AI chip demand remains structurally strong. The sell-off ran with the political tape: two days earlier, voters passed data-center restrictions or bans in 40 of 41 local ballot measures Ballotpedia tracked, the Democratic sweep handed Congress to the party in power, and Texas governor-elect Gina Hinojosa said she would pause new data-center projects until the legislature writes industry rules.

Yahoo Finance · CNBC · Reuters · 2026-11-05 · Source ↗

Thursday turned the AI capex trade into a rout. The Dow Jones Industrial Average closed down 1,833 points, or 3.44 percent, the S&P 500 fell 4.36 percent to 7,353, and the Nasdaq Composite dropped 5.52 percent to confirm a technical correction more than ten percent below its October 6 record. The Nasdaq-100 shed 6.3 percent and erased roughly $2.3 to $2.5 trillion of market value in one session depending on who counted, with the CBOE Volatility Index jumping 69.66 percent to 28.18. Chip stocks led the move down. The Philadelphia Semiconductor Index fell about 12 percent, its worst session since the March 2020 COVID shock.

The damage, name by name

Nvidia fell 9.22 percent to $181.67, losing close to $450 billion of market capitalization. AMD dropped roughly 15 percent and is now down 22 percent over the past month. Qualcomm closed 9.49 percent lower on fiscal-Q1 guidance of $2.15 to $2.35 EPS on $10.4 to $11.2 billion of revenue, centered at $2.25 against a $2.32 Street. Micron slid about 12 percent, Arm Holdings 13 percent, Broadcom another 10 percent. Dell lost 9 percent. Palantir and CrowdStrike each traded down more than 10 percent intraday. SpaceX and Tesla each fell more than 9 percent after Yorkville's Dan Ives said a prospective SpaceX-Tesla combination would face "a tougher regulatory path in 2027" under Democratic control. Bitcoin fell 5.41 percent to about $76,113, a tell that the derisking spanned asset classes.

Neocloud took the deepest cut

The second shock arrived mid-morning when Bloomberg reported Microsoft is tightening oversight of some data-center projects run by Nebius, IREN and Nscale, is considering whether to sign new neocloud deals at all, and will not exercise an option to more than double its existing Nscale Texas agreement. Microsoft has also stepped away from being anchor customer on Nscale's planned Loughton, Essex site after grid-connection delays pushed first delivery into the 2030s. Nebius fell more than 13 percent, IREN dropped 14.05 percent to $18.66, and CoreWeave lost double digits as the selloff spread across the complex. IREN confirmed that Microsoft had requested network design changes on a Texas site, including cabling issues, that pushed delivery dates; IREN will not pay financial penalties, but part of its publicly guided "more than $4 billion" annualized run-rate target now slips from year-end 2026 into January 2027.

"They're shooting the generals." — Charlie McElligott, Nomura cross-asset strategist

What actually changed

Strategists on the Yahoo Finance tape attributed the move mainly to hedge funds locking in profits in AI leaders ahead of year-end rather than a change in fundamentals. Vanda Research strategist Viraj Patel described retail flow as "a total capitulation by retail investors," with roughly $358 million of selling in single U.S. stocks by mid-afternoon, the heaviest since March 2020. But the political backdrop added fuel the technicals could not explain. Tuesday's midterm delivered Democratic control of Congress alongside wins for governors-elect in Texas, Virginia and other data-center states. Voters passed data-center restrictions or bans in 40 of 41 local ballot measures Ballotpedia tracked, including 14 Ohio cities. Texas governor-elect Gina Hinojosa said she would pause new data-center projects until the state legislature writes industry rules. Nasdaq-100 breadth was ugly in a specific way: every leading AI name was down, but AppLovin, up about 28 percent on strong earnings, was the one notable gainer.

"The market can sometimes be a little bit short-sighted. We don't have a demand issue; it's a supply issue." — Lisa Su, AMD CEO, speaking into the selloff

The read from the inside

AMD CEO Lisa Su used Thursday morning airtime to insist AI chip demand remains structurally strong, repeating that supply, not demand, has been the gating constraint. Qualcomm CEO Cristiano Amon used the same framing on his Wednesday-night call, pointing to memory pricing and packaging-capacity shortages as the near-term lid on margins. Nvidia's forward guidance already excludes China data-center compute revenue; Reuters reported Thursday that roughly 600,000 H200 chips sit in Hong Kong awaiting Beijing's import clearance, a second-order drag that sell-side analysts began working into their Q4 China models during the session. Peter Boockvar of OnePoint BFG Wealth Partners captured the shape of the move for the capex thesis: if the physical buildout slows, "that's what stocks are going to trade off," independent of near-term fundamentals. Richard Clode at Janus Henderson added that the political overhang will not resolve quickly.

What the tape means

Thursday was the first session of the AI cycle where every major vector of worry priced at once. Public AI chip names lost roughly $2.5 trillion of market value while private AI-adjacent companies closed $4 billion of new rounds the same day: Shield AI at a $28.8 billion post-money, Zipline at $21.5 billion, Ocient at a $100 million Series C, Dropbox buying Tigris Data for $350 million. Alphabet's Google used Thursday morning to announce a $17 billion Wyoming data center with Bloom Energy fuel cells and air cooling, a template clearly written for the post-midterm political environment. The split-screen is the point. The private AI stack is still financing itself at full speed. The public AI-infrastructure stack, from Nvidia to the neoclouds, just took the largest single-session repricing since the trade had meaningful size. The next earnings print on the tape is Nvidia's November 19; the next political catalyst is state-legislature sessions opening in January. Between the two, risk premia on the physical AI buildout have been set substantially higher than they were on Wednesday.

Moonshot publishes Kimi K3.1 Pro open weights at 2.8T parameters.

i10x · Hugging Face · 2026-11-05 · Source ↗

Moonshot met its October 23 commitment and published Kimi K3.1 Pro to Hugging Face as a 2.8-trillion-parameter sparse mixture-of-experts with 104 billion active per token, 1,048,576-token context, MXFP4 weights under quantization-aware training, and a MoonViT-V2 vision encoder. The license is custom rather than Apache 2.0 and gates only the largest commercial operators. Moonshot stripped cyberattack training data before release, cutting an internal exploit score from 90.5 percent to 81.0.

The weights live at moonshotai/Kimi-K3.1-Pro, in a public ungated repository that Moonshot flagged with a Kimi.ai post on X. The model carries 2.8 trillion total parameters and 104 billion active per token across 93 layers, with 896 routed experts, 16 selected per token, and two shared experts per layer. Twenty-four of the layers replace full attention with Gated DeepSeek Sparse Attention. Weights were produced with quantization-aware training in MXFP4 and activations in MXFP8, so the full checkpoint is about 1.56 TB across 96 safetensors shards.

What Moonshot ran

The vendor benchmark stack comes in strong across agentic and knowledge tasks. Moonshot reports Terminal-Bench 4.0 at 65.7 using the Claude Code harness, 94.2 on GPQA-Diamond, 92.1 on BrowseComp, 79.6 on OSWorld V2.1, 70.0 on HLE-Full with tools, 65.7 on Terminal-Bench 4.0, and 77.1 on the internal Kimi Code Bench 2.0, benchmarked against Claude Opus 5.5, GPT-6 Astra and DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash. Third-party evidence is thinner but directionally supportive: Agent Arena, cited by MarsBit and Huo Xing Finance, ranks Kimi K3.1 Pro (Max) first among open-weight models and 13th overall across 17,500 long-duration agent sessions.

The cyber cut

Moonshot paired the open release with a deliberate capability reduction. The lab stripped cyberattack-related training data before publishing weights. The company's internal exploit benchmark fell from 90.5 percent on an unfiltered checkpoint to 81.0 percent on the released model, against 85.7 percent for DeepSeek V4.1-Flash and 33.3 percent for the older K3. The model card also lists 74.7 on ExploitBench and 20.7 on ExploitGym. The timing is pointed: on the same day Moonshot's weights hit Hugging Face, OpenAI launched GPT-6 Cyber restricted to Daybreak Red because of its 48.5 ExploitGym score. Moonshot chose the opposite trade: release the weights, cap the capability.

"One of the largest open-weight multimodal models ever released." — Featherless AI on X

The license

The license is not Apache 2.0 and not MIT. Model-as-a-service operators with more than $20 million in revenue across any consecutive 12 months need a separate agreement with Moonshot. Products with more than 100 million monthly active users, or more than $20 million in monthly revenue, must display "Kimi K3.1-Pro" prominently in the user interface. Internal use and use through Moonshot certified partners are exempt from both conditions. vLLM 0.34.0-plus and SGLang recipes landed with the weights. Hosted API is priced at $2 per million input and $10 per million output tokens.

Baidu opens ERNIE 5.5 Flash Base, a raw 280B MoE under custom license.

AlphaSignal · Hugging Face · 2026-11-05 · Source ↗

Baidu published a 280-billion-parameter sparse MoE pre-training checkpoint on Hugging Face with no mid-training, no SFT and no RLHF, in both FP8 and BF16. The custom license bans use in the EU, UK and South Korea and requires written authorization from Baidu for entities with revenues above $50 million over 12 months. Vendor benchmarks claim first place across 24 of 30 tests versus DeepSeek-V4-Flash-Base, Hy3-preview-Base and MiMo-V2.5-Base.

The repository baidu/ERNIE-5.5-Flash-Base landed on Hugging Face on November 5, in the morning US time, in both FP8 and BF16 weights. The model has 280 billion total parameters and 14 billion active per token, built from 43 layers (one dense feed-forward, 42 Multi-Router Latent MoE layers) with 512 routed experts, top-10 routing, and one shared expert per MoE layer.

A pre-training checkpoint, not a chat model

Baidu posted the raw end-of-pretraining weights, before any mid-training, supervised fine-tuning or RLHF. The model has no refusal policy, no chat template, and no tool-use format baked in. Attention is hybrid: 37 Heavily Compressed Attention layers (head dim 512, one cached entry per 128 tokens) and 6 Multi-head Latent Attention layers (head dim 256) with dynamic sparse attention. ECHO, Baidu's final-layer reuse mechanism, doubles as multi-token prediction and speculative decoding draft. Training ran on 33.5 trillion tokens at 128K context using Muon with communication-efficient distributed sharding and a FlashMask-4 kernel; fine-tuning and inference stretch to 1,048,576 tokens.

Vendor benchmarks

Baidu's own table puts ERNIE 5.5 Flash Base at MMLU-Pro 78.4 (DeepSeek V4 Flash 69.2), GPQA 60.2 (55.9), HumanEval+ 82.3 (69.5), LiveCodeBench 40.1 (31.9), RepoQA 91.7 (81.0) and LongBench-v2 48.7 (44.5). Across the 30 benchmarks disclosed, Baidu claims first place in 24. The model trails at least one competitor on ChineseSimpleQA, INCLUDE-lite, HELMET-Rerank and InfiniteBench.

"The checkpoint at the end of pre-training, before any mid- or post-training, meant as a starting point for domain adaptation, instruction tuning, and reinforcement-learning research." — Baidu model card

The license shift

Prior ERNIE 4.5 releases used Apache 2.0. ERNIE 5.5 Flash Base ships under a custom "ERNIE 5.5 Flash Base Model License Agreement v1.0" that prohibits use in the European Union, the United Kingdom and South Korea, and gates commercial redistribution behind a $50 million revenue threshold. FP8 weights are about 280 GB and BF16 about 560 GB before KV cache. Baidu publishes SGLang and vLLM 0.32.0 recipes through a dedicated plugin; the example caps context at 131,072 tokens rather than 1 million.

Tencent ships Hunyuan Hy4, 770B MoE with native vision and computer-use.

PYMNTS · Caixin · 2026-11-05 · Source ↗

Tencent's Hunyuan launched Hy4 on November 4 as the first Hy-series model with native vision and computer-use, scaling from under 300 billion parameters to 770 billion. API pricing lands near a dollar per million input tokens with a 50 percent discount through year-end. Third-party platforms OpenRouter, Hermes and OpenCode integrated it on launch day; open weights are promised in the coming days.

Hy4 is a 770-billion-parameter mixture-of-experts transformer with a 1,048,576-token context. Tencent integrated vision into the core model rather than bolting on a separate ViT, so the model natively accepts both text and image input. Computer-use covers graphical interfaces, command execution, browser operation, desktop automation and multi-step workflows such as booking appointments, extracting spreadsheet data and managing email. Public computer-use benchmarks have not yet been published.

Where it runs

Hy4 is live in Tencent Cloud TokenHub, in WorkBuddy and CodeBuddy, in Yuanbao, and in the WeChat Work assistant Dayuan. OpenRouter, Hermes and OpenCode integrated it on launch day. API pricing comes in at roughly 6 yuan per million input tokens (about $0.90) and about $2.70 output, with a 50 percent discount running through December 31. Open weights were promised "in the coming days"; one checker reported HTTP 401 on the Hugging Face link on November 5.

Finance workspace focus

Tencent trained Hy4 on expanded financial workspace data including fund research, securities analysis and quantitative strategies. In a blind evaluation by 213 financial professionals, Hy4 ranked first among open models, ahead of DeepSeek and Kimi. Tencent reports the hallucination rate fell from 12.95 percent to 7.2 percent and guessing errors fell from 23.1 percent to 5.6 percent. These are vendor numbers; external verification is pending.

"Visual understanding and the ability to operate computers." — Tencent on Hy4, via Caixin

Ricursive Intelligence launches Ripple-65B Preview, US open-weight coding lead.

emergent.sh · Ricursive Intelligence · 2026-11-05 · Source ↗

A Palo Alto lab founded by Anna Goldie and Azalia Mirhoseini released a 65-billion-parameter dense transformer that scored 80.52 on TerminalBench 2.1, ahead of Reflection Beam 501B at 80.1 and NVIDIA Nemotron 3 Ultra 550B at 56.4. Total R&D cost was roughly $32 million, one-tenth the prior US open-weight coding leader. The model is Apache 2.0, runs on NVIDIA DGX Spark, and advertises up to a 1 million-token context.

Ricursive Intelligence released Ripple-65B Preview on November 4, with emergent.sh publishing the first detailed English writeup at 3:15 AM on November 5. The lab describes Ripple as a general-purpose variant of the proprietary system behind its chip-design platform; co-founders Anna Goldie and Azalia Mirhoseini both come from a deep-learning plus chip-design background.

Thin and deep, not sparse

Ripple-65B is a dense transformer with 65 billion parameters distributed across 80 layers at a narrow 5,120 d_model. Because it is dense, every forward pass uses all 65 billion parameters, which Ricursive argues is easier to serve and quantize than 500-billion-plus MoE systems. The company reports pretraining on 12 trillion tokens and up to 1 million-token context, though one review flagged OpenRouter listing a 16K ceiling so long context may depend on the provider.

Compute efficiency as the pitch

Ricursive says its pretraining ladder reached up to 16 times better compute efficiency on code at the 4B scale, measured by dolma_stack cross-entropy, starting from an Olmo 3 baseline. Post-training used about 1.4 million RL rollouts, roughly 70 times fewer than Reflection Beam. Total R&D spend including pretraining experiments, data work and post-training is about $32 million; Ricursive frames that as one-tenth the compute of the prior US open-weight coding SOTA.

"Surpassing significantly larger models including Muse Spark Open-816B, Beam-501B, Inkling-975B." — Ricursive Intelligence on X

Benchmarks

On TerminalBench 2.1, Ripple-65B Preview scored 80.52. Comparison table: Reflection Beam (501B) 80.1, Meta Muse Spark Open (816B) 76.4, Thinking Machines Inkling (975B) 63.8, NVIDIA Nemotron 3 Ultra (550B) 56.4. The model hit 79.1 percent on SWE-Bench Pro v2.0-Hard and 44.3 percent on DeepSWE v1.1. Distribution is wide: Hugging Face, Together AI, GMI Cloud and OpenRouter on launch day, with Fireworks following the same day. Apache 2.0 license puts Ripple on softer ground than the custom licenses attached to Kimi K3.1 Pro and ERNIE 5.5 Flash Base.

OpenAI releases GPT-6 Cyber, first "Critical" cyber tier, 81 new Firefox CVEs.

Fortune · BankInfoSecurity · The Register · 2026-11-05 · Source ↗

OpenAI shipped GPT-6 Cyber under Daybreak Red access only, the first model at the Preparedness Framework's Critical tier. ExploitGym score 48.5 percent versus 32.1 for GPT-5.6 Cyber. Researchers used it to find 81 unknown high-severity Firefox CVEs that Mozilla is patching and to build working exploits for 948 of 1,724 reproduced Linux kernel bugs, including 588 previously unknown. Out-of-scope avoidance 99.93 percent without a safety prompt.

GPT-6 Cyber launched on Thursday, restricted to approved Daybreak Red customers via the API and Codex with IP allowlisting. The model is OpenAI's fourth cyber-focused release of 2026 after April, June and August launches, and the first to carry the Preparedness Framework's Critical rating, which means capable of identifying and developing functional zero-day exploits and running end-to-end attacks. GPT-5.6 Cyber carried a High rating.

Numbers

ExploitGym score 48.5 percent, up from 32.1 for GPT-5.6 Cyber. On a harder variant with stronger exploit mitigations, 36.94 percent versus 29.69 for GPT-6 Astra. ExploitGym Honeypot: 99.93 percent out-of-scope avoidance without a deployment safety prompt, 100 percent with one, versus 94.49 percent for GPT-5.6 Cyber. On a remote-attack benchmark, GPT-6 Astra refused every task; GPT-6 Cyber completed 95 percent.

Real-world findings

Researchers used GPT-6 Cyber to find 81 previously unknown high-severity Firefox vulnerabilities; Mozilla has begun releasing fixes. The model reproduced 1,724 Linux kernel vulnerabilities and built working exploits for 948 of them including 588 previously unknown bugs; OpenAI disclosed 304 critical findings to maintainers with proposed patches. GPT-5.6 Sol generated only 166 Linux exploits in the same setup. The model also surfaced critical weaknesses in Brotli. Dragos ran a mock power-plant exercise and reported two zero-days found in engineering software managing protective relay data.

"As models become more capable and uncover more security vulnerabilities, we are also seeing more attempts to use them to cause harm." — OpenAI

Containment posture

OpenAI declined to say what happened in the 0.07 percent of runs that went out of scope or whether any caused real-world incidents. Chris Hughes of Noma Security told Fortune the rate "is concerning at scale" and noted the Patch the Planet dashboard shows fewer than 25 percent of surfaced flaws fixed so far. OpenAI raised bug-bounty payouts: critical findings now earn up to $30,000 base and $51,577 with bonuses; exceptional findings up to $172,000. The release lines up with Anthropic's Glasswing and Google's Fairwind as the three major defender-oriented cyber programs now in market.

Google commits $17 billion to first Wyoming data center with fuel cells and CCS.

Google blog · WyoFile · 2026-11-05 · Source ↗

Google will spend up to $17 billion long-term on a Laramie County campus anchored by a 702-megawatt Bloom Energy fuel-cell plant with carbon capture at Tallgrass's Eastern Wyoming Sequestration Hub. The campus will be air-cooled rather than water-cooled; Google will pay 100 percent of consumed power and cover infrastructure costs driven by its growth. The announcement landed two days after voters passed data-center restrictions in 40 of 41 local ballot measures nationwide.

Google's VP of Global Infrastructure and Energy, Steve Walter, laid out an unusually detailed energy-and-community package for the Laramie County campus, Google's first in Wyoming and the state's largest private AI infrastructure commitment. Power will come from a 702-megawatt fuel-cell project supplied by American Electric Power, built on Bloom Energy fuel cells and paired with carbon capture and storage at Tallgrass's Eastern Wyoming Sequestration Hub. Google calls the combined build the largest planned fuel-cell-plus-CCS project of its kind. Fuel cells run on natural gas and are manufactured in the United States; the move pushes Google's contracted CCS capacity close to 1 GW total.

Air cooling, no cost-shift

The site will be air-cooled rather than water-cooled, a direct answer to the complaint that fueled resident opposition across Ohio and Texas. Google pledged to "pay for 100% of the power we use" and to cover infrastructure upgrades "directly driven by its growth." Google is also running a front-end engineering and design study on adding carbon capture to Tallgrass's combined-cycle turbines, signaling further emissions commitments to come.

"We will pay for 100% of the power we use." — Steve Walter, Google VP of Global Infrastructure and Energy

Political timing

The timing is pointed. Two days earlier on November 3, voters passed data-center bans or restrictions in at least 14 Ohio cities and in 40 of 41 local ballot measures Ballotpedia tracked nationwide; Build American AI's $2.54 million Ohio ad buy did not prevent the sweep. Google held a community meeting in downtown Cheyenne that drew more than 200 residents before the announcement, and Google, its partners and Bloom Energy committed to form a Community Council. Cheyenne Mayor Patrick Collins told WyoFile "this is not speculative. This is happening." Councilman Larry Wolfe said local officials "are not easily dazzled anymore."

What's missing

The post does not state a construction start date, a completion date, a target gigawatt envelope or a jobs number. Google expects the project to generate hundreds of millions of dollars in local property taxes for Laramie County and School District 1 over its operating life, and is seeding K-12 STEM, workforce development and skilled-trades grants. On the same day, Bloom Energy shares fell more than 14 percent as the broader AI-infrastructure selloff ran, a tape that captures the asymmetry: Google can announce a $17 billion commitment and the supplier most directly tied to that commitment can still trade down because the market is pricing in a slower overall buildout.

Hugging Face's Daily Papers board for November 5 opened with arXiv 2611.06728 at 80 upvotes, almost twice the second-place entry. The authors argue that GRPO-based training of streaming video models has a structural flaw: prior work used a single trajectory-level advantage shared across all turns in a rollout, so a good silence could be penalized because a later redundant reply dragged the trajectory return down.

Two mechanisms

TurnWise introduces silence-completed prefix scoring, which rewrites credit assignment at the turn level by projecting the remaining return and building a per-turn advantage without needing a value head. Both pieces slot into standard GRPO loops and require no extra rollouts.

Setup

The training recipe uses gpt-oss-20b as the reward judge. The 16-page paper is listed under cs.CV, cs.CL and cs.LG with 4 figures, and the arXiv listing marks it as accepted to NeurIPS 2026. "Response and waiting decisions get equal weight regardless of length." — TurnWise method description

The practical case is live assistants that watch video calls, driving dashboards or security feeds, where constant replying would be useless but silence followed by a timely intervention is the whole point. arXiv 2611.06868 · 2026-11-05 · Source ↗

An eight-author team including Eric Xing shows that a generator LLM proposing N candidate actions per turn with an off-the-shelf open-weight selector LLM picking one gives roughly 5 to 10 percent accuracy improvement on long-horizon terminal tasks. On Turn-Level Best-of-N Sampling for Scaling LLM Agents landed in the November 5 arXiv cycle as 2611.06868, with Jalaj Bhandari, Dhawal Gupta, Hongli Zhan, Zhenting Wang, Varad Pimpalkhute, Zhengzhong Liu, Eric Xing and Rupesh Srivastava. Xing co-leads MBZUAI and is a well-known RL researcher.

Setup

Rather than training a new model, the authors apply best-of-N sampling at the turn level inside agent rollouts. At each turn, the generator LLM produces N candidate actions. A selector LLM, kept off-the-shelf and open-weight, compares the candidates and picks one to execute. The agent moves to the next turn and repeats. The selector does not need to be a specially trained critic, which is the paper's central empirical finding.

Result

Roughly 5 to 10 percent accuracy improvement on long-horizon terminal tasks, with more consistent results across repeated attempts (variance down). Trajectory analysis shows the agent makes fewer attempts to solve each task, follows instructions more carefully, and does more debugging and testing.

"Off-the-shelf, open-weight LLMs can compare candidate actions directly." — central empirical claim

Integration cost

The method is easy to drop into an existing agent loop: wrap each action-generation step in an N-way sampler and a selector call. No new training pipeline, no critic model to maintain, no fine-tuned judge. The cost is an N-plus-one factor on per-turn inference.
