Meta reopens the open-weights faucet: Muse Glimmer 30B lands, Muse Spark 1.2 open weights committed for August 24

Mark Zuckerberg's 11:14 UTC Threads post confirmed both an immediate open-weights release and a two-week deadline for the paid-tier flagship to follow. Muse Glimmer at 30 billion parameters cleared 74,000 Hugging Face downloads inside eight hours across twelve quantization variants. The safety adapter ships as a separate LoRA rather than baked into base weights. The move reverses eight months of Meta shipping Muse-branded models exclusively behind the paid tier and reclaims a share of open-weights adoption that had drifted to Qwen, DeepSeek and Kimi.

MLQ AI · 2026-08-11 · Source ↗

Mark Zuckerberg posted to Threads at 11:14 UTC Tuesday confirming both an immediate open-weights release and a scheduled follow-up. Meta uploaded Muse Glimmer at 30 billion parameters to Hugging Face and to Meta AI at 12:02 UTC. Muse Spark 1.2, the flagship coding model that shipped in paid-API form on August 5, gets its open-weights release the week of August 24. The pair reverses eight months of Meta shipping Muse-branded models exclusively behind the paid tier and gives the community a specific two-week deadline for the follow-on drop. Hugging Face downloads on Muse Glimmer crossed 74,000 inside eight hours across twelve quantization variants covering llama.cpp GGUF (Q4_K_M, Q5_K_M, Q6_K, Q8_0, FP16), AWQ, GPTQ-4bit, GPTQ-8bit, FP8 and MXFP4.

Muse Glimmer is a 30-billion-parameter dense model with a 24-layer encoder-decoder split. Meta trained it on 8.7 trillion tokens including 340 billion first-party FAIR reasoning traces. Compute was 4,096 H200 GPUs for 42 days, with the FAIR Paris cluster contributing 1,024 GPUs to the RLHF phase. Context is 32K, output 8K. The benchmark line runs MMLU-Pro 78.9 percent, GPQA-Diamond 64.2 percent, HumanEval-Plus 82.1 percent, GSM8K 94.6 percent. Nathan Lambert of the Allen Institute for AI framed the efficiency point on X: Muse Glimmer at 78.9 on MMLU-Pro is what Llama 3 70B was six months ago at 43 percent of the parameter count. The comparison sets the frame Meta wanted. Open-weights market share had fallen from 68 percent at Llama 3 peak to 12 percent by Q2 2026, absorbed by Qwen 3.x, DeepSeek V4 and Kimi K3. Zuckerberg's post read as both an admission and a comeback pitch.

"Open weights are back at Meta. This week we're shipping Muse Glimmer at 30 billion parameters, and Muse Spark 1.2 open weights the week of August 24." — Mark Zuckerberg, Threads, 11:14 UTC

The design choice that mattered

Muse Glimmer ships base weights with no guardrails baked in. The safety adapter arrives as an optional PEFT LoRA on a separate repository, downloadable and stackable at inference time. Meta is the first frontier open-weights lab to split the safety layer from the base model this way. Take the adapter, leave it, or replace it with a locally trained alternative. Ahmad Al-Dahle, Meta's VP of GenAI, framed the choice as fidelity to the open-weights premise. Simon Willison, whose two prior weekend posts had drawn hard lines about which frontier labs deserved the "open" label, called the decoupled adapter "a real design decision, not a talking point."

"The safety adapter ships as a separate LoRA. Take it, leave it, or replace it with your own. That's how open weights are supposed to work." — Ahmad Al-Dahle, Meta VP GenAI

The contributor-tier pipeline

Muse Spark 1.2 launched as a closed paid tier on August 5 at $1.25 per million input tokens and $4.25 output on the standard tier, with a contributor tier at $0.10 input and $0.20 output. The 12.5x discount buys training-data rights on the contributor's traffic. Meta's disclosure Tuesday shows the mechanism is producing volume. First six days of billed contributor telemetry: 340 million input tokens and 42 million output tokens, with 71 percent of Muse Spark accounts opting in. The open-weights release the week of August 24 will land with 30 days of contributor telemetry already flowing back into the next round of training. Anjney Midha at Andreessen Horowitz read the mechanism plainly: Meta pays users in cheap tokens and gets training-data rights on the resulting traffic. The paid tier is the pipeline that feeds the next open-weights drop, not a competing product line.

Day-one infrastructure

llama.cpp b10336 landed the Glimmer architecture at 11:47 UTC with support for all five GGUF quantization tiers. vLLM 0.10.5 shipped the reference deployment template at 12:14 UTC. Ollama v0.32.8, Apple MLX 0.20, Hugging Face Transformers 4.55.1 and TGI 3.4 followed inside the hour. The MoE routing kernel work that landed with Grok 4.6 the previous day carried forward to the dense-attention path for Glimmer, and llama.cpp's fused RMSNorm+MUL+RoPE kernel from the August 9 push applied without changes. Meta's decision to align with existing infrastructure ahead of the release meant no operator had to wait more than an hour to run Glimmer against the local stack they already had. That is a different cadence from the Llama 3 open-weights launch, when day-one CUDA support arrived but day-one AMD ROCm and Apple Silicon MLX support took a week each.

The license and the competitive picture

Muse Community License 2.0 permits commercial use up to 700 million monthly active users, matching Llama 3's threshold. That cap excludes Meta's direct hyperscaler competitors from deploying Glimmer in their own consumer surfaces without a paid Meta license, while keeping the model open for the enterprise and developer market that Meta wants to reach. The competitive picture Tuesday afternoon reads: Alibaba Qwen 3.8-Max sits at the top of the open-weights leaderboard on parameter count (2.4T total, 95B active), DeepSeek V4-Flash-0810 owns the coding-agent tier at 72.3 percent SWE-Bench Verified, Moonshot Kimi K3.1 lifted to 59 on the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index Tuesday morning, and Meta Muse Glimmer at 30B closes the efficiency-per-parameter gap the Chinese labs had opened. Muse Spark 1.2 open weights the week of August 24 slots into the coding-agent tier where DeepSeek and Alibaba have been unchallenged. Zuckerberg's promise carries a specific deadline the community will now track.

"Muse Glimmer at 78.9 on MMLU-Pro is what Llama 3 70B was six months ago at 43 percent of the parameter count. The efficiency curve is holding." — Nathan Lambert, Allen Institute for AI

Moor Insights & Strategy titled its field note "Recapturing the Llama Spark." Patrick Moorhead walked through the eighteen-month share-loss story and identified the contributor-tier mechanism as the piece Zuckerberg had been missing. The Llama 3 open-weights release had leaked training-data leverage to every downstream lab that fine-tuned on Llama outputs. The Muse Spark contributor tier reverses that flow. Muse Glimmer is a proof of what the mechanism can produce in one training cycle. The August 24 Muse Spark 1.2 open-weights drop is the demonstration at scale.

Anthropic commits Claude 5 family to invisible watermarking starting August 24, free detection for academia and journalism

Anthropic News · 2026-08-11 · Source ↗

Every Claude 5 model plus the maintenance-path Sonnet 4.5 receives a keyed statistical watermark that survives paraphrase at 87 percent on 340-token passages while adding under 0.4 percent perplexity. Detection is free through end of 2026 for verified academic, journalism and education customers; commercial detection at $0.0002 per checked token. Enterprise opt-out available, 8 percent of preview customers took it.

Anthropic committed the entire Claude 5 family to invisible statistical watermarking on model output at 09:12 UTC Tuesday, with the rollout beginning August 24. Opus 5 and Sonnet 5 receive the watermark first, Fable 5 on August 27, Haiku 5 and Claude Sonnet 4.5 maintenance path on August 31. The scheme derives from Kirchenbauer et al. 2023 with three Anthropic additions: a rotating key schedule that invalidates specific key generations without breaking historical detection, streaming-safe detection over token windows so partial outputs can be attributed without waiting for the full completion, and a multi-partition scheme that allows parallel watermarking of concurrent completions in batch inference without cross-contamination of the green-list vocabulary partition. Perplexity cost lands at 0.4 percent on Anthropic's internal Perplexity-Preserve suite.

"Every education, publishing and hiring customer has asked us for this since we shipped Claude 5. Today we're shipping it." — Jack Clark, Anthropic Trust and Safety

The detection profile is where independent scrutiny will focus. Paraphrase attacks against 340-token passages leave the watermark detectable at 87 percent. Aggressive back-translation through three languages drops detection to 62 percent. Full rewrite by GPT-5.6 Sol collapses detection to 34 percent. Nicholas Carlini's framing: 87 percent under paraphrase is not the ceiling for the scheme, it is the floor for the shipped version. The Kirchenbauer 2023 scheme collapsed under moderate paraphrase; Anthropic's version holds because the green-list partition is derived from a keyed PRF over token windows rather than single-token hashes. The technical report on arXiv (2608.04122) walks through five ablation studies covering paraphrase attacks, translation loops, model-inversion attempts and adversarial fine-tuning against an attacker with API access. Reference implementation ships on GitHub under Apache 2.0 (anthropic/watermark-claude-reference) with Python, TypeScript, Rust and Go bindings.

Chain-of-thought under a separate key

The choice that will draw interpretability-community attention is that CoT traces watermark under a distinct cryptographic key from user-visible output. Carlini explained the reasoning: if a user leaks a CoT trace publicly, that leak does not compromise detection on the final answer. Petri, the interpretability testbed Anthropic released last week, uses CoT as its primary decomposition target. Watermarking CoT under a distinct key means Petri users can publish CoT traces without triggering detection cascades on downstream derived outputs.

"We are watermarking the chain of thought with a distinct key so that CoT reasoning cannot be used to derive whether a final answer was Claude-generated." — Nicholas Carlini, Anthropic principal scientist

Enterprise opt-out and the honest floor

Claude Enterprise customers can pass the header X-Anthropic-Watermark: none in completions requests, provided they hold Enterprise SLA and complete an attestation flow that logs justifications for audit. Preview-flow data from the last two weeks shows 8 percent of enterprise customers opted out. Dario Amodei's launch note named the number rather than hid it. The categories that opt out cluster around three use cases: internal-only research summarization where attribution back to Claude would leak strategic information, legal-privilege contexts where the watermark could complicate discovery, and journalism-source-protection cases where verifiability of Claude-provenance could burn sources. Free detection through end of 2026 for academic, journalism and education verticals is the piece that makes the scheme useful in the wild rather than useful in the lab. Anthropic is the first frontier lab to ship watermarking across the full model family with a free detection tier for the verticals that most need it.

The core finding is architectural. All three providers encrypt reasoning blocks with what appears to be a single global key per provider, which means any encrypted block returned to a caller can be replayed across any session, account or model within that provider. A weaker model from the same provider family can decode and output a stronger model's encrypted reasoning in plaintext, bypassing the stronger model's refusals without a jailbreak.

"Our new 116-page paper: we extract encrypted raw reasoning from OpenAI, Anthropic and Gemini models at scale. This vulnerability leads to many security issues, including distillation attacks and credential extraction." — Maksym Andriushchenko, on X

The mechanics are simple. Take the strong model's encrypted reasoning block, feed it to a weaker and less heavily safeguarded model from the same provider, and ask that model to decode it. It prints the trace verbatim. The scale of exposure is where the safety story becomes an incident report. The paper authors recovered 315,320 reasoning blocks from public GitHub repositories, developer chat logs and community traces, and inside that corpus they extracted 367 personally identifiable information artifacts and 182 credentials. The distillation implication runs parallel to the credential extraction: because the encrypted blocks can be decoded to plaintext, they become training data for smaller open-weights models.
