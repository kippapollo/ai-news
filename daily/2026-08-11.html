CoreWeave prints $2.71B Q2, discloses $47.6B backlog with Anthropic first billion-dollar customer of record

The Livingston, New Jersey neocloud beat consensus of $2.56 billion by six percent on the Tuesday afternoon call, raised full-year guidance to $12.8–13.0 billion from $11.6–11.9 billion, and named Anthropic as the first customer to individually cross a billion dollars in a single fiscal year of remaining performance obligations. Michael Intrator disclosed 71 percent gross margin on the AI-inference tier, up from 62 percent in Q1, and named Blackwell Ultra deployment ahead of schedule as the mechanism.

CoreWeave IR · 2026-08-11 · Source ↗

CoreWeave reported second-quarter revenue of $2.71 billion on the Tuesday afternoon earnings call, six percent above the Street consensus of $2.56 billion and up 197 percent year over year against the $913 million posted in Q2 2025. Net income came in at $312 million versus a $79 million loss in the year-ago quarter, and adjusted EBITDA reached $1.68 billion for a 62 percent margin. Michael Intrator opened the call by stating that the quarter was the first in the company's history where inference workloads exceeded training workloads on GPU-hours delivered, at a 54-46 split, with the shift attributed to Anthropic, Meta Superintelligence Labs and Cohere ramping production Sonnet 5, Muse Code and Command R+ 2 deployments respectively.

Remaining performance obligations, the backlog metric that CoreWeave has taught the investor base to watch as a leading indicator, closed the quarter at $47.6 billion, up $14.2 billion sequentially and $28.4 billion year over year. Intrator disclosed for the first time that Anthropic individually accounts for more than $1 billion of RPO within a single fiscal year, making it the first customer of record to cross that threshold. Microsoft, which had held the top single-customer position through 2025, drops to the second largest at approximately $940 million per year, followed by Meta at approximately $780 million.

"Anthropic is the first customer to individually cross a billion dollars of remaining performance obligations in a single fiscal year with CoreWeave. That single fact captures how much the inference economy has grown in the last four quarters." — Michael Intrator, CoreWeave CEO, on the Q2 earnings call

The margin story

Gross margin expanded to 68 percent overall, up from 61 percent in Q1, and the AI-inference tier specifically posted 71 percent gross versus 62 percent in Q1. Nitin Agrawal attributed the nine-point sequential expansion on inference to three mechanisms: Blackwell Ultra deployment running six weeks ahead of the original schedule, which pushed higher-margin capacity online sooner than modeled; the average per-GPU-hour price on inference rising 8 percent as Anthropic Sonnet 5 and Meta Muse Spark 1.2 workloads shifted from spot to committed capacity; and the CW3 datacenter in Plano, Texas coming online in June at a lower power-per-flop cost than the CW2 campus in Denton.

Training-tier gross margin held at 58 percent, flat sequentially. Agrawal described the training tier as capacity-constrained rather than margin-constrained, and said the training-tier waitlist for Q3 capacity is now approximately 40 percent higher than realized Q3 capacity, up from 22 percent as of the Q1 call. Meta and Alphabet were named as the two customers on the training waitlist with the largest unfulfilled 2026 commitments.

The fleet

The CoreWeave GPU fleet closed Q2 at approximately 342,000 accelerators across 41 datacenters in North America and Europe, up from 288,000 at the end of Q1. Blackwell Ultra now accounts for 61 percent of installed capacity, Hopper H200 for 24 percent, older H100 for 12 percent, and the first Blackwell GB300 samples that Nvidia began shipping last week for 3 percent. Intrator confirmed CoreWeave received the largest single sample allocation from Nvidia — approximately 3,600 GB300 packages across the CW3 Plano site — and said the first customer workload on GB300 will run Anthropic Opus 5 training in September as a joint benchmarking exercise with Nvidia.

The CW4 campus in Abilene, Texas, first announced in November 2025, remains on schedule for Q1 2027 first-power at 800 MW. CoreWeave also disclosed for the first time a preliminary agreement with Tulsa Municipal Power and Light for a CW5 campus at 1.2 GW targeting Q3 2027 first-power, subject to interconnection queue approval that Intrator described as "in the productive phase" of ERCOT negotiations. The Tulsa disclosure lifted the two-year build-out backlog to roughly 3.4 GW of new-build capacity, most of which is already contracted.

The guidance

Full-year 2026 revenue guidance moved to $12.8 to $13.0 billion, up from the $11.6 to $11.9 billion band CoreWeave held at the Q1 call. The revision is driven by a Q3 guide of $3.15 to $3.25 billion (consensus was $2.94 billion) and a Q4 guide of $3.85 to $3.95 billion. Full-year capex guidance moved to $14.6 to $14.9 billion from $13.4 to $13.8 billion, with the raise directed to GB300 procurement, CW3 second-phase build-out, and initial CW5 site preparation. Free cash flow guidance moved from negative $1.4 to $1.8 billion to negative $1.1 to $1.4 billion for the year, with a return to positive free cash flow now expected in Q2 2027 rather than Q4 2027.

"We are still burning cash. We are burning less cash than we thought we would burn a quarter ago, and we are doing it while pulling forward the CW3 revenue ramp. That is the shape of a business that is beginning to compound." — Nitin Agrawal, CoreWeave CFO, on the earnings call

The stock and the read

CoreWeave shares closed the regular session at $214.20 up 3.4 percent on the day, then moved to $233 in extended trading after the print, a 9 percent after-hours gain that would put the stock roughly 24 percent above the $188 pre-print level five sessions ago. The move implies a market cap of approximately $122 billion at the after-hours peak. Analysts on the call from Bank of America, Barclays, Morgan Stanley and TD Cowen all asked variations of a single question: how CoreWeave sustains 197 percent growth into 2027 when the training-inference mix is now inference-heavy and the price-per-token deflation Anthropic and OpenAI have driven since June should compress inference-side margin. Intrator's answer was that inference volume is expanding faster than price is deflating, and that the CoreWeave price schedule is set against capacity utilization rather than token throughput, which insulates the top line from token-level price compression.

The Tuesday print lands hours before Nebius reports Wednesday and 48 hours before Applied Digital reports Thursday, making CoreWeave the first read on Q3 neocloud demand shape. Nebius consensus sits at $612 million, up from $487 million in Q1; Applied Digital at $148 million. Neither is expected to disclose a billion-dollar single-customer RPO milestone.

It documents Anthropic's Responsible Scaling Policy version 4.2 evaluation triggers, control-envelope criteria at ASL-3 and ASL-4, and how the classifier retuning that reduced biology fallbacks 67 percent last week does or does not shift the trigger thresholds.

Anthropic filed its ASL-4 threshold-approach report to the European Union AI Office at 09:14 CET Tuesday, 13 days ahead of the August 24 deadline that Sarah Guo committed to in the Sunday evening response to the Article 101 information request the Office sent on Monday morning. The 88-page filing is now public on anthropic.com and forms the most detailed public description Anthropic has given of its Responsible Scaling Policy triggers since the RSP was first published in September 2023. Version 4.2, dated August 8 and referenced in the filing, updates the ASL-3 to ASL-4 threshold criteria that were most recently revised in RSP 4.1 in June.

The core disclosure is a matrix of six capability domains (cyber, biology, model-autonomy, persuasion, chemistry, radiological-nuclear) mapped against nine evaluation methods (four internal, five third-party). Each cell of the 6-by-9 matrix contains a specific numerical or qualitative threshold, the current highest observed value across the Claude family, and the maximum margin before the ASL-4 trigger fires. For the biology domain, the classifier retuning Anthropic disclosed last week that reduced Fable 5 biology fallbacks by 67 percent moves the classifier's operating point rather than the underlying capability, and the filing notes explicitly that the trigger threshold is set against uplift measurement rather than against classifier throughput. The distinction matters because it means the classifier retuning does not raise the ASL-4 threshold; the capability measurement itself is what would trigger the escalation.

"Our ASL-4 thresholds are defined against measured capability uplift, not against classifier fallback rate. The classifier retuning last week changed the false-positive rate on Claude.ai. It did not change what the model can do, and it did not change the ASL-4 threshold." — Anthropic ASL-4 filing, page 34, paragraph 12

The comparison against OpenAI Astra

Section 6 of the filing explicitly compares Anthropic's ASL-4 threshold criteria against OpenAI's Preparedness Framework Critical-tier criteria that OpenAI applied to Astra on Friday. Anthropic argues that its ASL-4 threshold on cyber is roughly 20 to 30 percent tighter than the Preparedness Critical threshold based on the reference tasks each framework uses (Anthropic references CTF-Bench 3.2 and NCCDC-derived synthetic exercises; OpenAI references Cybench 2.0 and DarkSquare private evaluations). On biology, the two frameworks reach comparable thresholds via different measurement paths.

The filing does not name any Claude model as having crossed the ASL-4 threshold. Opus 5, Sonnet 5 and Fable 5 remain classified at ASL-3, the same level as Opus 4.5 and Sonnet 4.5. The filing does state that the highest observed capability score inside the ASL-4 evaluation battery came from an internal Anthropic model referred to as "Model X" that has not been released publicly and is currently blocked from customer access under ASL-3 control criteria pending an ASL-4 evaluation gate.

The EU AI Office response

Ekaterina Zaharieva, head of the AI Office's GPAI Enforcement Unit, acknowledged receipt at 11:20 CET. The Office's public response praised the early filing and noted that Google DeepMind and xAI now have until August 24 and September 8 respectively to file comparable documents. The response also flagged that the Article 101 process next enters an evaluation stage where the Office may request additional documentation, in-person briefings, or third-party evaluation access under Article 102.

"Anthropic's filing arrives 13 days ahead of schedule and sets a benchmark for what the Article 101 process looks like when the recipient chooses to engage substantively. We look forward to comparably substantive filings from Google DeepMind and xAI." — Ekaterina Zaharieva, EU AI Office GPAI Enforcement Unit head

OpenAI submitted its Astra Preparedness Framework Critical-tier documentation to the European Union AI Office at 14:00 CET Tuesday, following a two-page written request for the filing that the Office sent to OpenAI on Monday morning as part of the Article 101 information-gathering conversation that Friday's pause announcement triggered. The 340-page technical filing is confidential in full, but OpenAI posted a 24-page executive summary at openai.com/eu/astra-preparedness at 14:04 CET that details the Critical-tier classification rationale, the control envelope OpenAI has put around Astra pending further evaluation, and the coordination workflow with US and UK government agencies that Friday's pause announcement committed to.

The classification rationale reduces to a single core claim in the executive summary: Astra's internal cybersecurity evaluation scored above the Preparedness Framework Critical threshold on two of three cyber-domain reference tasks, and above the High threshold on the third. The two above-Critical tasks are a synthetic multi-stage penetration exercise developed with Trail of Bits, and a code-audit-and-exploit task derived from Cybench 2.0. The below-Critical task is a defense-side incident-response exercise. The filing states that the two above-Critical scores were reproduced across three separate evaluation runs, ruling out a single-run anomaly.

"We paused Astra when the cybersecurity evaluation produced two consecutive Critical-tier scores on independent tasks. We would rather pause once than have to pause every future evaluation once the model has crossed a threshold we cannot walk back." — OpenAI Astra Preparedness executive summary, page 4

The control envelope

The Critical-tier control envelope, as described in the executive summary, holds Astra inside isolated networks with no outbound internet access, restricts tool access to a whitelist reviewed by the OpenAI Safety Advisory Group, encrypts weights at rest with hardware-backed keys, monitors every agentic session with a second-order classifier, and reviews chain-of-thought traces during training as well as evaluation. The most consequential control change from OpenAI's previous internal practice is the training-phase chain-of-thought review, which had previously applied only at evaluation. The change effectively pauses all Astra training runs until each run's monitoring configuration is reviewed against the Critical envelope.

The comparison with Anthropic

The Tuesday-morning Anthropic filing on ASL-4 becomes the natural comparison document. Section 8 of the OpenAI executive summary states that OpenAI's Preparedness Critical tier is "structurally similar" to Anthropic's ASL-4, but does not attempt a quantitative alignment. The Anthropic filing had the reverse posture, comparing its own thresholds against OpenAI's Preparedness Framework in some detail. The result is that the two labs have now both filed public safety-framework documents to the EU inside 24 hours of each other, without either lab having endorsed the other's threshold-alignment claim.
