Google TPUs leave Google Cloud for the first time: Oracle to run eighth-gen TPU 8t and 8i across a multi-year, multi-gigawatt deployment from 2027.

Google and Oracle used the Tuesday keynote at Oracle AI World in Las Vegas to break a decade of single-cloud TPU distribution. OCI will begin deploying Google's eighth-generation TPUs in calendar 2027, ramping across a multi-year window; the two chips in the family are the TPU 8t for training and the TPU 8i for inference. Each eighth-gen chip delivers up to 12.6 PFLOPS of native FP4 compute, over 100x TPU v1's 92 TFLOPS, with up to 2x the previous generation's performance per watt and up to 80 percent better performance per dollar for low-latency serving. Optical Circuit Switching plus on-die Inter-Chip Interconnect reroute around hardware or link faults without job restarts, sustaining above 97 percent goodput on large training runs across thousands of chips. TPU racks plug into Oracle Acceleron networking with SmartNIC tenant isolation, High Performance Fabric, and Zero Trust Packet Routing, placing fine-tuning and inference next to Oracle databases without cross-cloud transfers. Oracle SVP Karan Batta told reporters the deal is a "multi-year, multi-gigawatt partnership with Google," and the companies disclosed no initial deployment size. Oracle stock rose about 5 percent on the news.

Google Cloud blog · Oracle AI World keynote · SiliconANGLE · 2026-10-27 · Source ↗

Oracle and Google used the Tuesday keynote at Oracle AI World in Las Vegas to announce that Oracle Cloud Infrastructure will begin deploying Google's eighth-generation TPUs in calendar 2027. The companies described it as the first time Google TPUs will be offered on a hyperscale cloud platform outside Google, closing a near-decade stretch in which TPU capacity was reachable only through Google Cloud. The companies did not disclose an initial deployment size and framed the ramp as multi-year.

What ships in 2027

Google's eighth-generation TPU family comprises two chips: TPU 8t for training workloads and TPU 8i for inference. Each chip delivers up to 12.6 PFLOPS of native FP4 compute, which Google says is more than 100 times the throughput of TPU v1 at 92 TFLOPS. The eighth generation delivers up to 2 times the performance per watt of the prior generation, more on-chip SRAM and HBM capacity, and a new low-diameter interconnect topology that cuts network hops by more than 50 percent. Google cites up to 80 percent better performance per dollar for low-latency serving against the previous generation.

"A multi-year, multi-gigawatt partnership with Google." — Karan Batta, SVP of Oracle Cloud Infrastructure, Oracle AI World, 2026-10-27

The reliability story that lets the thing run at scale

Google's system design pairs Optical Circuit Switching with on-die Inter-Chip Interconnect routing to reroute around hardware or link faults without job restarts, which it says sustains more than 97 percent goodput across thousands of chips on long training runs. Software covers the usual list: TorchTPU as Google's native PyTorch stack for TPUs, open source on GitHub, with support for vLLM, SGLang, llm-d, TorchTitan, Ray, TorchRec, Hugging Face, and LiteRT. Google released TorchTPU as Apache 2.0 at PyTorch Conference North America the week before.

How the TPU racks sit inside OCI

TPU racks are designed for direct liquid cooling, which fits Oracle's newer AI-dense data-center designs. The TPU capacity plugs into Oracle Acceleron networking with SmartNIC tenant isolation, High Performance Fabric, and Zero Trust Packet Routing, allowing fine-tuning and inference to run next to Oracle databases without cross-cloud transfers. The multi-architecture pitch to OCI customers is that TPUs sit alongside Nvidia GPUs and other accelerators so teams can match each workload stage to the best silicon.

"Oracle customers who are using TPUs will be able to query data stored in Oracle databases directly from them." — Google and Oracle joint announcement, 2026-10-27

The rest of the Oracle AI silicon stack

On the same day Oracle also extended its Nvidia collaboration toward multi-gigawatt scale with Nvidia Vera Rubin systems, added Cerebras wafer-scale accelerators, and said it would host OpenAI's Jalapeño inference processor. The combined read is that Oracle is becoming the first hyperscaler to run four different frontier-training or frontier-inference silicon stacks in production: Google TPUs, Nvidia Rubin, Cerebras wafer scale, and OpenAI's in-house chip. Oracle shares closed roughly 5 percent higher. The Information first reported the TPU deal under a shorter headline that morning before the keynote confirmation.

Why this is the strategic story

Google TPU capacity has been the lever Anthropic, Midjourney, Character and others pulled when they needed a hedge against Nvidia; the lever only reached customers already on Google Cloud. Running TPUs inside OCI puts the chip in reach of Oracle's enterprise and database customer base, including workloads that could not or would not relocate to Google Cloud. The near-term question is how much of Google's TPU fleet is actually free to allocate to OCI given Anthropic's $430 billion-plus locked-in cloud commitments disclosed in Monday's S-1 and Google's own internal demand. Google answered none of that on Tuesday.

Baidu open-sources Unlimited OCR 2 under MIT with Sparse Reference Sliding Window Attention and a 7.0× speedup on 100-page documents.

Baidu AI on X · MarkTechPost · Hugging Face · 2026-10-27 · Source ↗

The 3.34-billion-parameter long-document OCR model keeps a fixed working set of 512 reference tokens, pairs a 128-token local window with top-16 selection over 32-token blocks, and keeps per-step decoding cost roughly flat as documents grow. Self-reported Baidu numbers: 7.0x per-step decoding over Full Attention and 5.6x over R-SWA on 100-page documents, 84.59 MPDocBench, 77.06 MarathonDocBench. SGLang and vLLM serving recipes ship with the weights.

Baidu released Unlimited OCR 2 as an open-source model on Tuesday under the MIT license, published on GitHub and Hugging Face together with SGLang and vLLM serving recipes and a Transformers interface. The model is 3.34 billion parameters, runs on a single CUDA GPU in BF16, and was tested on an NVIDIA A800-80GB. The headline technical change from June 2026's Unlimited OCR is Sparse Reference Sliding Window Attention, which Baidu abbreviates as SR-SWA.

What SR-SWA actually does

SR-SWA condenses text-visual attention interactions into a fixed-size set of working references. The example in Baidu's model card uses a 512-token working reference set. The model configuration pairs a 128-token local window with top-16 selection over 32-token blocks and releases KV caches that are no longer needed. The design goal, per Baidu's X post, is keeping per-step decoding cost roughly flat as the document grows. On 100-page documents Baidu reports its model is 7.0 times faster per step than Full Attention and 5.6 times faster than R-SWA, which was the attention mechanism in its June 2026 version.

"Longer documents can mean a lot more attention computation. Our new SR-SWA mechanism keeps that in check." — Baidu Inc., model announcement on X, 2026-10-27

Benchmark numbers and the self-report asterisk

Baidu's self-reported scores are 84.59 on MPDocBench against 78.30 for Qwen3.8-Max, and 77.06 on MarathonDocBench. MarathonDocBench is a benchmark Baidu released alongside the model and is designed around long-document parsing, so the first-place result is both self-reported and partly a home-field advantage. MarkTechPost describes the first-place MPDocBench and MarathonDocBench results as self-reported. Independent testing has not yet landed; the serving recipes make it reproducible once third parties pick it up.

Why document OCR has become an active research surface again

The June 2026 Unlimited OCR used R-SWA, a less selective sliding-window variant. The move to SR-SWA with explicit reference selection is a step in the same direction as recent work on long-context attention sparsity, and the config choices mirror choices in work on multimodal document retrieval and vision-language models that handle hundreds of pages at once. Unlimited OCR 2 keeps the KV cache flat rather than growing with document length, which is the specific failure mode most multimodal OCR models hit beyond fifty pages.

OpenAI releases a year of consumer ChatGPT work-activity data mapped to 165 O*NET activities across nearly 100 countries.

Sam on AI brief · OpenAI research disclosure · 2026-10-27 · Source ↗

OpenAI released quarterly aggregated consumer ChatGPT usage mapped to 165 O*NET work activities across nearly 100 countries covering Q1 2025 through Q3 2026. Enterprise messages and Codex traffic are excluded. The dataset is pitched at labor economists and ships without row-level artifacts; the activity-level aggregates are the unit.

OpenAI released a quarterly dataset mapping consumer ChatGPT usage to 165 O*NET work activities across nearly 100 countries, covering Q1 2025 through Q3 2026. The release is pitched to labor economists and policy researchers as the first longitudinal picture of what people ask a general-purpose chat assistant to do that overlaps with wage-earning work activities. The aggregates are activity-level rather than row-level; individual messages or sessions are not disclosed.

What's in and what's out

Enterprise messages and Codex traffic are excluded, so the dataset is specifically the consumer tier. OpenAI frames the activity assignments as per-message, with multiple-activity attribution allowed where a single message spans activities. The 165-activity list follows the US Department of Labor's O*NET Work Activities taxonomy, which is used by the Bureau of Labor Statistics and treated as the de facto schema in automation-exposure research.

"Enterprise messages and Codex are excluded." — Sam on AI brief, 2026-10-27, on the dataset's scope

Methodological questions already in flight

Three questions have already surfaced in the research community in the first 24 hours. First, how the activity assignment is done at scale (classifier type, inter-rater methodology, confidence thresholds). Second, how the geographic breakdown is handled for countries with very small sample sizes, where privacy-preserving noise can distort the signal. Third, whether the activity frequency reflects labor-market exposure or just query distribution across a sample skewed toward professionals who talk to ChatGPT for work.

Why OpenAI shipped this now

The release follows a run of academic and consultancy papers that estimated ChatGPT's labor-market overlap from leaked, scraped or proprietary samples. By publishing an aggregated version, OpenAI gets to anchor the number in its own data, with its own definitions, before the next round of policy-side exposure estimates land. It is also a quiet admission that the "we don't comment on usage" position has become untenable as regulators ask directly.

Infisical announced a $40 million Series B at a $600 million valuation on Tuesday, led by Elad Gil, who also led the company's $16 million Series A in June 2025. Y Combinator and Gradient Ventures participated in the round. Infisical's own blog adds Gallery Ventures, Pace Capital, and Dynamic Capital as participants. The product thesis is that AI agents are becoming a new class of users inside every enterprise, and that giving them live credentials is unworkable both from a breach-risk standpoint and from a least-privilege standpoint.

What Agent Vault does

Agent Vault lets agents complete tasks without receiving the raw credential. The agent calls into Infisical, which executes the authenticated action on the agent's behalf under a short-lived scoped token. If the agent is breached or if its context window leaks, the token has already expired and the live credential never left the vault. Dealroom reports that the alternative, which is passing credentials into the agent's context, means those secrets can leak in a breach or be passed to LLM providers through the usual training-and-logging pipelines.

"AI agents are a new class of users inside every enterprise." — Elad Gil, framing the Infisical thesis, Series B announcement, 2026-10-27

The customer and traction lines

Forbes names Airbus and Volkswagen as customers that are using agents in production without sharing private credentials. Infisical says it has more than 1,300 customers and that its open-source project has passed 30,000 GitHub stars. The company says it was already cash-flow positive before the raise and describes the Series B as optional rather than necessary.

Where the money goes

Infisical says the proceeds fund product development, engineering and go-to-market hiring, and the build-out of security infrastructure specifically for agents. The implied platform is a new per-agent identity primitive that sits beside human IAM rather than replacing it.

The public reproduction script uses 2,323 tokens as a supplied prefix. The papers worth tracking are RynnVLA-Latent (3D-grounded latent actions for scaling robotic manipulation foundation models), RoboHarness (a simpler harness outperforms VLA and world-action models), RoboJev (an open vision-language judge for robot success detection), EmbodiedLab (a benchmark for embodied agents that act to know), and IQuest-Q1 (agentic CLI foundation capabilities from a different angle).

Why 3D-grounded latent actions matter

Most VLA models work in a 2D image + text action-token space. The hard part is that physical actions are 3D and have continuous geometry, which 2D token representations struggle to compress efficiently. RynnVLA-Latent's bet is that a 3D-grounded latent space for actions is the right unit for scaling, with the latent action codebook learned alongside the vision-language backbone. The paper's claim is that this scales better than per-task fine-tuning or discrete-action-token approaches, though the independent evaluation is still pending.

"A simpler harness outperforms VLA and world action models." — RoboHarness paper headline claim, Hugging Face Daily Papers, 2026-10-27

RoboHarness's counter-narrative

RoboHarness argues that a sufficiently well-designed harness beats more expressive VLA and world-action models on the actual task benchmarks. The claim is deliberately provocative in a field that has spent eighteen months arguing that the model needs more capacity. If the result replicates, it points to the same conclusion the Shandong-led harness study pointed to for coding agents last week: harness design is doing more of the work than the field assumes, and the comparison across models is contaminated by harness choice.

Why this clusters with Machenta's $40M

Machenta's Tuesday raise builds on the research substrate the Hugging Face papers represent. The $40 million seed check is roughly a 24-month runway for a 100-engineer robotics-foundation-model team, which is the capability scale needed to compete with Physical Intelligence and FieldAI. The research cluster says the surface is live; the capital says the field is pricing physical AI like coding agents were priced a year ago.
