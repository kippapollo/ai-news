OpenAI ships GPT-6 Astra, prices it at Claude Fable, and calls it the start of the AGI era

Rollout began Thursday to enterprise Daybreak customers at $10 per million input tokens and $50 per million output, 2.5x GPT-5.6 Sol and exactly on Claude Fable 5.1. Aidan Clark called it OpenAI's largest training run "by far," on more than 100,000 GPUs at the Texas Stargate site. Astra posted 97.6 percent on FrontierMath Tier 4 v2, 99.9 percent on ARC-AGI-3 with the custom Provider Adapter harness, and 72.6 percent on OSWorld 2.0 computer use in 40 minutes per task versus GPT-5.6 Sol at 65.7 in 75 minutes. It is the first frontier model OpenAI has classified at the Preparedness Framework's Critical cybersecurity tier: 100 percent on ExploitBench, 99.2 percent on SRE-Bench binary reverse engineering, and two previously unknown vulnerabilities autonomously discovered during evaluation and reported to vendors.

Tech · Safety · Fortune / Simon Willison / The Decoder · 2026-09-03 · Source ↗

OpenAI shipped GPT-6 Astra on Thursday, September 3, opening a rollout that began with limited-access Daybreak enterprise customers before extending to ChatGPT Plus, Pro, Business and Enterprise, the OpenAI API, AWS Bedrock and Azure over the following days. The API model identifier is gpt-6-astra. Standard pricing is $10 per million input tokens and $50 per million output tokens, exactly matching Anthropic's Claude Fable 5 and Fable 5.1 rates and running 2.5x GPT-5.6 Sol's existing $4-in / $20-out. A Fast tier at $20-in / $100-out delivers roughly 2x throughput. Prompt-cache reads discount 90 percent; cache writes carry a 25 percent premium.

Aidan Clark, OpenAI VP of Research, told Fortune that Astra came out of the company's largest training run "by far," pre-trained on more than 100,000 GPUs at OpenAI's Stargate facility in Texas. Company president and co-founder Greg Brockman used the release to make what OpenAI has previously avoided saying out loud.

"It's not unreasonable to feel that we are now in the AGI era, and I think that if you want to say this is the first one, I think it's reasonable." — Greg Brockman, OpenAI president and co-founder, to Fortune, 2026-09-03

The benchmark sweep

OpenAI reported 97.6 percent on FrontierMath Tier 4 v2, 96 percent on GPQA Diamond, 95.9 percent on BenchCAD, and 74.1 percent on DeepSWE v1.1. On ARC-AGI-3, the headline number is 99.9 percent using OpenAI's custom Provider Adapter harness that preserves opaque reasoning state between requests and compacts long conversations to reuse prior work; with the default harness the same model scores 98.6 percent, and 62.7 percent in the more restrictive default form Simon Willison flagged as a rare public demonstration of harness sensitivity to headline numbers.

OSWorld 2.0 computer-use benchmarking put Astra at 72.6 percent in an average 40 minutes per task, against GPT-5.6 Sol at 65.7 percent in 75 minutes. Terminal-Bench Science 0.1 came in at 64.6 versus Fable 5.1's 52.6. HealthBench Professional posted 63.4 to Fable 5.1's 56.6. Automation-Bench moved from GPT-5.6 Sol's 31.4 to Astra's 41.4. Long-context recall was 100 percent from 256K to 512K tokens and 96.3 percent from 512K to 1M on the eight-needle benchmark.

"GPT-6 Astra can zip through spreadsheets, fill out forms, and navigate across web pages often at superhuman speed." — Greg Brockman, on the computer-use capability, to Fortune

The Critical cybersecurity tier

Astra is the first frontier model OpenAI has classified at the Preparedness Framework's Critical cybersecurity capability tier. ExploitBench pass rate hit 100 percent, up from GPT-5.6 Sol's 78.5 percent. ExploitGym reached 42.4 percent, up from Sol's 30.3 percent. SRE-Bench binary reverse engineering scored 99.2 percent within four attempts, against Sol's 68.7 percent. During evaluation Astra autonomously identified and exploited two previously unknown vulnerabilities in software the lab does not name in the release. Both were reported to vendors and patched before publication.

The consequence is a two-tier ship. Advanced cybersecurity capability is gated behind OpenAI's enterprise Daybreak program for vetted government and cybersecurity partners. The general-availability Astra refuses "advanced cybersecurity tasks." OpenAI briefed the incoming Center for AI Standards and Innovation and the UK AI Security Institute ahead of launch and characterized the process as "a very good partnership." Brockman declined to detail the review structure "because there's nuances on exactly how the process works."

The independent read

Artificial Analysis published its Astra deep-dive within hours. Its Intelligence Index puts Astra at 61 at max effort, tied with GPT-5.6 Sol and five points below Claude Fable 5.1 at 66. AA-Omniscience shows Astra hallucinating 51 percent at max effort against Sol's 92 percent, a rare structural improvement rather than a headline delta. AA-Briefcase, the knowledge-work benchmark, moves roughly 80 points over Sol. On coding, the Coding Agent Index scores Astra 67 in the Codex harness against Fable 5.1 in Claude Code at 70. Astra runs 70 percent more token-efficient than Sol on coding but end-to-end cost per task at max effort is 75 percent higher than Sol because output pricing more than triples.

"GPT-6 Astra makes significant gains in the Artificial Analysis Coding Agent Index, scoring equal to Fable 5 at lower cost. Pricing is 2.5x GPT-5.6 Sol's current prices across the board, up from $4/$20 to $10/$50 per million input/output tokens." — Artificial Analysis, benchmarking changelog, 2026-09-03

The framing

Brockman used the Fortune interview to lean on the operational side rather than the capability numbers, pointing to Astra's ability to keep multi-hour agentic tasks on the rails without human hand-holding. Mia Glaese, OpenAI VP of Research, put the computer-use gain in evolutionary terms, saying it "shows how far we've come from sort of aspirationally training for computer use to bringing real value to people every day." Sam Altman framed the release as an enabler rather than a terminal: "we hope it will begin to enable a new generation of entrepreneurship, scientific discovery, and building." The AGI framing is the marketing peg; the substantive shifts are the doubled input price, the shipped-to-Daybreak cybersecurity gate, the long-context recall improvement, and the fact that on the standardized third-party intelligence index Astra ties Sol and trails Fable 5.1.

Flagship scored 47 on Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index, a 30-point jump over the prior K2.

MBZUAI's Institute of Foundation Models released K2 Horizon Thursday, September 3, a six-model family aiming squarely at the "full openness" position. The sizes are 0.9B, 3.7B, 7B, 32B dense, 36B/4B-active Mixture-of-Value-Attention MoE, and the flagship K2-Horizon-375B-A23B. The 375B is a sparse MoE with 23B active parameters, a 524,288-token (512K) context window, and BF16 serving on SGLang and vLLM. All six carry Apache 2.0 licenses and distribute through Hugging Face, Ollama, Unsloth and IFM's own repository.

Pretraining ran on roughly 20 trillion tokens with about 17 percent of the corpus explicit reasoning trajectories and about 10 trillion synthetic tokens generated in-loop. What IFM ships alongside the weights is where the release breaks from the Meta or Alibaba model of "open weights, closed data." Weights, source code, training data, methodology, intermediate checkpoints, training logs and evaluation results all publish together.

"Open source is much more than open weights. Science works when others can see the data, follow the method, reproduce the result, and improve on it." — Dr Eric Xing, MBZUAI President, on the K2 Horizon release

Sub-flagship results held up. The 0.9B scored above 48 on AIME 2026. The 7B is claimed as the industry's best model under 10B. The 32B dense ranks among the strongest under 40B. "The most important technology of our time should be built with the world, not kept from it. Openness isn't a slogan we ask you to accept. It's a list we ask you to check." — Dr Hector Liu, IFM Director, to Moor Insights & Strategy

The release lands as Meta continues to publish Muse Glimmer under a decoupled safety LoRA and Alibaba pushes Qwen3.8-Flash-Next and Qwen3.8-Max snapshots without a training-data drop. NVIDIA Nemotron Nano-CC and Ultra-CC clear IOI 2026 gold live under human-contestant rules

arXiv 2609.02849 / Hugging Face Papers · 2026-09-03 · Source ↗

Nvidia's Ultra-CC (550B total / 55B active MoE from Nemotron-3-Ultra) posted 535.4 out of 600 at the live IOI 2026 competition under the same time, internet and submission rules as human contestants, exceeding both the 361.12 gold threshold and the top human's 498.27.

The Nvidia Nemotron team of Aleksander Ficek, Sean Narenthiran, Mehrzad Samadi, Somshubra Majumdar and Boris Ginsburg published "Post-Training Language Models for Gold-Medal Performance in Coding Competitions" as arXiv 2609.02849, featured on the Sept 3 Hugging Face Daily Papers list. The paper documents two systems: Nano-CC at 30B total / 3B active MoE and Ultra-CC at 550B total / 55B active MoE built from Nemotron-3-Ultra-550B-A55B. Both use a hybrid mamba-transformer backbone with 262K context.

On the IOI 2025 static evaluation Nano-CC scored 291 out of 600 at Score@1 (48.5 percent) and 461 at Score@200 (76.8 percent). Ultra-CC scored 304 (50.7 percent) and 502 (83.5 percent). The gold-medal threshold is 438.3. The more consequential number came from IOI 2026 live: under the same time limits, internet access rules and submission constraints as human contestants, Ultra-CC scored 535.4 out of 600, exceeding both the 361.12 gold threshold and the top human's 498.27.

Comparative baselines

On IOI 2025 the baselines Nano-CC and Ultra-CC clear include DeepSeek-V4-Flash at 55.3 percent, DeepSeek-V4-Pro at 56.8 percent, GLM-5.2 as the strongest open baseline at 66.0 percent, and gpt-oss-120b at 40.7 percent. ICPC 2025 pass@1 came in at 51.0 percent for Nano-CC and 57.4 percent for Ultra-CC. LiveCodeBench Pro pass@1 hit 71.6 percent for Nano-CC and 74.5 percent for Ultra-CC.

The training recipe combined large-scale problem curation, synthetic data generation, supervised fine-tuning, reinforcement learning, and iterative test-time refinement the authors call GenCorrect. Nano-CC ran from 130 to 291 after post-training and from 291 to 468 with GenCorrect layered on. The paper is the highest-scoring live-competition result any AI lab has publicly posted at an IOI competition and lands alongside the DeepMind AlphaCode 2 track that has held closed since 2024.

BAAI's Repo-To-Skill tops Hugging Face Daily Papers with a 5,000-skill research-agent library

Hugging Face Papers / arXiv 2609.02749 · 2026-09-03 · Source ↗

Beijing Academy of AI paper distilled 1,000 ML repositories into AREX-Skill, an Apache-2.0 library of 5,000+ executable skills across 20 areas and 178 capability families, lifting a GPT-5.5-backed research agent 134 percent on MLE-bench and 34 percent on PaperBench at constant execution budget.

Repo-To-Skill topped the Hugging Face Daily Papers list on September 3 with 494 upvotes. The paper, authored by BAAI's VectorSpaceLab team, documents a pipeline the authors call DisCo that distills GitHub repositories into what they term "operational knowledge — the know-how that separates knowing a method from making it work." The output is AREX-Skill, a library of 5,000+ verified skills mined from 1,000+ ML repositories, spanning 20 areas and 178 package families, all Apache 2.0.

The DisCo pipeline runs in four stages. Scoping identifies executable capabilities within a repository. Evidence grounding attaches code paths and test coverage to each skill. Graph construction organizes skills into a directed capability graph. Verification runs each skill through an executor to confirm it produces the expected artifact. The output is not documentation about the repo but callable skills a downstream agent can invoke.

"Operational knowledge — the know-how that separates knowing a method from making it work." — Repo-To-Skill paper, BAAI VectorSpaceLab, arXiv 2609.02749

Benchmark deltas

Plugged into a GPT-5.5-backed research agent at constant execution budget, AREX-Skill lifted MLE-bench performance 134.3 percent, PaperBench 34.4 percent, FrontierCS 9.2 percent, and PassNet 14.0 percent. The library ships through arXiv, GitHub (VectorSpaceLab/AREX-Skill) and an npm distribution installable as @arex-skill/disco.

The rest of the Sept 3 Papers of the Day list rounded out with CUHK Shenzhen's SolarWM (273 upvotes) on open data and scalable training for long-horizon video world models; ByteDance Seed's HarnessDev (224) benchmarking LLMs on their ability to build and evolve their own agent harness across 2,207 instances, where generated harnesses match or exceed human-engineered references on writing and ML experimentation but lag on code and search; ByteDance Seed's Aspire (173) on model self-evolution from vague goals; and SJTU's EarlyEval (110) on cheaper agent evaluation via early outcome prediction. The through-line is that skill libraries, harness generation and self-evolution are converging as the three dominant post-training research directions for late 2026.
