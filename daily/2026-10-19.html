Anthropic places 5% of its stock into a Public Benefit Fund, with a 2% top-up for every 50% rise in company value.

Anthropic announced on Sunday that it will commit 5% of its shares, subject to stockholder approval, to a wholly-owned LLC called the Anthropic Public Benefit Fund, with an additional 2% added each time the company's value climbs 50% above an undisclosed baseline. CEO Dario Amodei framed the fund as a response to what he has called a crisis of trust around AI, with uses that will range from large donations to traditional nonprofits to direct distributions to specific populations hit by labor-market effects. The share pledge sits on top of a prior 80% equity commitment from co-founders Dario and Daniela Amodei through the Giving Pledge, bringing roughly 20% of Anthropic's stock earmarked for public good. The announcement arrives alongside a quieter OpenAI test release and the Disney stalking-horse bid for Character.AI's workforce, and ahead of Anthropic's November share sale pricing talks around a $1.5 trillion handle.

Economic · TechCrunch, Anthropic newsroom · 2026-10-18 · Source ↗

Anthropic's board approved and the company disclosed on Sunday a mechanism that converts its for-profit stock into charitable-use capital over time. The initial 5% share commitment, subject to stockholder approval, flows into a wholly-owned LLC named the Anthropic Public Benefit Fund. Each time Anthropic's valuation climbs 50% above a baseline the company has not disclosed, the fund receives another 2% top-up at board discretion. The structure makes the fund grow with the company and gives it bigger ammunition in nominal dollars as the pre-IPO valuation moves higher.

Co-founders Dario and Daniela Amodei have separately pledged 80% of their personal Anthropic equity to philanthropy through earlier Giving Pledge commitments. Taken together with the new corporate pledge, roughly 20% of Anthropic's outstanding stock is now earmarked for public-benefit uses, pending stockholder sign-off on the 5% transfer. The company said it will publish fuller details on governance, grantmaking criteria, and timeline for disbursements in the coming months. The TechCrunch write-up names the move as broader than Anthropic's $200 million prior commitment to study AI's effects on labor markets, with Anthropic itself framing the labor-market work as a precedent that "bolder approaches may be needed" to extend.

What the fund can do

Anthropic listed two broad categories of use. The first is traditional grantmaking to nonprofits, the model familiar from the Chan Zuckerberg Initiative and OpenAI's startup fund. The second is more unusual: direct distributions to specific populations, which the company flagged as a possible response to concentrated labor-market disruption from AI-driven automation. Anthropic did not disclose whether distributions would be means-tested, geographic, or occupational. The company said the LLC structure gives it flexibility to run both approaches without touching the operating business.

"The current climate is fundamentally a crisis of trust. AI is moving faster than our institutions can absorb it, and the companies that build it bear a share of the responsibility to make that absorption gentler." — Dario Amodei, Anthropic CEO, quoted in TechCrunch, 2026-10-18

Timing and the IPO handle

The announcement lands with Anthropic running pricing talks for a November share sale after Q3 revenue printed at $16.8 billion, up 45% year on year but slower sequentially than Q2. Investor-day guidance from last week walked the pre-IPO expectation from a September $2 trillion handle to roughly $1.5 trillion. Converting a slice of equity to a public-benefit vehicle before pricing softens the public image of a 1.5 trillion dollar valuation and locks in a growth-indexed charitable pool that will be bigger than the sum of the Amodei personal pledges once the business crosses a few 50% valuation steps. The $200 million labor-market work previously announced is understood to continue as a prior program distinct from the new fund.

What is not clear

The announcement is thin on governance. Anthropic did not name trustees, a board for the LLC, or the officer who will run disbursements. It did not disclose the baseline valuation against which the 50% ladder is measured. The timeline for the stockholder vote is open. The company said more will be published "in the coming months." Separately, the fund's charter-level language does not appear to carry veto rights for Anthropic's Long-Term Benefit Trust, which already holds a measure of control over the company; whether the LTBT governs the PBF LLC is not stated. TechCrunch's Anthony Ha noted the structural novelty: a traditional Delaware public-benefit corporation gives directors legal cover to weigh public purpose against shareholder return, but this LLC sits inside the parent and routes value out, which is a different plumbing.

"A public-benefit fund structured as a wholly-owned LLC is unusual. It is not a foundation. It is not a nonprofit. It is a lever Anthropic itself still controls." — Anthony Ha, TechCrunch, 2026-10-18

Peer frames

OpenAI has run the OpenAI Startup Fund since 2021 and announced a 20% employee-equity vehicle in 2023 under its earlier restructuring; both are different in design. Google DeepMind operates DeepMind Impact Accelerator. Meta's Chan-Zuckerberg Initiative is Zuckerberg's personal structure, not Meta's. The Anthropic PBF is unique in that it is funded corporate equity on a growth ratchet, not a one-time grant from the operating business and not a founder's personal pledge routed through a separate 501(c)(4). The design implies that if Anthropic reaches a $10 trillion valuation while its baseline is $500 billion, the fund would accrue roughly five additional 2% top-ups on top of the initial 5%, bringing the fund alone to around 15% of outstanding stock.

Anthropic's own announcement page carried the headline "Investing in the Public Benefit." The newsroom entry is dated October 18. The full governance disclosure is pending. The next concrete milestone is the stockholder vote; Anthropic did not set a date.

Samsung taps TSMC's 3nm line to make the base dies for Nvidia's custom HBM.

Seoul Economic Daily · 2026-10-18 · Source ↗

Samsung's own 1c DRAM process makes the memory core dies; TSMC's 3nm handles the base. First time Samsung has outsourced HBM base-die production.

Samsung Electronics has agreed to use TSMC's 3-nanometer process to manufacture the base dies for Nvidia's custom HBM stacks, with Samsung's own 1c DRAM line producing the memory core dies above them. The arrangement is the first time Samsung has sent HBM base-die production outside its own fabs. The deal was reported by the Seoul Economic Daily on Sunday. HBM base dies carry the logic that moves data between the stacked DRAM and the processor; shifting them to a more advanced node reduces power and extends bandwidth headroom.

Why it matters

Nvidia's custom HBM, understood to be branded HBM4e, is the memory specification for the Rubin GB300 and later Blackwell successors. The custom part sits between pure commodity HBM, where all three major memory vendors (Micron, Samsung, SK Hynix) ship interchangeable parts, and fully bespoke silicon. By letting Nvidia dictate the base-die logic and routing it to TSMC, Nvidia gets closer to the ASIC vendors like Broadcom and Marvell on memory, while the DRAM core still comes from the top memory makers. Samsung's willingness to split the stack is a signal that it needs to retain the DRAM socket even if it loses the base-die advanced-node line.

"HBM demand is forecast to rise 91% in 2027, and the shortage of advanced-node base-die capacity has become the throughput bottleneck. Splitting the stack between Samsung and TSMC is the only way to make the math work." — Industry analyst cited by Seoul Economic Daily, 2026-10-18

Industry forecast

The source names HBM demand growth of about 91% in 2027. SK Hynix remains Nvidia's primary HBM volume supplier; Micron qualified in October 2026 across all three memory partners for HBM4e; Samsung has been the stress case because of yield questions on earlier HBM3e. The TSMC base-die split is read as Samsung's path to stay in Nvidia's qualification bill despite its own 1c DRAM teething pains. Samsung and TSMC have not published joint statements. Nvidia has not confirmed the arrangement on the record.

Why humanoid

Altman's framing is that general-purpose robots need a body that matches human infrastructure. Doorways, stairs, tool handles, and vehicles were all designed around human proportions. A wheeled or quadrupedal form cannot climb a staircase in a residential home; a humanoid can. The counterargument from Boston Dynamics and others has been that specialized forms are more efficient per task, but OpenAI's bet is that the breadth of tasks a single humanoid can address pays back the inefficiency.

"The humanoid form matches the world we have already built. The real challenge is the software. The physics still has the final say." — Sam Altman, quoted by Engadget, 2026-10-18

Research lineage

OpenAI's prior robotics work includes Dactyl, a project that trained a robotic hand in simulation and then applied the policy to solve a Rubik's Cube on physical hardware. Dactyl showed that reinforcement learning in simulation could transfer to the real world under aggressive domain randomization. The company shelved the dedicated robotics team in 2021 to focus on language models; the current announcement reverses that and reunites perception, planning, and actuation under a single product goal.

Schwab and JPMorgan block outside AI agents from moving money as private ordering fills the regulatory gap.

Forkast · 2026-10-18 (reported from Bloomberg TV, Oct 16) · Source ↗

Schwab blocks "tens and twenties" of unauthorized AI-agent transaction attempts weekly. JPMorgan excludes outside agents from account access. MCP servers get read-only, no-transfer scope.

Charles Schwab CEO Rick Wurster, speaking to Bloomberg TV on October 16, said the firm actively blocks unauthorized AI agents including Meta Muse from executing client transactions. A weekly report, he said, shows "tens and twenties" of such attempts across major agent platforms. JPMorgan CEO Jamie Dimon separately confirmed that the bank blocks outside AI agents from accessing customer account information. Priya Nair's Sunday analysis in Forkast argues that this pattern, which she calls "private ordering," is becoming the de facto liability framework for AI agents in financial services while federal regulation stalls.

Schwab's architecture

Wurster described a two-tier split. Clients are steered toward Charley, Schwab's in-house assistant, for administrative and informational tasks. Charley cannot place trades. Outside LLMs are allowed access only through read-only Model Context Protocol (MCP) servers that expose balance queries and research but refuse any transaction primitive. The combination gives agents a workable research surface without exposing custody. The design is a reversal of the OAuth-style "connected app" model where third-party software gets scoped write permission.

"The question is who is accountable when a client's AI agent makes a mistake. If the client cannot control it, and we cannot control it, no one is responsible. That is not a workable system." — Rick Wurster, Schwab CEO, Bloomberg TV, 2026-10-16

JPMorgan and the stalled federal response

Dimon's confirmation, delivered without a quoted sentence in the article, matches the broader JPMorgan line that outside agents and the credential disclosures they require are a security and audit problem rather than a capability problem. Nair's analysis names three stalled federal bills: the Warner AI AGENT Act, the Hawley-Murphy AI Agent Accountability Act, and the Great American AI Act. The Department of Justice has indicated a hands-off approach to standalone AI enforcement, leaving liability definition to civil courts and private contracts.

Why it matters

The precedent is significant beyond finance. If large regulated institutions can unilaterally refuse to serve third-party AI agents at the transaction layer, the agent economy's assumed future of a user-side agent orchestrating paid actions across the web becomes much narrower. Agents become reporters and researchers, not actors. Nair's piece ends on that frame: "Agents may observe, but they are not yet trusted to act."

A Samsung Galaxy phone held to a microscope eyepiece diagnoses 96 of 96 vaginal infection specimens correctly.

Frontiers in Artificial Intelligence (via Bioengineer) · 2026-10-18 · Source ↗

Chiang Mai University's RT-DETR vision transformer scored 98.25% image-level accuracy and 100% specimen-level accuracy across five diagnostic categories.

Researchers at Chiang Mai University in Thailand published a paper in Frontiers in Artificial Intelligence (vol. 9, article 1985517, DOI 10.3389/frai.2026.1985517) describing a smartphone-plus-microscope diagnostic system for vaginal discharge that scored 98.25% image-level accuracy and 100% specimen-level accuracy across five diagnostic categories. The authors trained a Real-Time Detection Transformer (RT-DETR), an anchor-free vision transformer, on 1,966 Gram-stained images with 11,069 annotated diagnostic objects. The imaging rig was a Samsung Galaxy S23 Ultra held to the eyepiece of a conventional light microscope at 400x total magnification.

Results

The independent test set carried 514 images representing 96 unique specimens drawn prospectively from Maharaj Nakorn Chiang Mai Hospital. RT-DETR reached mAP50 of 0.382 for detection, 98.25% image-level accuracy (F1 0.9807, mean AUC 0.9846), and correctly diagnosed all 96 specimens at the specimen level by majority vote across fields. YOLOv26, the anchor-free CNN baseline, trailed closely at 95.72% image-level and lighter at 116.80 GFLOPs vs RT-DETR's 421.73 GFLOPs. EfficientDet-D0, the anchor-based baseline, trailed far behind at mAP50 of 0.116.

"Specimen-level accuracy of 100% across 96 cases, with activation maps 2.4 times more concentrated inside annotated diagnostic regions. The design is low-cost and portable, pending multi-center validation." — Sookkhee et al., Frontiers in AI, 2026

Caveats the authors flag

The study is retrospective and single-institution with a standardized imaging workflow. No documented mixed infections were included, so the single-label framework may miss co-existing conditions. Bounding-box annotations may underrepresent irregular structures like pseudohyphae. Pregnancy status and prior treatment history were unavailable in the de-identified data. The authors explicitly call for prospective multi-center validation across varied microscopes, stains, smartphones, and populations before any autonomous deployment.

Why it is interesting

The paper is one of the clearest demonstrations in 2026 of a vision transformer running in a resource-limited clinical setting with a smartphone camera as the imaging surface. Expert review (eight clinicians and microbiologists) rated roughly 85% of RT-DETR's bounding boxes "Good" or "Excellent", with no significant difference between RT-DETR and YOLOv26 on human-rated interpretability. For primary care clinics without lab infrastructure, the economics are compelling: a conventional microscope and a mid-range Android phone covers the hardware stack.

Claude Code 2.1.302 defaults fork subagents on, lands the official plugin system, and adds an auto-mode model variable.

GitHub (Anthropic) · 2026-10-18 (release 2.1.302) · Source ↗

63 changelog entries. 43 fixes, 9 changes, 7 improvements, 4 additions. Fork subagents are on by default outside the terminal; a CLAUDE_CODE_AUTO_MODE_MODEL variable selects the client-side classifier.

Anthropic shipped Claude Code 2.1.302 with 63 changelog entries, breaking down to 43 fixes, 9 changes, 7 improvements, and 4 additions. The headline operational shift is that fork subagents (subagent_type: "fork") are now on by default in claude -p, the Agent SDK, the desktop app, and VS Code; they were already on in the terminal. The desktop and VS Code now always background the main conversation's subagents, matching terminal behavior. claude mcp serve never offers fork subagents. To opt out, set CLAUDE_CODE_FORK_SUBAGENT=0.

Auto-mode routing

A new CLAUDE_CODE_AUTO_MODE_MODEL environment variable selects the model used by the client-side classifier that decides which model handles a given turn in auto mode. The server-side auto mode is unchanged. /autocompact and /effort now save to the correct model when a gateway (Vertex, Bedrock) prefixes Opus 4 or 4.1 names (e.g., anthropic/claude-opus-4-1). A conversation too long for the safety check is now compacted instead of denying every checked tool call. Prompt suggestions are skipped after a reply billed to credits.

Plugin system

No new plugin features. The release treats the plugin system as formalized: plugin command and agent files load with far fewer file-system calls (helpful on Windows with many plugins), marketplaces no longer re-clone on every refresh, newly installed git plugins no longer report "unknown" version under reftable, and PowerShell now finds commands in a plugin's bin/ folder. Windows file writes no longer fail on exFAT or FAT32 under --plugin-dir.

"Fork subagents on by default outside the terminal is the most consequential operational change. Agent SDK scripts that assumed foreground execution need to review their flow." — Release notes summary, Anthropic engineering, 2026-10-18

Reliability

Image reads no longer load entire files into memory; sources over 128 MB are refused. Prompt caching cache-misses after mid-session tool additions through ToolSearch or compaction are fixed. MCP servers that negotiate protocol version 2026-07-28 but omit resultType, ttlMs, or cacheScope now show their tools. Hooks are capped at 12 concurrent prompts or agent hooks per event. Cloud sessions report "Connection lost" or "Server error" instead of "Something went wrong."

Platform specifics

VS Code fixes include text-after-unclosed-code-block in lists, MCP sign-in with query-string URLs, and the misleading "Browser connected" banner for @browser. Claude Tag (Slack) ships about ten fixes including DM handling on Enterprise Grid, image attachments (HEIC, SVG, TIFF, BMP), and a reorganized admin settings view with four tabs: Bundles, Connectors, Skills and plugins, Channels. Remote Control now reports disconnect instead of a spinning subagent pane. The /design-sync and /design-login commands warn that standalone Claude Design (claude.ai/design) closes December 14, 2026.

DeepSeek pushes V4.1-Flash-1017: 763B parameters, 1M context, MIT license, 77.0 on DeepSWE v1.1.

Hugging Face · 2026-10-17 (model page) · Source ↗

A "minor update" to V4.1-Flash. Scores 77.0 on DeepSWE v1.1 (up from 74.2), 42.3 on Terminal-Bench 4.0 (up from 31.2), 42.6 on HLE without tools.

DeepSeek posted V4.1-Flash-1017 to Hugging Face late Saturday (Oct 17 UTC), with the Oct 18 Western-outlet aggregation lifting the release into the Sunday rundown. The model card calls the release a "minor update to DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash" and ships weights under the MIT license. The Safetensors metadata reports 763 billion total parameters with mixed BF16, F32, F8_E4M3, and I8 tensor types. The recommended context window is one million tokens, with a suggested max_tokens of at least 256,000 for generation. Recommended sampling is temperature 1.0 and top_p 1.0.

Benchmarks

Reported scores against the prior V4.1-Flash: DeepSWE v1.1 at 77.0 (up from 74.2), Terminal-Bench 4.0 at 42.3 (up from 31.2), HLE without tools at 42.6, BabyVision with tools at 92.5. MMLU is not listed on the card. The DeepSWE jump of 2.8 points on a frontier agentic coding benchmark is the headline capability improvement; the Terminal-Bench 4.0 jump of 11 points is the larger relative lift. No benchmark table against Opus 5.6 Mythos or GPT-6 Astra is on the card.

"Pushing the Limits of KV Cache Compression." — Citation title on the DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash-1017 model card, 2026

Architecture signals

The citation title on the model card ("Pushing the Limits of KV Cache Compression") is the only architectural hint. The card provides a Python reference implementation (encoding.py) and the Rust-based deepseek-recipe toolkit, but no Jinja chat template. The implication is a continuation of DeepSeek's work on compressed KV cache designs that scale long-context inference without a quadratic memory footprint. The company's prior V4.1 line used a Multi-Level Attention (MLA) variant; the 1017 suffix fits the pattern of DeepSeek's monthly stealth updates rather than a numbered major release.

Licensing

MIT on both the repository and the weights. There is no revenue cap. Day-zero inference support is understood to flow through vLLM, llama.cpp, SGLang, and Hugging Face Transformers, though the model card does not include a day-zero stack list the way Moonshot's K4 card did on Saturday.
