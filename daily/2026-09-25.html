Anthropic asks for 50.1% founder voting control the same day it commits $11.6B to Akamai and the White House brands Amodei a doomer.

Anthropic's board asked shareholders on Wednesday to approve a Palantir-style dual-class structure that would concentrate 50.1 percent of voting power in the seven co-founders as long as three of them keep minimum equity, according to The Information's proxy scoop. The same afternoon the company disclosed a seven-year $11.6 billion commitment to Akamai for AI infrastructure with a warrant for roughly 5 percent of Akamai equity and expansion rights that Reuters put at $20 billion total; Akamai stock jumped 17 percent after hours. Hours later, Axios reported a White House policy memo circulating inside the West Wing that names Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei as the face of "AI doomerism," ties him to effective altruism, and argues his agenda runs "counter to America First." The three fronts land the week before a reported Anthropic IPO and follow Amodei's September 12 "Pace the Frontier" essay.

Economic · The Information / Reuters / Axios · 2026-09-24 · Source ↗

The Information first reported Wednesday morning that Anthropic's board is asking shareholders to approve a Palantir-style dual-class voting structure that concentrates 50.1 percent of voting power in the seven co-founders as a bloc, on the condition that at least three of them each retain a specified minimum equity floor. The provision would survive an initial public offering. Anthropic's founding cohort includes CEO Dario Amodei, President Daniela Amodei, chief scientist Jared Kaplan, and researchers Tom Brown, Sam McCandlish, Jack Clark, and Chris Olah. The mechanism echoes the founder-supermajority arrangements at Palantir, Meta, Snap, Google and other post-2004 tech IPOs that give sitting management structural protection against activist investors demanding they change strategy after a public listing.

Two hours later Reuters broke the second story. Anthropic will commit $11.6 billion over seven years to Akamai for AI infrastructure services, with expansion options that could bring the total to $20 billion over the life of the arrangement, and receives a warrant to acquire up to 5 percent of Akamai's fully diluted equity at set strike prices tied to Akamai's stock at signing. Akamai shares closed the regular session flat and jumped roughly 17 percent in extended trading; Reuters said the after-hours move added around $4 billion to Akamai's market cap by the New York close. Akamai is not among the historical hyperscaler set that Anthropic has previously named as compute suppliers (Amazon Web Services, Google Cloud, and CoreWeave), and the arrangement adds a fourth infrastructure vector at a moment when Anthropic's frontier training runs are increasingly bounded by contiguous power rather than accelerator supply.

"This is the first time a frontier-lab compute contract has come with a founder-voting reservation and an infrastructure warrant landing the same day, and the market is telling you it thinks the second one is worth more than the first." — Ben Thompson, Stratechery, in a note to subscribers Wednesday afternoon

Axios published the third piece of the day at approximately 4pm Eastern. A White House policy memo, drafted for internal circulation and shared with a small number of allied think tanks, casts Amodei personally as the face of "AI doomerism," argues the effective altruism movement that funded early Anthropic work is "an ideological project counter to America First," and frames the September 12 "Pace the Frontier" essay as a bid to slow US industry to Chinese speed. The memo identifies Anthropic's Federal Contracting relationships and its recent Center for AI Safety Fund grants as vectors the administration should scrutinize. Axios named White House AI advisor David Sacks and deputy chief of staff Stephen Miller as the memo's principal in-house recipients; neither responded on the record. Anthropic declined to comment.

"The Anthropic worldview says AI is so dangerous that only Anthropic can be trusted to build it. That is not a safety position. It is a regulatory-capture position." — Excerpt from the White House policy memo, quoted by Axios, 2026-09-24

The three items rhyme by design. Anthropic's board and legal team clearly staged the voting proposal and the Akamai commitment for the same trading day to give the market one coherent Anthropic-is-durable narrative ahead of the reported IPO window that Reuters described on Saturday as "twelve days" out. The Akamai warrant is the equity-side signal that the incoming compute partner is putting skin in the game. The voting reservation is the message to public-market investors that Amodei intends to stay in the operator seat even if a large index fund starts arguing that safety commitments should be relaxed to compete with OpenAI. The White House memo, meanwhile, is the counter-signal: an incoming administration that is inclined to accept OpenAI's more permissive framing and treat Anthropic's caution as a liability rather than a moat.

The stakes are visible in the pricing. Anthropic is heading into a public listing at a reported $900 billion post-money valuation, three times the level Reuters reported when the company was raising private capital in March 2026, and roughly 60x its most recent disclosed annualized revenue run-rate. Founder-supermajority structures at Palantir and Meta were criticized at their respective IPOs and are now typically praised as the reason those companies retained strategic optionality; the same case will be made for Anthropic. The Akamai deal is smaller than Anthropic's $30 billion AWS multi-year commitment and its Google Cloud commitment (which Google disclosed as "in the tens of billions" but has never itemized), but it is the first Anthropic compute contract to include an equity warrant back the other direction. The White House attack, per Axios, is aimed at pricing risk into the IPO in the weeks it takes to walk investors through the deck.

Reactions ran the political spectrum. Silicon Valley VC Marc Andreessen posted "Founder-controlled is a feature, not a bug." Anthropic board member Neil Buddy Shah issued a brief statement noting the voting proposal "codifies a commitment already implicit in how the company has operated for four years." Effective Altruism Forum threads treated the White House memo as vindication of prior warnings that Anthropic's positioning would attract political blowback. Senator Bernie Sanders' office, which one day earlier had joined Representative Greg Casar to introduce a bill imposing criminal penalties on superintelligence development, told Bloomberg the White House campaign against Amodei "confirms exactly what Senator Sanders warned about at Yale in 2024, that when the safety people become inconvenient to the money, the state discards them." Anthropic's proxy filing is expected in October.

DOJ tells US Attorneys that AI-datacenter opposition may be Foreign-Agent activity.

Ken Klippenstein Substack · 2026-09-24 · Source ↗

A DOJ guidance document circulated to US Attorneys frames domestic opposition to AI datacenter siting as potential FARA activity. Trump called skeptics "Treasonists" and "Traitors" on Truth Social.

Independent reporter Ken Klippenstein published a document dump Wednesday afternoon containing what he identified as a Department of Justice guidance memo, distributed via the Executive Office for United States Attorneys, that directs US Attorneys to treat certain forms of organized opposition to AI datacenter siting as potentially subject to the Foreign Agents Registration Act. FARA, adopted in 1938 and expanded during World War II, requires anyone who acts "at the order, request, or under the direction or control" of a foreign principal to publicly register with the Attorney General or face fines and up to five years' imprisonment. The Klippenstein document reproduces language directing prosecutors to consider whether local activism against datacenter zoning, water-permit challenges, and grid-interconnect litigation are "materially assisted, funded, or directed by foreign state or state-linked interests."

The memo landed hours after Xi Jinping used a Wednesday-morning White House press availability to argue that "AI must remain under human control," a phrasing that the Chinese Ministry of Foreign Affairs subsequently amplified in a WeChat statement. Neither document accuses any named individual or organization, but the practical target set that fits the memo's language is small. Datacenter opposition in Fairfax County, in the Columbus suburbs, and around Reno, Nevada, has drawn press coverage this year over grid strain and water consumption; the largest organized coalitions include the Data Center Coalition Watch, a network of local ratepayer groups, and Environmental Data Investigators, a nonprofit that publishes analysis of hyperscaler power draw.

"There is no evidence in this document that any US datacenter opponent has ever taken foreign money. The document is a legal predicate for prosecutorial discretion, not a factual finding." — Kel McClanahan, national security lawyer, quoted in Klippenstein's post, 2026-09-24

Trump, in a Wednesday-evening Truth Social post, wrote that "Radical Left Traitors are trying to STOP the biggest technological boom in American history" and named "Chinese-adjacent groups" without further specificity. The post used the words "Traitors" and "Treasonists." The White House press office did not respond to press inquiries and referred reporters to the DOJ. Attorney General Pam Bondi did not comment. The American Civil Liberties Union's Brennan Center weighed in inside two hours with a statement warning that FARA's language is "notoriously elastic" and calling the guidance "a chilling instrument aimed at First Amendment activity that has no foreign nexus in any of the coalitions I know about." A former DOJ National Security Division attorney told Klippenstein the memo "would not survive an as-applied constitutional challenge" but could still have "substantial deterrent effect during the pendency of any indictment."

The move follows Trump's Truth Social announcement over the weekend of a Space-Force-style "AI Force" and a promised replacement for outgoing AI advisor David Sacks. It also lands the same week the Trump administration told OpenAI and Anthropic to hold their frontier models back from UK AI Security Institute pre-release testing, a related pattern of positioning the federal government as the singular gatekeeper of frontier-AI risk assessment. Sanders and Casar's Wednesday bill to impose criminal penalties on superintelligence development runs against the same current from the opposite direction. Bond, futures, and equities markets did not visibly react.

White House tells OpenAI and Anthropic to skip UK AI Security Institute pre-release testing.

Politico · 2026-09-24 · Source ↗

The Trump administration told OpenAI and Anthropic to hold frontier models back from UK AI Security Institute pre-release testing pending a US review. Anthropic had already declined AISI access to Claude Mythos 5.1.

Politico reported Wednesday that the White House Office of Science and Technology Policy sent a joint letter to OpenAI and Anthropic in late August requesting that both labs delay providing frontier-model weights and evaluation harnesses to the UK AI Security Institute pending an interagency review being led by the National Security Council. The AI Security Institute, standing since November 2023 under UK Prime Minister Rishi Sunak's initial mandate and expanded under Keir Starmer's government, has been the primary Anglosphere pre-release safety evaluator for frontier models since Anthropic gave AISI early access to Claude 3 in mid-2024.

Politico's Christine Mui and Josh Sisco named three senior White House officials as principal signatories of the request: OSTP director Michael Kratsios, National Security Advisor Michael Waltz, and NSC senior director for technology Sriram Krishnan. The letter argues that unilateral pre-release access to non-US safety institutes may violate emerging US federal preemption doctrine on model deployment risk classifications. The White House position, Politico wrote, is that any pre-release safety testing should be routed through a not-yet-named US federal body first, and that access to non-US institutions could proceed only "after concurrent review and, where necessary, a national security determination."

"AISI access has been the single most useful independent evaluator for our models. Losing it is not costless." — Anthropic policy staffer, granted anonymity by Politico, 2026-09-24

Anthropic already declined AISI pre-release access for Claude Mythos 5.1, according to Politico. OpenAI has not confirmed whether it complied. UK AI Security Institute director Ian Hogarth issued a brief statement noting AISI "remains open to voluntary evaluation partnerships with all developers of frontier systems." A Downing Street spokesperson confirmed the UK government "was informed of the US position through appropriate channels and views the matter as a bilateral technical question, not a treaty commitment." Neither the National Security Council nor OSTP responded on the record.

The move follows a Bloomberg report last week that the White House is quietly assembling a "Frontier AI Standards Agency" as a voluntary industry body launching in late 2026 or early 2027, with Sriram Krishnan reportedly approached to serve as chief executive. The AISI request is consistent with the administration's stated preference for a US-based standard-setter and for treating pre-release access to foreign safety bodies as a matter of national security discretion rather than routine research cooperation.
