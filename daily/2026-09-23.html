Anthropic and OpenAI ship rival flagships 90 minutes apart and both cut prices in half.

Anthropic released Claude Opus 5.5 at $4 per million input and $20 per million output tokens on Tuesday afternoon, a 20 percent list-price cut against Opus 5 and roughly 40 percent cheaper on typical workloads once caching and speed gains flow through. OpenAI answered 90 minutes later with GPT-6 Sol at $2/$10 and GPT-6 Luna at $0.10/$0.50, roughly half of the 5.6-series equivalents. Opus 5.5 pulled Terminal-Bench 4.0 from 52.3 to 66.4 percent, past Fable 5.1's 55.8, and Anthropic reports containment-boundary circumvention attempts dropped 85 percent. GPT-6 Sol scored 33.2 percent on AutomationBench at $0.27 per task and 68.8 percent on DeepSWE v1.1. Both labs framed the day as a pricing reset. Neither slowed anything down.

Tech · TechCrunch / SiliconANGLE / CNBC · 2026-09-23 · Source ↗

Anthropic and OpenAI shipped rival flagships on Tuesday afternoon within 90 minutes of each other, and both used the release to reset frontier pricing. Anthropic announced Claude Opus 5.5 at 2:00 PM ET at $4 per million input tokens and $20 per million output tokens, down from Opus 5's $5 and $25. Cache reads dropped from $0.50 to $0.20 per million, a 60 percent reduction. Anthropic said generation is 30 percent faster than Opus 5 and that on typical mixed workloads the model costs roughly 40 percent less to run. OpenAI answered at 3:30 PM ET with two GPT-6 variants. Sol, the coding and reasoning tier, prices at $2 per million input and $10 per million output tokens, half the 5.6-series equivalent. Luna, the lightweight clerical tier, prices at $0.10 per million input and $0.50 per million output tokens, roughly a five-times cut against GPT-5.6-mini. Cached input reads carry an additional 90 percent discount.

The benchmark deltas are the reason the pricing sticks. Opus 5.5 pulled Terminal-Bench 4.0 from Opus 5's 52.3 percent to 66.4 percent, ahead of Claude Fable 5.1's 55.8 percent, and posted 40.0 percent on AutomationBench (up from 26.9), 58.7 percent on Terminal-Bench-Science 0.1 (from 29), a GDPval-AA v2.1 Elo of 1846 (from 1708), and 67.7 percent on Humanity's Last Exam. Anthropic said Opus 5.5 leads GPT-6 Astra on agentic coding, knowledge work, computer use, visual chart recognition and multidisciplinary reasoning while trailing on business-workflow automation and agentic science. GPT-6 Sol posted 33.2 percent task completion on AutomationBench at $0.27 per task and 68.8 percent on DeepSWE v1.1; Luna posted 66.6 percent on DeepSWE. OpenAI said the 6-series overall makes about half as many factual mistakes as the 5.6-series and that Sol reaches "Astra-level reliability at much lower cost."

Anthropic pitched Opus 5.5 as a safety-first release inside the pacing framework Dario Amodei introduced with the September 12 "Pace the Frontier" essay. Attempts to circumvent containment boundaries dropped 85 percent versus Opus 5, prompt-injection resistance matches Fable 5.1, and external evaluators Frontier Design and METR ran the model before launch. Anthropic said Opus 5.5 was "the strongest-performing model METR has tested to date" on the group's automated behavioral audit and that Opus 5.5 ships watermarked at no quality or cost penalty. Anthropic also removed the five-hour usage caps on Pro, Max, Team and seat-based Enterprise plans and added a rate-limit reset feature.

"Opus 5.5 performs at the level of Claude Fable 5.1 on most work, but costs 40 percent less to run than Opus 5. We are passing these efficiency savings on to our customers in the form of price cuts and rate limit increases." — Anthropic company statement

OpenAI's counter-programming ran along a parallel axis. The company said the 6-series pricing reflects "improvements in caching and inference," announced a policy to let third-party groups conduct technical safety assessments during training, evaluation and deployment (earlier than the post-development standard the company had previously used), expanded OpenAI Academy with role-based courses for developers, leaders, educators and college students, and named an Advisory Group on Mathematics and AI. OpenAI wrote that the new models handle tasks "substantially better than Anthropic's top models like Fable and Opus." Sol is available in ChatGPT Work, Codex and the API for most paid accounts; Luna is on the desktop app and the Free and Go tiers with a gradual rollout to ChatGPT.

"GPT-6 Astra introduced a new generation of intelligence. These models extend its benefits by making that intelligence more efficient and accessible." — OpenAI blog post, 2026-09-22

Early integration data landed the same afternoon. Box VP of AI Products Yashodha Bhavnani said Opus 5.5's answers on internal enterprise-search tests were "40 percent less verbose without losing accuracy." GitHub CPO Mario Rodriguez said the model "solved more terminal tasks than Opus 5 in less than half the steps" in Copilot's coding harness. Anthropic tester examples included a 680,000-line code migration completed in under a day, a 200,000-line codebase audit finishing in under 3 hours versus 20+ hours on Opus 5, and an HAProxy C-to-Rust port completed in 9.5 hours versus 12 hours on Fable 5.1. Opus 5.5 shipped same-day across Claude apps, the API, AWS Bedrock, Google Vertex, Microsoft Foundry, GitHub Copilot (Pro+, Max, Business, Enterprise), VS Code, Visual Studio, JetBrains, Xcode, Eclipse, Copilot CLI and the Copilot coding agent. The model id is `claude-opus-5-5`, and Anthropic also priced a "faster serving" tier at $8/$40.

"Fully addressing the risks requires even more prudence." — Dario Amodei, Anthropic CEO, on the Opus 5.5 safety card

The 90-minute gap between the two releases matters. Anthropic's launch window was scheduled against a planned Wednesday flagship push from OpenAI; when Anthropic moved forward into Tuesday afternoon, OpenAI compressed its own launch calendar and shipped Sol and Luna the same day. Neither lab was willing to concede the frontier-pricing headline. The competitive backdrop is Bloomberg's Monday reporting that Harvey, Abridge and Ramp are quietly swapping OpenAI-hosted inference for Moonshot's open-weight Kimi K3 to protect gross margins, and Xiaomi's MiMo-V2.6-Pro release the prior evening at one-fiftieth of Fable 5.1's per-task cost under an MIT license. Both frontier labs read the enterprise cost sensitivity in the same direction and re-priced at once. The pacing coalition's rhetoric about slowing down now sits alongside a Tuesday afternoon where the two labs that lead the coalition dropped list prices by 20 to 50 percent and shipped stronger models the same day.

Google Research posted "RRSI: Regularized Recursive Self-Improvement of Agent Harnesses" to arXiv on Tuesday under the identifier 2609.24972, with code on GitHub at `google-research/rrsi`. The paper took the top slot on Hugging Face's daily papers ranking for September 22 with 54 upvotes at the end of day. RRSI targets the failure mode where naive recursive self-improvement of an agent harness produces large in-distribution gains that vanish on out-of-distribution benchmarks. The paper's contribution is a two-stage architecture. The proposer runs on a temporally annealed edit budget and pushes toward unexplored trajectories using the evolution history. The selector uses a critic that screens benchmark-specific proposals and a pruner that removes too-small, too-expensive or obsolete changes.

Evaluation spans eight coding, agentic-workspace and engineering-design benchmarks. RRSI reports up to +14.1 points on the split it evolves against and up to +4.7 points on the five out-of-distribution benchmarks, and the final harness runs on 30 percent fewer policy tokens than unregularized evolution baselines. The paper explicitly targets the RSI overfitting failure mode where in-distribution gains vanish OOD. The authors note that the temporal annealing schedule matters: too-fast annealing collapses to hillclimbing; too-slow annealing spends the compute budget on exploration that never converges.

"The final harness runs on 30 percent fewer policy tokens than unregularized evolution baselines while retaining +4.7 points on five out-of-distribution benchmarks." — RRSI paper, section 5.2

RRSI arrives four days after NVIDIA's SoL-Pi paper, which pushed harness-layer recursive self-improvement to a 44.7 to 49.0 percent reduction in recorded token traffic.
