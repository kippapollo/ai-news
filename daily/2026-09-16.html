Tech · Nvidia Blog · 2026-09-15 · Source ↗

The AI Infra Summit opened Tuesday at the Santa Clara Convention Center with an attendee count that jumped from 3,500 in 2025 to more than 8,000 this year, making it the largest US-based AI-infrastructure conference of 2026 by a wide margin. Nvidia's Ian Buck, VP of hyperscale and HPC, drew the 9:55 a.m. main-stage slot and used it to move the pacing debate away from capability and toward power. Vera Rubin NVL72, now shipping into hyperscalers, delivers up to 30x higher throughput per megawatt than GB300 NVL72 on agentic workloads, up to 40 percent more GPUs inside an existing power budget, and up to 35 percent higher token throughput without new grid interconnects.

DSX MaxLPS is the layer on top. A factory-wide power-optimization scheduler that Nvidia said boosts performance per watt by a further 23 percent, bringing headline efficiency to roughly 1.4x more tokens per megawatt above the raw Vera Rubin gain. Lambda's live demo swapped the numbers into a concrete result: a 19-node cluster running inside a 16-node power envelope produced 5 million tokens per second, up from 4 million, a 24 percent lift with no capex.

"AI factories are becoming the new industrial platforms. Every watt now translates to a business outcome, and Vera Rubin is engineered to convert power into intelligence faster than anything before it." — Ian Buck, VP of hyperscale and HPC, Nvidia

Partner news around the keynote

Amazon's Annapurna Labs signed on as the first NVHBM customer through the Trainium4 line, giving AWS access to Nvidia's high-bandwidth-memory roadmap on a chip AWS designs itself. D-Matrix integrated its Raptor XPUs directly into NVLink Fusion with Vera CPUs, meaning an alternative training accelerator now shares Nvidia's coherent memory fabric rather than talking to Vera over PCIe. Emerald AI and Silicon Valley Power staged an on-stage demonstration of an automated AI-factory load-shed responding to hundreds of grid demand signals while holding critical workloads flat, the first public grid-integrated AI-factory demo built on production Nvidia stack. Perplexity reported 1.9x faster sandbox starts for secure agentic AI on Vera CPU, Redpanda cited 5.5x lower latencies and 73 percent higher throughput, Starburst claimed 3x faster query throughput, and Kinetica reported 2.7x on analytical queries.

Why it hit the pacing story

The Nvidia case against pacing is not a rebuttal. It is a reframing. Dario Amodei's essay treats the frontier as a capability curve to be paced. Buck argued from the stage that the tokens-per-megawatt curve is a separate axis where every hyperscaler already faces a hard power ceiling, and that gains on that axis compound whether or not capability racing slows. Grace CPU generated $5 billion in trailing-twelve-month revenue as of fiscal Q2 2027; Nvidia guided data-center CPU revenue to more than double this fiscal year to next; Vera adopters span Anthropic, OpenAI, xAI, ByteDance, CoreWeave and Oracle Cloud Infrastructure. Intel CEO Lip-Bu Tan followed Buck at 10:15 with a fireside titled "The AI Opportunity: Silicon to Systems," a defensive framing that named 18A foundry, Xeon 6, Gaudi 3 and rack-scale integration as the pieces Intel intends to hold together.

"Vera hardware delivers performance and efficiency that significantly exceed those of any other data center CPU in key areas." — Nvidia, quoted in Motley Fool coverage of the Vera CPU ramp

The tape did not buy the reframe cleanly. Nvidia gave back roughly 3.4 percent on Monday and closed near flat Tuesday even as AMD picked up 2 percent on Anthropic's confirmed MI450 deployment. The story markets are pricing is that the pacing coalition will slow demand faster than the tokens-per-megawatt curve can compensate. Buck's keynote is Nvidia's response.

Reproduction paper finds Orthrus "lossless" decoding matches only 45 percent at BF16.

arXiv · 2026-09-15 · Source ↗

Koziev, Sinev and Oseledets ran 1,190 prompts across 12 domains through Orthrus at both precisions. Only 45 percent produced exact trajectory matches at BF16 versus 100 percent at FP32, calling into question a widely cited local-inference economics claim.

"How Lossless Is Lossless Speculative Decoding? The Role of Numerical Precision in Orthrus" (arXiv:2609.15504, submitted Sept 14 late-UTC) landed on Hugging Face Papers of the Day Sept 15 with an argument that could reshape the local-inference economics narrative. Across 1,190 prompts drawn from 12 domains, the authors report that under BF16 inference exact trajectory matching to the reference autoregressive path occurs in only 45 percent of prompts for the Orthrus authors' own checkpoint and 43 percent for an independently trained variant, while under FP32 the match rate is 100 percent.

Trajectory divergence at BF16 correlates with conditional perplexity of the reference model but does not systematically degrade downstream benchmark performance, meaning Orthrus behaves as a well-performing speculative decoder without behaving as a strictly lossless one at the precision most vLLM, SGLang, and TensorRT-LLM deployments use by default. The paper argues that evaluations of "lossless" LLM acceleration should specify both the operational criterion for equivalence and the numerical precision at which it is measured, a methodological point with immediate consequences for the local-inference economics case that Orthrus helped anchor over the summer.

"The practical losslessness of Orthrus depends on numerical precision and exact trajectory equivalence should be evaluated separately from downstream task performance." — Koziev, Sinev, Oseledets, headline conclusion

arXiv · 2026-09-15 · Source ↗

AetherLabs-AI's training-free multi-agent framework with autonomous memory construction outperforms frontier closed-source models including GPT-6 on OSWorld-v2 and Agent's Last Exam using only open-weight backbones.

RSIAgent: Autonomous Exploration for Recursive Self-improvement in New Environments (arXiv:2609.15364) landed on the Sept 15 Hugging Face board at 151 upvotes, submitted Sept 14 by Sibo Zhu, Shicheng Fan, Xinyue Wang, Wenyi Wu, Kun Zhou and Biwei Huang of AetherLabs-AI. The 49-page paper introduces a training-free multi-agent framework for recursive self-improvement by autonomous memory construction. Multiple agent roles coordinate to explore a new environment, validate outcomes against ground truth or execution results, and retain environment-specific knowledge including causal relationships between actions and effects.

The framework uses a broad-then-deep exploration strategy: agents fan out to enumerate the environment's affordances, then drill down into promising branches once initial validation lands. The headline result is that the RSIAgent scaffold, running open-source Kimi K3 and GLM-5.3, outperforms frontier closed-source models including GPT-6 on OSWorld-v2 and Agent's Last Exam tasks, closing a gap that had persisted since OSWorld-v2 launched.
