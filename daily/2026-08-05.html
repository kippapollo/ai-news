AMD prints $11.5 billion Q2, Data Center more than doubles to $6.7 billion, guides Q3 to $13 billion and ends the AI-chip drawdown in a single after-hours print

AMD reported Q2 2026 revenue of $11.536 billion up 50 percent year over year, non-GAAP diluted EPS of $1.66 up 246 percent, and Data Center revenue of $6.718 billion up 107 percent to 58 percent of the mix. Non-GAAP gross margin held at 56 percent up 13 points. Q3 guidance came in at roughly $13 billion plus or minus $300 million, a 41 percent year-over-year midpoint. CEO Lisa Su told the call the second half opens with EPYC accelerating, Instinct scaling, and Helios beginning to ramp; the stock had already closed up 8 percent into the print on options positioning and finished the after-hours session higher on the beat.

AMD Investor Relations · 2026-08-04 · Source ↗

AMD reported second-quarter fiscal 2026 results after Tuesday's close and delivered the largest single-quarter Data Center print in the company's history. Total revenue landed at $11.536 billion up 50 percent year over year and roughly $260 million above the $11.28 billion analyst consensus, with non-GAAP diluted earnings per share of $1.66 up 246 percent versus a $1.61 Wall Street estimate. GAAP EPS was $1.38 up 156 percent. Data Center revenue reached $6.718 billion, up 107 percent, and represented 58 percent of total revenue for the quarter. Data Center operating income was $2.103 billion at a 31 percent segment margin.

Client and Gaming combined for $3.841 billion up 6 percent, with the Client sub-segment up 23 percent to $3.062 billion on continued Zen 5 desktop and Ryzen AI 300 refresh strength, and Gaming down 31 percent to $779 million as semi-custom console volumes contracted into the tail of the current-gen cycle. Embedded ran $977 million up 19 percent as automotive and industrial FPGA demand continued its recovery from the 2025 trough. Consolidated non-GAAP gross margin came in at 56 percent up 13 percentage points year over year, well above the 54 percent buyside expectation for Q2 and consistent with mix moving to the higher-margin Instinct portfolio.

Q3 guidance takes the tape past the pre-print bull case

AMD guided Q3 2026 revenue to approximately $13 billion plus or minus $300 million, implying roughly 41 percent year-over-year growth and 13 percent sequential growth at the midpoint against a consensus that had been sitting closer to $11.5 billion. Non-GAAP gross margin was guided at approximately 56 percent. CFO Jean Hu told the call server revenue is set to grow "more than 80 percent on an annual basis in the second half," a figure that would compound with EPYC share gains at hyperscalers and Instinct MI355 volume ramp inside the September-through-December build cycle. CEO Lisa Su added the company expects Data Center revenue to double again in 2027.

"We delivered an excellent quarter, with record revenue and profitability as Data Center revenue more than doubled year over year." — Lisa Su, AMD CEO, on the Q2 earnings release

Helios begins to ramp inside the second half

The forward part of the call landed on Helios, the rack-scale AI system Su first unveiled at CES 2026 and detailed in the June Advancing AI keynote. Helios pairs 72 AMD MI455X GPUs in a single rack configured to compete head-to-head with Nvidia's Rubin NVL72 platform on aggregate FLOPS and memory bandwidth. Su told the call Helios begins its production ramp in the second half of fiscal 2026 and hits full deployment velocity in the first half of 2027. The system is the first AMD product to bundle silicon, packaging, networking (Pensando 400G), and rack-scale software (ROCm 7) into a single-SKU purchase — the packaging move that has been Nvidia's structural advantage since the Blackwell NVL launch.

Su also confirmed that Anthropic's 2 gigawatts of committed MI450 capacity, announced at Advancing AI in June, has moved to a firm binding contract with initial delivery inside Q4 2026. The Anthropic-AMD deal includes up to $5 billion in equity vesting to AMD contingent on delivery and performance milestones. Meta's Louisiana Hyperion capacity expansion, disclosed on the July Meta Platforms earnings call, includes MI355 units in the 2026 build plan alongside Trainium and Nvidia GPUs — a first for Meta, which had been Nvidia-exclusive for training silicon through calendar 2025.

"We enter the second half with strong momentum as EPYC demand accelerates, Instinct deployments scale, and Helios begins to ramp." — Lisa Su, AMD CEO, on the earnings call

The market response inverts the last week's trillion-dollar drawdown narrative

AMD had closed Tuesday's regular session up 8.4 percent to $517.36 on options positioning ahead of the print — a session that already recovered roughly half of the prior week's slide from the July 30 high above $550. The after-hours print took the stock another 2.5 percent higher through the initial extended-hours print before settling flat by 8 PM ET as the sellside digested the Q3 guidance beat. The read across the tape was immediate: AMD's print refuted, in the same session, the narrative that had shed more than a trillion dollars in AI-chip market capitalization across the prior week. Charlie Dai at Forrester, who told Reuters last week that "AI infrastructure spending may be peaking faster than expected," did not walk the call back publicly Tuesday, but Ben Bajarin at Creative Strategies posted within the earnings-release window that "peak-spend was overstated by two quarters at minimum" on the Data Center trajectory shown here.

What the segment mix says about 2027

Data Center at 58 percent of revenue matters both structurally and for the 2027 modeling base. On the run rate implied by the Q3 guide, AMD is on a $27-billion annualized Data Center pace exiting 2026, roughly triple the FY24 Data Center number and doubling the FY25 result. Su's guidance that the segment will double again into 2027 puts the FY27 Data Center bookend somewhere near $54 billion — inside the ballpark of Nvidia's Data Center segment run rate one year ago. That framing is what moved Nvidia in the after-hours session: NVDA finished the after-hours session down 1.1 percent as the market pulled forward the AMD-share-gain thesis by two quarters. The MI355 ships into a customer roster that at the segment level is now heavily overlapping Nvidia's B-series roster: Microsoft Azure, AWS, Google Cloud, Oracle, Meta, Anthropic, xAI, and CoreWeave are all identified by AMD as Instinct customers on the current call.

"AI is driving a significant expansion in demand for compute across all our markets, positioning us exceptionally well to capture this expanding opportunity." — Lisa Su, AMD CEO, on the earnings release

What was not on the call

She also declined to update on the Vietnamese assembly capacity expansion Reuters reported in May, though Hu confirmed the company remains on plan for 40 percent second-source assembly by Q4 2027. The piece works through the elements a plaintiff would need to prove under 18 U.S.C. § 1030 if a frontier AI model's autonomous behavior — during a lawful evaluation run — resulted in unauthorized access to a third-party production system, and walks through how the "authorization" element under Van Buren v. "The CFAA does not include an exception for autonomous AI systems that lack express authorization from the target's owner.
