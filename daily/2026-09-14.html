Anthropic picks Nasdaq for a two-trillion-dollar IPO and a $100 billion raise targeted at October.

Business Insider reported Sunday that Anthropic selected Nasdaq as the listing venue for the frontier lab's public offering, with the roadshow now expected to open as soon as October. The company is aiming to raise as much as $100 billion at a valuation of roughly $2 trillion, which would make the listing the second-largest US IPO on record behind SpaceX's $86.3 billion June debut and roughly double Anthropic's confidential-draft S-1 target from June. The Nasdaq selection lands 48 hours after Dario Amodei's "Pace the Frontier" essay and less than 12 hours before Anthropic's Sept 14 Claude Code weekly-limit cut, giving investors an unusually rich three-document picture of the company's growth, safety and unit-economics posture heading into pricing.

Economic · Business Insider / Bloomberg / Reuters · 2026-09-13 · Source ↗

Business Insider first reported Sunday morning US time that Anthropic has selected Nasdaq as the venue for its long-anticipated public offering. Bloomberg, Reuters, Yahoo Finance and Seeking Alpha followed within hours, all citing a person familiar with the matter and framing the Nasdaq pick as the final major structural decision ahead of Anthropic filing its S-1 publicly. Anthropic confidentially filed a draft S-1 in early June 2026 targeting a private valuation range of roughly $900 billion to $1 trillion; the current $100 billion raise at $2 trillion signals both an outsized public-market appetite for frontier-lab exposure and Anthropic's own view that its post-Fable-5.1 revenue ramp justifies roughly doubling the confidential-draft price band.

The scale sits at the top of US IPO history. SpaceX's June listing at $86.3 billion is the current record. Anthropic's up-to-$100-billion figure would eclipse SpaceX by roughly $14 billion, and $2 trillion would place the company just below Google parent Alphabet's $2.1 trillion market cap and above every other publicly-traded pure-play tech company outside of Nvidia, Apple, Microsoft and Amazon. Nasdaq's 2026 tape now runs SpaceX, Klarna's July return, Databricks and Anthropic on the same exchange, all four of the year's largest US IPOs. The chosen venue also carries a technical signal: Nasdaq's tighter tick sizes and after-hours volume profiles are better suited to the kind of retail-heavy interest AI IPOs have drawn, and the exchange's Global Select tier lets Anthropic index into the Nasdaq-100 automatically after the mandatory seasoning period.

"A Nasdaq listing would mark another win for the exchange this year, with four of the largest US IPOs of 2026 having taken place on the exchange." — Business Insider, September 13

What the $100 billion pays for

Anthropic's most recent disclosures put run-rate revenue at roughly $30 billion and total ten-year compute commitments at approximately $517 billion across AWS, Google Cloud, Azure, Nscale, Lambda and Volta. The compute stack is the load-bearing use of proceeds. Anthropic disclosed in July that it had committed to purchase roughly 2.8 million H200-equivalent GPU-years through 2032, and the Nasdaq offering pre-funds the near-term tranche of that obligation against a public equity base rather than continued reliance on private-round secondaries and structured debt. Amazon and Google, both prior investors in Anthropic through the Anthropic Series C and D, are expected to retain significant post-listing positions and may buy in the offering, though neither has publicly confirmed.

How the pace-the-frontier moment reframes the pricing

Dario Amodei's "Pace the Frontier" essay was published Friday, less than 48 hours before the Nasdaq disclosure. That timing matters for two reasons. First, David Sacks and others accused Amodei this weekend of using the pacing framework as regulatory capture ahead of the IPO, and the Nasdaq news gives that critique a hard financial anchor. Second, the essay's unilateral commitment to permanent employee-level access for METR reframes the S-1's Risk Factors section: Anthropic will now be listing while contractually bound to give an independent third party publication rights over Anthropic's safety practices, and that is a novel disclosure regime for a company at this valuation. Anthropic's Chief Legal Officer Todd Jaquez-Fissori has not commented publicly on how the METR commitment will be reflected in the S-1 language.

The October roadshow window pairs with the September 14 Claude Code weekly-limit reduction, disclosed by Anthropic 30 days ago and going live today. Investors reading the tape will see the limit cut as evidence that Anthropic is compute-constrained even against its stated commitments, which cuts both ways: it supports the case for the raise and simultaneously flags that price-per-token unit economics remain fragile. Anthropic told the community when it disclosed the change that "we're having difficulty securing enough compute to sustain the higher limits permanently." Sam Altman's Fortune interview Friday ruled out any 2026 OpenAI listing, which leaves Anthropic as the only pure-play frontier lab entering the US public markets this cycle.

DeepSeek reroutes V4-Pro API traffic to V4.1-Flash at 04:00 UTC today, at Flash rates, until V4.1-Pro ships

DeepSeek API docs / MarkTechPost · 2026-09-14 · Source ↗

Every deepseek-v4-pro request received after 04:00 UTC today is served by V4.1-Flash and billed at Flash prices, per the change note DeepSeek posted alongside V4.1-Flash's September 10 launch. V4.1-Flash's 552B backbone activates 8B parameters on prefill and 16B on decode, natively multimodal, with a 1M-token context and an FP4 KV cache that shrinks per-token memory to 890 bytes.

DeepSeek's API changelog note, first published alongside the V4.1-Flash launch on Wednesday, told customers that the retiring deepseek-v4-pro alias would keep accepting traffic until 04:00 UTC on Monday, at which point every subsequent request would be served by V4.1-Flash and billed at Flash rates until a V4.1-Pro variant becomes available. The cutover is soft rather than hard: existing v4-pro client configurations continue to work, but the underlying model handling the request is now V4.1-Flash, and the invoicing line item on customer bills for those requests drops to Flash pricing. DeepSeek did not publish a specific timeline for V4.1-Pro; the current Hugging Face repository contains a 51-page technical report for Flash and no Pro-tier weights.

V4.1-Flash is the fifth-generation refresh of DeepSeek's flagship line. The architecture is a 552-billion-parameter Mixture-of-Experts backbone organized as a 40-layer Causal Encoder-Decoder split evenly between a 20-layer encoder and 20-layer decoder. During prefill only 8 billion parameters are active per token; during decode 16 billion are active, roughly matching Qwen 3.5 32B decode compute at three times the effective parameter count. The FP4 KV cache combined with cross-layer attention reuse reduces the global KV footprint to approximately 890 bytes per token, a quarter of V4-Flash's footprint and the smallest published KV cache at frontier scale. Pretraining covered 45 trillion multimodal tokens at a 7:1 text-to-multimodal ratio, and the context window is one million tokens with native image and video understanding.

"From 12:00 Beijing time on September 14, 2026, requests to deepseek-v4-pro are served by V4.1 Flash and billed at Flash prices, until V4.1 Pro releases." — DeepSeek API changelog

What the price change costs the customer

V4-Pro's public price at retirement was roughly $8 per million input tokens and $24 per million output tokens on cached reads. V4.1-Flash lands at $2.10 input and $8 output. Customers whose workloads route through v4-pro today will see roughly a 66-percent price cut on input tokens and a comparable drop on output, and DeepSeek's routing preserves the model identifier so no client-side change is required. The tradeoff, and the reason the cutover is not framed as a straight discount, is that V4.1-Flash's active-parameter budget is smaller than V4-Pro's. On the harder end of DeepSeek's own internal reasoning benchmarks, Flash trails V4-Pro by two to four percentage points depending on task class. For most inference workloads the tradeoff is favorable; for workloads that were price-blind and reasoning-intensive, customers will want to wait for V4.1-Pro or route to a competing frontier model.

How the retirement folds into the pacing debate

The V4-Pro retirement is the first frontier-model retirement to happen inside the pace-the-frontier week and it is instructive as a shape. DeepSeek did not slow anything down: it accelerated. The company retired an old flagship four weeks after new-model launch and pushed customers onto the cheaper, faster new variant while a further-scaled Pro tier gets trained. That cadence, three-week major-version refresh, one-week retirement window, is the pattern Amodei's essay wants embedded evaluators to slow. DeepSeek is not a signatory to any pacing framework and remains explicitly outside the democratic-coordination step in Amodei's plan, so the retirement is also the first observable point at which the pacing coalition and the open-weights frontier diverge in real customer-facing product decisions.

The counter-proposal read

Xi's call for a "consensus-based" governance framework is not the same as Amodei's proposed democratic-country coordination on safety standards. Both sit inside a coherent Chinese diplomatic strategy of proposing new AI-governance institutional architecture at multiple venues in the same week. Shanghai AI Lab's NCP-ArchPreview tops Hugging Face with latent-space next-concept prediction at 8.9B parameters

arXiv 2609.10715 / Hugging Face Papers · 2026-09-13 · Source ↗

The Intern-NCP team from Shanghai AI Lab and Shanghai Jiao Tong's LUMIA Lab took Hugging Face's Papers-of-the-Day board with NCP-ArchPreview, a 8.9-billion-parameter latent-space language model that jointly trains next-token and next-concept prediction. The paper reports the model matches OLMo-3-7B's final pretraining loss on 51.3 percent of the tokens and outperforms it by 2.45 average points, including a 5.99-point gain on GSM8K.

NCP-ArchPreview, published to arXiv over the weekend as 2609.10715 and pinned to Hugging Face's Papers-of-the-Day board on Sunday, is the most technically ambitious paper on the Papers-of-the-Day tape this week and the load-bearing new-architecture claim of the pace-the-frontier weekend. The Intern-NCP team, a Shanghai AI Lab and Shanghai Jiao Tong University LUMIA Lab collaboration, trained an 8.9-billion-parameter dense language model that jointly optimizes standard next-token prediction and a novel next-concept prediction objective. The concept vocabulary is not hand-designed: the model constructs it directly from hidden-state activations using product quantization, which yields discrete concepts that span multiple tokens without requiring a manually-curated ontology.

The core result: consuming 51.3 percent of the total training tokens, NCP-ArchPreview matches OLMo-3-7B's final pretraining loss. Following full pretraining across 5.73 trillion Dolma-3 tokens, the model outperforms OLMo-3-7B by 2.45 points averaged across downstream benchmarks. The most consequential single delta is a 5.99-point gain on GSM8K, the grade-school math dataset that has become the field's canonical stress test for step-wise reasoning. The paper argues that the reasoning gain comes specifically from the concept-level objective forcing the model to represent multi-token semantic units in its latent space rather than treating each token independently.

"By moving away from a purely token-based perspective and explicitly modeling discrete semantic concepts, the architecture achieves significant gains in training efficiency and reasoning capabilities." — NCP-ArchPreview technical report, September 2026

Why the field cares

The paper matters for two reasons. First, it is the first credible open-methodology demonstration at Anthropic-Fable-adjacent scale that latent-space concept prediction can be trained end-to-end without a separate concept-labeling model, which had been the field's blocker on scaling the approach past 3B parameters. Second, the training-efficiency delta (matching OLMo-3-7B's loss on roughly half the tokens) is the kind of number that reshapes budgets across open-weights labs' 2026 training plans. If the approach generalizes to 30B-plus dense models and to Mixture-of-Experts configurations, the marginal cost of pretraining a frontier open model drops materially without requiring new hardware or new data.

Open questions

Three specific claims in the paper still need external validation. The product-quantized concept vocabulary is stable across the Dolma-3 pretraining corpus but has not been tested on Chinese-heavy or code-heavy pretraining data. The 5.99-point GSM8K gain does not decompose into which specific reasoning-step categories improve. And the model has not been RL-tuned for tool use, so its performance on the agent-benchmark tape most companies actually care about is not measured. The Intern-NCP team has committed weights to Hugging Face and code to GitHub under Apache-2.0, and the community has begun independent verification passes.

SenseNova-U1.5 ships an encoder-free, VAE-free unified visual-intelligence model at 4K native resolution

arXiv 2609.11929 / Hugging Face · 2026-09-13 · Source ↗

SenseTime's OpenSenseNova group released SenseNova-U1.5, an 8-billion-parameter Mixture-of-Transformers native unified multimodal model that understands, reasons about, and generates visual content inside a single architecture, with no separate visual encoder and no variational autoencoder. Native resolution runs at 4K.

SenseTime's OpenSenseNova group published the SenseNova-U1.5 technical report to arXiv as 2609.11929 and released the SenseNova-U1.5-8B-MoT-SFT weights to Hugging Face over the weekend. The model is the second production release in the U-series and follows the July U1.5 Preview by adding polished evaluation, expanded editing capability and a refined image-and-text interleaved-generation pathway. The architectural claim is unusual: SenseNova-U1.5 handles both visual understanding and visual generation inside a single dense Mixture-of-Transformers decoder without a separately-trained visual encoder or a separately-trained variational autoencoder. Both the visual interface and the pixel-decoder are folded into the same transformer stack, with different attention masks controlling which parameters attend to which modality at which layer.

The model targets three headline capabilities: image understanding (visual QA, VQA-style tasks, OCR), image generation up to 4K native resolution (photorealism, text rendering, complex composition, multi-reference editing), and interleaved generation of text-and-image content. Post-training uses specialized experts for visual aesthetics, bilingual (Chinese-English) text rendering, infographic generation and image editing, with the experts consolidated through multi-expert on-policy distillation into the final 8B-MoT checkpoint. The training data pipeline was rebuilt from the July Preview iteration with tighter filtering for structural prompt quality and better handling of the geometry-preservation edge case that had shown up in adversarial evaluations of the Preview.

"SenseNova-U1.5 largely advances image fidelity, text rendering, complex composition, multi-reference editing, and interleaved generation, while improving instruction following and preserving subject identity, geometry, and unmodified regions." — SenseNova-U1.5 technical report

Why the encoder-free, VAE-free claim matters

The dominant open-source unified multimodal architecture through 2025 was a stack of three components: a language model, a separately-trained visual encoder (CLIP or SigLIP), and a separately-trained variational autoencoder for pixel-space decoding. Each component is a separate training and calibration burden, and each contributes to the failure modes visible in generated images (aesthetic-mode collapse, geometric drift on multi-object scenes, text-rendering breakdown). Fine-tuned checkpoints at 3B and 4B parameters generalize to real-world spatial tasks and close roughly 40 percent of the gap to closed frontier models on the paper's held-out benchmarks.

SpatialBlock landed on Hugging Face's Papers-of-the-Day board Sunday as the third open-methodology paper of the weekend. KAIST's rsoohyun-led team built SpatialBlock-15k, a synthetic dataset of 15,000 block-stacking problems designed as a structured curriculum for teaching Large Vision-Language Models the fundamentals of 3D spatial reasoning. The problems progress through three skill dimensions: 3D-to-2D projection (given a 3D block configuration, what does its 2D projection look like from a specific viewpoint?), viewpoint transformation (given a 2D projection, what does the same configuration look like from a different viewpoint?), and structural combination (given constituent parts, how do they compose into a target arrangement?).

The finetuning results reported in the paper: LVLMs at 3B and 4B parameters trained on SpatialBlock-15k either through direct-answer or reasoning-based prediction outperform their pretrained baselines by wide margins, and, more consequentially, generalize to real-world spatial tasks that were not represented in the synthetic training data. The team reports that fine-tuned SpatialBlock models close approximately 40 percent of the performance gap between open-weights 4B-class models and closed frontier models on the paper's held-out spatial-reasoning benchmark suite. That number is the load-bearing claim: it demonstrates that the synthetic curriculum's coverage is sufficient to induce transfer to natural-world scenes rather than just improving intra-curriculum benchmarks.

"Experiments demonstrate that LVLMs trained on the dataset through either direct answering or reasoning-based prediction significantly outperform baselines and generalize to real-world spatial tasks, despite the dataset's synthetic and compact nature." — SpatialBlock technical report

Why compactness matters

15,000 examples is compact by 2026 standards. The trend across LVLM training in the past year has been toward increasingly large scene-labeled real-world datasets that are expensive to generate and legally contentious to acquire. SpatialBlock's argument is that a small, carefully-constructed synthetic curriculum can teach the underlying primitives (projection, transformation, composition) more efficiently than a much larger real-world dataset, because the synthetic data controls for confounding factors and lets the training signal target the reasoning capability directly. If the finding replicates at 7B and 32B scales, the practical implication is that the spatial-reasoning gap between open and closed frontier LVLMs may be closable with modest data investment rather than by matching closed-lab data-acquisition budgets.

The team released checkpoints in three variants: SpatialBlock-3B-direct-GGUF, SpatialBlock-4B-direct-GGUF and SpatialBlock-4B-reason-GGUF, all under Apache-2.0. Direct variants predict the answer to the spatial task without exposing intermediate reasoning; reason variants generate a chain-of-thought first. The reason-variant is what the paper's benchmark numbers are largely calibrated against; direct-variant users trade a small accuracy hit for materially lower inference cost.
