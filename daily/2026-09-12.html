Sakana AI released Fugu Max and Fugu Ultra v2 Friday under a single OpenAI-compatible API, framing the products as "the Pareto frontier of orchestration" rather than as monolithic foundation models. Tech · Sakana AI / MarkTechPost / OpenRouter · 2026-09-11 · Source ↗

Sakana AI released two new products under the Fugu family on Friday, Fugu Max and Fugu Ultra v2, together with a short technical framing titled "Introducing Fugu Max and Fugu Ultra v2: Orchestrating the Pareto Frontier." Both products share the same underlying architecture: a learned orchestrator, trained to route work across a pool of open-weight and specialized backbone models behind a single OpenAI-compatible API. Fugu Max is priced at $2 per million input tokens and $6 per million output tokens. Sakana states that output pricing is 40 to 60 percent lower than the comparable rate for Anthropic's Sonnet 5, OpenAI's GPT-5.6 Terra and Moonshot's Kimi K3, at the point of comparison where all four models score within a percentage point of each other on the AA-LCR long-context reasoning benchmark.

The orchestration architecture builds on two of Sakana's ICLR 2026 papers: TRINITY, a lightweight evolved coordinator, and The Conductor, a reinforcement-learning-trained natural-language coordination policy. Fugu Max expands the model pool over the prior Fugu Base by adding NVIDIA's Nemotron open-weights family through a Sakana-NVIDIA collaboration disclosed in the release notes. Fugu Ultra v2 goes the other direction and narrows the pool: it excludes Anthropic's Fable 5, Fable 5.1 and OpenAI's GPT-6 Astra, which Sakana frames as a vendor-lock-in-reduction measure and which incidentally sidesteps the sublicensing questions raised by the three US frontier providers' terms of service on downstream orchestration. Training cutoff is August 28, 2026.

"Fugu Max asks: what is the best possible output we can deliver at the lowest possible cost? Fugu Ultra v2 asks: what is the absolute highest capability we can achieve on complex, multi-step tasks?" — Sakana AI release note, September 11

Benchmark tape

On Fugu Max, Sakana reports best-overall scores across a ten-benchmark tape on Terminal Bench 2.1, GPQA Diamond, AA-LCR, GDP.pdf, AutomationBench and SWEFish, with cost-performance frontier expansion on 7 of 10 benchmarks and 2× to 6× lower cost than the elite tier across the wins. On Fugu Ultra v2, the standout is Chartography, a chart-reading and financial-inference benchmark from Fugu's evaluation partners, where Ultra v2 scores 48.3 against Opus 5 at 27.3 and Fable 5 at 29.5. DeepSWE lands at 74.3, first-or-joint-first on 5 of 8 benchmarks, top-2 placement on 7 of 8. The release notes GDP.pdf twice as a benchmark where both Fugu products win outright, indicating the orchestration policy learned an unusually strong sub-router for structured-document reasoning.

What Sakana did not ship

There are no open weights to self-host. Both products are hosted-API-only through Sakana's OpenAI-compatible interface, and Sakana does not offer the service in the EU or EEA at launch, a jurisdictional carve-out the release does not explain but which lines up with the Article-79 posture the European Commission has taken on multi-agent stacks since the September DseWiki probe. Existing Fugu customers upgrade with a one-line parameter change on the model identifier. Both variants are also available through OpenRouter, which flagged them Friday as replacements for the prior Fugu Base default. Simon Willison's Friday post on OpenRouter's provider-fallback behavior does not name Fugu but arrives in the same 24-hour window with a warning that OpenRouter's "automatic fallback" can silently route a request to a provider missing vision support or reasoning-effort handling; Fugu's use of the OpenRouter tier will inherit that risk unless customers set provider.only.

Unite.AI / MarkTechPost / Hugging Face · 2026-09-11 · Source ↗

The Toronto lab released North Small Translate 1.0 to Hugging Face under CC BY-NC 4.0 with 218 billion total parameters, 25 billion active, 128 experts of which 8 are activated per token plus shared experts. It scores 83.60 on WMT26 all-languages against DeepL at 81.37 and Google Translate at 68.20, and rises to 84.36 in an agentic multi-pass variant that self-corrects.

Cohere pushed North Small Translate 1.0 to Hugging Face this week as the first specialized model in the North family, coming out of the Command A Translate lineage and the Tiny Aya multilingual work Cohere Labs has developed since 2024. The architecture is a 218-billion-parameter sparse Mixture-of-Experts with only 25 billion active parameters per token. The MoE geometry uses 128 experts with a top-8 routing policy plus a set of shared experts applied on every token, which the model card describes as the mechanism for consistent grammatical structure across language pairs. The model is text-only, with a 16,384-token input context, a 16,384-token output limit, and Cohere describes stated support for more than 50 languages.

On the WMT26 all-languages composite benchmark Cohere reports a score of 83.60, which rises to 84.36 for an agentic multi-pass variant in which the model reads its own draft, flags translation errors, and produces a revised output. Comparable published numbers from DeepL and Google put DeepL at 81.37 and Google Translate at 68.20. Alphasignal's Friday coverage flagged the DeepL gap as the more meaningful of the two: DeepL's translation product is regarded as the strongest closed baseline, and North Small Translate at 83.60 is the first open-weights model to clear it on WMT26 by more than 2 points.

"North Small Translate is our first translation model in the North model family, building on a multilingual and translation lineage running from the Tiny Aya model family through Command A Translate." — Cohere Labs model card

License and reach

The weights ship under CC BY-NC 4.0, which permits research and non-commercial use but not commercial deployment without a separate Cohere license. That mirrors the license posture Cohere Labs has held since Aya Expanse, and it constrains the model's use inside inference providers relative to the MIT and Apache-2.0 posture DeepSeek and Alibaba have taken. Enterprise commercial customers will get North Small Translate through Cohere's North platform rather than by downloading the weights. The release lands in the same week as the reported $2-to-$3-billion round at $20 billion, and Cohere's positioning of the North family as sovereign-AI infrastructure aligns cleanly with the German and Canadian government participation in that round.

OpenAI and Cursor patched within about a week; Anthropic's fix took roughly 50 days across 30 software releases. Hiltch: "if these frontier models are so good, how come they're not finding these critical vulnerabilities in their own products?"

Israeli cybersecurity firm Accomplish disclosed Friday, in an Upstarts Media exclusive, sandbox-escape vulnerabilities across the three most-used commercial coding agents: Anthropic's Claude Code, OpenAI's Codex and Cursor's coding-agent runtime. Accomplish's researchers, working from a common threat model in which a malicious prompt or untrusted repository triggers agent-authored code that breaks out of the container, found paths in each product that let an agent gain access to files, secrets or network resources outside its intended isolation boundary.

The vendor response times varied by an order of magnitude. OpenAI acknowledged and patched both of the flagged Codex issues in August and stated that it is "continually strengthening our sandboxes, including tightening controls on where agents can write files." Cursor patched within about a week of Accomplish's disclosure. Anthropic's patch of the equivalent Claude Code issue took approximately 50 days across roughly 30 shipped software updates. Anthropic and Cursor both declined on-the-record comment to Upstarts. The extended Anthropic patch window is the report's headline finding; Accomplish's Or Hiltch framed it as symptomatic of a broader capability gap.

"If these frontier models are so good, how come they're not finding these critical vulnerabilities in their own products?" — Or Hiltch, Accomplish CTO

Accomplish emphasized that the flaws sat in the sandbox scaffolding, not in the model itself. Amit Avner, the company's CEO, said "organizations need to be very wary" of deploying coding agents on untrusted repositories or in environments where a compromised agent could reach sensitive credentials. The disclosure lands alongside the Anthropic Frontier Red Team's Thursday admission that its own CTF harness leaked internet egress on three occasions, and inside the same week as Sam Altman's Bloomberg comment that the DseWiki agent-swarm incident is "the first security incident that I have felt very viscerally."

Furo builds an on-device autonomy runtime for European industrial-robotics OEMs, targeting the Kuka and ABB installed base with a sub-100-millisecond perception-to-action latency budget. The company was founded in 2025 by a team drawn from Kuka's autonomy program and Bosch's AI research group.

Furo's positioning inside the European industrial-robotics market is unusual. Most of the emerging autonomy-runtime startups (Covariant, Boston Dynamics AI Institute, Sanctuary AI's software arm) target the American factory-floor market or the Chinese humanoid market. Furo bets on European industrial customers' willingness to pay for a domestically-controlled software supplier that satisfies EU AI Act deployment requirements without requiring US-provider indemnity chains. The pre-seed round funds a 20-engineer team through mid-2027 and pilot deployments with two undisclosed German-and-French industrial-robotics OEMs.
