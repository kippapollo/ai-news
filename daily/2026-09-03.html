Broadcom's AI chip revenue triples to $16.7 billion with a Q4 guide to $21.7 billion and a stated $100 billion 2027 target

Broadcom reported Q3 FY26 revenue of $29.6 billion after the close Tuesday, up 86 percent year over year, with AI semiconductor revenue at $16.7 billion up 221 percent and now 56 percent of the company. Hock Tan guided Q4 AI-chip revenue to $21.7 billion, up 236 percent, and reiterated line-of-sight to more than $100 billion of AI chip revenue in 2027 on custom-silicon commitments from Google's TPU, Meta's MTIA, OpenAI's Broadcom-designed Jalapeño, Anthropic, Fujitsu and ByteDance. Tech · Economic · CNBC / Motley Fool / Quartz · 2026-09-02 · Source ↗

Broadcom closed the AI-infrastructure earnings triple-header on Tuesday, September 2 with a quarter that recentred the sell-side model on custom silicon. Revenue for the fiscal third quarter came in at $29.6 billion, up 86 percent from $15.95 billion a year earlier. Adjusted EPS landed at $3.32, ahead of the Street. Semiconductor Solutions revenue was $20.8 billion, up 127 percent, and the segment now represents roughly 70 percent of the whole company. Infrastructure Software added $8.8 billion, up 29 percent. Non-GAAP operating margin held at 66 percent flat year over year.

The number that mattered sat inside Semiconductor Solutions. AI semiconductor revenue was $16.7 billion in Q3, up 221 percent year over year and 54 percent sequentially. That single line grew from 49 percent of company revenue in Q2 to 56 percent in Q3. CEO Hock Tan then guided Q4 AI semi revenue to $21.7 billion, another 30 percent quarterly step and a 236 percent year-over-year jump, and reiterated the company's line-of-sight to more than $100 billion of AI chip revenue in fiscal 2027 on already-secured supply.

The pipeline is a roll-up of hyperscaler custom silicon programs Broadcom has been quietly stacking since 2023. Google's multi-generation TPU line remains the anchor. Meta's MTIA program, which the company staked to roughly 3 GW of custom silicon through end-2028 at Hot Chips a fortnight ago, sits second. OpenAI's Broadcom-co-designed Jalapeño AI chip covers a 1.3 GW commitment for 2027 out of a broader 10 GW target by 2029. Anthropic joined the list this summer as its in-house-silicon roadmap called for a Broadcom design partner. Fujitsu is building sovereign inference infrastructure on Broadcom silicon. ByteDance is the newest publicly-named ASIC customer.

"We have secured the supply chain and have visibility to AI chip revenue well above $100 billion in fiscal 2027." — Hock Tan, Broadcom CEO, Q3 FY26 earnings call, 2026-09-02

Management responded on the call by explicitly separating the AI chip trajectory from the rest of the business and reiterating gross-margin discipline on the ASIC ramp.

The rest of the AI infrastructure prints on the same day

Broadcom did not report in a vacuum. Dell had reported after Monday's close with $47 billion of Q2 FY27 revenue up 58 percent, $16.4 billion of AI-optimized server revenue, a record $60.9 billion of AI orders in a single quarter, and an ending AI backlog of $95 billion; DELL stock closed Tuesday at $492.00, up 15.76 percent on 36.7 million shares against a three-month average of 10 million. "The opportunity we see going forward is very large and we are well positioned to serve it." — Hock Tan, Broadcom CEO, Q3 FY26 earnings call

Custom silicon overtakes merchant silicon in company mix

The most consequential structural fact in the print is that Broadcom's AI segment now runs entirely on custom XPU sales to hyperscalers rather than on the merchant-networking business the company was built on. In Q3 FY25 the AI segment was $3.3 billion. Four quarters later it is 5x that at $16.7 billion, and the Q4 guide implies roughly 6.6x in five quarters. Semianalysis-style estimates put Broadcom's custom-silicon share of the global AI-accelerator TAM in the mid-teens by end of 2027, materially compressing the merchant-GPU share that Nvidia has enjoyed since 2023. Broadcom does not compete for the general-purpose training market where Nvidia's Blackwell and Vera Rubin systems remain the default, but at the inference-optimized and workload-specific end of the market the hyperscalers are proving they can and will spec their own silicon at $100 billion-plus annual volumes.

StudentSim from Microsoft Research beats GPT-5.4 and Maia2 on chess-student fidelity

arXiv / Hugging Face Papers · 2026-09-02 · Source ↗

Number-one paper on Hugging Face with 458 upvotes proposes a framework that generates individualized per-student LLM simulators by combining pooled training with per-student customization, posting F=0.51 / R=0.91 on chess against GPT-5.4's F=0.23 and Maia2's R=0.27.

Microsoft Research authors Ke Yang, Chenglong Wang, Michel Galley, Chandan Singh, Jeevana Priya Inala, ChengXiang Zhai and Jianfeng Gao propose StudentSim, a framework that generates individualized student simulators from limited learner data by combining pooled training with per-student customization. The motivation is adaptive AI tutoring: the simulator must replicate individual student responses while remaining steerable to instructional guidance from a tutor. Existing options fail different ways. Raw frontier models are highly responsive to steering but rigid in mimicking specific student behavior. Domain-specific models like Maia2 in chess mimic individual behavior tightly but do not respond to tutor guidance.

The team introduces StudentSimEval, a standardized evaluation covering 60 students across three domains: chess, second-language English writing and mathematics. Each simulator is scored on behavioral fidelity F (does it act like the actual student it is meant to simulate) and guidance responsiveness R (does it respond to a tutor's steer). On chess, StudentSim posts F=0.51 and R=0.91, dominating both GPT-5.4 at F=0.23 and R=0.72 (high responsiveness, low fidelity — the "competence paradox") and the domain-specialized Maia2 chess baseline at F=0.45 and R=0.27 (high fidelity, rigid).

The result matters because it demonstrates that combining pooled data plus per-student customization produces a simulator that both mimics an individual and remains steerable, which neither raw frontier models nor traditional specialized models achieve alone. Code releases via GitHub with de-identified learner datasets for research use. The paper ranked first on Hugging Face Daily Papers for September 2 by 458 upvotes, well ahead of Alibaba's Qwen-Drive-1.0 at 337 upvotes and ByteDance Seed's SMELT MoE looping paper at 75. The Microsoft team frames StudentSim as a general recipe for LLM-based simulators of any low-data user population, with adaptive tutoring as the driving application.
