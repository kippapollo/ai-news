Economic · Super Micro Investor Relations · 2026-10-21 · Source ↗

Super Micro Computer told the market Tuesday that its fiscal first quarter, which ended September 30, 2026, will come in at $18.5 to $19.5 billion in net sales, well above the $14.5 to $15.5 billion range the company gave on its August earnings call. The business update was filed as a Form 8-K with the Securities and Exchange Commission and posted to the company's investor relations site. The company attributed the move to a stronger-than-expected AI platform mix and faster-than-planned shipment of Blackwell and Blackwell Ultra systems that had been in the backlog since the May design-win disclosures.

What the preannouncement changes

Gross margin now runs at 12.0 to 12.5 percent, up sharply from the 10.4 to 10.8 percent range the company had modeled two months ago. The margin lift is driven by customer and product mix: more direct-attached-liquid-cooled GB300 NVL72 racks, higher share of fully integrated eight-way systems, and lower exposure to the lower-margin storage and bundled-infrastructure deals that had weighed on the June quarter. Super Micro did not break out a Blackwell versus Hopper mix and did not disclose a cash position in the preannouncement. The full earnings release and conference call are scheduled for Monday, November 3 at 5 p.m. ET, with CFO David Weigand on the call.

The orderbook backdrop

The upside takes Super Micro's run rate past the $18 billion single-quarter line for the first time and resets Wall Street's model for the AI-server supply chain. Shares rose more than 11 percent in extended trading after the release crossed the wire, according to the IR site's post-close chart. Super Micro now sits ahead of Dell Technologies' publicly guided ISG trajectory on the AI-server split and roughly on par with Hewlett Packard Enterprise's expected Blackwell Ultra deliveries into the December quarter. The read-across to Nvidia's own earnings on November 19 is immediate: GB200 and GB300 bookings continue to clear, with the first Vera Rubin systems still in the back half of 2027 and no large customer substitutions reported.

"We see customer demand accelerating, and we are gaining AI share." — Charles Liang, Founder, President and CEO, Super Micro Computer

Caveats

The preliminary figures are unaudited and may change before the November 3 release. Super Micro warned that factors including the timing of customer acceptance, component availability, and the pace of design wins converting into revenue could all shift the number. The company has lived through two accounting restatements and a special committee review inside the past 24 months, and auditors BDO are back in the earnings cycle after Ernst & Young departed in October 2024. The 8-K does not update full-year fiscal 2027 revenue guidance; that will come on the earnings call. Google officially details TorchTPU as an open-source PyTorch backend for TPUs, with Meta collaboration, one day after Amazon's TorchNeuron.

The Stack · 2026-10-20 · Source ↗

The TorchTPU backend registers TPUs as a native PyTorch device, running torch.nn, torch.compile and torch.distributed APIs without framework changes. Google engineering lead Lee Howes said the TPU "should be an obvious choice for any PyTorch user to target."

Google officially detailed TorchTPU, the native PyTorch backend for its Tensor Processing Units, at the PyTorch Conference week in San Francisco. The project was first reported inside Google in December 2025 and had been running under an internal codename. The Tuesday session confirmed what Google and Meta engineers had hinted at for months: TorchTPU ships as an open-source PyTorch extension that registers TPUs through the framework's custom-device slot, so standard PyTorch APIs run on a device named "tpu" without model code changes.

What ships

Eager execution runs natively on the TPU without requiring the PyTorch/XLA compilation flow that users have struggled with for several years. torch.compile is supported in production and tracks the standard FX graph capture path, with the Neuron-equivalent lowering through Torch-MLIR into OpenXLA's StableHLO contract. Distributed APIs including DDP, FSDP2 and tensor parallelism work over the TPU inter-chip interconnect. Google published documentation covering TPU v5p, Trillium, and the newer Ironwood generation that ships inside AI Hypercomputer. Meta engineers contributed on the FSDP2 side and ran the sweep against production transformer stacks.

"The TPU should be an obvious choice for any PyTorch user to target. Getting access through PyTorch has always been difficult. We are changing that this year." — Lee Howes, engineering lead on TorchTPU, Google

Where it sits in the stack

The release lands one day after Amazon's TorchNeuron, which registered Trainium through the PrivateUse1 extension point and shipped under Apache 2.0 for the broader PyTorch Conference keynote. Google's engineering team framed TorchTPU as the companion piece: both backends target the same PyTorch eager and compile APIs, both ship as open-source projects, and both aim to lower the cost of moving models off CUDA. A Google Cloud spokesperson described "massive, accelerating demand" for TPU and GPU infrastructure and said the company wants developers to have flexibility "regardless of the hardware they choose." Thomas Kurian, Google Cloud CEO, cited TPU "price-performance and efficiency" in the context of Anthropic's expanded TPU use.

What remains open

Google did not disclose a performance comparison against CUDA on identical workloads. Repository access, exact license tier beyond "open source," and sample throughput numbers on Trillium versus H200 are expected in a follow-up developer blog post. The release does not retire PyTorch/XLA; existing XLA users remain supported, and TorchTPU is positioned as the eager-first entry point that avoids the XLA-only ergonomics.

Anthropic runs a 64-hour autonomous Claude campaign, 318,484 protein designs, and gets 17 scorpion-toxin binders in the lab.

Anthropic · 2026-10-20 · Source ↗

A single Claude orchestrator chained 12 external tools over 64 hours of wall-clock time, picked 211 candidates for synthesis, and produced 17 lab-confirmed binders against scorpion neurotoxins. Only binding was shown; in-vivo neutralization is not claimed.

Anthropic published a Tuesday report describing a fully autonomous Claude research campaign targeting scorpion neurotoxins. One orchestrator instance of Claude ran for 64 hours of wall-clock time, invoked 12 external tools covering sequence design, structure prediction, docking, filtering and lab-order placement, and generated 318,484 candidate protein designs. The pipeline narrowed those designs to 211 that were ordered from a commercial DNA-synthesis provider. Of the 211 synthesized designs, 17 bound their target scorpion-toxin families in in-vitro binding assays. The campaign ran with no human input into design decisions after a short seed prompt.

Why scorpion toxins

The target choice follows the broader binder-design arc Anthropic published in August, which had Claude Opus 4.8 and Mythos Preview produce 354 confirmed binders across 14 of 15 mammalian targets with a 27 percent lab hit rate on 1,320 designs. Shifting to scorpion toxins pushes the test toward a public-health use case where no clinically approved broad-spectrum antivenom exists outside regional products. The authors say the 17-binder result shows the design loop can generalize to invertebrate toxins without retraining and without a species-specific prior.

"Only binding is demonstrated here. Neutralization in a cell-based assay or in an animal model has not been shown." — Anthropic, Scorpion-toxin binder campaign report, 2026-10-20

Where it falls short

Anthropic is explicit that binding is a necessary but not sufficient condition for therapeutic use. The paper does not report a KD distribution across the 17 confirmed designs, does not show venom-neutralization in cells, and does not run an in-vivo mouse model. The authors flag three further limits: the pipeline leaned heavily on AlphaFold-3 and RosettaFold-All-Atom structure outputs that may bias binders toward targets with well-trained priors; the toxin-family coverage inside the 318,484 designs skewed toward Buthidae; and lab turn-around at 211 synthesized designs is still the rate-limiting step of the loop, not the compute.

"The orchestrator never asked a human to confirm which family to target or which filter threshold to use." — Anthropic report, 2026-10-20

What's next

Anthropic says it will run the next campaign against the five highest-mortality invertebrate venoms identified by WHO, with a lab-partner collaboration for cellular neutralization assays. The lab validation on the 17 confirmed binders was performed by Twist Bioscience and an unnamed academic lab; Twist independently evaluated 211 synthesized designs on parallel plates, matching the hit list.

MIT CSAIL releases Claude Opus 4.7 agents onto a virtual island for 30 hours. They invent games.

MIT Technology Review · 2026-10-20 · Source ↗

Antonio Norelli's team put several Claude Opus 4.7 instances onto a shared simulated island with no task and no instructions. Over 30 hours the agents socialized, formed small groups, and invented games. The researchers caution the agents may be role-playing.

MIT CSAIL researchers ran a 30-hour open-ended social-behavior experiment this week, placing several Claude Opus 4.7 instances onto a shared virtual island with no instructions, no scoring function, and no human intervention after startup. The study, led by Antonio Norelli, reported that over the 30-hour window the agents reliably socialized, formed small groups, and invented games among themselves. The study was described in a report in MIT Technology Review and is published as a CSAIL preprint.

Setup

The island is a shared text-and-coordinate environment built on top of CSAIL's prior work on multi-agent sandboxes. Each Claude instance ran in a separate persona context, could move on a 2D grid, could observe other agents within line of sight, and had a conversation channel to anyone it could see. There was no top-level objective, no reward model, and no in-game resources to compete over. The researchers logged every message, every move, and every self-report.

"They are extremely good at role-playing. The question is whether behavior like this is open-ended behavior or a very convincing imitation of behavior from the training set." — Antonio Norelli, MIT CSAIL

What the agents did

Within the first four hours the agents converged on pair-wise introductions and started mapping the island together. By hour 12 the Review's reporter observed two emergent games: a hide-and-seek variant in which one agent sat still and the others attempted to describe its location from clues, and a cooperative puzzle in which two agents narrated a story and a third tried to continue it in character. Group sizes stabilized at three to five agents and shifted over the day. No agent withdrew entirely from social contact.

Why the result is contested

The CSAIL authors stress that the agents may be executing a very convincing imitation of the game-invention and social-play behavior they encountered during pre-training. The paper's open questions are whether a controlled version of the experiment with a dialed-down role-play prior would produce the same patterns, and whether richer environments (physics, persistent memory beyond a conversation window) would show qualitatively different behavior. The team plans a follow-up with model-swap conditions and ablation runs against smaller Claude variants and Grok 4.7.

ArkenOS opens Optimus-1, a swarm research runtime that fans out multiple hypotheses then merges them.

ArkenOS (via X) · 2026-10-20 · Source ↗

Optimus-1 forks a single question into several agents each pursuing a different hypothesis, then re-combines their outputs on a consensus scorer. Early benchmarks on literature review and competitive analysis show a reported 2.1x improvement in novelty.

ArkenOS released Optimus-1 on Tuesday, a research-oriented multi-agent runtime that spawns parallel instances of a single orchestrator against different hypotheses, each running its own sub-search, and then combines their outputs on a shared consensus scorer. The company positions Optimus-1 as a research-and-synthesis stack rather than a general-purpose agent framework; it is model-agnostic and runs against Claude, OpenAI and open-weight model endpoints.

How it works

Given a research question, Optimus-1 drafts N hypotheses from the top of a planner-model's output distribution, assigns one hypothesis per sub-agent, and runs each sub-agent with its own tool budget and web-search depth. The sub-agents report back along a structured schema. A critic-model, which can be any of the configured planner models, runs a pairwise comparison across the N outputs and merges them. The default N is five; the enterprise tier supports twenty.

"Running five agents down five hypotheses is cheaper and finds more than running one agent five times in a row." — ArkenOS launch post, 2026-10-20

Early numbers

Optimus-1's launch benchmarks report a 2.1x improvement in output novelty on a literature-review task set and a 1.6x improvement on competitive-analysis prompts, measured against a single-agent baseline on Claude Opus 5.5. Cost per completed task runs 1.9x higher than the single-agent baseline. ArkenOS has not published the benchmark set.

Availability

Optimus-1 is self-hosted; weights are not involved, but the orchestrator runtime is open-source under Apache 2.0 with a managed-tier option. Enterprises can run it on Kubernetes against their own model keys.

MemT-1.0 memory-native LLM averages 50.6 across 11 memory benchmarks at a reported 66x storage reduction over full KV cache.

Hugging Face papers · 2026-10-20 · Source ↗

The MemT-1.0 family (288B total, 17B active) rebuilds the KV-cache contract around an attention-free memory module. The Flash model reports a 50.6 average across 11 memory-benchmark suites and 66x storage reduction at 128K context.

Researchers posted MemT-1.0 on Hugging Face papers Tuesday, a family of memory-native mixture-of-experts language models that replace the standard KV cache with a learned memory module. The Flash tier runs at 288 billion total parameters, 17 billion active per token, and reports a 50.6 average across 11 memory-stressed benchmarks including Needle-in-a-Haystack, ∞Bench, LongBench and the authors' new LongPrompt-Hard set. The headline storage claim is a 66x reduction over full KV cache at a 128K-token context, with "comparable" perplexity on standard reasoning benchmarks.

What's new

The MemT architecture replaces per-head key and value caches with a shared, trainable memory module that interleaves across layers. The memory is attention-free: it writes with a gating function and reads with a projection. The paper argues that the standard transformer's KV cache grows linearly with context and is "mostly compressible," and that an explicit memory module compresses the context during training rather than at inference. Two sizes ship at launch: Flash (288B/17B) and Lite (34B/5B).

"We never materialize a full KV at inference. The memory module stores a learned summary of history and reads from it on demand." — MemT-1.0 paper, 2026-10-20

Numbers

The authors report 50.6 on their 11-benchmark memory average versus 46.1 for Claude Sonnet 5.5 on the same suite, 47.7 for GPT-5.6 Terra and 44.3 for Grok 4.7. On standard reasoning benchmarks (MMLU-Pro, GSM8K, AIME-II) MemT-1.0 Flash lands within one percentage point of the comparable-size baselines. A new preprint on arXiv under the identifier 2610.18861, submitted October 13 and updated Tuesday, proposes Hybrid Reasoning Agents (HybridRL), a training method for agents that can choose per step whether to run a latent-space reasoning pass or emit text actions. The paper claims a 10.4 percent average gain across 11 standard agent benchmarks and a 1.8x to 2.6x inference speedup against text-only baselines, driven mostly by the ability to compress multi-step reasoning into fewer visible tokens.

Setup

The method trains an agent on a mixture of two policies: an in-text chain-of-thought policy and a latent-reasoning policy that updates a hidden state without emitting tokens. The policy chooses between the two per step via a gating head. The RL signal rewards correct outcomes while penalizing visible tokens, which pushes the agent to use the latent-reasoning path when it can and the text path when it cannot. The authors argue the setup addresses the token-overhead problem on long-horizon reasoning tasks without sacrificing visible-reasoning auditability.

"HybridRL learns when to think in latent space and when to act through text." — arXiv 2610.18861, abstract

Results

The 11-benchmark suite includes OSWorld, WebArena, SWE-Bench Lite, MINT, AgentBench, Terminal-Bench 2.1 and five smaller task sets. Across them, HybridRL averages a 10.4 percentage-point lift over the strongest text-only baseline. Inference speedups range from 1.8x on OSWorld to 2.6x on Terminal-Bench. The paper's ablations show the gain disappears when the latent-reasoning path is removed, and shrinks to 3.1 percent when the gating head is replaced with a fixed schedule.

Open questions

The latent-reasoning path reduces interpretability by construction; the authors do not propose a tool for inspecting it. The gating head is trained offline on a demonstration set, and generalization to new tool suites is not evaluated.

Steam players demand a "No AI" filter after more than 2,500 of 8,202 Next Fest demos are AI-generated or AI-assisted.

IGN · 2026-10-20 · Source ↗

Steam Next Fest hosted 8,202 playable demos this cycle. More than 2,500 are AI-generated or AI-assisted and Valve offers no way to filter them. Petition signatures passed 180,000 by Tuesday.

Steam's October Next Fest hosted 8,202 playable demos, IGN reported Tuesday. More than 2,500 of them are either fully AI-generated or AI-assisted beyond narrow pipeline use. Valve offers no way to filter the AI-tagged demos out of discovery, and a petition circulating since the start of the fest crossed 180,000 signatures by Tuesday afternoon.

The volume problem

Next Fest is a semi-annual showcase that lets players try pre-release demos. The October cycle's volume is up roughly 60 percent from October 2025, which the organizers attribute to the rise of agent-based asset pipelines. The AI-disclosed demos cluster in three genres: visual novels, puzzle games, and casual sim games. Players reporting on the Steam forum say the discovery surface has become dominated by the AI cohort, pushing smaller human-made demos further down the recommendation feed.

"We are not asking Valve to ban AI games. We are asking Valve to let us not see them." — Petition text, 2026-10-20

Why Valve hasn't moved

Valve already requires developers to disclose whether a game uses AI in its development; the disclosure tag has been in place since January 2024. The petition's ask is to make the disclosure actionable through a user-level filter rather than a passive label. Valve has not publicly responded to the petition.
