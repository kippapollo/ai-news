The Decoder (Jonathan Kemper) · 2026-11-16 · Source ↗

Jonathan Kemper's Decoder piece Monday walks through Alibaba's Qwen4-Max release, which lands as the Qwen4 family's top-end flagship, built against Claude Opus 5.5, GPT-6, and Moonshot's Kimi K3.1 Pro. The model is a mixture-of-experts transformer with 2.4 trillion total parameters and about 71 billion active per token, native image processing without a separate encoder, a 1-million-token input context, and roughly a quarter fewer active parameters than its predecessor Qwen3.8-Max. Availability is via Alibaba's QwenCloud, QwenWork, Qoder, and QwenStudio. The flagship weights are closed, but Alibaba plans to open-source smaller Qwen4 models later. Chinese models now have a credible midrange price leader that beats Kimi K3.1 Pro on cost and a comparable score on agent benchmarks, which is the market-moving piece.

Architecture: hybrid attention, N-gram embeddings, and parallel residuals

The attention stack alternates three "Gated DeltaNet" layers that compress prior context into a condensed state with a fourth "Qwen Sparse Attention" layer that selects the most relevant parts, which Alibaba says cuts compute on long inputs. Four parallel residual connections between layers run with dynamic gates for information-flow stability. The N-gram embedding layer carries 328 billion parameters and supplies common token sequences as building blocks for the main stack. Alibaba reports training loss fell steadily from about 1.8 to just above 1.0 with no spikes. Against five base-model checkpoints including Qwen3.8-Max-Base, GLM-5.3, DeepSeek-V4-Pro-Base, and Kimi K3.1 Pro, Qwen4-Max-Base posted the best bits-per-byte across knowledge, code, QA, and STEM, 5 to 13 percent above Qwen3.8-Max-Base with a quarter fewer active params per token.

Commerce tightens AI chip export controls to block remote compute access from China, sets 12M-TPP data-center licensing threshold, rescinds Biden's AI Diffusion Rule.

TechCrunch (Ram Iyer) · 2026-11-16 · Source ↗

The Trump administration is rolling out an interim final rule requiring licenses to export, reexport or transfer AI chips and servers to data centers anywhere in the world where the provider may give compute to companies in China or the D:5 group. The threshold is 12 million total processing performance, about the capacity of 750 Nvidia H100 or H200 chips. Malaysia, Singapore, Indonesia, Thailand and Brazil picked up new license requirements for presenting "a higher-than-normal risk of diversion." Existing operators get 90 days to come into compliance.

Ram Iyer's Monday TechCrunch piece covers the Commerce Department's interim final rule on AI chips and remote compute. The rule requires licenses to export, reexport or transfer AI chips and servers to data centers anywhere in the world if the provider may give compute to companies in China or the rest of the D:5 country group — which covers Russia, Iran, North Korea and 18 others. The thrust is to shut the back door that overseas data centers opened on paper: a Chinese firm renting GPUs through an intermediary in Singapore or Malaysia now triggers licensing on the chips themselves.

The threshold

The rule applies to data centers or networks capable of delivering more than 12 million total processing performance (TPP), described as the computing power of about 750 Nvidia H100 or H200 chips. Exporters and data-center operators must monitor customer ownership structures and activities to confirm they aren't controlled by anyone in the D:5 group. Access through intermediaries and brokers is covered. New license requirements apply to Malaysia, Singapore, Indonesia, Thailand and Brazil, which Commerce says "present a higher-than-normal risk of diversion." Licenses don't apply to chips destined for US-based companies. Existing data-center operators offering covered services get 90 days to come into compliance.

"A higher-than-normal risk of diversion." — US Department of Commerce, on the five newly named countries, per TechCrunch, 2026-11-16

What it rescinds

The interim final rule rescinds the Biden administration's January 2025 AI Diffusion Rule, which had set country-level ceilings on advanced AI chip exports. Commercial recommendation systems used in e-commerce or social media are excluded. The rule names no effective date beyond the 90-day licensing window; no dollar figures or industry responses are in the piece. Context Iyer surfaces: Jensen Huang resigned as Nvidia CEO days earlier amid the Megaspeed affair and the alleged Chinese-intelligence tie, and the US government has reportedly wanted to place Megaspeed on a special entity list. Reuters reported Chinese AI firms are rushing to secure overseas computing capacity before the rule bites.

Manuel Uth's Monday Decoder piece covers UCSB particle theorist Nathaniel Craig's result, produced with GPT-6 Astra as collaborator. Putting the full Standard Model on a lattice has been blocked for decades because the weak force treats left- and right-handed particles differently; on simple lattices this creates unwanted "ghost particles." Special lattice particles called overlap fermions can filter out the ghosts, but for over 25 years no one could set their phase correctly across all the cases the physics required. Craig had a strategy: fix the phase by comparing progressively finer lattices. He could not prove it worked in every case. GPT-6 Astra turned the idea into a working construction and took the lead on the detailed proof.

Review and context

Reviewers Maarten Golterman, Andreas Kronfeld and Yigal Shamir asked the clarifying questions that helped build confidence in the result; the article is careful to frame the outcome as a possible, not settled, solution. Submission: arXiv 2610.09093. Craig: "much more a beginning than an end"; a lot of work remains before realistic simulations are possible. Uth sets the result alongside the AI math run that began in January 2026: OpenAI's May disproof of Erdős's unit distance conjecture; Google DeepMind's AlphaProof Nexus solving nine of 353 open Erdős problems for a few hundred dollars (peer-reviewed by October); OpenAI's September 166-page proof for the Navier-Stokes Millennium Prize, which the Clay Mathematics Institute called "apparently settled" and mathematicians have since questioned.

Anthropic agent-memory research: the skill is knowing what to forget — Sonnet 4.6 at 0.330 bare, 0.641 with memory and refined prompting; Stainless wiki hits 61 percent of criteria.

The Decoder (Jonathan Kemper) · 2026-11-16 · Source ↗

Anthropic Engineering's Lance Martin lays out five recommendations for agent memory: be selective, explain purpose and let the model decide, run offline "Dreaming" to fix errors, build wikis alongside search, limit what loads. Pokémon Red: Sonnet 3.7 wrote 413 files for one badge; Fable 5 wrote ~10 files for four badges; Opus 5.5 beat the game using a small goal-tracking set. Stainless wiki (~350,000 files, 420M tokens): wiki plus search hit 61 percent of criteria over 128 sessions against 53 for search alone. Harvey LAB: wiki 87 percent on multi-doc questions vs search 73 percent.

Anthropic Engineering's "Components of Agent Memory" post, written by Lance Martin on November 6 and picked up broadly Monday via The Decoder's Jonathan Kemper, lays out a working theory of agent memory built around five rules. The opening framing is an analogy to human sleep-time consolidation; the technique Martin calls "Dreaming" is offline review of logs to correct errors and build notes or representations, borrowing from Letta and UC Berkeley sleep-time compute work. Memory here means knowledge Claude gains from experience and keeps between sessions. Weight changes, in-context learning, and compaction are excluded.

The five recommendations

Be selective: store strategies, plans, and verified rules instead of logging every step; exhaustive note-taking in the Pokémon Red test overwhelmed the model with stale data. Explain purpose and let the model decide: rigid rules like "only record mistakes" reduced the benefit for stronger models; older models gained from specific instructions. Use Dreaming to correct: between sessions, Claude reviews earlier transcripts, checks its notes, fixes errors; in test it corrected a wrong landmark note and reached Cerulean City at action 1,935 (the uncorrected run never got past the mountain). Build wikis alongside search: Claude built a linked wiki from ~350,000 Stainless documents and Dreaming added pages and fixed gaps, duplicates and broken links. Limit what loads: wikis and notes need upkeep; loading too much consumes context and spreads bad notes.

Numbers

CL-Bench 1.0 database task (baseline → with memory): Opus 5.5 0.655→0.838; Sonnet 5.5 0.644→0.833; Opus 5 0.564→0.828; Opus 4.7 0.383→0.700; Sonnet 5 0.306→0.610; Sonnet 4.6 0.330→0.364 (0.641 with refined prompting). Stainless wiki alone rose from 13 to 48 percent of graded criteria over 128 sessions; wiki plus search hit 61 percent versus 53 for search alone; wiki plus search saved ~20,000 tokens per question. Harvey LAB: wiki 87 percent on multi-doc merger questions vs search 73 percent. Contract-term task: facts went from 41 of 57 (72 percent) to 57 of 57 (100 percent) after instructions were changed so each contract got its own page. A second dreaming pass added three pages and nine broken-link fixes to a 38-entry wiki.

"Tell the model what memory is for, but not exactly how to use it." — Lance Martin, Anthropic Engineering, "Components of Agent Memory," per The Decoder, 2026-11-16
