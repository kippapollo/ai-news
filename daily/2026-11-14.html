"Across 2.3 million questions from one million real requests, v1.3 agrees with the human judge 93.98 percent of the time." — Perplexity Decider v1.3 release, 2026-11-12

The architecture choice

The models start from Qwen generative weights. The core modification: Perplexity makes the attention layers bidirectional, so each token can use context from the whole input. Decision models of this family return yes/no, multiple-choice, or ordered-score answers rather than open-ended text; they are optimized for the routing and classification layer that feeds every production RAG, agent, and tool-use system. Finnish logician Jan von Plato at the University of Helsinki has worked on Gödel's notebooks since 2017 at roughly a page per week. In September he gave Claude his transcriptions and six of his own books on Gödel; within days Claude returned a 650-page explanation and a 663-theorem catalogue. The Guardian published a dispatch from Helsinki Friday on what is the most consequential formal-reasoning application of a frontier model this quarter. Jan von Plato, the Finnish logician at the University of Helsinki, has worked since 2017 on the Nachlass of Kurt Gödel, 1,009 pages of notebooks written in Gabelsberger, a German shorthand already obsolete in Gödel's own lifetime. The pace had been roughly one page per week.

The method

In September 2026, von Plato gave Claude his accumulated transcriptions and six of his own books on Gödel's work. Within days, Claude returned a 650-page explanation of the notebooks and a catalogue of 663 theorems Gödel proved, sketched or outlined across the notebooks. Von Plato then ran the catalogue through Coq, the standard formal-proof verifier. Coq validated roughly 400 theorems as stated. Roughly 200 required sharper statements or additional assumptions to check. Roughly 50 were false. Gödel's own marginal annotations, including "falsch" (incorrect) and "schlecht" (bad), fit the error pattern.

"It is a task basically impossible for a human to do." — Jan von Plato, University of Helsinki, 2026-11-13

The headline find

The Guardian's focus is on a near-complete reconstruction of the proof of the independence of the axiom of choice inside the notebooks. Gödel claimed the result in 1966 but never published it. Paul Cohen later won the Fields Medal for proving the same result. Claude's reconstructed version of Gödel's draft argument passed Coq verification. If the reconstruction holds under independent review, it reassigns the credit timeline of one of the twentieth century's most-cited results in mathematical logic. Von Plato's framing: the notebooks also predict results that only became known in the 1970s and 1980s, and they contain 14 further logic topics that remain undeveloped in subsequent literature.

"Separating Claude's contribution from Gödel's is a job for the historians." — Joel David Hamkins, Notre Dame

The caveats

Joel David Hamkins of Notre Dame told The Guardian that disentangling what Claude added from what Gödel put on the page is "a job for the historians" — a bounded acknowledgement that the 650-page explanation is a collaboration whose credit boundary is not yet fixed. The axiom-of-choice reconstruction still awaits independent logician review. No data release has yet enabled outside replication; von Plato's protocol is privately archived. The Guardian's open question, which AI Weekly's editorial note echoes: how the ~200 "needed sharper statements" entries divide between Claude's phrasing and von Plato's own interpretive repairs.

ggml-org released llama.cpp v1.0.0 and ggml v1.0.0 on Friday, three and a half years after Georgi Gerganov started the project. The release is a versioning commitment rather than an API shake-up: both libraries now carry an explicit semantic-versioning contract. Major bumps mark breaking changes to the public C API in `./include`. Minor bumps mark backward-compatible features, new backends, new model support. Patch bumps mark bug fixes that do not change the API. The team said both projects will signal API and ABI incompatibilities through major-version bumps and will make those boundaries clearer to downstream users as adoption grows.

Scope

llama.cpp supports more than 150 architecture variants at the 1.0 mark, including every mainstream speculative decoding method. Pre-release artifacts cover Linux, macOS, Windows, Android and iOS. Packaging is available through Docker, Debian, Homebrew, conda-forge, MacPorts, Winget and an XCFramework for iOS/macOS. Why the loop works
