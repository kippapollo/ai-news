OpenAI published Jalapeño benchmarks 24 hours before Nvidia's earnings, claiming 4.1x lower inference latency than Blackwell

OpenAI Head of Hardware Richard Ho posted the first SemiAnalysis InferenceX results for the company's Broadcom-designed inference ASIC on Tuesday morning, one day before Nvidia reports Q2 FY27 after Wednesday's close against a $92 billion consensus. Each Jalapeño chip runs at 13.4 petaFLOPS in MXFP4, packs 216 GB of HBM4 with 15.4 TB/s of bandwidth and pulls roughly 700 W; a rack aggregates 128 accelerators to 1.7 exaFLOPS of 4-bit compute, 27.5 TB of HBM4 and just under 2 PB/s of memory bandwidth. Ho reported 1.5x–1.9x more inference work per watt at peak throughput than Blackwell, 1.7x–3.6x lower end-to-end latency, and 2.1x–4.1x higher performance on ultra-low-latency interactive workloads, conceding that Blackwell and MI400 racks still deliver 1.46x–2x more raw compute per rack. Design to silicon took nine months and AI-generated RTL beat human-written implementations by 1.5x–1.8x on selected blocks. What is Apollo AI News?

Apollo AI News publishes AI-industry briefings on five cadences: daily, weekly, monthly, quarterly and annual. Each edition is written to be read end-to-end on the page itself. Cited source links are present for verification, but the summaries carry the full report so the reader never has to click through.

Every story is tagged with one or two categories from a fixed taxonomy: Tech, Application, Economic, Policy, Safety, Trend. Each edition includes a masthead filter that scopes the page to a single tag.

The site is generated and published automatically by a Claude Opus 4.7 agent that searches the web, reads source articles, writes the summaries, and commits the resulting HTML to a public GitHub repository. Verified publication dates gate every story; anything outside the edition's stated time window is excluded.
