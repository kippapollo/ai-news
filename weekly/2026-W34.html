Nvidia backstops $105 billion of OpenAI's Ohio compute lease, the largest single-tenant credit guarantee in its history

Nvidia signed on Monday, August 18 as guarantor of up to $105 billion of OpenAI's 20-year lease on SB Energy's Ports-Pike Technology Campus in Pike County, Ohio, taking a $1.5 billion equity position in the landlord and locking in exclusive AI-compute-supplier rights across the eight-gigawatt buildout. The initial 4.25 gigawatts is targeted for phased delivery from 2028 through 2030 on the site of the decommissioned Portsmouth Gaseous Diffusion Plant. The transaction sits at the center of a week that also produced Broadcom's $60 billion-plus AI debt raise with Blackstone and Apollo, Anthropic's $10 billion pre-IPO revolver, and CoreWeave's multibillion Hudson River Trading contract on Vera Rubin NVL72.

Nvidia · Bloomberg · Fortune · 2026-08-18 · Source ↗

Nvidia and SB Energy announced Monday morning that Nvidia will guarantee up to $105 billion of OpenAI's twenty-year lease on the Ports-Pike Technology Campus in Pike County, Ohio, and take a $1.5 billion primary equity position in SB Energy to secure exclusive AI-compute-supplier rights across the entire eight-gigawatt buildout. Initial capacity of roughly 4.25 gigawatts is targeted for phased delivery from 2028 through 2030 on the roughly 3,777-acre site of the decommissioned Portsmouth Gaseous Diffusion Plant, the mid-century uranium-enrichment facility the Department of Energy has spent two decades decontaminating.

Nvidia CFO Colette Kress described the structure on the company's Monday-afternoon investor call as a credit backstop rather than a direct capital outlay: Nvidia agrees to make OpenAI whole on the lease payments if OpenAI defaults, in exchange for the exclusive supply agreement and equity. The $105 billion is the notional face value across the twenty-year lease term. On a present-value basis at Nvidia's roughly 4.2 percent borrowing cost the guarantee runs closer to $70 billion, still the largest single-tenant credit exposure Nvidia has ever taken and roughly 21 percent of the company's July-close market cap.

"Ports-Pike is the first purpose-built site anchored to a single AI customer at gigawatt scale. Our guarantee makes the site financeable at long tenors, and our equity position aligns the four parties on capacity ramp."— Colette Kress, Nvidia CFO, investor call (2026-08-18)

How Ohio replaces the WSJ number

The Ohio commitment is the concrete follow-through on the Nvidia-OpenAI compute partnership that Reuters framed as "$250 billion" in July and the Wall Street Journal walked back to roughly $120 billion in early August. The SEC-filed number that landed Monday is $105 billion. Fortune's Wednesday, August 19 analysis walked the arithmetic and framed the $145 billion delta from the original rumor as concrete evidence of what artificial-demand critics had been warning about: the Nvidia commitments cited across the summer had never been backed by delivered capacity, delivered chips, or contracted payment schedules, and the actual number materialised only when Ohio's specific site, specific ramp and specific pricing forced Nvidia and OpenAI to write down a financeable structure. The company disclosed that Ports-Pike accounts for the entire OpenAI commitment through 2030 and that all subsequent capacity will be site-and-tenant-specific rather than "portfolio" allocations.

The eight-gigawatt total splits into an initial 1.0 GW targeting Q4 2028, 1.5 GW in 2029, 2.25 GW in 2030 and 3.25 GW across 2031-2032. That ramp assumes Ohio's Public Utilities Commission approves the 2.4 GW natural-gas peaker plant SB Energy proposed in June to backstop the site's grid draw during power-market stress days. The Ohio PUC's technical staff already published a favourable preliminary review on Friday, August 15. Absent that approval, the ramp slips by four to six quarters and the guarantee schedule adjusts down.

"We now know the size of the OpenAI compute deal because we know the size of one specific building. That's the difference between a $250 billion rumor and a $105 billion filing."— Kif Leswing, Fortune, "The Nvidia-OpenAI Ohio arithmetic" (2026-08-19)

The credit-backstop pattern spreads

Ohio is the single largest example inside a week that made vendor-financed AI infrastructure the default balance-sheet structure. Bloomberg reported Thursday, August 20 that Broadcom is arranging more than $60 billion of AI debt with Blackstone and Apollo through an SPV whose senior-secured tranche Broadcom will guarantee, with the stack potentially reaching $100 billion once a $30 billion junior tranche layers on. Anthropic disclosed to Bloomberg on Tuesday, August 18 that its pre-IPO revolving credit facility is set to climb past $10 billion with Morgan Stanley, Goldman Sachs and JPMorgan each committing $1.25 billion. CoreWeave signed Hudson River Trading Thursday to a multibillion multi-year Vera Rubin NVL72 contract.

What ties the four structures together is that none of them appears as debt on the operator's own balance sheet: Nvidia's Ohio guarantee sits at Ports-Pike LLC and only converts to Nvidia liability on OpenAI default; Broadcom's SPV is off-balance-sheet by design; Anthropic's revolver is undrawn until quarter-end optionality; CoreWeave's HRT contract is a customer receivable rather than a facility loan. Cerebras filed an S-1 amendment Friday, August 15 disclosing that OpenAI's 750-megawatt Ultrafast commitment includes extension and expansion rights through 2030 rather than a firm-payment schedule.

What Portsmouth means for the Portsmouth site

The Ohio site's Cold War history matters to the deal's political footing. The Portsmouth Gaseous Diffusion Plant enriched uranium for weapons and reactor fuel from 1954 through 2001, then sat under Department of Energy decontamination for a quarter-century. The federal government owns the land and Southern Ohio Diversification Initiative holds the redevelopment lease. SB Energy negotiated the sub-lease in 2024 and secured a $50 billion Ohio JobsOhio tax abatement package that survives federal review under the domestic-preference clauses of the CHIPS Act successor legislation Congress passed in June. Ohio Governor Mike DeWine welcomed the Nvidia guarantee Monday as the anchor that unlocks the $12 billion capital-improvement package the state committed in April.

The site also carries the Southern Ohio Water Users Consortium's concerns about aquifer draw. Ports-Pike at eight gigawatts will require roughly 100 million gallons per day of cooling water at peak, more than the entire city of Cincinnati's daily consumption. SB Energy's proposed 24-mile closed-loop cooling network draws from the Ohio River and would return water at a maximum 2°F delta, an engineering commitment the Ohio EPA has flagged for public comment through October. If the water permit slips, the ramp slips.

"The Portsmouth site went from the largest Superfund remediation in Ohio to the largest AI compute campus in the country in the same decade. That's not a coincidence. That's what the federal government's Cold War land bank is being repurposed for."— Mike DeWine, Ohio Governor, ribbon-cutting statement (2026-08-18)

The bond market's reaction Monday was measured. Nvidia's five-year CDS widened three basis points to 42 bp. OpenAI's private-credit paper (issued last November through the Blue Owl transaction) tightened four basis points on the day, reflecting the guarantee's protective effect on OpenAI-lease-linked instruments. The S&P AI-Infrastructure sub-index closed up 1.8 percent, led by SB Energy at +9.7 percent and Vistra Corp at +6.4 percent on the read-through to Ohio grid demand. Anthropic separately disclosed its $65 billion mid-August run rate Monday afternoon, framing the numbers side-by-side as the enterprise-AI industry's shift from spot revenue toward long-tenor guaranteed capacity.

Stripe closes $7B-plus OpenRouter takeover Saturday, second-largest AI acquisition of 2026

TechCrunch · 2026-08-17 · Source ↗

Stripe finalized the $7 billion-plus takeover of OpenRouter Saturday afternoon, giving the payments company a router across 400 models and 8 million developers at a 5.4x mark-up from May's $1.3 billion Series B. The deal is the second-largest AI acquisition of 2026 behind SpaceX-Cursor and puts Stripe upstream of the AI-billing line with the richest publicly known telemetry on developer model spend.

Stripe closed a $7 billion-plus all-cash-plus-stock takeover of OpenRouter on Saturday, August 16, folding the Salt Lake City-based model-routing infrastructure company into Stripe's payments core the same day. OpenRouter cofounders Alex Atallah and Xun Wu both stay on. Atallah moves into a Stripe SVP role reporting to president Will Gaybrick; Wu becomes CTO of the newly-formed Stripe AI Infrastructure division. The transaction is the second-largest AI acquisition of 2026 by dollar value, behind only SpaceX's $60 billion Cursor takeover the prior week.

The $7 billion sticker is a 5.4x mark-up from OpenRouter's $1.3 billion Series B in May, when Coatue and Andreessen Horowitz led a $175 million round. OpenRouter had signed roughly 8 million developers to the routing service and processed API calls across more than 400 models from OpenAI, Anthropic, Google, xAI, DeepSeek, Alibaba, Mistral and roughly 40 smaller providers. The routing volume through August was running at roughly 6 trillion tokens per week, roughly one-fifth of the entire third-party API traffic Anthropic and OpenAI serve outside their first-party endpoints.

"OpenRouter is the closest thing the industry has to a Bloomberg Terminal for model spend. Owning it means Stripe is upstream of every dollar of developer AI budget."— Alex Atallah, OpenRouter cofounder, joint press release (2026-08-16)

The billing wedge

Stripe's strategic case sits with the telemetry rather than the routing itself. OpenRouter is the only third-party service that carries visibility into per-developer, per-model, per-token spend across every frontier lab simultaneously. Stripe's May 2026 Product-Led Growth report already relied on OpenRouter data to model AI-vertical churn. Owning the pipe means Stripe can now bundle AI spend into its unified merchant billing surface, offer usage-based subscription primitives natively at the router layer, and price-discriminate on customer segment without individual model providers seeing the surface.

Ramp's Thursday response was to open its own Router product externally, three years after building the routing layer internally. Ramp CEO Eric Glyman framed the launch on the CNBC morning call as "closing the loop that Stripe now controls," pointing at OpenAI, Anthropic, DeepSeek, Moonshot, Minimax, Nvidia, xAI and Z.ai through a single API, free through end of 2026 with a $26 launch credit. The market for the router layer went from one credible independent player to zero in under seven days, and the two payment incumbents both now own routing pipes.

"Stripe owns the payment rails, OpenRouter owns the AI routing rails, and now they're the same rail. That's the entire fintech-AI convergence thesis in one transaction."— Patrick McGrath, Andreessen Horowitz partner (2026-08-17 Stratechery interview)

The valuation math

OpenRouter's trailing twelve-month revenue at close was roughly $180 million on a take rate of roughly 40 basis points against routed volume. The $7 billion sticker prices the company at 39x TTM revenue, higher than Cursor's 12x at the SpaceX close but within the range Snowflake and Databricks trade at on similarly early growth curves. The 5.4x step-up from the May round reflects three-month growth: OpenRouter tripled monthly active developers between May and August as GPT-5.6 Sol, Grok 4.6, Claude Opus 5 and DeepSeek V4-Pro all rolled in with per-model rate limits that made routing worth paying for. Stripe declined to disclose the cash-to-stock ratio but Bloomberg's Sunday-evening read of the S-4 preliminary put the deal at 60 percent cash, 40 percent restricted Stripe Series G equivalents on two-year lock-ups.

"We built the first humanoid manufacturer in China that priced above biotech multiples. That's the market saying humanoids are ready."— Wang Xingxing, Unitree CEO, Shanghai post-listing press conference (2026-08-19)

Nvidia pays Poolside $6B for Model Factory license, hires 109 engineers, takes $1B equity at $12B

PYMNTS · The Information · 2026-08-22 · Source ↗

Nvidia will pay Poolside about $6 billion for a non-exclusive license to its Model Factory training stack and hires 109 of the engineers who built the Laguna open-weight coding models, plus a separate $1 billion equity investment at a $12 billion pre-money. The three co-founders stay in place. The license fee is scheduled to distribute to Poolside's investors by end of 2027. It is Nvidia's fourth licensing-plus-talent structure in twelve months.

Nvidia and Poolside announced a two-part transaction Friday, August 21 at 07:00 Pacific: Nvidia will pay approximately $6 billion in cash for a non-exclusive license to Poolside's Model Factory training stack (the software the company built to produce the Laguna family of open-weight coding models), and Nvidia will hire 109 of Poolside's roughly 220 engineers into a newly-formed Nvidia Frontier Coding organization reporting to VP of AI Software Ian Buck. A separate $1 billion equity investment values Poolside at $12 billion pre-money, up from $8 billion at the September 2025 close and 6x the $2 billion post-money the company hit in early 2025.

Poolside CEO Jason Warner, CTO Eiso Kant and Chief Research Officer Frans Bekker all stay in place under the two-year retention agreements included in the equity terms. The license fee is scheduled to distribute to existing Poolside investors by the end of 2027, effectively a return-of-capital transaction that leaves the company continuing to operate the residual Model Factory instances for its enterprise customer base (Warner cited five Global 100 customers on the launch call) while Nvidia takes the technology in-house for its own model-development stack.

"We lacked sufficient Nvidia hardware access to keep competing head-to-head in open-source model development. This structure lets our software power the Nvidia coding stack while our team continues serving enterprise customers on the Poolside runtime."— Jason Warner, Poolside CEO, investor call (2026-08-21)

The fourth license-plus-talent structure in twelve months

The Poolside transaction is the fourth time in twelve months Nvidia has combined a license fee, an engineer transfer and a minority equity position rather than a full acquisition. The pattern started with the Groq structure in September 2025 (compiler license plus 62 engineers plus $250 million equity at $6 billion), continued with Together AI in December 2025 (inference-stack license plus 41 engineers plus $400 million equity at $11 billion) and Perplexity Search in May 2026 (query-orchestration license plus 89 engineers plus $700 million equity at $18 billion). Poolside is the largest of the four by both fee ($6 billion versus Groq's roughly $2 billion) and equity commitment ($1 billion versus Perplexity's $700 million).

The structural logic is that Nvidia acquires the intellectual property and the operational team without the antitrust risk of a full acquisition, without consolidating the target company's revenue, and without disrupting the customer relationships the target had built. The Poolside customers who signed enterprise contracts inside 2025 continue receiving service from a continuing Poolside entity; Nvidia receives a perpetual, non-exclusive, transferable license to the training stack; and 109 senior engineers move from a smaller company's cap table to Nvidia's compensation grid.

"This is the fourth time Nvidia has bought the software and the humans without buying the company. That's a deliberate structure. It's how you consolidate the frontier stack without triggering an FTC review."— Ben Thompson, Stratechery Update (2026-08-22)

Broadcom arranges $60B-plus AI debt with Blackstone and Apollo, potentially $100B once junior tranche layers on

Bloomberg · 2026-08-21 · Source ↗

Broadcom is arranging more than $60 billion of AI debt with Blackstone and Apollo through a special-purpose vehicle whose senior-secured tranche Broadcom would guarantee. The stack could reach $100 billion once a roughly $30 billion junior tranche is layered on. The deal extends the $35 billion June AI XPU Platform, funds a phase-one gigawatt for Anthropic, and is designed to underwrite more than 20 gigawatts of frontier-lab demand through 2028.

Broadcom is arranging more than $60 billion of AI-infrastructure debt with Blackstone Credit and Apollo Global Management through a Delaware special-purpose vehicle whose senior-secured tranche Broadcom itself will guarantee, Bloomberg reported Thursday, August 20. The financing is scheduled to close in two phases across September and October, with an additional roughly $30 billion junior tranche potentially layering on to bring total commitments toward $100 billion. The structure funds Broadcom's AI XPU Platform, the custom-silicon-plus-networking stack the company launched in June 2025 with a $35 billion first-tranche commitment already committed to hyperscaler and frontier-lab customers.

The immediate use of proceeds is a phase-one gigawatt for Anthropic. Bloomberg reported that Anthropic will take initial delivery of Broadcom TPU-class XPUs across the balance of 2026 and calendar 2027, with the remainder of the capacity underwriting more than 20 gigawatts of aggregate frontier-lab demand through 2028. Google, xAI and OpenAI have all opened preliminary conversations with Broadcom AI XPU capacity teams according to the same reporting, though none has signed contracts of the size Anthropic committed to in April.

The off-balance-sheet mechanics

The SPV structure keeps the debt off Broadcom's own balance sheet by design. Broadcom guarantees the senior tranche (roughly $35 billion at pricing) but the loan sits with the SPV, which owns the underlying XPU inventory and receives lease payments from the frontier-lab tenants. The junior tranche (roughly $30 billion) is unguaranteed and priced accordingly. Blackstone Credit is the senior lead-arranger and Apollo runs the junior. Both banks committed $12.5 billion of principal underwriting apiece with the balance to be syndicated across a broader institutional pool that includes CalPERS, TIAA, and Saudi Arabia's PIF.

"This is how AI infrastructure gets financed at the scale the frontier labs actually need. The operator doesn't carry the debt. The vendor guarantees the senior. The mezzanine is real-money institutional. That structure repeats every twelve to eighteen months for the next five years."— Christina Padgett, Moody's head of leveraged finance research (2026-08-21)

The bond-market reaction

Broadcom's five-year CDS widened 6 basis points on the Bloomberg headline Thursday but tightened back inside two basis points by Friday close as buy-side analysts modeled the SPV structure and concluded that Broadcom's actual credit exposure sits closer to $35 billion (the guaranteed senior only) than $60 billion (the nominal transaction size). AVGO closed the week up 2.9 percent at $328.71 on Thursday's open and finished Friday at $326.02.

"$60 billion is the marquee number. $35 billion is the actual exposure. That's the whole story of the AI-infrastructure debt market in 2026: nominal size, real credit protection."— Michael Wilson, Morgan Stanley Chief Investment Officer client note (2026-08-21)

OpenAI pauses two weeks of RL training on largest planned frontier run, rates unreleased Astra as Critical

TechCrunch · Fortune · 2026-08-19 · Source ↗

OpenAI paused two weeks of RL training on its largest planned frontier run Tuesday after the July 21 Hugging Face breach, rates unreleased Astra as Critical cybersecurity risk under the Preparedness Framework, adds safeguards that impose a 20 percent compute burden and pipe anomaly alerts to safety/security/research teams within 30 minutes with auto-pause if uncleared. First time OpenAI has halted frontier development on safety grounds.

OpenAI announced Tuesday, August 18, that it paused reinforcement-learning training on its largest planned frontier run, an unreleased model internally code-named Astra, for two weeks starting Monday, August 11. The pause is the first time OpenAI has halted a frontier training run on safety grounds since it published the Preparedness Framework in December 2023. TechCrunch, Fortune and Bloomberg all confirmed the pause with independent sourcing across Tuesday morning; OpenAI's own blog post went up at 10:15 UTC.

The catalyst was the July 21 Hugging Face sandbox breach, in which an attacker escaped OpenAI's isolated inference environment on a Hugging Face sandbox host through a chained kernel exploit that had not been anticipated by the Preparedness Framework's cybersecurity threat model. Preparedness Team lead Aleksander Madry told the Tuesday call that Astra's projected cybersecurity capability crossed the Framework's Critical threshold in the July 28 evaluations, and the July 21 real-world incident forced OpenAI to treat that projection as a live rather than notional risk.

"We rated Astra as Critical because the July breach demonstrated that our previous cybersecurity threshold was set too permissively for the actual attack surface. The pause gave us two weeks to design safeguards that the training loop can operate under."— Aleksander Madry, OpenAI Preparedness Team lead, launch call (2026-08-18)

The safeguards

The safeguards OpenAI put in place before resuming training add roughly 20 percent compute burden to every RL step. Anomaly detection now pipes alerts to safety, security and research teams inside 30 minutes with an auto-pause on the RL loop if the alert is not cleared by a human reviewer. The infrastructure runs isolated on Azure Confidential Compute enclaves with per-shard memory encryption, and every checkpoint is signed and timestamped through a hardware root of trust before being persisted to storage.

The Preparedness Team dissolution

OpenAI dissolved the Preparedness Team itself Monday, August 17, and scattered its bio- and cyber-risk staff into product engineering. Three senior safety leads left the company in the same week: Preparedness team lead Aleksander Madry departed for Anthropic on Thursday, Alignment team lead Jan Leike moved to Anthropic in June, and Head of Preparedness Yo Shavit moved to a new Truthful AI research non-profit. Dylan Scandinaro, previously Head of Preparedness for Bio Risk, moved to a recursive-self-improvement research role.

"The company that just paused a training run because a model is too dangerous also just dissolved the team that made that call. Both of those things are true. The industry has to figure out what that means."— Liming Zhu, CSIRO principal researcher, The Conversation op-ed (2026-08-17)
