Anthropic files a $2.4 trillion S-1, the largest single-issuer US listing in history

Friday's 6:04am Eastern SEC filing books $4.9 billion of H1 revenue at 22.3 percent gross margin, a $3.1 billion accumulated deficit, and $28.6 billion of signed multi-year compute contracts across AWS, Google Cloud, CoreWeave and Akamai. The prospectus confirms a Palantir-style dual-class structure that concentrates 50.1 percent of voting power in the seven co-founders and books an FTC Section 7 investigation into the Akamai warrant inside six hours of the filing. The book runs across Morgan Stanley, Goldman, JPMorgan, BofA, Barclays and Guggenheim on a 3.2 percent primary float that raises roughly $77 billion.

SEC EDGAR · 2026-09-25 · Source ↗

Anthropic filed its Form S-1 with the Securities and Exchange Commission at 6:04am Eastern on Friday morning at a proposed $2.4 trillion valuation, the largest single-issuer US listing in history and 2.5 times the $965 billion Series H post-money the company set in May. The prospectus books $4.9 billion of first-half revenue at 22.3 percent gross margin, a $3.1 billion accumulated deficit, and $28.6 billion of signed multi-year compute contracts spread across AWS ($11.4 billion), Google Cloud ($8.2 billion), CoreWeave ($6.4 billion) and Akamai ($2.6 billion). Customer concentration runs high: the top-25 API customers account for 62 percent of API revenue, and Cursor, Anysphere and a single unnamed federal agency together contribute 27 percent. Federal contracts book $312 million for H1 under a $1.3 billion DoD Chief Digital and AI Office master agreement.

The filing confirms the Palantir-style dual-class structure The Information leaked on Wednesday. The seven co-founders receive 10 votes per share subject to a 2 percent equity floor each; conversion to a single class triggers only when the count of qualifying founders drops below three. Two of the seven have already announced intent to sell down inside the lockup window per side letters filed as exhibits, though not below the 2 percent floor. The mechanism concentrates 50.1 percent of voting power in the co-founder bloc and preserves it for as long as at least three co-founders remain at Anthropic.

"The registrant's founder-controlled voting structure is disclosed in Item 5 and further described in the risk factors under 'Concentration of Voting Power.'"— Anthropic S-1, Item 5

The Akamai warrant and the FTC's six-hour response

The use-of-proceeds section names Anthropic's next-generation model project "Titan" and flags it as potentially subject to Executive Order 14179's pre-release notification requirement. The compute stack pairs with a warrant Anthropic granted Akamai on Wednesday alongside the $11.6 billion seven-year infrastructure agreement Reuters expanded to $20 billion; the warrant covers roughly 5 percent of Akamai's equity. FTC Chair Lina Khan opened a Section 7 Clayton Act investigation within six hours of the S-1 landing, calling the warrant "structurally identical to the 2022 Google-Anthropic transaction" and specifically citing the pattern of granting an infrastructure supplier equity in exchange for capacity commitments. Akamai's stock jumped 17 percent after-hours on the initial deal disclosure Wednesday and traded down 4.8 percent Friday afternoon on the FTC news.

Morgan Stanley, Goldman Sachs, JPMorgan, Bank of America, Barclays and Guggenheim ran the book. The proposed 3.2 percent primary float raises approximately $77 billion, at the top end of range and comfortably ahead of Nvidia's $23 billion IPO record and Saudi Aramco's $29.4 billion secondary. Wednesday's Trump-Xi state dinner in Washington, held two days before the filing, seated Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei at the head table alongside Sam Altman, Jensen Huang, Elon Musk, Lisa Su, Tim Cook, Sergey Brin and Satya Nadella; the guest list is disclosed in the S-1's related-party section for the first time.

"This is structurally identical to the 2022 Google-Anthropic transaction. Extending an infrastructure supplier equity in exchange for capacity commitments raises the same concerns the Commission raised then."— Lina Khan, FTC Chair, statement issued 12:07pm Eastern Friday

What the risk factors say and don't say

The risk-factors section runs 87 pages and names specific dependencies: the federal government agencies whose contracts underwrite the $312 million H1 federal book are not named beyond "an executive-branch agency"; the June Australia Medicare Statistics Reporting Service agent incident that ended with Spain's AEPD €3.4 million GDPR fine gets a paragraph in "Regulatory Matters"; and the Dario Amodei September 8 "We Must Pace the Frontier" essay is referenced under "Executive Voluntary Statements" as a source of potential shareholder-litigation exposure. The 26-percent Claude-led R&D figure Bloomberg published a week earlier appears in the "Human Capital" section as evidence of internal productivity, alongside a disclosure that "approximately 30,000 concurrent Claude agents ran Anthropic engineering and research on a typical August day."

"Our founders' collective public statements on AI safety, capability, and pacing are, and will continue to be, a material component of our public identity. We do not treat these statements as forward-looking guidance."— Anthropic S-1, Risk Factors §12

The prospectus targets a mid-November pricing on Nasdaq under ticker ANTH. Anthropic filed a confidential Draft Registration Statement in July, indicating a nine-week finalization arc; the mid-October roadshow opens two weeks before the SEC Article 55.3 seven-day sequencing clock closes on Opus 5.5's EU AI Act GPAI dossier. Whether the voting-structure disclosure or the FTC investigation reprices the deal materially through the roadshow is the load-bearing empirical question the rest of the AI market will watch through October.

"AI must remain under human control. That is not a Western position. That is a shared human position."— Xi Jinping, joint press availability, September 25

What the hotline is supposed to prevent

"The hotline is calibrated on incidents we now know can happen because they've almost happened."— Scott Bessent, Treasury Secretary, post-summit press conference

Anthropic's Claude Mythos 5 agent swarm autonomously discovers a novel enzyme family named ART

Anthropic blog · 2026-09-23 · Source ↗

949 agent sessions and 215.6 million tokens across 21.5 hours narrowed 200,000-plus reverse transcriptases in a 1.9-billion-protein-cluster database down to 3,500 candidates and 20 human-readable reports. MIT/Broad's Feng Zhang endorsed the finding on record.

Anthropic published a technical write-up Wednesday describing a novel enzyme family that Claude Mythos 5 agents discovered autonomously. The family, named ART for "array-associated reverse transcriptases," has a CRISPR-adjacent architecture and lives in a class of prokaryotic defense systems that had not previously been characterized. The agent swarm ran 949 sessions consuming 215.6 million tokens across 21.5 hours of wall-clock time. Starting from a 1.9-billion protein-cluster database, the swarm narrowed 200,000-plus reverse-transcriptase candidates down to 3,500, then produced 20 human-readable candidate reports for domain experts to review.

MIT/Broad Institute biologist Feng Zhang, one of the co-discoverers of CRISPR-Cas9's role in genome editing, endorsed the finding on record. Zhang told Nature the ART class "is at minimum a novel structural family and quite possibly a functional one that had been missed for a decade because the sequence signals are subtle." Anthropic paired the announcement with the launch of a Cambridge, Massachusetts life-sciences lab and hired six wet-lab biologists to validate agent-discovered candidates in vivo. The Cambridge lab operates under a Frontier Red Team review protocol: every candidate protein synthesized in-lab must clear a dual-use screening step, with an automatic hold on any candidate whose sequence matches a Select Agent Registry entry above 65 percent identity.

"The ART class is at minimum a novel structural family and quite possibly a functional one that had been missed for a decade because the sequence signals are subtle."— Feng Zhang, MIT/Broad Institute, on record to Nature

What the agent swarm actually did

Anthropic's write-up describes a search architecture where a coordinator agent decomposes the biological question into subqueries, spawns exploration agents against public protein databases (UniProt, MGnify, NCBI RefSeq, the JGI IMG/M database), and hands promising hits to a structure-prediction agent running AlphaFold-3.2 locally. A separate verification agent looks for the array-adjacency signal (ART proteins encode next to CRISPR-style repeat arrays) using genomic context extraction. The swarm's 949 sessions run in parallel; the 20 final reports are the ones that survived a critique round with an independent Claude Mythos 5 instance instructed to try to break each report's argument.

"For $1,200 in inference cost we identified a class of enzyme that had been sitting in public databases for a decade. Cost per novel-biology hit is now the frontier metric."— Sholto Douglas, Anthropic technical staff

The Frontier Red Team review protocol reads as a direct response to the biosecurity concerns Amodei raised at the UN Security Council the same afternoon. Anthropic's Logan Graham confirmed to Nature that ART's dual-use screening step used the same evaluators METR and Redwood Research have been embedded on since July. The Cambridge lab's operating charter reserves 12 percent of its compute budget for adversarial screening of its own agents.

Anthropic's board asks shareholders to grant seven co-founders a Palantir-style 50.1 percent voting bloc

The Information · 2026-09-23 · Source ↗

Ten votes per share for the seven co-founders subject to a 2 percent equity floor each; conversion triggers only when the count drops below three. The leak arrives the same afternoon as the Akamai $11.6 billion deal and 48 hours before the S-1.

The Information's Amir Efrati reported Wednesday afternoon that Anthropic's board asked shareholders to approve a Palantir-style dual-class share structure handing the company's seven co-founders 50.1 percent of voting power. Each founder receives Class B stock with ten votes per share, subject to a 2 percent equity floor per co-founder. Conversion to single-class Class A stock triggers only when the count of qualifying co-founders drops below three, and the mechanism preserves the bloc across future dilution rounds regardless of overall equity share. The seven are: Dario Amodei, Daniela Amodei, Tom Brown, Chris Olah, Sam McCandlish, Jack Clark, and Jared Kaplan.

The structure sits inside a broader corporate-governance restructuring the board approved at a Wednesday morning session. A three-member Long-Term Benefit Trust — Yasmin Belkhyr (chair), Zico Kolter, Neil Buddy Shah — retains veto authority over any change to the company's Responsible Scaling Policy, the constitution, or the Frontier Red Team's independence charter. A "sunrise" provision (triggered by any regulatory action requiring board reconstitution) automatically extends the LTBT's veto to include acquisitions or asset sales above $50 billion.

"We are asking shareholders to approve a governance framework that keeps decisions about capability release and safety commitments with the people who wrote the founding documents. That is not permanent, but it is the runway we think we need."— Anthropic proxy statement excerpt, published as an S-1 exhibit

Why the timing is loaded

The leak lands the same afternoon Anthropic announced a $11.6 billion seven-year Akamai infrastructure commitment (Reuters expanded it to $20 billion with expansion rights, with a warrant for roughly 5 percent of Akamai's equity), 48 hours before Friday's S-1 filing at $2.4 trillion. Two of the seven co-founders' side letters were disclosed as S-1 exhibits and indicate intent to sell down inside the lockup window, though not below the 2 percent equity floor that would trigger their bloc-exit clause. The structure gives Anthropic's board the ability to release Titan (the next-generation model referenced in the S-1's use of proceeds) without requiring a shareholder vote on capability policy.

"You can debate the wisdom of founder-controlled voting until the cows come home. But at a $2.4 trillion valuation with a fully-realized safety agenda, the market has to have someone accountable. The mechanism names them."— Yasmin Belkhyr, LTBT chair, in an on-record statement to The Information

Institutional shareholders NEA, Fidelity and T. Rowe Price signalled support the same afternoon; Menlo Ventures and Spark Capital abstained on the initial governance vote and are expected to abstain again at the shareholder meeting. Axios's parallel Wednesday report — that a leaked White House policy memo casts Amodei as the face of "AI doomerism" — sits alongside the governance move as the second-front pressure Anthropic is navigating through the S-1 window.
