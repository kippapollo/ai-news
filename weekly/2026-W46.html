After 33 years, Nvidia's board accepts Jensen Huang's resignation

Nvidia's board announced Thursday November 12 that Jensen Huang had resigned as president, chief executive and a director, effective immediately. CFO Colette Kress stepped in as interim CEO while retaining the CFO role; Harvey C. Jones was named the company's first-ever chair. The announcement came four days after the board opened an independent probe — run by Wachtell, Lipton, Rosen & Katz and former acting US Attorney Joon H. Kim — into reports that Huang was targeted by a foreign intelligence operation through his contact with Alice Huang, a former executive at Singapore-based data-centre company Megaspeed. Nvidia said it had "no reason to believe that either national security or NVIDIA secrets were compromised." Q3 fiscal 2027 earnings stay on Monday November 17.

NVIDIA Investor Relations · 2026-11-12 · Source ↗

Nvidia's board announced Thursday November 12 that Jensen Huang had resigned as president, chief executive officer and a member of the board, effective immediately. Chief financial officer Colette Kress will serve as interim chief executive while retaining the CFO role until a permanent successor is named. Harvey C. Jones, a decade-long director, was appointed the company's first-ever chair of the board. The announcement was a one-page press release from NVIDIA Investor Relations that gave no reason for the departure and included no letter from Huang.

The resignation came four days after the November 8 independent probe the board had opened into reports that Huang was targeted by a foreign intelligence operation. Reuters later reported that Huang had affairs with two Nvidia employees; the investigation itself focused on his contact with Alice Huang, a former executive at Singapore-based data-centre company Megaspeed International. A WSJ analysis of customs records reported by Bloomberg put Megaspeed's Nvidia hardware imports at at least $4.6 billion over two years and 136,000 Nvidia GPUs, though Bloomberg said it had found no evidence any of the chips reached China.

"The Board has full confidence in Colette and the leadership team. We expect operations, customer commitments and the product roadmap to continue without interruption."— Harvey C. Jones, Chair of the Board, Nvidia press release, Nov 12

The investigation set up the exit

The board's November 8 announcement opened an inquiry run by Wachtell, Lipton, Rosen & Katz and former acting US Attorney for the Southern District of New York Joon H. Kim. Board statements insisted the company had "no reason to believe that either national security or NVIDIA secrets were compromised." Huang, through the company, said he supported the inquiry and would cooperate fully. A WSJ-reported criminal investigation into Megaspeed was transferred from Commerce to the Justice Department over the week. Three House committees and the Senate Banking Committee chair had asked Huang to testify before the November 12 resignation; Fortune's Sebastian Herrera noted on November 9 that Huang had held the CEO post for 33 years, that Nvidia had no chief operating officer, that about 60 people reported directly to him, that he held a 3.56 percent stake without super-voting shares and that Nvidia had operated without a chief compliance officer.

The shareholder pushback

Altimeter Capital's Brad Gerstner and Atreides Management's Gavin Baker posted a letter to Nvidia's board on X Friday November 13 urging the directors to weigh every option for permanent leadership, including "the potential return of Jensen Huang." They asked for "a slow, measured, and deliberate process regarding the future leadership of Nvidia" and said the company would be "capably led" by Kress in the interim. The letter stopped short of calling for Huang's immediate reinstatement, noting Huang "has earned the right for a deliberate process that avoids any needless rush to replace him." As of June 30, Altimeter held 9.4 million Nvidia shares worth about $1.9 billion — Nvidia was its largest disclosed holding — and Atreides held 1.2 million shares worth about $250 million. By Saturday the Financial Times reported Huang was already discussing a comeback with allies; Elon Musk had signed a petition on X to reinstate him and warned that without Huang "critical deals will be lost."

"We will continue advancing the work that has made NVIDIA a leader in accelerated computing and AI. Our focus remains on our customers, partners, developers and shareholders."— Colette Kress, Interim CEO and CFO, Nvidia press release, Nov 12

Nvidia's Q3 fiscal 2027 earnings call stays on Monday November 17 as scheduled, where Kress will be the company's first voice on investor-call duty without Huang in 33 years. The quarter was already set up as the test of whether Nvidia's $500-plus-billion-run-rate data-centre franchise held up through the data-centre political backlash that W45 cataloged. Kress inherits that quarter, an open independent inquiry, a Congressional subpoena list, an activist-letter campaign and an ex-CEO in talks about returning — on a one-week timeline the board did not plan.

Gemini Robotics 2.5 hits 96 percent on a Mercedes-Benz line; DeepMind open-sources ASIMOV-Jailbreak

AI Weekly · 2026-11-11 · Source ↗

The vision-language-action model ran Apptronik's Apollo 2 humanoid on Mercedes-Benz lines in Kecskemét and Sindelfingen picking metal brackets from bulk bins. DeepMind released it through Agile Robots, Apptronik and Boston Dynamics and open-sourced the ASIMOV-Jailbreak embodied-safety benchmark the same day.

Google DeepMind introduced Gemini Robotics 2.5 on Wednesday November 11 with a 96 percent success rate on a Mercedes-Benz proof-of-concept in Kecskemét, Hungary and Sindelfingen, Germany. The task was pulling metal brackets from bulk bins into sequencing containers; the active production lines were not a controlled lab. Across more than 200 evaluation tasks the model reports a 30 to 100 percent success-rate range, with the harder multi-step jobs still landing between 40 and 75 percent per DeepMind's own charts. Across the task set the new generation runs 20 to 30 percent faster than the prior version. Per The Decoder's independent read, DeepMind also reports 33 percent fewer safety incidents near obstacles and 43 percent fewer near people.

The model is a vision-language-action model running alongside Gemini Robotics ER, which handles task-level reasoning. ER breaks a workstation sequence into actions; 2.5 controls the hands and arms. The release supports Apptronik's Apollo 2 humanoid and the Franka Duo platform with two-fingered parallel grippers natively, with partners Agile Robots SE, Apptronik and Boston Dynamics running it on their own bodies. DeepMind did not open an API: Gemini Robotics 2.5 is going out only through those robotics partners.

"This is what we built Apollo for: useful work alongside people in real industrial settings."— Jeff Cardenas, Apptronik Co-founder and CEO

The ASIMOV-Jailbreak benchmark

DeepMind released ASIMOV-Jailbreak alongside the model as an open-source embodied-safety benchmark with hundreds of adversarial prompts. The benchmark is scoped to embodied agents, not chat-tier jailbreaks. DeepMind did not post a Gemini Robotics 2.5 jailbreak-resistance score in the launch material — the 99.5 percent resistance figure that circulated the same week belongs to Gemini 4 Argon, not to the robotics model. CellCog noted DeepMind did not publish a technical paper for Robotics 2.5 and did not name the baselines, so the 96 percent Mercedes number cannot be read against a comparison.

"The road from 96 percent to near-human reliability is a steep one."— Kanishka Rao, DeepMind Robotics Director (via Heise)

What the pilot actually showed

Analysts pointed out that human workers performing the same bracket-picking task are effectively error-free; 4 percent failure at the pace of Apollo 2 still generates an industrial-engineering problem. DeepMind framed the pilot as a proof-of-concept rather than a line-worthy deployment. Mercedes-Benz did not commit to a unit deployment schedule, and no partner published a cost-per-task figure. Across the week the story stayed in the "frontier moves out of the chat window and onto the line" column — matching the AlphaGenome 2 single-cell release the same day and Google's confidential-inference TPU announcement 48 hours earlier.

AlphaGenome 2 adds methylation and cell-type conditioning, nearly doubles causal variants

Google DeepMind · 2026-11-11 · Source ↗

DeepMind released AlphaGenome 2 with DNA methylation as a new readout, cell-type conditioning that resolves heart muscle and immune cells separately, and 30 percent causal-variant identification on the TenK10K immune-cell dataset against 16 percent for AlphaGenome 1 fine-tuned on single-cell data.

Google DeepMind released AlphaGenome 2 on November 11 as a conditional single-cell model that predicts DNA effects for one biological condition at a time, taking a million-letter DNA sequence as input. Where the first AlphaGenome predicted many tissues in parallel, the new version resolves specialized cells — the DeepMind post gives heart muscle and blood immune cells as examples. Added readout: DNA methylation, which DeepMind calls "a widely requested readout" and which sits alongside the first model's gene expression, histone modifications and chromatin accessibility. The post credits lead authors Adam Gayoso, Natasha Latysheva, Pushmeet Kohli and Žiga Avsec.

On the TenK10K dataset of 28 primary immune cell types, AlphaGenome 2 identified causal variants at 30 percent, against 16 percent for AlphaGenome 1 fine-tuned on single-cell data and other specialized tools. Directional gene-effect identification improved from 23 to 43 percent. Overall, the post reports the model identifies nearly twice as many variants that change gene expression as the previous state of the art. Training data is cited as over 107 million cell profiles across 68 tissues. The model supports cross-modality imputation: given one measurement such as single-cell gene activity, it can predict unmeasured properties such as chromatin accessibility.

"AlphaGenome 2 adapts directly to new single-cell data without retraining."— Prof. Sarah Teichmann, Co-founder of the Human Cell Atlas, University of Cambridge

Access and applications

AlphaGenome 2 is available today for non-commercial research through the AlphaGenome API; commercial research is in private preview through Google Cloud. DeepMind said "AlphaGenome 2 Skills" will be released in Google Antigravity soon. Researchers can express interest through the AlphaGenome community forum or submit a use-case form by November 30 for possible extra quota and credits. The model is for research use only, with no clinical or diagnostic validation.

Peter Donnelly, CEO of Genomics Limited, commented on the drug-discovery application and said the company plans to incorporate the model into its Mystra platform. The post catalogs applications including mapping enhancer-gene links in primary human cells (brain astrocytes listed), pinpointing causal GWAS variants, cell types and effect direction for traits like height and type 2 diabetes with precomputed predictions, reproducing a published IBD-linked variant effect on the ETS2 gene in gut macrophages, and distinguishing vaccine-activated T cells from resting ones in COVID-19 mRNA vaccine data. One finding: a severe-COVID-19-linked variant disrupts gene activity more strongly in activated T cells than resting ones.

Google ships TPU 8tx for confidential inference and TPU 8i Max for long context

Constellation Research · 2026-11-09 · Source ↗

Google expanded the eighth-generation TPU portfolio: TPU 8tx pairs with AMD EPYC for hardware-enforced confidential inference and claims 2x lower latency than TPU 8t; TPU 8i Max packages two TPU 8i chips with double the SRAM and HBM bandwidth for million-token context.

Google announced TPU 8tx and TPU 8i Max on November 9, extending the TPU 8t and TPU 8i chips the company first unveiled at Google Cloud Next in April 2026. TPU 8tx pairs the same silicon as the TPU 8t training chip with AMD EPYC hosts and adds hardware-level confidential computing that keeps sensitive data and model weights isolated while in use. AMD called it "the first cloud TPU system with hardware-enforced confidential computing." Google reports up to 2x lower latency against TPU 8t on the confidential-inference workload. TPU 8i Max packages two TPU 8i chips in one module with double the SRAM and double the HBM bandwidth of a single TPU 8i to attack the memory wall on long-context workloads.

Google positioned the pair as building blocks: 8t for scaled-up training, 8i for low-latency reasoning, 8tx for confidential compute and 8i Max for compute-density per rack at long context. Google did not announce pricing or general-availability timing for either new system. Confidential inference is the sharper read: it lets regulated-vertical tenants ship model weights and workload data to Google's TPUs without trust in Google's operator plane. The AMD CPU pairing is the first acknowledged joint Google-AMD system.

"The first cloud TPU system with hardware-enforced confidential computing."— AMD on the TPU 8tx/EPYC pairing

Where this lands against Nvidia

The release is Google's second TPU step in seven months. The first Google Cloud Next generation was aimed at training throughput; TPU 8tx is aimed at the piece of Nvidia's enterprise business that hinges on confidential compute. Anthropic's $10 billion strategic placement in the amended S-1 week keeps Nvidia inside the Anthropic cap table, but the TeraWulf-Kentucky TPU-only clause from W45 and the $10 billion concurrent Google line-item from this week keep the Google TPU story as the inferencing alternative Anthropic is paying to build. One Singularity Moments headline framed the package: "Google thinks stapling two TPUs together will break Nvidia's grip."

According to 9to5Mac, Creator Studio updates scheduled for November 2026 are expected to extend Intelligence Usage Packs to Final Cut Pro, Pixelmator Pro and iWork applications; that extension is a press expectation rather than an Apple confirmation. "Creator Studio apps can be purchased individually or accessed via subscription, but the extra usage packs require an active Creator Studio subscription."— MacDailyNews summary of Apple's purchase mechanics

What this signals
