SpaceX buys Cursor for $60 billion, the largest AI-tools acquisition on record

SpaceX closed its all-stock takeover of Anysphere at market close on Friday, August 15, folding Cursor into the newly-named SpaceXAI unit alongside Grok Build, Grok Bot and the freshly-shipped Grok 4.6 frontier model. The $60 billion sticker is a 105 percent premium to Cursor's June direct-listing IPO price and roughly 12x its trailing $5 billion ARR. Morgan Stanley projects $2.5 billion of incremental 2026 revenue from the combination and $13 billion in 2027. Anthropic and OpenAI both filed European Commission comments Friday afternoon urging deeper regulatory scrutiny of third-party model-access foreclosure, previewing what looks like a multi-jurisdiction antitrust fight over Cursor's future support of non-xAI models.

Yahoo Finance · The Decoder · TechCrunch · 2026-08-15 · Source ↗

SpaceX and Anysphere jointly announced the closing of a $60 billion all-stock takeover at the New York Stock Exchange close on Friday, August 15. The deal folds Anysphere, the four-year-old company behind the Cursor code editor, into the newly renamed SpaceXAI unit alongside the Grok Build agent product, Grok Bot, and Grok 4.6, the frontier model xAI shipped Monday morning through the SpaceX-controlled inference stack. Cursor CEO Michael Truell and cofounder Sualeh Asif both stay on and now report to SpaceXAI head Igor Babuschkin.

At $60 billion the transaction is the largest software-company acquisition on record and the largest AI-tools acquisition on any measure. The sticker is a 105 percent premium to the direct-listing IPO price Cursor traded at on June 3, roughly 12x Cursor's trailing $5 billion annualised revenue, and 6x the $10 billion valuation Anysphere raised at in November 2025. SpaceX financed the deal entirely in stock, with the newly-issued SPCX shares subject to a two-year lock-up on the Anysphere founders and a three-year retention grant for the roughly 380 engineers who joined SpaceXAI on Monday, August 18.

The strategic logic

The strategic case Musk laid out on the Friday-afternoon SpaceX conference call was that Cursor's 5 million daily active developers represent the single largest concentrated cohort of AI users on the planet, and that owning the distribution surface for those developers gives SpaceXAI a moat that vertical model quality alone cannot match. Morgan Stanley's initiation note published over the weekend projects $2.5 billion of incremental 2026 revenue from the combination (Cursor plus Grok Build) and $13 billion in 2027, on the assumption that non-xAI models remain accessible inside Cursor at parity pricing through calendar 2027.

That last assumption is the piece the deal hinges on for regulatory purposes. Cursor's current model-router surfaces Claude Opus 5, GPT-5.6 Sol, Grok 4.6, Gemini 3.6 Pro and DeepSeek V4-Pro as roughly co-equal options, with per-token pricing passed through at cost plus a small margin. If SpaceX preserves that neutrality, Cursor remains a competitive marketplace with SpaceX collecting rent as landlord. If SpaceX narrows the router to favour Grok models, third-party frontier labs lose direct developer distribution.

"Cursor is the most important developer-productivity surface in the industry. Owning it means owning the venue where developers choose their models. We plan to keep that choice wide open."— Elon Musk, SpaceX post-close conference call (2026-08-15)

Anthropic and OpenAI file for European scrutiny

Both Anthropic and OpenAI filed comments with the European Commission's DG COMP inside Friday's business day, before the close of trading, urging deeper regulatory review of third-party model-access foreclosure. Anthropic's filing highlights Claude's roughly 42 percent share of Cursor's model routing over the trailing 90 days and asks the Commission to require a formal Article 22 review under the EU Merger Regulation's residual jurisdiction, notwithstanding that neither party has revenue in the EU crossing the standard notification thresholds. OpenAI's filing is procedurally similar but adds a request for behavioral remedies: specifically, a five-year post-closing commitment that non-xAI models remain available inside Cursor at rates no higher than xAI models are offered to third-party editor competitors.

The US Federal Trade Commission has not commented publicly as of Sunday evening but Bloomberg reports that DOJ Antitrust Division staff have opened an informal inquiry. The DOJ inquiry is not itself a challenge; it is a paper-review process that would typically resolve in favor of the transaction absent a specific competitive theory. The theory Anthropic and OpenAI are pushing (foreclosure of the developer-tools distribution channel to competing models) is novel enough that it is likely to require months of investigation before any enforcement position crystallises.

"The Cursor router is the primary distribution surface for frontier models to enterprise developers. A single-owner acquirer of that surface with vertical integration into a competing frontier model raises straightforward foreclosure concerns."— Anthropic European Commission comment brief (2026-08-15)

The comparable that matters

The transaction is the second-largest AI acquisition of 2026 by dollar value, behind only the SpaceX-Cursor deal itself's own precedent set by Nvidia's June 2026 acquisition of Weights & Biases. It comfortably eclipses last year's $32 billion Adobe-Figma restart (which Adobe had abandoned in 2023 over EU antitrust concerns and re-closed at a higher valuation this January), the $28 billion Microsoft-Nuance deal, and the $27 billion Meta-Zoom acquisition. In the AI-tools category specifically, the next-largest deal on record is Google's $2.7 billion acqui-hire of Character.AI in August 2024, making the SpaceX-Cursor transaction more than 22x the previous benchmark.

The Cursor deal came at the tail end of the largest week for AI M&A in 2026. Anthropic disclosed on Thursday that it is in $6 billion talks to acquire Israeli video-and-inference startup Decart, its own largest-ever acquisition. Cognition opened talks Wednesday at a $40 billion valuation on $1 billion ARR, less than five months after its $26 billion round on $492 million ARR. Lovable closed a $400 million Series C at $13.3 billion. Legora is now chasing $10 billion on $150 million ARR. The pattern the four transactions collectively establish is that the enterprise-AI M&A market has moved past traditional valuation multiples and is now pricing on strategic-optionality-plus-lockup, with individual transactions large enough to shift the S&P sector composition when they close.

What Decart brings

The strategic value to Anthropic is concentrated in DOS. Serving Claude across four accelerator families with per-token-cost-competitive throughput requires either a large internal kernel-engineering team or acquisition of a compiler stack that automates the work. Decart's DOS technology is the most mature multi-vendor compiler in the open market and would compress Anthropic's runway to per-accelerator optimisation by 18 to 24 months.

"Anthropic's compute strategy is now the most complex in the industry across four accelerator families and, next year, our own silicon. DOS is the compiler layer that makes that strategy operable at scale."— Krishna Rao, Anthropic CFO (Fortune interview, 2026-08-14)

The attack

The core observation the paper builds on is that even when the reasoning tokens themselves are encrypted at the API layer, the output-token distribution conditioned on the reasoning is not encrypted. If an attacker has access to a large corpus of (prompt, output) pairs from a provider, and the provider's model exposes deterministic-per-seed behaviour, the attacker can build a distributional signature that maps back to canonical reasoning fragments.
