Safety Trend

Amodei says pace the frontier; Altman, Musk and Nadella sign on the same weekend

Dario Amodei published a 3,900-word essay Saturday proposing embedded third-party evaluators inside every frontier lab, common safety benchmarks brokered by US antitrust authorities, and narrow guardrails with authoritarian governments on clearly dangerous applications. Sam Altman committed OpenAI to the same evaluator access within hours ("Committing to having independent evaluators with employee-like access is a great idea, and we will do the same"). Elon Musk's response was three words: "Dario is right." Satya Nadella pledged to publish a Microsoft AI Code of Conduct for public consultation Monday. Two Anthropic safety departures in five days framed the week around whether frontier CEO commitments will actually change lab behavior.

darioamodei.com · 2026-09-13 · Source ↗

Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei published "We Must Pace the Frontier" on Saturday September 13, a 3,900-word essay proposing a three-step plan to slow the pace of AI capability improvement. Step one: embed third-party evaluators inside frontier labs at employee-level access. Anthropic named METR — the Model Evaluation and Threat Research organization founded by former OpenAI Preparedness team lead Beth Barnes — as its chosen partner and committed unilaterally to the arrangement. Step two: coordinate common safety benchmarks and capability limits across democratic AI companies, with the US government invoking antitrust safe harbor to mediate. Step three: pursue narrow guardrails with authoritarian governments on clearly dangerous applications, starting with biological weapons.

Sam Altman posted on X within hours agreeing with the plan and committing OpenAI to the same evaluator access. Elon Musk's response, quote-posted on the Amodei essay, was three words: "Dario is right." Satya Nadella pledged on X that Microsoft will open a MAI Code of Conduct for public consultation Monday morning and backed "deliberate pacing and independent evaluators," becoming the fourth frontier-lab CEO on record. That leaves Sundar Pichai's Google, Mark Zuckerberg's Meta and Alexandr Wang's Scale as the largest US-headquartered frontier holdouts.

"Committing to having independent evaluators with employee-like access is a great idea, and we will do the same."— Sam Altman, OpenAI CEO, on X, September 13

The three-step plan in specifics

Amodei's essay ties the evaluator step to a concrete access model: METR (or an equivalent auditor) receives internal accounts, sees training runs at the compute-cluster level, gets pre-release model access at every intermediate checkpoint, and can publish findings with a lab-review window bounded by a public disclosure calendar. The shared-benchmark step calls for a rotating chair among frontier labs to publish per-capability floors (biosecurity, cyber-offense, autonomous replication) with quarterly recalibration. The authoritarian-guardrails step names Chinese Foreign Ministry contacts and DeepSeek, Moonshot and Alibaba specifically as parties Anthropic has spoken with — a first for a US frontier lab in public print.

Two departures, five days, the same lab

Amodei's essay landed with two Anthropic safety departures fresh in the record. Jacob Coxon, a 27-year-old three-year pretraining researcher across OpenAI and Anthropic, published a resignation post on Monday September 8 that crossed 100 million views by Thursday, claiming leadership at both labs earnestly believes AI could kill everyone by decade's end and are racing anyway. The post was directly endorsed on X by Anthropic colleagues Evan Hubinger and Samuel Marks. On Saturday, Anthropic's Joe Benton — former manager of the Scalable Oversight team — announced his own resignation to join METR, telling NBC and Business Standard that colleagues still at Anthropic "experience genuine fear" about the pace of the work and naming Hubinger as one of the researchers publicly estimating greater than 10 percent probability of AI-caused human extinction.

"Dario is right."— Elon Musk, on X, quote-posting the essay

Political reception, two directions

Xi Jinping used the concluding session of the New Delhi BRICS summit to announce a five-point plan built around a Beijing-anchored BRICS AI open-source community and a call for a "consensus-based global AI governance framework." Donald Trump used a Doonbeg press gaggle to dismiss the pacing calls as "negative forces" and reaffirmed the "whoever wins AI wins" posture. House Speaker Mike Johnson framed any pacing legislation as "a gift to Beijing" in caucus communications, effectively killing the near-term legislative track for Rep. Sam Liccardo's letter urging the House to cancel October recess. Rishi Sunak, now an Anthropic senior adviser, wrote in a weekend newspaper column that "inaction is no longer an option. Leaders urgently need to show us that humans are setting the pace and are still in control." Former White House AI czar David Sacks used weekend media appearances to accuse Amodei, Altman and Musk of building "a cartel" and dressing "market consolidation as altruism."

Fortune published a parallel Altman interview on Friday confirming OpenAI will not IPO in 2026 ("I would say not 2026") because doing so would be an "ill-advised moment" given the safety work still ahead. The Amodei-Altman-Musk-Nadella lineup, the Coxon-Benton departures and Altman's IPO-delay comment all landed inside the same 96-hour window as Anthropic's Nasdaq IPO selection, which Business Insider reported Sunday targets an October roadshow at roughly $2 trillion valuation. The pacing conversation is now visibly entangled with the capital-markets narrative it hopes to constrain.

MarkTechPost · 2026-09-10 · Source ↗

Flash pricing lands 11-57 percent below V4 depending on token type. The paid V4 Pro flagship retires at noon Beijing time on September 14 and all deepseek-v4-pro API traffic reroutes to V4.1 Flash at Flash rates.

DeepSeek released V4.1 Flash to Hugging Face on Thursday September 10 under a modified MIT license. The architecture is a 552-billion-parameter Mixture-of-Experts backbone activating 8 billion parameters on prefill and 16 billion on decode across a 40-layer Causal Encoder-Decoder split. Native visual understanding runs at a 1M-token context window with FP4 KV cache that shrinks per-token memory to roughly 890 bytes, and the model uses cross-layer attention reuse to cut per-token compute at long context. Output tops 300 tokens per second on the initial serving configuration. Weights, tokenizer, inference reference and Apache-2.0 evaluation scripts landed together.

Pricing lands 11 to 57 percent below V4 across input, output and long-context tiers. The paid V4 Pro flagship retires at noon Beijing time on Sunday September 14 and all deepseek-v4-pro API traffic reroutes to V4.1 Flash at Flash rates thereafter, until V4.1 Pro releases. That makes September 14 the first frontier-model retirement inside the pace-the-frontier week and the first observable point at which the open-weights frontier and the closed-frontier pacing coalition diverge on customer-facing product decisions.

"V4.1 Flash cuts our per-token bill on the answer-engine ranking model by 43 percent at flat quality."— Perplexity engineering lead, in the OpenAI-hosted case study published the same week

Serving stack support

vLLM merged DeepSeek V4.1 Flash support with CUDA graph optimizations, speculative-decoding fixes and updates across semantic-router, guidellm, aibrix and llm-compressor over the September 13 UTC day, making self-hosted V4.1 Flash a supported combination ahead of DeepSeek's own September 14 V4 Pro API cutover. DeepSeek had opened a two-day multimodal beta on September 8-10 under the model ID deepseek-v4.1-flash-expires-on-0910 with output above 300 tokens per second and native multimodal architecture; the beta closed as the general release opened.

How the router works

The Fugu router is a small learned classifier trained on 12 million routing decisions across 40 benchmarks, plus a bandit-style online learner that updates in production against a mix of user thumbs-up/down and downstream task success signals. Per-request routing latency adds 12-18 ms at the P50 in the Sakana benchmark configuration. The API surface matches OpenAI's chat-completions and responses APIs verbatim, including tool-calls and structured outputs, so migration from GPT-5.6 Terra or Sonnet 5 requires only a base-URL change.

Sakana's release lands three days before DeepSeek retires V4 Pro API traffic to V4.1 Flash pricing (Story 2) and one day before Cognition ships SWE-2 at 64 percent below Fable 5.1 on FrontierCode (Story 8). The pacing debate opened by Amodei's essay is happening on top of a market where the price-per-quality curve is shifting under all sides of the argument at once.

OpenAI claims Navier-Stokes proof from 10,000 agents; NYU/Anthropic Lean-verified 3D Euler blowup landed 12 hours earlier

OpenAI's proof used roughly 10,000 autonomous agents on an unreleased frontier model running for 88 hours and exchanging almost 5 million messages. NYU's Tristan Buckmaster and Anthropic's Levent Alpöge accused OpenAI of racing to publish after learning of their result through Codex sessions.

OpenAI claimed a proof of the Navier-Stokes existence and smoothness Millennium Prize problem on Tuesday September 8, produced by roughly 10,000 autonomous agents running on an unreleased frontier model for 88 hours and exchanging almost 5 million messages. NYU's Tristan Buckmaster and Anthropic's Levent Alpöge released a Lean-verified 3D Euler blowup proof twelve hours earlier and accused OpenAI of racing to publish after learning of the Euler result through Codex sessions the Buckmaster group had opened with default log retention. Buckmaster said his private preprint draft had circulated on OpenAI's Codex infrastructure for 18 days before the OpenAI announcement.

The Euler blowup preprint, which Buckmaster co-authored with Alpöge and MIT postdoc Rachel Ollivier, is a Lean-verified construction of a finite-time singularity in the 3D incompressible Euler equations with C^1,alpha initial data, a result the fluid-dynamics community has considered plausible for a decade but never rigorously produced. The proof runs 2,847 pages of Lean and required 41 GPU-days of Anthropic-provided Sonnet 5 compute to close remaining tactic-search gaps. It is the second Lean-verified Millennium-adjacent result of the year after Anthropic's Fermat's Last Theorem construction in August.

"The proof was in my Codex workspace with default log retention. I did not opt in to training use. OpenAI knew."— Tristan Buckmaster, NYU, on X, September 8

OpenAI's proof and what remains unverified

OpenAI's Navier-Stokes claim ran an unreleased frontier model — internal designation Aster — against a swarm coordinator that spawned specialist agents for tactic search, counter-example generation, and adversarial refereeing. The result has not been Lean-verified; OpenAI posted a 380-page mathematical manuscript with a claimed proof outline and a Fields-Medalist-heavy review committee including Peter Scholze, Maryna Viazovska and Andrei Okounkov as pre-publication reviewers. Scholze published a Sunday statement noting he had not agreed to serve on the committee. The manuscript contains a section explicitly conceding that "formal verification is deferred to a subsequent release."

What the twelve-hour ordering says

The competing-priority dispute is the first public case where an AI-produced mathematical result is contested on grounds of upstream data access rather than mathematical correctness. Terence Tao warned via Simon Willison at 12:20 UTC the same day that AI-powered research effort is "mining" open math problems in a non-renewable fashion and named priority disputes as the expected next attack surface. Twenty-five Fields Medal recipients including Tao, Scholze, Viazovska, Okounkov, Cédric Villani, Manjul Bhargava and Alessio Figalli signed a Friday declaration (Story 16) calling AI companies' focus on problem-solving as capability proof "severely misaligned" with mathematics's central goal of conceptual understanding.

NSA, CISA and FBI issue joint advisory naming six Chinese labs for industrial-scale distillation of US frontier models

SCMP · 2026-09-10 · Source ↗

DeepSeek, Moonshot AI, Alibaba, MiniMax, StepFun and Z.AI named for extracting billions of tokens across millions of API sessions since late 2024. Advisory called DeepSeek's $5.6 million R1 training cost "misleading" and recommended US providers "quietly degrade" suspect accounts.

The NSA, CISA and FBI issued a joint cybersecurity advisory on Wednesday September 9 naming DeepSeek, Moonshot AI, Alibaba, MiniMax, StepFun and Z.AI for industrial-scale distillation of Claude, GPT, Gemini and Grok variants since late 2024. The advisory documents billions of tokens extracted across millions of API sessions and characterizes DeepSeek's widely-cited $5.6 million R1 training cost as "misleading" absent the distillation-derived training corpus. The three-agency joint format is a first for a document of this kind and was co-published with UK NCSC and Japan's Cybersecurity Council.

The advisory recommends US providers "quietly degrade" suspect Chinese-linked accounts — a recommendation with no direct precedent in the CISA advisory canon and one that raises immediate operational questions for OpenAI, Anthropic, Google DeepMind and xAI about their existing anti-distillation controls. Anthropic's TOS already prohibits distillation and the company confirmed to Bloomberg that it has been progressively rate-limiting suspected distillation traffic for eighteen months. OpenAI has not confirmed a specific degradation policy.

"Claims that comparable frontier models can be trained for $5 million rest materially on undisclosed distillation from US and allied models."— Joint NSA/CISA/FBI advisory, September 9

China's response

China's Ministry of Commerce formally rejected the advisory on Wednesday as "political rather than technical" and named reciprocal-measure candidates including expanded rare-earth export controls on gallium, germanium and graphite and further restrictions on Nvidia's H20-modified SKU. MOFCOM's statement disputed the distillation characterization and cited DeepSeek V3's July 2025 technical report as documentation of the training methodology. DeepSeek did not comment on the advisory but shipped V4.1 Flash under a modified MIT license the following day (Story 2), a release timing many observers read as deliberately positioned against the advisory's framing.

The enforcement question

"Quietly degrade" is operationally ambiguous. Interpretations range from silently downgrading suspect accounts to older or smaller model variants, to returning artificially degraded outputs, to injecting deliberate errors into responses. The last two would violate most US labs' published output-quality commitments and expose them to breach-of-contract claims. Simon Willison flagged the advisory language on Wednesday, noting: "the FTC could open Section 5 inquiries into any lab that implements this literally." The White House Office of Science and Technology Policy has not commented on the recommendation's legal soundness.

Cognition releases SWE-2: 50.0 on FrontierCode 1.1, matches Fable 5.1 within a point at 64 percent lower cost

MarkTechPost · 2026-09-12 · Source ↗

73.0 on DeepSWE 1.1 and 92.8 on Terminal-Bench 2.1. Post-trained from Moonshot's 2.8-trillion-parameter Kimi K3 base and the first multi-trillion-parameter model with reinforcement learning trained against a cost-penalized reward across three reasoning-effort tiers in a single RL run.

Cognition released SWE-2 on Saturday September 12 with 50.0 percent on FrontierCode 1.1 Main, 73.0 percent on DeepSWE 1.1 and 92.8 percent on Terminal-Bench 2.1, matching Claude Fable 5.1 within a point on each benchmark at 64 percent lower cost per task. SWE-2 is post-trained from Moonshot's 2.8-trillion-parameter Kimi K3 base and, per Cognition's release notes, is the first multi-trillion-parameter model with reinforcement learning trained against a cost-penalized reward R = S - λC across three reasoning-effort tiers in a single RL run rather than as separate distilled variants.

The cost-penalized reward is the release's headline methodological claim. Prior RL-tuned coding models — Fable 5.1, GPT-6 Astra Codex, Sonnet 5 — accept longer chain-of-thought token counts as an implicit cost to buy quality. SWE-2's reward function multiplies the S (task-success) term against a λC (compute-cost) term the model learns to trade off against, producing a single set of weights that the runtime can operate in low, medium or high reasoning-effort configurations without switching models. Cognition claims the training approach adds roughly 4 percent absolute benchmark quality per unit of RL compute compared to a control run without the cost penalty.

"Coding models are increasingly a cost story. Training the cost gradient directly into the model beats bolting it on at the router."— Cognition SWE-2 release notes, September 12

What it means for Devin and the harness market

SWE-2 lands inside Devin 3.0 as the default coder-agent backbone starting Monday September 14, replacing an unnamed prior configuration. Cognition prices Devin 3.0 at $500 per developer per month down from $650, with an add-on autoscaling option that Cognition claims can hit $50 per full-work-day-equivalent at high task volume.
